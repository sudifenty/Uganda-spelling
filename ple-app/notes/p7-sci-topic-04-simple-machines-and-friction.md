## 1. SIMPLE MACHINES

### Definitions

**Machine:** A device that makes work easier.

**Effort:** The force applied to the machine by the user.

**Load:** The weight or resistance the machine works against.

**Fulcrum (pivot):** The fixed point about which a lever turns.

### How machines make work easier

- They allow a **small effort to move a big load**.
- They change the **direction** of a force (pulling down to lift up).
- They make work **faster and more comfortable**.
- They let us work at a **distance** from danger.

### Mechanical advantage

**Mechanical advantage (M.A.) = Load ÷ Effort**

*Example:* A load of 600 N is lifted using an effort of 150 N.
M.A. = 600 ÷ 150 = **4** (the machine multiplies the force 4 times)

*A machine has a mechanical advantage of 5 and lifts a load of 450 N. What effort is used?*
Effort = 450 ÷ 5 = **90 N**

## 2. THE PRINCIPLE OF MOMENTS

### Definition

**Moment of a force:** The turning effect of a force, found by multiplying the force by its distance from the fulcrum.

### The principle

**Load × distance of load from the fulcrum = Effort × distance of effort from the fulcrum**

When these are equal, the lever **balances**.

### Worked examples

1. *A load of 40 N is placed 2 m from the fulcrum. An effort acts 4 m from the fulcrum on the other side. Find the effort needed.*
   40 × 2 = E × 4 → 80 = 4E → E = **20 N**

2. *A see-saw balances with a boy of 30 kg sitting 2 m from the pivot. A girl of 20 kg sits on the other side. How far must she sit?*
   30 × 2 = 20 × d → 60 = 20d → d = **3 m**

**Lesson:** the further the effort is from the fulcrum, the **less** effort is needed.

## 3. LEVERS

### Definition

**Lever:** A rigid bar that turns about a fulcrum.

| Class | Order | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **First class** | Fulcrum **between** effort and load | See-saw, pair of scissors, claw hammer, crowbar, pliers, beam balance |
| **Second class** | Load **between** fulcrum and effort | Wheelbarrow, bottle opener, nutcracker, door, oar |
| **Third class** | Effort **between** fulcrum and load | Fishing rod, broom, spade, tongs, human forearm, a pair of forceps |

**A quick way to remember:** in class 1 the **F**ulcrum is in the middle; in class 2 the
**L**oad is in the middle; in class 3 the **E**ffort is in the middle — **F, L, E: 1, 2, 3**.

## 4. OTHER SIMPLE MACHINES

### Inclined plane (ramp or slope)

A slanting surface used to raise a load. It is easier to **push a drum up a plank** than to
lift it straight up, though you must move it a longer distance.
*Examples:* ramps, hilly roads that zigzag, staircases, loading planks.

### Wedge

Two inclined planes joined back to back. It is pushed into something to **split or cut** it.
*Examples:* axe, knife, panga, nail, chisel, needle, teeth, pin.

### Screw

An inclined plane wound round a cylinder. It changes turning movement into a strong
pushing or pulling force.
*Examples:* screws, bolts and nuts, screw jack, vice, drill bit, corkscrew, bottle top.

### Pulleys

A wheel with a groove in which a rope runs.

| Type | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Fixed pulley** | Changes only the direction of the force — you pull down to lift up (M.A. = 1) |
| **Movable pulley** | Reduces the effort needed (M.A. = 2) |
| **Block and tackle** | A system of several pulleys used for very heavy loads, as in cranes and garages |

*Examples:* flag pole, well bucket, crane, lift, curtain rail.

### Wheel and axle

A big wheel fixed to a smaller shaft (the axle). Turning the big wheel gives a strong turn
on the axle.
*Examples:* steering wheel, door knob, bicycle pedals and wheels, windlass on a well, tap handle, screwdriver handle.

### Gears

Toothed wheels that work together to change speed or turning force, as in a bicycle, a
clock or a maize mill.

## 5. FRICTION

### Definition

**Friction:** The force that opposes movement between two surfaces that are in contact.

### Examples of friction in nature

Walking without slipping · a car stopping when brakes are applied · rubbing hands to warm them · a match lighting when struck · a bicycle tyre gripping the road · a parachute slowed by air resistance · a boat slowed by water.

### Useful friction and how to increase it

| Useful friction | Way to increase it |
|---|---|
| Walking and running | Rough soles on shoes; rough ground |
| Braking in vehicles and bicycles | Brake pads and rough tyre treads |
| Writing with a pencil, striking a match | Rough paper, rough match box |
| Holding things in the hand, tying knots | Rough surfaces; using sand or resin |
| Nails and screws holding wood | Rough threads |

Ways of increasing friction: making surfaces **rougher**, adding **treads and grooves**,
increasing the **weight** pressing the surfaces together, using rubber or sand.

### Friction as a nuisance and how to reduce it

| Problem it causes | Way to reduce it |
|---|---|
| Wearing out of tyres, shoes and machine parts | **Lubrication** with oil and grease |
| Making machines heat up | Fitting **ball bearings and rollers** |
| Wasting energy and fuel | **Streamlining** vehicles, boats and aeroplanes |
| Making it hard to push heavy objects | Using **wheels, rollers or trolleys** |
| Noise in machines | **Polishing and smoothing** surfaces |

### Important Points

- Friction always **acts against motion**.
- Friction produces **heat** and **sound**.
- Friction is greater on **rough** surfaces than on smooth ones.
- Without friction we could not walk, write or stop a moving vehicle.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **M.A. = Load ÷ Effort.**
- **Load × load distance = Effort × effort distance** (principle of moments).
- Class 1 — fulcrum in the middle (see-saw, scissors). Class 2 — load in the middle (wheelbarrow). Class 3 — effort in the middle (fishing rod, forearm).
- A **wedge** is two inclined planes; a **screw** is an inclined plane wound round a cylinder.
- A **fixed pulley** only changes direction; a **movable pulley** reduces effort.
- Friction always **opposes motion** and produces **heat**.
- Reduce friction by **lubricating, using ball bearings, rollers, polishing and streamlining**.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Wheelbarrow — second class lever. Scissors — first class. Fishing rod — third class.
- Turning point of a lever — fulcrum.
- Machine that changes only the direction of force — fixed pulley.
- An axe and a knife are examples of a **wedge**.
- A screw jack is used to lift a car — it is a **screw**.
- Oil and grease are used to **reduce** friction.
- Treads on tyres **increase** friction.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. A simple machine is a device that makes work easier. It allows a small effort to move a big load, and it can change the direction of the force applied.
2. **Effort** is the force applied to the machine; **load** is the weight or resistance the machine works against; **fulcrum** is the fixed point about which a lever turns.
3. M.A. = 800 ÷ 200 = **4**
4. Effort = 540 ÷ 6 = **90 N**
5. Load × distance of the load from the fulcrum = effort × distance of the effort from the fulcrum.
6. 50 × 3 = E × 5 → 150 = 5E → E = **30 N**
7. First class — see-saw or scissors; second class — wheelbarrow; third class — fishing rod.
8. **First class** (the fulcrum is between the effort and the load).
9. An inclined plane is a slanting surface used to raise a load. Examples: a ramp and a plank used to roll a drum onto a lorry (also staircases and zigzag hill roads).
10. Wedge: axe, knife (also panga, nail, chisel). Screw: bolt and nut, screw jack (also vice, corkscrew).
11. A fixed pulley only changes the direction of the force, so the effort equals the load; a movable pulley reduces the effort needed, giving a mechanical advantage of about 2.
12. Any three of: steering wheel, door knob, bicycle wheel and pedals, windlass on a well, tap handle, screwdriver.
13. Friction is the force that opposes movement between two surfaces in contact. Examples: brakes stopping a bicycle; shoes gripping the ground when walking; a match lighting when struck.
14. **Increase:** make surfaces rougher; put treads on tyres and shoes; press the surfaces together more firmly. **Reduce:** lubricate with oil or grease; use ball bearings or rollers; polish surfaces or streamline the shape.
15. It wears out tyres, shoes and machine parts, and it wastes energy as heat, so machines use more fuel. (It also causes noise and overheating.)
