# PRIMARY 7 ENGLISH

# TOPIC 3: EXAMINATIONS

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Seven Curriculum, Set One, English Topic 3, 26 periods
(`curriculum/PRIMARY-SEVEN-SET-ONE.pdf`, topic outline page 30 and pages 40–43).
Sub-topics: **3A Preparation for Examinations** and **3B Sitting Examinations**.*

**What you already know:** You have sat tests since P.1. In P.7 you are preparing for your
**first national examination**, so this topic gives you the language, the instructions and
the good habits you need.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- use vocabulary about preparing for and sitting examinations
- read and interpret a timetable, an examination card and a result sheet
- follow oral and written instructions exactly
- use the structures *or else, look forward to, whereas, could, in spite of, despite*
- use adverbs such as *quite* and *rather*, and possessives correctly
- write compositions about examinations

## SUB-TOPIC 3A: PREPARATION FOR EXAMINATIONS

### Vocabulary from the syllabus

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| timetable | A table showing what is done and at what time |
| instructions | Orders telling you what to do and how |
| index number | The number given to a candidate to identify him or her |
| candidate | A person sitting an examination |
| time allowed | The length of time given for a paper |
| examiner | The person who sets or marks the examination |
| examination room | The room where candidates sit the examination |
| answer sheet | The paper on which answers are written |
| examination centre | The school or place where candidates sit |
| briefing | A short talk giving important information before an activity |
| registration | Entering a candidate's names officially for the examination |
| revision | Going over work already learnt |

### How to prepare well

- Make a **revision timetable** and follow it.
- Revise a **little every day** rather than everything on the last night.
- Do **past papers** under the correct time.
- Ask the teacher about anything you do not understand.
- Form a small **discussion group** with serious classmates.
- Keep your **index number, examination card and instruments** safely.
- Eat well, drink water and **get enough rest and sleep** — the syllabus tells the teacher to caution learners about this.
- Do not cram at the last minute; do not use "leakage".

### Reading a timetable, an examination card and a result sheet

| Document | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Examination timetable** | The date, the paper, the starting time and the time allowed |
| **Examination card** | Your names, index number, examination centre and the subjects entered |
| **Result sheet** | The subjects, the score or grade in each, the **aggregate** and the **division** |

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **… or else …** | *You must revise your notes, **or else** you will fail the examination.* |
| **… look forward to …** | *We **look forward to sitting** our final examinations.* |

**Careful:** after *look forward to* the verb takes **-ing**: *I look forward to **meeting**
you* (not "to meet").

### Grammar focus: adverbs of degree

**Quite, rather, very, fairly, too, almost** tell us **how much**.

| Adverb | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **quite** | a good amount, but not completely | *The paper was **quite** easy.* |
| **rather** | more than expected, often about something unwelcome | *The paper was **rather** difficult.* |
| **very** | to a high degree | *She was **very** confident.* |
| **too** | more than is wanted or possible | *The time was **too** short.* |
| **almost** | nearly | *I had **almost** finished.* |

## SUB-TOPIC 3B: SITTING EXAMINATIONS

### Vocabulary from the syllabus

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| invigilator | The person who watches over candidates during an examination |
| supervisor | The officer in charge of an examination centre |
| pass mark | The lowest mark needed to pass |
| aggregate | The total of the grades scored in the subjects |
| division | The grade band a candidate falls in from the aggregate |
| duration | The length of time a paper takes |
| malpractice | Cheating or any dishonest act in an examination |
| leakage | Examination questions getting out before the examination |
| disqualify | To cancel a candidate's results for breaking the rules |
| pass slip | The paper showing a candidate's results |
| certificate | The official document showing what a candidate achieved |

### Rules of the examination room

Arrive **early** · carry your **examination card** and the correct instruments · do not
carry books, phones or notes into the room · listen carefully to the **invigilator** ·
write your **name and index number** on every answer sheet · read **all instructions**
first · use **ink** (a pencil only for drawings and maps) · work **silently and alone** ·
never look at another candidate's work or exchange papers — this is **malpractice** and
leads to **disqualification** · watch the time · check your work before handing in.

### Following instructions — the words that matter

| Instruction | What you must do |
|---|---|
| **Underline** | Draw a line under the word |
| **Circle / Ring** | Draw a circle round the answer |
| **Tick** | Put a ✓ against the right one |
| **Fill in** / **Complete** | Write the missing word or words |
| **Rewrite** | Write the sentence again as told |
| **Give one word for** | Answer with a single word |
| **Arrange** | Put in the correct order |
| **State / Name / Mention** | Give a short answer, no explanation |
| **Explain / Describe** | Give a fuller answer in sentences |
| **Study the passage and answer** | Read carefully first, then answer in your own words where asked |

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Whereas …** | ***Whereas** many candidates passed, Okello failed.* |
| **… could …** | *Cheating **could** lead to closure of the examination centre.* |
| **… in spite of …** | *Aboke came for the examination **in spite of** her being sick.* |
| **In spite of …** (at the start) | ***In spite of** her being sick, Aboke came for the examination.* |
| **Despite …** | ***Despite** the fact that Zziwa was late, she completed her examination on time.* |

**Careful:** *despite* is **never** followed by "of". Write *despite the rain* or *in spite
of the rain*, never "despite of the rain".

### Grammar focus: possessives

| Possessive adjective (before a noun) | Possessive pronoun (stands alone) |
|---|---|
| my | mine |
| your | yours |
| his | his |
| her | hers |
| its | its |
| our | ours |
| their | theirs |

- *This is **my** pen. This pen is **mine**.*
- *Those are **their** results. Those results are **theirs**.*

**Two traps:**

- **its** = belonging to it; **it's** = it is. *The school lost **its** book. **It's** raining.*
- Possessive pronouns take **no apostrophe**: *hers, yours, ours, theirs* — never "her's".

| Invigilator | The person who watches over candidates during an examination |
| Supervisor | The officer in charge of an examination centre |

| Aggregate | The total of the grades scored |
| Division | The grade band a candidate falls in |
| Malpractice | Cheating or any dishonest act in an examination |
| Leakage | Examination questions getting out before the examination |
| Disqualify | To cancel a candidate's results for breaking the rules |
| Revision | Going over work already learnt |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- After **look forward to**, the verb ends in **-ing**.
- **Despite** takes no "of"; **in spite of** always has "of".
- *Whereas* joins two contrasting ideas in one sentence.
- **Quite** = fairly; **rather** = more than expected, often unwelcome.
- **its** = belonging to it; **it's** = it is.
- Possessive pronouns (*hers, ours, yours, theirs*) take **no apostrophe**.
- The person who watches candidates is the **invigilator**; the one in charge of the centre is the **supervisor**.
- Cheating is **malpractice** and can lead to **disqualification**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- *We look forward to ____ (meet) you.* → **meeting**
- *____ the rain, the candidates arrived on time.* → **Despite** / **In spite of**
- *Whereas many passed, ____* — complete with a contrasting idea.
- Word for the total of the grades scored → **aggregate**.
- Word for the person who watches over candidates → **invigilator**.
- Word for questions getting out before the examination → **leakage**.
- *This book is ____ (she).* → **hers**

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing *"despite of"*.
- Writing *"look forward to see you"* instead of *look forward to **seeing** you*.
- Confusing **its** and **it's**.
- Writing *her's, our's, their's* with an apostrophe.
- Starting to write before reading the instructions.
- Forgetting to write your **index number** on the answer sheet.

# REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Give one word for: a person sitting an examination.
2. Give one word for: the person who watches over candidates in the examination room.
3. Give the meaning of *aggregate* and *malpractice*.
4. What is *leakage* in an examination, and give one of its effects?
5. Complete: *You must revise your work ____ ____ you will fail.* (two words)
6. Complete: *We look forward to ____ (write) our examinations in November.*
7. Rewrite using *Despite*: *Although she was sick, she came for the examination.*
8. Rewrite using *In spite of*: *Although it rained heavily, the paper started on time.*
9. Join using *Whereas*: *Many candidates passed. Okello failed.*
10. Complete: *Cheating ____ lead to the closure of an examination centre.* (could / can hardly)
11. Fill in: *The paper was ____ difficult, so many candidates complained.* (quite / rather)
12. Fill in with the correct possessive: *This pen is ____ (I) and that one is ____ (they).*
13. Fill in: *The school lost ____ trophy because ____ raining heavily.* (its / it's)
14. Give any five rules a candidate must follow in the examination room.
15. Give any five ways of preparing well for an examination.
16. Say exactly what each instruction asks you to do: underline, tick, arrange, state, explain.
17. What three things should a candidate check on the examination card?
18. Give two effects of examination malpractice on a candidate.
19. Use the word *invigilator* in a sentence of your own.
20. Write a short paragraph (about six sentences) advising a P.6 pupil on how to prepare for examinations.

---

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. A **candidate**.
2. An **invigilator**.
3. **Aggregate** — the total of the grades scored in the subjects. **Malpractice** — cheating or any dishonest act in an examination.
4. Leakage is examination questions getting out before the examination. One effect: the examination may be **cancelled** and the candidates disqualified (it also makes the results unfair and spoils the name of the school).
5. **or else**
6. **writing**
7. **Despite** being sick, she came for the examination. *(Also: Despite her sickness, she came for the examination.)*
8. **In spite of** the heavy rain, the paper started on time.
9. **Whereas** many candidates passed, Okello failed.
10. **could**
11. **rather**
12. This pen is **mine** and that one is **theirs**.
13. The school lost **its** trophy because **it's** raining heavily.
14. Any five of: arrive early; carry your examination card and instruments; leave books, notes and phones outside; listen to the invigilator; write your name and index number on every sheet; read all instructions first; use ink; work silently and alone; never copy or exchange work; check your work before handing in.
15. Any five of: make and follow a revision timetable; revise a little every day; do past papers under time; ask the teacher what you do not understand; join a serious discussion group; keep your card and instruments safely; eat well and get enough sleep.
16. **Underline** — draw a line under the word; **tick** — put a ✓ against the right answer; **arrange** — put the items in the correct order; **state** — give a short answer without explaining; **explain** — give a fuller answer in sentences.
17. His or her **names**, the **index number**, and the **examination centre** (and the subjects entered).
18. The candidate's **results are cancelled (disqualification)**, and he or she may be **barred from sitting** the examination for some years; it also brings shame to the candidate, the family and the school.
19. *Learner's sentence,* for example: *The **invigilator** told us to stop writing when the time was over.*
20. *Learner's paragraph.* It should be one paragraph, in the present or future tense, with clear advice such as making a revision timetable, revising daily, doing past papers, asking teachers, resting well and avoiding cheating.
