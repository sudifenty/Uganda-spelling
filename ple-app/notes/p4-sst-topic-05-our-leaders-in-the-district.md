# TOPIC 5: OUR LEADERS IN THE DISTRICT

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Four Social Studies Syllabus, Term III, Topic 5,
25 periods (`curriculum/P4-SOCIAL-STUDIES-2010.pdf`, pages 31–35).*

**How to use this topic.** The syllabus asks about the leaders of **your own district**.
These notes teach you the **groups of leaders**, what each does and how leaders are chosen.
You fill in the names of the leaders where you live.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- name the different groups of leaders in the district
- say what each group of leaders does
- describe the local council structure from LC1 to LC5
- explain how leaders are chosen
- describe how an election is carried out
- say what makes a good leader

---

## 1. WHAT A LEADER IS

A **leader** is a person who **guides, plans and organises other people** so that work is
done well and people live in peace.

**Why we need leaders:** to keep law and order · to plan development · to settle quarrels ·
to speak for the people · to look after public property · to represent the people to
government.

---

## 2. THE FOUR GROUPS OF LEADERS

The syllabus puts the leaders of a district into **four groups**.

### (a) Political leaders

Chosen by the people, or appointed by government, to govern.

| Leader | Where they work |
|---|---|
| **LC1 chairperson** | The village |
| **LC2 chairperson** | The parish |
| **LC3 chairperson** | The sub-county |
| **LC4 chairperson** | The county |
| **LC5 chairperson** | The **whole district** |
| **RDC** — Resident District Commissioner | Represents the **President** in the district |
| **DISO** — District Internal Security Officer | Looks after security in the district |

### (b) Civic leaders

These are **government officers**. They are **appointed** because of their training, not
elected.

| Leader | Work |
|---|---|
| **CAO** — Chief Administrative Officer | The head of all civil servants in the district |
| **District Health Officer** | In charge of health services and hospitals |
| **District Agricultural Officer** | Advises farmers on crops and animals |
| **Magistrate** | Hears cases in court |
| **District Police Commander (DPC)** | Head of the police in the district |

### (c) Religious leaders

They lead people in worship and teach good behaviour.

**Bishops · priests · reverends · pastors · sheikhs · khadis.**

### (d) Voluntary leaders

They lead **without being paid**, because they want to help.

**Scouts and Guides leaders · leaders of NGOs** such as **UWESO**, **TASO** and **the Red
Cross**.

> **UWESO** — Uganda Women's Effort to Save Orphans. **TASO** — The AIDS Support
> Organisation. These two are the syllabus's own examples of NGOs.

## 3. HOW LEADERS ARE CHOSEN

The syllabus gives **four** ways:

| Way | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **By election** | The people **vote** for the person they want | LC1 to LC5 chairpersons, Members of Parliament, class prefects |
| **By appointment** | Someone in authority **chooses** the person | The RDC, the CAO, the District Health Officer |
| **Through inheritance** | Leadership **passes down in a family** | Cultural leaders — a king, a chief, a clan head |
| **By volunteering** | A person **offers himself or herself** to serve, unpaid | Scout leaders, Red Cross helpers, church group leaders |

---

## 4. HOW AN ELECTION IS CARRIED OUT

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. **Registration** | Names of those old enough to vote are written on the **voters' register** |
| 2. **Nomination** | Those who want the post put their names forward |
| 3. **Campaigning** | The candidates tell the people what they will do if chosen |
| 4. **Voting** | On polling day each voter marks a **ballot paper** and puts it in the **ballot box** |
| 5. **Counting** | The votes are counted in front of everyone |
| 6. **Declaring** | The **returning officer** announces the winner |
| 7. **Swearing in** | The winner takes an oath and starts work |

**Words to know**

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Candidate** | A person standing for a post |
| **Voter** | A person who votes |
| **Ballot paper** | The paper on which a voter marks a choice |
| **Ballot box** | The box in which ballot papers are put |
| **Secret ballot** | Voting in a way nobody else can see |
| **Manifesto** | What a candidate promises to do |
| **Free and fair election** | An election with no cheating and no force |

> In Uganda, **lining up behind a candidate** was used long ago; today voting is by
> **secret ballot**.

## 5. WHAT MAKES A GOOD LEADER

| A good leader | A bad leader |
|---|---|
| Is **honest** and does not steal | Is corrupt and takes bribes |
| **Listens** to the people | Refuses to listen |
| Is **fair** to everybody | Favours only friends and relatives |
| **Keeps promises** | Forgets the promises made |
| Is **hard-working** | Is lazy and often absent |
| Is **humble and patient** | Is proud and rude |
| Is **educated enough** for the work | Cannot read the papers of the office |

### Duties of the people towards their leaders

Obey the law · pay taxes · attend meetings · vote wisely · respect leaders · report wrong
acts · take part in community work.

## 6. PREFECTS — LEADERS AT SCHOOL

Your school is like a small district. Prefects are chosen the same way — by **nomination,
campaigning and voting**.

| Prefect | Work |
|---|---|
| Head prefect | Leads all the other prefects |
| Time keeper | Rings the bell and keeps time |
| Sanitation prefect | Sees that the compound and toilets are clean |
| Games prefect | Organises games and sports |
| Dining hall prefect | Keeps order at meals |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The four groups of leaders: **political, civic, religious and voluntary**.
- The councils run **LC1 (village) → LC2 (parish) → LC3 (sub-county) → LC4 (county) →
  LC5 (district)**.
- The **LC5 chairperson** heads the district council; the **CAO** heads the civil servants.
- The **RDC represents the President** in the district.
- Leaders are chosen **by election, by appointment, through inheritance or by
  volunteering**.
- Voting today is by **secret ballot**.
- A good leader is **honest, fair, hard-working** and **keeps promises**.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Saying the **RDC** is elected — the RDC is **appointed**.
- Mixing up the **LC5 chairperson** (political, elected) with the **CAO** (civic, appointed).
- Putting the LC levels in the wrong order.
- Calling a bishop a political leader.
- Saying a cultural leader is elected — cultural leadership is usually **inherited**.
- Writing "voting" as the only way leaders are chosen.
