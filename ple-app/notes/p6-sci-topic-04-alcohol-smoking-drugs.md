## 1. ALCOHOL AND ALCOHOLISM

### Definitions

**Alcohol:** A drug found in drinks such as beer, waragi, wine and spirits, which slows down the working of the brain and body.

**Alcoholism:** The condition of being unable to stop taking alcohol — addiction to alcohol.

**Alcoholic:** A person who is addicted to alcohol.

### Effects of alcoholism

| On the individual | On the family | On the community |
|---|---|---|
| Damage to the **liver** (cirrhosis), stomach and brain | Money is wasted on drink instead of food, fees and medicine | Crime, fighting and insecurity |
| Loss of memory, poor judgement and shaking | Quarrels, violence and broken marriages | Road accidents caused by drunk drivers |
| **Accidents and injuries** | Children drop out of school | Loss of a productive workforce |
| Loss of job, poverty and debt | Neglect of children and the elderly | Spread of diseases including HIV, through careless behaviour |
| Poor health, malnutrition and early death | Shame and loss of respect | Increased burden on hospitals and police |

---

## 2. SMOKING

**Smoking:** Taking in the smoke of burning tobacco in cigarettes, pipes or shisha.

Tobacco smoke contains **nicotine** (which causes addiction), **tar** (which causes cancer)
and **carbon monoxide** (which reduces oxygen in the blood).

### Active and passive smoking

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Active smoking** | Smoking the cigarette yourself |
| **Passive smoking** | Breathing in the smoke of somebody else who is smoking |

**Passive smokers, including babies and pregnant mothers, suffer the same diseases** —
which is why smoking near others, and in public places, is discouraged and in many places
prohibited.

### Effects of smoking

On the individual: **lung cancer**, chronic cough, bronchitis, tuberculosis, heart disease,
stained teeth and fingers, bad breath, poor appetite, addiction, loss of money.
On the family: money wasted; children copy the habit; passive smoking harms the children;
the risk of fire in the home.

## 3. DRUGS

### Definitions

**Drug:** Any substance that changes the way the body or mind works.

**Essential drugs:** The medicines a community most needs, which should always be available at a health centre — for example ORS, panadol (paracetamol), anti-malarials, antibiotics, deworming tablets and immunisation vaccines.

### Characteristics of good (essential) drugs

They are **within the expiry date** · properly **sealed and labelled** · from a **licensed**
health centre or pharmacy · the right **name, dose and instructions** · not discoloured,
crushed or damp.

### Storage of drugs

Keep in a **cool, dry, dark place** · in the **original container** with its label ·
tightly closed · **out of reach of children**, preferably locked · never store medicine
with food or paraffin · check the **expiry date** and return or destroy expired drugs
safely · never keep left-over prescription drugs for another illness.

### Drug abuse, misuse and dependence

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Drug abuse** | Using a drug for a purpose it was **not meant for**, or using a harmful substance | Sniffing petrol or glue; smoking marijuana; drinking alcohol as a child |
| **Drug misuse** | Using a proper medicine **wrongly** | Taking the wrong dose, stopping treatment early, using another person's prescription, using expired medicine |
| **Drug dependence (addiction)** | The body or mind cannot do without the drug | An alcoholic who shakes without a drink |

**Commonly abused substances:** alcohol, tobacco, marijuana (njaga), khat (mairungi),
petrol and glue fumes, cocaine and heroin, and wrongly used sleeping tablets or painkillers.

### Effects of drugs

| On the individual | On the family | On the community |
|---|---|---|
| Mental illness, brain damage, addiction | Poverty and quarrels | Crime and insecurity |
| Poor performance and school dropout | Loss of trust and respect | Accidents |
| Diseases and early death | Neglect of children | Loss of workforce |

## 4. WHY PEOPLE FALL INTO THESE HABITS

**Peer pressure** · curiosity and experimenting · **idleness** and too much free time ·
copying parents, older relatives or heroes in films and music · **advertising** ·
frustration, stress and family problems · easy availability and low prices · lack of
guidance and correct information · wanting to look grown up or brave.

---

## 5. LIFE SKILLS THAT PROTECT A YOUNG PERSON

| Life skill | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Assertiveness** | Saying **NO** firmly and repeating it without fear |
| **Peer pressure resistance** | Choosing friends wisely and leaving bad company |
| **Decision-making** | Thinking about the consequences before acting |
| **Critical thinking** | Questioning adverts and false promises |
| **Self-esteem** | Valuing yourself enough not to harm your body |
| **Coping with stress** | Talking to a trusted adult instead of drinking or smoking |
| **Effective communication** | Explaining your refusal politely but clearly |
| **Using free time well** | Sports, clubs, reading, music, farming and helping at home |

### Other ways society can help

Health education in schools, on radio and in churches and mosques · guidance and
counselling · laws against selling alcohol and cigarettes to children · rehabilitation
centres for those already addicted · parents setting a good example · reporting sellers of
illegal drugs to the police.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Alcoholism** damages the **liver** most of all.
- Tobacco contains **nicotine** (addiction), **tar** (cancer) and **carbon monoxide**.
- **Passive smokers suffer the same diseases** as smokers.
- **Drug abuse** = wrong purpose; **drug misuse** = wrong use of a proper medicine.
- Store drugs in a **cool, dry place, out of reach of children**, and always check the **expiry date**.
- **Peer pressure** is the commonest reason young people start.
- The strongest protection is **assertiveness** — the ability to say NO.

- One reason young people abuse drugs → peer pressure (or idleness, curiosity).

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Alcoholism is the condition of being unable to stop taking alcohol — addiction to alcohol. An **alcoholic** is a person who is addicted to alcohol.
2. Any four of: damage to the liver, stomach and brain; loss of memory and poor judgement; accidents and injuries; loss of job and poverty; poor health, malnutrition and early death.
3. **Family:** money wasted instead of buying food and paying fees; quarrels and violence; children dropping out of school; neglect of the family. **Community:** crime and insecurity; road accidents; loss of a productive workforce; extra burden on hospitals and police. (Any three each.)
4. **Nicotine** — causes addiction; **tar** — causes cancer; **carbon monoxide** — reduces the oxygen carried by the blood.
5. **Active smoking** is smoking a cigarette yourself; **passive smoking** is breathing in the smoke of somebody else who is smoking.
6. Because passive smokers breathe the same harmful substances and suffer the same diseases, and children's lungs and unborn babies are still developing, so the harm is greater — it can cause coughs, chest infections, poor growth and low birth weight.
7. Any four of: lung cancer; chronic cough and bronchitis; tuberculosis; heart disease; stained teeth and bad breath; poor appetite; addiction.
8. Essential drugs are the medicines a community most needs and which should always be available at a health centre. Examples: ORS, paracetamol, anti-malarial tablets, antibiotics, deworming tablets, vaccines (any four).
9. Any four of: within the expiry date; properly sealed and labelled; from a licensed health centre or pharmacy; with the correct name, dose and instructions; not discoloured, crushed or damp.
10. Any five of: keep them in a cool, dry, dark place; keep them in the original labelled container; close them tightly; keep them out of reach of children, preferably locked; do not store them with food or paraffin; check the expiry date; destroy or return expired drugs safely.
11. **Drug abuse** is using a drug for a purpose it was not meant for, for example sniffing petrol. **Drug misuse** is using a proper medicine wrongly, for example taking the wrong dose or using somebody else's prescription.
12. Drug dependence is when the body or the mind can no longer do without the drug, so the person suffers when he or she stops taking it.
13. Any four of: alcohol, tobacco, marijuana (njaga), khat (mairungi), petrol or glue fumes, cocaine, heroin, wrongly used sleeping tablets.
14. Any five of: peer pressure; curiosity; idleness and too much free time; copying parents or film and music stars; advertising; frustration and family problems; easy availability; lack of guidance.
15. Any five of: assertiveness, peer pressure resistance, decision-making, critical thinking, self-esteem, coping with stress, effective communication, using free time well. **Assertiveness**, for example, is the ability to say **NO** firmly and to repeat it without fear or apology, and to walk away from the situation.
