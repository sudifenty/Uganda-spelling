# PRIMARY 5 ENGLISH

# TOPIC 7: PEACE AND SECURITY

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, English Topic 7, 15 periods
(`curriculum/P5-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, pages 31–32).*

**What you already know:** You know that people must live together peacefully. In P.5 you
learn the **words used about peace, security and the law**, and you use **conjunctions**, the
**past simple tense** and the **present perfect tense**.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- use the vocabulary of peace and security correctly
- name the people who keep law and order and say what each does
- use the conjunctions *so … that* and *and*
- use *has* and *have* in the present perfect tense
- use *about* correctly after verbs of speaking
- tell and write a story about keeping peace

## 2. WHO KEEPS PEACE AND SECURITY

| Person or group | What they do |
|---|---|
| **The police** | Keep law and order, arrest offenders, take statements |
| **The army** | Defend the country from attack |
| **Local council leaders (LCs)** | Settle small quarrels and keep order in the village |
| **Magistrates and judges** | Listen to cases and decide them fairly |
| **Parents and teachers** | Teach children to live peacefully with others |
| **Every citizen** | Reports wrong acts and refuses to take part in them |

## 3. WHAT BUILDS PEACE AND WHAT DESTROYS IT

| Builds peace | Destroys peace |
|---|---|
| Sharing and helping one another | Quarrelling and fighting |
| Talking problems over calmly | Insulting and abusing others |
| Forgiving and apologising | Stealing and lying |
| Respecting other people's property and beliefs | Drunkenness and idleness |
| Obeying the law and reporting wrong acts | Keeping quiet when a crime is done |

**If something wrong happens to you or to another child, tell a trusted adult at once — a
parent, a teacher, an LC leader or the police. Reporting is not telling tales; it is how
peace is kept. If a child has been hurt or badly treated, it is never the child's fault.**

## 4. GRAMMAR: THE CONJUNCTION "SO … THAT"

**So … that** shows a **result**: the first part is so strong that something follows.

| Example |
|---|
| The village has **so** many thieves **that** there is no peace. |
| The noise was **so** loud **that** the neighbours called the police. |
| He ran **so** fast **that** nobody could catch him. |

**Form:** *so* + adjective (or *many / much* + noun) + **that** + a full clause.

---

## 5. GRAMMAR: JOINING WITH "AND"

**And** joins two ideas of equal weight.

| Two sentences | Joined with *and* |
|---|---|
| The policeman arrested the murderer. He took him to court. | The policeman arrested the murderer **and** took him to court. |
| The witness came to court. She gave her evidence. | The witness came to court **and** gave her evidence. |

When the doer is the same person, we do **not** repeat the name after *and*.

- ✗ The policeman arrested the thief and the policeman took him to court.
- ✓ The policeman arrested the thief **and took** him to court.

---

## 6. GRAMMAR: HAS AND HAVE (PRESENT PERFECT)

```
has / have  +  past participle
```

| Sentence |
|---|
| Okumu **has given** his statement to the police. |
| The witnesses **have given** their statements about the case. |
| The magistrate **has heard** the case. |
| The police **have arrested** two suspects. |

**Has** with *he, she, it* and one person. **Have** with *I, you, we, they* and more than
one.

**Useful past participles:** give → **given** · take → **taken** · steal → **stolen** ·
catch → **caught** · hear → **heard** · see → **seen** · write → **written**.

---

## 7. GRAMMAR: USING "ABOUT"

**About** means *concerning* or *on the subject of*.

| Example |
|---|
| The witnesses have given their statements **about** the case. |
| They talked **about** the theft for a long time. |
| The teacher warned us **about** fighting. |
| We read a story **about** peace. |

Verbs that often take *about*: **talk, speak, write, read, hear, complain, warn, worry, ask,
think, quarrel**.

---

## 8. THE PAST SIMPLE IN A STORY

| Present | Past |
|---|---|
| arrest | **arrested** |
| report | **reported** |
| steal | **stole** |
| catch | **caught** |
| take | **took** |
| run | **ran** |
| give | **gave** |
| hear | **heard** |

**A short story to read aloud**

> Last Sunday evening, a thief broke into a shop at our trading centre. The shopkeeper's
> neighbour heard a noise and reported it at the police post. Two policemen came quickly and
> arrested the thief. They put handcuffs on him and took him to the police station, where he
> gave a statement. On Tuesday the case was heard by a magistrate. The neighbour stood as a
> witness. Since that day the traders have slept peacefully, and they have thanked the
> neighbour for being alert.

**Questions on the story:** What happened on Sunday evening? Who reported the crime? Where
was the thief taken? Who heard the case? Why have the traders slept peacefully?

| Conjunction | A word that joins two parts of a sentence |
| Present perfect | The tense formed with *has* or *have* and a past participle |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **So … that** shows a result and needs a full clause after *that*.
- After **and**, do not repeat the doer if it is the same person.
- The present perfect is **has / have + past participle**.
- *Give* becomes **given** and *steal* becomes **stolen**.
- **About** follows verbs of speaking, writing, hearing and worrying.
- A **magistrate** sits in a lower court; a **judge** sits in a higher court.
- Reporting a wrong act to a trusted adult is the right thing to do.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- The village has **so** many thieves **that** there is no peace.
- The policeman arrested the murderer **and** took him to court.
- Okumu **has given** his statement to the police.
- The witnesses **have given** their statements **about** the case.
- The place where soldiers live is the **barracks**.
- The room where a suspect is kept at a police station is a **cell**.
- The past simple of *catch* is **caught**.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing *so much thieves* instead of **so many thieves**.
- Leaving out *that* in a *so … that* sentence.
- Repeating the subject after *and*.
- Writing *has gave* instead of **has given**.
- Writing *talked on the case* instead of **talked about the case**.
- Confusing a **witness** (who saw it) with the **defence** (who answers the charge).
