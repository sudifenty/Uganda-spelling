# PRIMARY 5 ENGLISH

# TOPIC 4: LETTER WRITING

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, English Topic 4, 10 periods
(`curriculum/P5-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, pages 22–23).*

**What you already know:** You can write short messages and notes. In P.5 you learn to write
**personal (friendly) letters** — invitations, seasonal greetings and news — and you use
**interjections**, **punctuation**, **personal pronouns** and the structures **would** and
**could**.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- name the parts of a personal letter
- write a personal letter with the parts in the right order
- punctuate a letter correctly
- use personal pronouns correctly
- use *would* and *could* in statements and in questions
- write an invitation and a reply

| **envelope** | The paper cover in which a letter is put |
| **address** | The details showing where a person lives or where a letter is sent |
| **sender** | The person who writes and sends the letter |
| **receiver** | The person the letter is sent to |

| **dear** | The word that begins the greeting of a letter |

---

## 2. THE PARTS OF A PERSONAL LETTER

| Part | Where it goes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **The sender's address** | Top **right** corner | P.O. Box 24, Mukono |
| **The date** | Under the address | 3rd March, 2026 |
| **The salutation (greeting)** | On the left, under the date | Dear Aunt Grace, |
| **The introduction** | First paragraph | How are you? I hope you are well. |
| **The body** | The middle paragraphs | The news or the message |
| **The conclusion** | Last paragraph | Greet everybody at home for me. |
| **The subscription (ending)** | Below the body, on the left or right | Yours affectionately, |
| **The signature (name)** | Below the ending | Sarah |

### A sample personal letter

```
                                              P.O. Box 24,
                                              Mukono.
                                              3rd March, 2026

Dear Aunt Grace,

How are you? I hope you and the family are well. We are all fine here at
school.

I am writing to invite you to our Parents' Day, which will take place on
Saturday, 21st March, at 10:00 a.m. I will be reciting a poem and my class
will present a play. It would make me very happy if you came.

Could you please come with my cousin Peter? Greet everybody at home for me.

                                              Yours affectionately,
                                              Sarah
```

### The envelope

```
                     Aunt Grace Nakato
                     P.O. Box 105
                     Iganga
```

The **receiver's** address goes on the front of the envelope. The **sender's** address may
be written on the back.

## 3. PUNCTUATION IN A LETTER

| Mark | Where it is used |
|---|---|
| **Capital letter** | Names, places, months, the first word of a sentence |
| **Comma (,)** | After the greeting — *Dear Aunt Grace,* — and after the ending — *Yours affectionately,* |
| **Full stop (.)** | At the end of a statement |
| **Question mark (?)** | At the end of a question — *How are you?* |
| **Exclamation mark (!)** | After an interjection — *Hurrah!* |
| **Apostrophe (')** | To show belonging — *Parents' Day* — or a short form — *I'm* |

---

## 4. GRAMMAR: INTERJECTIONS

An **interjection** is a word that shows sudden feeling. It is usually followed by an
**exclamation mark**.

| Interjection | Feeling shown | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hullo! | Greeting | **Hullo,** my friend. |
| Hurrah! | Joy | **Hurrah!** We have won. |
| Oh! | Surprise | **Oh!** What a big cake. |
| Alas! | Sorrow | **Alas!** He did not come. |
| Well done! | Praise | **Well done!** You passed. |

---

## 5. GRAMMAR: PERSONAL PRONOUNS

A **personal pronoun** stands in place of the name of a person or thing.

| Doing the action (subject) | Receiving the action (object) | Showing belonging |
|---|---|---|
| I | me | my, mine |
| you | you | your, yours |
| he | him | his |
| she | her | her, hers |
| it | it | its |
| we | us | our, ours |
| they | them | their, theirs |

```
Maria wrote the letter.       →  She wrote it.
I invited Peter and Sarah.    →  I invited them.
The letter belongs to me.     →  It is mine.
```

---

## 6. GRAMMAR: WOULD AND COULD

**Would** is used for something that depends on a condition, and for polite requests.
**Could** is used for ability in the past, for possibility, and for polite requests.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| … **would** … | He **would** come to our party if we invited him. |
| **Would** …? | **Would** you write a letter to your mother if she was sick? |
| … **could** … | Maria **could** write a letter inviting Maggy to a birthday party. |
| **Could** …? | **Could** he reply to that letter immediately? |

**Polite requests** use *would* and *could*, not the plain verb:

- **Could** you please come early?
- **Would** you like some tea?

## 7. WRITING AN INVITATION CARD

```
                  YOU ARE INVITED

              To the birthday party of
                   MAGGY NAMULI

    Date:  Saturday, 14th June, 2026
    Time:  2:00 p.m.
    Place: Plot 7, Church Road, Mukono

           R.S.V.P.  Tel. 0700 000 000
```

An invitation gives the **occasion, the date, the time and the place**, and asks for a
reply.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Personal letter | A friendly letter to a person you know |
| Salutation | The greeting at the start of a letter |
| Subscription | The ending, such as *Yours affectionately* |
| Sender | The person writing the letter |
| Receiver | The person the letter is written to |
| Invitation | A written or spoken request to come to an occasion |
| Interjection | A word showing sudden feeling |
| Personal pronoun | A word used in place of the name of a person or thing |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The **sender's address** goes at the **top right**, with the **date** under it.
- A **comma** follows the greeting and the ending.
- A personal letter to a relative or close friend ends **Yours affectionately**; to a friend
  it may end **Yours sincerely** or **Your friend**.
- The **receiver's** address goes on the **envelope**, not at the top of a personal letter.
- An interjection takes an **exclamation mark**.
- Use **would** and **could** to be polite.
- Never begin a personal letter with *Dear Sir* — that is for a formal letter.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- **Hullo, my friend.**
- He **would** come to our party if we invited him.
- **Could** he reply to that letter immediately?
- The part of a letter that says *Dear Aunt Grace* is the **salutation**.
- The person who receives a letter is the **receiver**.
- *Yours affectionately* is followed by a **comma**, then the writer's name.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Putting the sender's address on the left of a personal letter.
- Forgetting the comma after the greeting.
- Writing the date as 3/3 without the month and year.
- Signing the letter with a full name and title in a friendly letter.
- Writing *Yours faithfully* in a letter to a relative.
- Using *would* with a second verb ending in *-ed*: ✗ *He would came*. ✓ *He would come*.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Any four of **the address, the date, the salutation, the introduction, the body, the conclusion, the subscription, the signature**
2. In the **top right corner**
3. The **date**
4. A **comma**
5. **Yours affectionately,**
6. The **receiver's** address
7. **An answer to a letter**
8. A **host** is a man who receives guests; a **hostess** is a woman who receives guests.
9. Any one of **Hullo! · Hurrah! · Oh! · Alas! · Well done!**
10. **Hurrah! We have won.**
11. **She wrote it.**
12. **I invited them.**
13. **would**
14. **Could you please reply to that letter immediately?**
15. **Would**
16. **The occasion, the date, the time and the place** (a request for a reply is also expected)
17. To **reply**, saying whether or not you will come
18. For example: **P.O. Box 24, Mukono.** on the first lines and **3rd March, 2026** under it
19. **Dear Peter,**
20. **Receiver**

# P.5 QUICK REVISION

- Address top **right** · date under it · greeting on the **left**
- *Dear Aunt Grace,* — comma after the greeting
- Ending: **Yours affectionately,** then your name
- Interjection + **!**
- **would** / **could** = polite and conditional
- Invitation: occasion · date · time · place · R.S.V.P.
