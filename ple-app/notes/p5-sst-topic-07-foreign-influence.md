# TOPIC 7: FOREIGN INFLUENCE IN UGANDA

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, Social Studies Topic 7
(`curriculum/P5-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, pages 208–211).*

**What you already know:** In Topic 6 you saw how the ethnic groups came into Uganda. In
this topic you learn about the **foreigners** — the Arab traders, the European explorers,
the missionaries and the colonial administrators — why they came and what they left behind.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- identify the foreigners who came to Uganda and where they came from
- explain why they came
- describe what each group did and the problems they faced
- state the positive and negative effects of foreign influence
- locate on the map the routes and places connected with them

---

## 1. THE FOREIGNERS WHO CAME TO UGANDA

| Group | Where they came from | Why they came |
|---|---|---|
| **Arab traders** | **Arabia (Asia)**, through the East African coast | Trade in ivory, slaves and skins; to spread **Islam** |
| **Swahili and Indian traders** | The coast and India | Trade; later shops and the railway |
| **European explorers** | Europe (mainly Britain) | To find the **source of the Nile**, map the interior, seek fame |
| **Missionaries** | Europe | To spread **Christianity**, stop the slave trade, start schools and hospitals |
| **Colonial administrators** | Britain | To govern the country, get raw materials and markets, and control the source of the Nile |

---

## 2. THE ARAB TRADERS

The first Arab to reach Buganda was **Ahmed bin Ibrahim**, who arrived at the court of
**Kabaka Suna II in about 1844**.

| They brought | They took away |
|---|---|
| Guns and gunpowder, cloth, carpets, **cowrie shells**, beads, mirrors, plates, sugar and tea | **Ivory, slaves, gold, salt, animal skins** and hides |

### Their effects

**Positive:** trade grew and cowrie shells came into use as money; **Islam** was introduced;
Kiswahili words entered our languages; new goods and dress arrived.
**Negative:** the **slave trade** took away many people; **guns** spread and increased wars;
some communities were raided and destroyed.

---

## 3. THE EUROPEAN EXPLORERS

| Explorer | Year | What he did |
|---|---|---|
| **John Hanning Speke and James Grant** | **1862** | Reached the **source of the Nile at Jinja**; Speke named it Ripon Falls |
| **Sir Samuel Baker and his wife Florence** | 1864 | Reached **Lake Albert** and **Murchison Falls** |
| **Henry Morton Stanley** | 1875 | Visited **Kabaka Muteesa I** and wrote the famous **letter** inviting missionaries to Buganda |
| **Dr David Livingstone** | 1850s–1873 | Explored Central Africa and fought the slave trade |

### Why they came

To find the **source of the Nile** · to **map** the interior · to look for **raw materials
and markets** · for **adventure and fame** · to see how the **slave trade** could be
stopped · some were sent by geographical societies.

### Problems they faced

Tropical **diseases** such as malaria and sleeping sickness · **hostile people** ·
**wild animals** · **thick forests, swamps and rivers** · **language barriers** ·
shortage of food and clean water · **desertion by porters** · long distances on foot.

---

## 4. THE MISSIONARIES

| Group | Year | Leaders |
|---|---|---|
| **Church Missionary Society (Protestants)** | **1877** | Rev. **Shergold Smith** and Rev. **C. T. Wilson** |
| **White Fathers (Catholics)** | **1879** | Fr. **Simeon Lourdel (Mapeera)** and Bro. **Amans** |

They came because of **Stanley's letter**, to spread Christianity, to fight the slave trade
and to bring education and medicine.

### What they did

Built **churches**, **schools** (Namilyango, Mengo, Gayaza, Kisubi) and **hospitals**
(Mengo, Nsambya, Rubaga) · translated the Bible and wrote down **local languages** ·
introduced **new crops** and skills · trained teachers, catechists and nurses.

**A hard memory:** in 1885–1887, during the reign of **Kabaka Mwanga**, a number of young
Christian converts were killed at Namugongo; they are remembered as the **Uganda Martyrs**,
and Namugongo is visited by pilgrims every **3rd June**.

---

## 5. THE COLONIAL ADMINISTRATORS

- **1888** — the **Imperial British East Africa Company (IBEACo)** was given a charter to trade and administer.
- **1890** — **Captain Frederick Lugard** arrived in Buganda for the Company and signed a treaty with Kabaka Mwanga.
- **1894** — Britain declared a **Protectorate over Buganda**, later extended to the rest of the country.
- **1900** — the **Buganda Agreement** was signed between Sir Harry Johnston and the Buganda regents, dividing land into **mailo** land and Crown land and fixing taxes.
- Britain ruled through **indirect rule** — using the Kabaka and the chiefs.

---

## 6. EFFECTS OF FOREIGN INFLUENCE

### Positive effects

- **Christianity and Islam** were introduced.
- **Schools and hospitals** were built; formal education began.
- **Roads and the railway** were built; the railway reached Kampala in 1931.
- **New crops** — coffee, cotton, tea, maize, cassava and sweet potatoes — became cash crops.
- The **slave trade was stopped**.
- **Trade and money** replaced barter; towns grew.
- **Local languages were written down**; Kiswahili and English spread.
- The different peoples were joined into **one country, Uganda**.

### Negative effects

- Uganda **lost its independence** and was ruled by foreigners.
- **Forced labour** (*kasanvu*) and heavy **taxes** were introduced.
- **Land was taken** and given out under the 1900 Agreement.
- Traditional **rulers lost power** and some kingdoms were weakened.
- African **culture and religion were despised**.
- **Religious wars** broke out between Catholics, Protestants and Muslims.
- New **diseases** and the exploitation of Uganda's raw materials.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Foreigner | A person who comes from another country |
| Explorer | A person who travels to an unknown place to find out about it |
| Missionary | A person sent to spread a religion |
| Protectorate | A territory protected and controlled by a stronger country, ruled through its own leaders |
| Indirect rule | Ruling a colony through the existing chiefs |
| Mailo land | Land given out in square miles under the 1900 Buganda Agreement |
| Cowrie shells | Shells used as money before coins |
| Barter trade | Exchanging goods for goods |
| Forced labour | Being made to work without pay |
| Uganda Martyrs | The Christian converts killed at Namugongo in 1885–1887 |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The first Arab to reach Buganda was **Ahmed bin Ibrahim**, about **1844**, during **Kabaka Suna II**.
- **Speke and Grant** reached the source of the Nile at **Jinja in 1862**.
- **Stanley's letter** of 1875 invited the missionaries.
- **CMS (Protestants) came in 1877**; the **White Fathers (Catholics) in 1879**.
- **Mapeera** is the name Ugandans gave Fr. Simeon Lourdel.
- Uganda became a **British Protectorate in 1894**; the **Buganda Agreement** was signed in **1900**.
- Britain used **indirect rule**.
- The **Uganda Martyrs** are remembered on **3rd June** at Namugongo.
