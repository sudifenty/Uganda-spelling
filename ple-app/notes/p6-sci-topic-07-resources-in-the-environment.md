# PRIMARY 6 INTEGRATED SCIENCE

**Theme: The Environment**

# TOPIC 7: RESOURCES IN THE ENVIRONMENT

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Six Curriculum, Set One, Integrated Science Topic 7,
10 periods (`curriculum/P6-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, topic outline page 68 and pages 97–99).*

**What you already know:** You know that we get food, water and firewood from around us. In
P.6 you learn what a **resource** is, how resources are **harvested and used**, which ones
**run out**, and how to **conserve** them.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- name living and non-living things used as resources
- describe how resources are harvested
- explain how people make use of the resources available to them
- give the difference between renewable and non-renewable resources
- explain ways of caring for and conserving resources

---

## 1. WHAT A RESOURCE IS

**Resource:** Anything in the environment that people use to satisfy their needs.

| Group | Examples | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-living resources** | Soil, water, air, sunlight, minerals, rocks, wind | Farming, drinking, breathing, building, energy, industry |
| **Living resources** | Plants, animals, birds, fish, insects, human beings | Food, medicine, timber, clothing, labour, tourism |

**The human being is also a resource** — people supply labour, skills and ideas.

---

## 2. HARVESTING RESOURCES

**Harvesting:** Collecting or taking a resource from the environment for use.

| Resource | How it is harvested |
|---|---|
| Crops | Digging, picking, cutting, threshing |
| Fish | Nets, hooks, traps, trawling |
| Timber | Felling trees and sawing |
| Water | Fetching, piping, boreholes, harvesting rain from roofs |
| Minerals | Mining, quarrying, drilling, panning |
| Wild animals | Controlled hunting where it is allowed; poaching is illegal |
| Salt | Evaporating water from salt lakes such as Katwe |
| Honey | Harvesting from beehives |

### Good harvesting practices

Take **only what is needed** · use the **right tools and methods** · leave young plants,
fish and animals to grow · avoid **poison, explosives and undersized nets** · **replant**
after cutting trees · avoid harvesting in the breeding season · avoid wasting the resource
after harvest through poor storage.

---

## 3. HOW PEOPLE USE THE RESOURCES AVAILABLE

| Resource | Uses |
|---|---|
| **Soil** | Growing crops, making bricks and pots, building |
| **Water** | Drinking, cooking, washing, irrigation, fishing, transport, hydro-electric power |
| **Air** | Breathing, drying, winnowing, wind energy, burning |
| **Sunlight** | Photosynthesis, drying, solar energy, warmth, vitamin D |
| **Plants** | Food, medicine, timber, firewood, shade, raw materials |
| **Animals** | Food, hides, manure, transport, work, tourism |
| **Minerals** | Building, industry, money from export |

People in different places use what they have: fishing communities on the lakes, herders in
the dry areas, farmers on fertile soils, and miners where minerals are found.

---

## 4. RENEWABLE AND NON-RENEWABLE RESOURCES

| Renewable — can be replaced or never run out | Non-renewable — get used up |
|---|---|
| Sunlight, wind, water, air | Minerals: gold, copper, limestone |
| Plants and animals (if replaced) | Fossil fuels: coal, oil, natural gas |
| Soil fertility (if cared for) | Soil itself, once eroded away |

**Careful:** plants, animals and fish are renewable **only if we allow them to reproduce
and replace themselves**. Over-fishing and over-cutting can finish them completely — this
is called **depletion**, and species can become **extinct**.

---

## 5. CARING FOR AND CONSERVING RESOURCES

**Conservation:** Protecting and using resources carefully so that they are not destroyed or used up.

| Resource | How to conserve it |
|---|---|
| **Soil** | Mulching, terracing, contour ploughing, crop rotation, manure, cover crops, controlled grazing |
| **Water** | Harvesting rain water, protecting springs and wetlands, avoiding pollution, closing taps, irrigating carefully |
| **Forests and plants** | Afforestation and reafforestation, agroforestry, energy-saving stoves, gazetting forest reserves |
| **Animals and fish** | Game parks and sanctuaries, laws against poaching, closed fishing seasons, correct net sizes, fish farming |
| **Minerals** | Careful mining, avoiding wastage, adding value at home, restoring mined land |
| **Air** | Reducing smoke, planting trees, servicing vehicles, proper waste disposal |
| **Energy** | Solar and biogas, energy-saving bulbs and stoves, switching off what is not in use |

### Why conservation matters

So that resources **last for the future** · to keep the environment healthy · to keep the
soil fertile and the rain reliable · to protect wildlife and tourism · to avoid famine,
poverty and conflict over scarce resources.

---

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resource | Anything in the environment used to satisfy human needs |
| Living (biotic) resource | A resource that has life, such as plants and animals |
| Non-living (abiotic) resource | A resource with no life, such as soil, water and minerals |
| Harvesting | Collecting a resource from the environment for use |
| Renewable resource | A resource that can be replaced or does not run out |
| Non-renewable resource | A resource that gets used up and cannot be replaced |
| Depletion | Using up a resource until very little is left |
| Extinct | No longer existing anywhere |
| Conservation | Protecting resources so that they are not destroyed or used up |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Resources are **living** (plants, animals, people) and **non-living** (soil, water, air, minerals).
- **Renewable:** sunlight, wind, water, plants and animals. **Non-renewable:** minerals and fossil fuels.
- Plants, animals and fish are renewable **only if allowed to reproduce**.
- **Harvest only what you need**, and replant or restock.
- Soil is conserved by **mulching, terracing, contour ploughing, crop rotation and manure**.
- **Afforestation** = new trees; **reafforestation** = replanting.
- Conservation keeps resources for the **future generations**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- One living resource → a plant (or animal). One non-living resource → soil (or water, minerals).
- Two renewable resources → sunlight and wind.
- Two non-renewable resources → gold and petroleum.
- One way of conserving soil → mulching or terracing.
- One way of conserving fish → using the correct net size / closed seasons / fish farming.
- Word for using up a resource completely → depletion.
- An animal species that no longer exists is said to be → extinct.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. A resource is anything in the environment that people use to satisfy their needs. **Living:** plants, animals, fish (also birds, insects, people). **Non-living:** soil, water, air (also minerals, sunlight, rocks).
2. Because people provide **labour, skills, ideas and leadership** that are needed to use all the other resources and to develop the country.
3. Harvesting is collecting a resource from the environment for use. **Fish** — nets, hooks and traps; **timber** — felling trees and sawing; **salt** — evaporating water from salt lakes such as Katwe; **minerals** — mining, quarrying, drilling or panning.
4. Any five of: take only what is needed; use the right tools and methods; leave young plants, fish and animals to grow; avoid poison, explosives and undersized nets; replant after cutting trees; avoid harvesting in the breeding season; store the harvest well.
5. **Water:** drinking, cooking and washing, irrigation, fishing, transport, hydro-electric power (any four). **Soil:** growing crops, making bricks, making pots, building, holding plant roots (any four).
6. **Renewable** resources can be replaced or never run out, such as sunlight and wind; **non-renewable** resources get used up and cannot be replaced, such as gold and petroleum.
7. **Renewable:** sunlight, wind, forests. **Non-renewable:** copper, petroleum.
8. Because they can only replace themselves if they are given time and conditions to **reproduce**. If people cut trees or catch fish faster than they can reproduce, they are used up completely and may become extinct.
9. Depletion is using up a resource until very little is left, for example **over-fishing** on a lake until the fish stocks nearly disappear (or over-cutting of forests).
10. It means the species **no longer exists anywhere** — every member of it has died and it can never be brought back.
11. Conservation is protecting and using resources carefully so that they are not destroyed or used up.
12. Any five of: mulching, terracing, contour ploughing, crop rotation, applying manure, planting cover crops, controlled grazing, planting trees.
13. Any four of: harvesting rain water, protecting springs and wetlands, avoiding pollution, closing taps, careful irrigation, repairing leaking pipes.
14. **Forests:** afforestation, reafforestation, agroforestry, energy-saving stoves, forest reserves (any four). **Animals and fish:** game parks and sanctuaries, laws against poaching, closed fishing seasons, correct net sizes, fish farming (any four).
15. Any five of: so that resources last for future generations; to keep the environment healthy; to keep soil fertile and rainfall reliable; to protect wildlife and tourism; to avoid famine and poverty; to prevent conflicts over scarce resources.

# P.6 QUICK REVISION

- Resources: **living** (plants, animals, people) · **non-living** (soil, water, air, minerals)
- **Renewable**: sun, wind, water, plants and animals (if allowed to reproduce)
- **Non-renewable**: minerals and fossil fuels
- Harvest only what you need · replant and restock
- Soil: mulch · terrace · contour plough · rotate crops · manure
- Conserve so that resources **last for the future**
