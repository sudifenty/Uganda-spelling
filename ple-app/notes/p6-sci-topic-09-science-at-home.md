## 1. CLEAN AND SAFE WATER

### Definitions

**Clean water:** Water that has no dirt you can see.

**Safe water:** Water that is free from germs and harmful substances, so it can be drunk without causing disease.

**Water is not the same as safe water** — water may look clear and still carry germs.

### Impurities found in water

| Type | Examples | Where they come from |
|---|---|---|
| **Human wastes** | Faeces, urine, sewage | Poor latrines, open defecation, burst sewers |
| **Animal wastes** | Dung and urine | Animals drinking at, and standing in, water sources |
| **Pollutants from farms** | Fertilisers, pesticides, soil washed by rain | Gardens near rivers and lakes |
| **Industrial and domestic waste** | Oil, chemicals, soap, rubbish, polythene | Factories, garages, homes |
| **Natural matter** | Silt, mud, leaves, dead insects | Rain washing the land |
| **Germs** | Bacteria, worms' eggs, cholera and typhoid organisms | All of the above |

**Diseases from unsafe water:** cholera, typhoid, dysentery, diarrhoea, bilharzia, worms,
hepatitis.

## 2. METHODS OF MAKING WATER CLEAN AND SAFE

| Method | How it is done | What it removes |
|---|---|---|
| **Decanting** | Let the water stand until the dirt settles, then pour off the clear water carefully | Heavy solid particles |
| **Filtering** | Pass the water through a clean cloth, sand-and-charcoal filter or ceramic filter | Dirt and some germs |
| **Boiling** | Bring the water to a rolling boil for a few minutes, then cover and cool | **Kills germs** |
| **Treating (chemical)** | Add water-treatment chemicals such as chlorine tablets or solution, following the instructions | Kills germs |
| **Distilling** | Boil the water and cool the steam back into liquid | Germs **and** dissolved substances — gives the purest water |
| **Solar disinfection** | Put clear bottles of water in strong sunshine for several hours | Kills many germs |

**The order that works best:** first **decant** or filter to remove the dirt you can see,
then **boil or treat** to kill germs, then store safely.

### Storing water safely

Use a **clean covered container** · use a **tap or a long-handled cup** so hands and cups do
not enter the water · never mix treated and untreated water · clean the container regularly ·
keep it out of the reach of animals and small children.

### Protecting the water source

Fence the spring or well · build a **drainage channel** so dirty water flows away ·
keep latrines at least **30 metres away** and downhill from the source · do not wash clothes,
bathe or water animals at the source · do not dig or farm right next to it · plant grass
around it to stop soil washing in.

## 3. MAKING SALT FROM PLANT MATERIALS

Long before shops sold salt, people made a salty substance from plants. It is still made
in many villages today.

### How it is done

1. Collect suitable plant material — **dry banana peelings**, banana stems, bean or millet
   stalks, papyrus reeds or water-lily plants.
2. **Dry them well** and then **burn them** into ash.
3. Put the ash in a container or a funnel-shaped basket lined with grass.
4. **Pour clean water** slowly through the ash so that it dissolves the salts — this is
   called **leaching**.
5. Collect the filtered liquid (the **filtrate**) in a pot.
6. **Boil the liquid** until all the water evaporates.
7. What remains is the salt (locally called *kayu*, *omunyu* or *ebiyaayi*).

**The science in it:** the salt dissolves in water while the rest of the ash does not; then
**evaporation** leaves the solid salt behind. This shows **dissolving, filtering and
evaporation** all in one activity.

**Uses:** cooking (especially traditional dishes and vegetables), softening beans and
meat, and as a mineral lick for animals.

## 4. CLEANING CLOTHES IN A HOME

### Materials used

Clean water · **soap** (bar soap, washing powder, liquid soap) · a basin or trough · a
brush · a washing line or clean bushes · pegs · a flat iron or charcoal iron · a sieve or
cloth for straining.

### Steps in washing clothes

1. **Sort** the clothes: white from coloured, heavily soiled from lightly soiled,
   delicate from strong.
2. **Empty the pockets** and shake off dust.
3. **Soak** the dirty clothes in soapy water to loosen the dirt.
4. **Wash** by rubbing gently or brushing collars, cuffs and stains.
5. **Rinse** two or three times in clean water until no soap remains.
6. Hang them **inside out** on a clean line, in the sun, to dry — the sun's heat also
   **kills germs**.
7. When dry, **iron** them: ironing removes creases and **kills jiggers, lice and their eggs**.
8. **Fold and store** them in a clean, dry place.

**Why we wash clothes:** to remove dirt, sweat and smell · to kill germs and prevent skin
diseases such as scabies and ringworm · to make clothes last longer · to look smart and
keep self-respect.

**Safety and care:** use the right amount of soap; do not use a very hot iron on light
materials; hang the line where clothes will not touch the ground; and never dry clothes on
the grass, where jiggers and worms can get onto them.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Clean water | Water with no visible dirt |
| Safe water | Water free from germs and harmful substances |
| Impurity | Anything in water that should not be there |
| Decanting | Letting dirt settle and pouring off the clear liquid |
| Filtering | Passing water through material to remove solid particles |
| Distilling | Boiling water and condensing the steam back to liquid |
| Leaching | Passing water through ash or soil so that it dissolves the salts |
| Filtrate | The liquid that has passed through a filter |
| Evaporation | The changing of a liquid into vapour |
| Sorting | Separating clothes into groups before washing |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Clean water is not always safe water.**
- Methods: **decanting, filtering, boiling, treating, distilling** (and solar disinfection).
- **Boiling kills germs**; **distillation gives the purest water**.
- **Filter or decant first, then boil or treat.**
- Keep latrines at least **30 m** from a water source and downhill from it.
- Salt from plants: **burn → leach with water → filter → boil away the water**.
- Making plant salt shows **dissolving, filtering and evaporation**.
- **Ironing kills jiggers and lice**; drying in the sun kills germs.
- Always **rinse** clothes until no soap remains.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Method that removes germs from water → boiling (or treating with chemicals).
- Method that gives the purest water → distillation.
- Letting dirt settle and pouring off the clear water → decanting.
- One source of water impurities → human waste (or animal waste, farm chemicals).
- One disease caused by unsafe water → cholera (or typhoid, dysentery, bilharzia).
- Process of passing water through ash to dissolve salts → leaching.
- Why clothes are ironed → to remove creases and kill jiggers and lice.
- Distance a latrine should be from a water source → at least 30 metres.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Clean water** has no dirt that you can see, while **safe water** is free from germs and harmful substances and can be drunk without causing disease. Water can look clean and still be unsafe.
2. Any five, for example: human wastes — from poor latrines and open defecation; animal wastes — from animals at the water source; fertilisers and pesticides — from gardens; oils and chemicals — from factories and garages; silt and leaves — washed in by rain; rubbish and polythene — from homes.
3. Any four of: cholera, typhoid, dysentery, diarrhoea, bilharzia, worms, hepatitis.
4. Boiling, filtering, treating with chemicals, distilling and decanting (also solar disinfection).
5. The dirty water is left to stand undisturbed in a container until the heavy particles **settle at the bottom**. The clear water on top is then **poured off carefully** into another clean container, leaving the sediment behind.
6. Because it is **cheap and easy** — every home has a fire — and it **kills the germs** that cause disease, needing no special chemicals or equipment.
7. **Distillation**, because boiling turns the water into steam, leaving germs, salts and other dissolved substances behind; the steam is then cooled back into very pure water.
8. First **decant or filter** to remove the dirt you can see, then **boil or treat** to kill the germs, then store in a clean covered container. Filtering first is important because dirt and solid matter can protect germs from the treatment and make chemicals less effective.
9. Any four of: use a clean covered container; use a tap or long-handled cup so hands do not enter; never mix treated with untreated water; clean the container regularly; keep it away from animals and small children.
10. Any five of: fence the source; build a drainage channel; keep latrines at least 30 m away and downhill; do not wash clothes or bathe at the source; do not water animals there; do not dig or farm next to it; plant grass around it.
11. Any four of: dry banana peelings, banana stems, bean stalks, millet stalks, papyrus reeds, water-lily plants.
12. Dry the plant material → **burn it into ash** → put the ash in a container or funnel-shaped basket lined with grass → **pour clean water slowly through the ash (leaching)** → collect the filtrate → **boil it until all the water evaporates** → the solid salt is left behind.
13. **Dissolving, filtering and evaporation.**
14. Any six of: sort the clothes; empty the pockets and shake off dust; soak them in soapy water; wash by rubbing or brushing the dirty parts; rinse two or three times; hang them in the sun to dry; iron them; fold and store them cleanly.
15. Any four of: to remove dirt, sweat and bad smell; to kill germs and prevent skin diseases; to kill jiggers and lice by ironing; to make the clothes last longer; to look smart and keep self-respect.
