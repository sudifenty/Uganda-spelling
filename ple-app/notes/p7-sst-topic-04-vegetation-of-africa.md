## 1. WHAT VEGETATION IS

### Definitions

**Vegetation:** All the plants that grow in an area — trees, shrubs, grass and herbs.

**Natural vegetation:** Vegetation that grows on its own without being planted by people.

**Planted vegetation:** Vegetation that people plant, such as crops, plantations and woodlots.

### Important Points

- The type of vegetation of an area depends mostly on its **climate**, especially rainfall.
- Vegetation belts in Africa follow the climate belts, so they also repeat north and south of the Equator.

---

## 2. TYPES OF VEGETATION IN AFRICA

### (a) Equatorial (tropical rain) forest

- **Where:** The Congo Basin, coastal West Africa (Nigeria, Ghana, Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire), parts of the Lake Victoria basin.
- **Characteristics:** Tall evergreen trees growing close together; trees form three layers or **canopies**; hard wood; trees have buttress roots; little grass on the dark forest floor; many climbing plants.
- **Trees:** mahogany, ebony, iroko, rubber, oil palm.

### (b) Savannah vegetation

- **Where:** Most of Africa north and south of the forest belt — Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, northern Nigeria, Zambia, Zimbabwe.
- **Characteristics:** Tall or short grass with scattered trees; trees are deciduous and umbrella-shaped; trees have thick bark, long roots and small leaves to survive the dry season.
- **Trees:** acacia, baobab, shea butter tree, thorn trees.
- **Types:** savannah woodland (tall grass, more trees), savannah grassland (short grass, fewer trees).

### (c) Mangrove forest

- **Where:** Along the muddy coasts and river deltas — the Niger Delta, the coast of Kenya, Tanzania, Mozambique, Senegal.
- **Characteristics:** Short salt-tolerant trees growing in salty, muddy water; they have **stilt roots** that stand above the water and breathing roots; they trap mud and protect the coast.

### (d) Mediterranean vegetation

- **Where:** Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, northern Egypt and the Cape area of South Africa.
- **Characteristics:** Short evergreen trees and shrubs; thick bark, waxy or leathery leaves and deep roots to survive the dry summer.
- **Trees and plants:** olive, cork oak, citrus, vines, cedar, eucalyptus.

### (e) Temperate grasslands (velds)

- **Where:** South Africa — the high veld, middle veld and low veld.
- **Characteristics:** Short, soft grass with very few trees; good for rearing cattle and sheep and for growing maize and wheat.

### (f) Desert and semi-desert vegetation

- **Where:** The Sahara, Kalahari and Namib deserts and the semi-desert lands around them (the Sahel).
- **Characteristics:** Scattered thorny bushes, cactus and short tough grass; plants have very long roots, few or no leaves and thick stems that store water; plants grow far apart.
- **Plants:** date palms in oases, cactus, thorn bushes.

### (g) Montane (mountain) vegetation

- **Where:** On high mountains — Rwenzori, Kilimanjaro, Mt Kenya, Ethiopian Highlands, Atlas, Drakensberg.
- **Characteristics:** The vegetation changes in bands as you climb: **forest → bamboo → heath → moorland → bare rock, ice and snow**.

### (h) Swamp (wetland) vegetation

- **Where:** The Sudd in South Sudan, the Okavango in Botswana, the swamps of Uganda.
- **Characteristics:** Papyrus, reeds and water lilies growing in waterlogged land.

---

## 3. FACTORS THAT INFLUENCE VEGETATION DISTRIBUTION

### (a) Climate

Rainfall and temperature matter most. Areas with heavy rain grow thick forests; areas with little rain grow short grass, thorn bushes or nothing at all.

### (b) Type of soil

Deep, fertile soils support big trees. Sandy or salty soils support only tough, scattered plants. Waterlogged soils support papyrus and reeds.

### (c) Relief and altitude

As you climb a mountain the temperature falls, so the vegetation changes with height. Steep slopes lose soil, so plants there are smaller.

### (d) Drainage

Well-drained land supports forests; waterlogged land supports swamp vegetation; very dry land supports desert plants.

### (e) Human activities

People clear forests for farms, settlements, roads and towns; they burn grass, graze animals and cut trees for firewood and timber. They also plant new vegetation such as forests, plantations and crops.

---

## 4. IMPORTANCE OF VEGETATION

- Trees give **timber, poles, firewood and charcoal**.
- Forests are a source of **medicine**, fruits, honey and other foods.
- Vegetation attracts **rainfall** and reduces global warming.
- Plant roots hold the soil and **prevent soil erosion**; fallen leaves make the soil fertile.
- Vegetation is a **habitat** for wild animals and birds.
- Forests and game parks attract **tourists**, who bring foreign exchange.
- Grasslands provide **pasture** for cattle, sheep and goats.
- Vegetation is a source of **raw materials** for industries — paper, furniture, rubber, medicine.
- Wetland vegetation such as papyrus filters water and is used for making mats and baskets.
- Trees provide **shade** and act as windbreaks.

## 5. ANIMALS AND VEGETATION ZONES

### Animals found in each zone

| Vegetation zone | Animals commonly found |
|---|---|
| Equatorial forest | Gorillas, chimpanzees, monkeys, okapi, forest elephants, snakes, colourful birds |
| Savannah | Lions, elephants, giraffes, zebras, antelopes, buffaloes, leopards, rhinos |
| Temperate grasslands (velds) | Springbok, cattle, sheep, hares |
| Desert and semi-desert | Camels, lizards, scorpions, snakes, foxes, ostriches |
| Montane | Mountain gorillas, hyrax, leopards, eagles |
| Swamps and rivers | Hippos, crocodiles, shoebill storks, fish |

### Why animals live in different vegetation zones

- They look for **food** — grass eaters follow grasslands, leaf eaters follow forests.
- They look for **water**.
- They need a **habitat** that hides and protects them from enemies.
- Some animals need **cool** places, others need warm places — this is the climate they are used to.
- Some animals follow the animals they feed on (predators follow prey).
- Some move away from **human settlement** and disturbance.

## 6. GAME PARKS AND CARING FOR WILD ANIMALS

### Definitions

**Game park (national park):** A large area of land set aside by government where wild animals are protected in their natural home and where hunting is not allowed.

**Game reserve:** An area where animals are protected but some controlled activities may be allowed.

**Game ranger:** A person employed to look after and guard animals in a park.

**Game warden:** The officer in charge of a game park or reserve.

### Some game parks in Africa

| Game park | Country |
|---|---|
| Murchison Falls, Queen Elizabeth, Kidepo Valley | Uganda |
| Serengeti | Tanzania |
| Maasai Mara, Tsavo | Kenya |
| Kruger | South Africa |
| Etosha | Namibia |
| Virunga | D.R. Congo |

### Importance of game parks

- They **protect wild animals** from being killed and from losing their homes.
- They attract **tourists**, who bring foreign exchange to the country.
- They create **employment** — rangers, guides, drivers, hotel workers.
- They are used for **research and education** by students and scientists.
- They help **conserve vegetation, water catchment areas and the environment**.
- They earn government **revenue** through taxes and park fees.
- They improve infrastructure — roads, hotels and airfields are built near them.

### Problems faced by game parks

- **Poaching** — illegal killing of animals for meat, ivory and skins.
- **Bush fires** which destroy pasture and animals.
- Long **droughts** that dry up water and pasture.
- **Encroachment** — people entering park land for farming, grazing and settlement.
- **Diseases and pests** among the animals.
- Wild animals **destroying crops** and attacking people nearby, causing conflict.
- **Insecurity** and wars in some areas.
- Limited **funds** to run the parks.

### Ways of caring for wild animals

- **Protection** by law and by employing game rangers and wardens.
- **Feeding** animals and providing water in the dry season.
- **Treatment** of sick animals by veterinary officers.
- Preserving their **habitat** — not cutting the forests or draining the swamps.
- **Educating** the community about the value of wildlife.
- Fighting **poaching** and punishing poachers.
- Fencing park boundaries so animals do not raid gardens.

## 7. POPULATION DISTRIBUTION AND VEGETATION

### Definitions

**Population distribution:** The way people are spread over an area.

**Dense population:** Many people living in a small area.

**Sparse population:** Few people living in a large area.

### Population in each vegetation zone

| Zone | Population | Reasons |
|---|---|---|
| **Savannah** | Dense | Enough rainfall, good soils, easy to clear, good for farming and grazing |
| **Mediterranean** | Dense | Pleasant climate, fertile soils, good ports and trade |
| **Montane / highlands** | Dense on lower slopes | Fertile volcanic soils, reliable relief rainfall, cool and free of tsetse flies |
| **Equatorial forest** | Sparse | Thick forest is hard to clear, poor leached soils, heavy rain, pests and diseases |
| **Desert** | Sparse | Too little water, extreme heat, no pasture, poor soils |
| **Very high mountain slopes** | Sparse | Too cold, too steep, thin soils |
| **Swampy areas** | Sparse | Waterlogged, mosquitoes and disease, hard to build on |

### Effects of population on vegetation

- **Deforestation** — trees cleared for farmland, settlement and firewood.
- **Overgrazing** — too many animals leave the land bare.
- **Bush burning** to open land for farming or hunting.
- **Swamp reclamation** for rice growing, building and industry.
- **Soil erosion and desertification** follow the loss of plant cover.
- Loss of **wild animal habitats** and extinction of some species.
- Some good effects: people plant trees, set up forest reserves and start plantations.

### Ways of conserving vegetation

Afforestation and reafforestation, agroforestry, using energy-saving stoves and biogas, gazetting forest reserves and national parks, teaching people about conservation, and passing and enforcing laws against illegal cutting of trees.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Vegetation belts follow **climate belts**.
- **Equatorial forest** — Congo Basin, tall evergreen hardwood trees.
- **Savannah** — grass with scattered acacia and baobab trees; home of most big game.
- **Mangrove** — muddy coasts and deltas; trees have stilt roots.
- **Velds** — temperate grasslands of South Africa.
- **Mediterranean** plants — olives, vines, cork oak; they resist dry summers.
- Montane vegetation changes with **altitude**.
- Savannah is the most densely populated vegetation zone; deserts and thick forests are sparsely populated.
- **Poaching** is the biggest problem of game parks.
