# PRIMARY 4 SOCIAL STUDIES

**Theme: Living Together in Our District**

# TOPIC 6: HOW TO MEET PEOPLE'S NEEDS IN OUR DISTRICT

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Four Social Studies Syllabus, Term III, Topic 6,
25 periods (`curriculum/P4-SOCIAL-STUDIES-2010.pdf`, pages 35–40).*

**How to use this topic.** In P.3 you were briefly shown social services at the
**sub-county**. Now you look at the whole **district**. These notes teach you the services,
who provides them, the problems met and the solutions. You name the actual centres in your
own district.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- explain what social services are
- name the types of social services in a district
- name the people who provide social services and match each to the service
- name the problems met in providing social services
- suggest solutions to those problems
- say how the social service centres should be cared for

---

## 1. WHAT SOCIAL SERVICES ARE

**Social services** are the **things provided to help people live well**, which people cannot
easily provide for themselves alone.

**Government has the responsibility of providing social services.** Government also allows
some others to provide them on its behalf — **religious groups, private medical
practitioners and business people**.

---

## 2. TYPES OF SOCIAL SERVICES

The syllabus lists these:

| Service | What it provides | Where it is found |
|---|---|---|
| **Education service** | Teaching and learning | Schools, technical institutes, libraries |
| **Medical service** | Treatment and medicines | Hospitals, health centres, clinics |
| **Security service** | Safety and law and order | Police stations, police posts, army barracks |
| **Transport service** | Moving people and goods | Roads, bus parks, taxi stages, landing sites |
| **Water service** | Clean, safe water | Boreholes, protected springs, taps, water tanks |
| **Electricity service** | Power for light and machines | Power lines, transformers, solar panels |
| **Housing service** | Places for people to live | Housing estates, staff quarters |
| **Road maintenance** | Keeping roads in good order | Graders and road gangs at work |
| **Banking** | Keeping and lending money | Banks, savings groups |
| **Postal and communication** | Sending messages | Post office, telephones, faxes, **e-mail and websites** |

> **Newer than the syllabus:** the syllabus (2010) lists **faxes** among the ways of
> communicating. Faxes are hardly used in Uganda today. **Mobile phones, mobile money and
> the internet** now do most of this work. The syllabus list is kept above because it is
> what the curriculum says, and the change is noted here.

## 3. THE PEOPLE WHO PROVIDE SOCIAL SERVICES

The syllabus lists these providers:

| Provider | Service they give |
|---|---|
| **Teachers** | Education |
| **Doctors and nurses** | Medical care |
| **Veterinary officers** | Treatment of animals |
| **Police officers** | Security |
| **Army officers** | Defence and security |
| **Drivers** | Transport |
| **Carpenters** | Furniture and building |
| **Bankers** | Banking and safe keeping of money |
| **Farmers** | Food |
| **Shopkeepers** | Goods for sale |
| **Local leaders and chiefs** | Organising the people, keeping order |

**Match each one:** *A doctor* → *treats the sick.* *A veterinary officer* → *treats
animals.* *A carpenter* → *makes doors, desks and furniture.*

---

## 4. PROBLEMS MET IN PROVIDING SOCIAL SERVICES

The syllabus lists these:

| Problem | What it causes |
|---|---|
| **Poverty** | People cannot pay for services, and government has little money |
| **Bad weather** | Floods and drought spoil roads and crops |
| **Shortage of food** | Hunger makes people weak and unable to work |
| **Theft** | Materials and equipment are stolen |
| **Corruption** | Money meant for services is taken by dishonest people |
| **Irresponsibility / not caring** | People damage and do not look after what is provided |
| **Laziness** | Work is left undone |
| **Idleness** | People waste time instead of working |
| **High population** | Too many people for the few services there are |
| **Poor attitude to work** | People do not do their work well |

---

## 5. SOLUTIONS TO THE PROBLEMS

| Problem | Suggested solution |
|---|---|
| **Poverty** | Start income-generating work · join savings groups · work hard · learn skills |
| **Bad weather** | Plant trees · irrigate · store food after harvest · build stronger roads |
| **Shortage of food** | Grow more food crops · store food well · practise better farming |
| **Theft** | Guard property · report thieves · fence and lock the centres |
| **Corruption** | Report corrupt officers · punish them by law · keep clear records |
| **Irresponsibility** | Teach people to care for public property · form user committees |
| **Laziness and idleness** | Educate people on the dignity of work · create jobs |
| **High population** | Family planning and education · build more schools and health centres |
| **Poor attitude to work** | Train and supervise workers · reward good work |

The syllabus also lists **providing medical services**, **controlling population growth** and
**introducing group farming** among the solutions, and asks learners to plant short-term
crops and draw posters on increasing food production.

---

## 6. CARING FOR SOCIAL SERVICE CENTRES

Social service centres belong to **all of us**. Caring for them is everybody's work.

| Centre | How to care for it |
|---|---|
| **School** | Sweep and slash the compound · do not write on walls or break desks · use the latrine properly |
| **Health centre** | Keep it clean · do not steal drugs · queue patiently |
| **Borehole or spring** | Keep the surroundings clean · repair the handle · do not wash clothes at the source |
| **Road** | Clear the trenches · do not dig in the road · fill small potholes |
| **Market** | Throw rubbish in the pit · do not damage the stalls |
| **Police post** | Report crime honestly · do not give false information |

**What a P.4 pupil can do:** take part in cleaning the school and the road to it, report
damage to a teacher, use water carefully, and tell others why these places matter.

**Road safety:** the syllabus asks learners to practise **safe ways of crossing the road** —
**stop, look right, look left, look right again, and cross when it is clear**. Use a zebra
crossing where there is one.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Social services | Things provided to help people live well |
| Service provider | A person or group that gives a service |
| Poverty | Lack of enough money and basic things |
| Corruption | Dishonest use of a position for personal gain |
| Veterinary officer | A person who treats animals |
| Population | The number of people living in a place |
| Public property | Property that belongs to everybody |
| Maintenance | Keeping something in good working order |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Government has the main responsibility** for providing social services.
- Religious groups, private medical practitioners and business people also provide services
  **on government's behalf**.
- The main services are **education, medical, security, transport, water, electricity,
  housing, road maintenance, banking, and postal and communication**.
- A **veterinary officer** treats **animals**; a **doctor** treats **people**.
- The biggest problems listed are **poverty, corruption, theft, high population and a poor
  attitude to work**.
- Social service centres are **public property** and must be cared for by everybody.
- Crossing a road: **stop, look right, left, right again, then cross**.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- What are social services? Things provided to help people live well.
- Name any four social services in your district.
- Who provides education? **Teachers.**
- Who treats animals? A **veterinary officer**.
- Name three problems met in providing social services.
- Give one solution to corruption.
- Give two ways of caring for a borehole.
- Who has the main responsibility of providing social services? **The government.**

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Saying a **doctor** treats animals — that is a **veterinary officer**.
- Naming a service centre that is **not** in your own district.
- Giving a **problem** when the question asks for a **solution**.
- Forgetting that **parents** are also service providers.
- Writing "money" alone as a solution to poverty — say **how** money is got.
- Thinking only government must care for the centres — **everybody** must.
