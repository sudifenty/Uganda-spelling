## About this topic

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Seven Curriculum, Set One, English Topic 1 — School
Holidays, 30 periods (pages 32–35). This app topic covers sub-topics 1A Holiday Plans and
1B Holiday Activities using the owner's question-and-answer notes.*

**Background:** During school holidays children meet different people whom they interact
with at varying levels, and they carry out various activities. This topic equips learners
with language skills and values to enable them to interact effectively with others.

**Main purpose of the topic:** It equips learners with language skills and values that
help them interact effectively with others during holiday situations.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- describe holiday plans and holiday activities using appropriate language
- narrate, read and write texts, stories, poems and dialogues about holidays
- use the near future (**am/is/are going to + verb**) correctly
- use **question tags**, **reported speech**, **participles**, **adjectives**, the **active voice** and the **third conditional** correctly
- spell holiday vocabulary correctly and present ideas logically
- demonstrate responsibility, cooperation, sharing, care, respect and love

## A. Understanding school holidays

**1. What is a school holiday?**

A school holiday is a period when learners do not attend their normal school lessons.

**2. Why do schools have holidays?**

Schools have holidays to give learners and teachers time to rest and to take part in other
useful activities.

**3. What can learners do during school holidays?**

Learners can:

- visit relatives.
- travel.
- study.
- attend remedial classes.
- help with housework.
- work on the family farm.
- visit friends.
- go on trips and tours.
- attend camps.
- take part in sports.
- attend concerts and shows.
- revise school work.
- learn new skills.

**4. What is a holiday plan?**

A holiday plan is an arrangement of activities that a person intends to carry out during a
holiday.

**5. Why should learners make holiday plans?**

Learners should make holiday plans so that they can use their holiday time properly and
productively.

**6. What should a good holiday plan contain?**

A good holiday plan should contain:

- activities to be done.
- places to visit.
- people to visit.
- time for each activity.
- study or revision time.
- household responsibilities.
- recreation and rest.

**7. Why is planning important during a holiday?**

Planning helps a learner organise time, avoid wasting time and accomplish useful
activities.

**8. What is a holiday activity?**

A holiday activity is something a person does during a school holiday.

**9. What is the difference between a holiday plan and a holiday activity?**

A holiday plan describes what someone intends to do, while a holiday activity is something
actually done during the holiday.

## C. Talking about holiday plans

**38. How can a learner talk about a future holiday plan?**

A learner can use the structure:

**am/is/are going to + verb**

**39. What does "going to" express in this topic?**

It is used to talk about a planned or intended future action.

**40. Give an example using "going to".**

I am going to visit my parents next holiday.

**41. Give another example.**

We are going to travel up-country during the holiday.

**42. Make a sentence using "is going to".**

Sarah is going to attend remedial classes.

**43. Make a sentence using "are going to".**

The children are going to visit their grandparents.

**44. Make a sentence using "am going to".**

I am going to revise Mathematics during the holiday.

**45. How do we form a negative sentence using "going to"?**

We use:

**am/is/are + not + going to + verb**

**46. Give an example.**

I am not going to travel this holiday.

**47. Give another example.**

They are not going to attend the concert.

**48. How do we ask a question using "going to"?**

We place the appropriate form of **be** before the subject.

**49. Give an example.**

Are you going to visit your relatives?

**50. Give another example.**

Is Mary going to attend the camp?

## D. Question tags

**51. What is a question tag?**

A question tag is a short question added to the end of a statement to confirm information
or seek agreement.

**52. Give an example of a question tag about a holiday.**

You will go to the village next week, won't you?

**53. What is the question tag in "You will visit your aunt, won't you?"?**

The question tag is **"won't you?"**

**54. What is the main statement in "You will visit your aunt, won't you?"?**

The main statement is **"You will visit your aunt."**

**55. What is the general rule for question tags?**

A positive statement normally takes a negative question tag, while a negative statement
normally takes a positive question tag.

**56. Give a positive statement with a negative tag.**

You are going to travel, aren't you?

**57. Give a negative statement with a positive tag.**

You aren't going to travel, are you?

**58. Add the correct question tag: John will visit his grandmother, ______?**

won't he?

**59. Add the correct question tag: Mary is going to Kampala, ______?**

isn't she?

**60. Add the correct question tag: They will travel tomorrow, ______?**

won't they?

## E. Reported speech in holiday plans

**61. What is reported speech?**

Reported speech is used to tell someone what another person said without using the
speaker's exact words.

**62. What is direct speech?**

Direct speech gives the exact words spoken by a person.

**63. Give an example of direct speech about a holiday.**

Peter said, "I am going to visit my uncle."

**64. Change into reported speech: Peter said, "I am going to visit my uncle."**

Peter said that he was going to visit his uncle.

**65. Why do we use reported speech?**

We use reported speech to tell another person what someone said.

**66. Change into reported speech: Mary said, "I am going to travel."**

Mary said that she was going to travel.

**67. Change into reported speech: John said, "I will visit my grandmother."**

John said that he would visit his grandmother.

**68. Change into direct speech: Sarah said that she was going to attend remedial classes.**

Sarah said, "I am going to attend remedial classes."

**69. What happens to pronouns when changing direct speech into reported speech?**

Pronouns may change according to who is reporting the speech.

**70. What happens to some verb tenses when direct speech is changed into reported speech?**

Some verb tenses normally change to agree with the reporting verb and the time of
reporting.

A participle is a form of a verb that can be used in verb phrases or as an adjective.

**75. What is the past participle?**

**76. Give examples of past participles.**

- travelled.
- prepared.
- written.
- eaten.
- gone.
- seen.

## J. If 3 conditional

**117. What is the third conditional?**

The third conditional is used to talk about an unreal or imagined situation in the past
and its possible result.

**118. What is the basic structure of the third conditional?**

**If + past perfect tense, would have + past participle**

**119. Give the example used in the topic.**

If I had seen him in the holiday, he would have given me money.

**120. What does the sentence mean?**

It means that the speaker did not see him during the holiday, so the possible result did
not happen.

**121. Give another example of the third conditional.**

If I had travelled to the village, I would have visited my grandmother.

**122. Give another example.**

If Mary had studied during the holiday, she would have performed better.

**123. Complete: If I had gone to the farm, I ______ have helped my father.**

would

**124. Complete: If John had visited us, we would ______ welcomed him.**

have

**125. Complete: If she had revised, she ______ have passed the test.**

would

## K. Active voice

**126. What is active voice?**

Active voice is a sentence structure in which the subject performs the action.

**127. Give an example of active voice.**

Peter cleaned the house.

**128. Identify the subject in "Peter cleaned the house."**

The subject is **Peter**.

**129. Identify the action in "Peter cleaned the house."**

The action is **cleaned**.

**130. Give another example of active voice related to holidays.**

Sarah visited her grandmother.

**131. Change into active voice: "The farm was visited by John."**

John visited the farm.

**132. Why is active voice useful?**

It makes it clear who performs an action.

## IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- A **holiday plan** is what a person **intends** to do; a **holiday activity** is what is **actually done** during the holiday.
- The near future structure is **am/is/are going to + verb**; negative: **am/is/are + not + going to + verb**.
- A **positive statement** takes a **negative tag**; a **negative statement** takes a **positive tag**.
- *You will go to the village next week, **won't you?*** is the topic's question-tag example.
- **Reported speech** repeats what someone said without the exact words; **"am going to"** becomes **"was going to"** in reported speech.
- The **present participle** ends in **-ing** (travelling, revising); the **past participle** (visited, written) forms perfect tenses and passive voice.
- Adjectives are formed by suffixes: **care → careful**, **interest → interesting**, **excitement → exciting**.
- In the **active voice** the **subject performs the action**: *Peter cleaned the house.*
- The **third conditional** = **If + past perfect, would have + past participle** — an unreal past.
- **Up-country** = places outside the main town or city; **urban** = towns and cities; **rural** = countryside.
- **Remedial classes** are **additional lessons** for learners who need more practice.
- The values of the topic are **responsibility, cooperation, sharing, care, respect and love**.

## EXAMINATION POINTS

- Expect **question-tag** gaps: *John will visit his grandmother, **won't he?** / They will travel tomorrow, **won't they?***
- Expect **reported speech** changes: *"I am going to visit my uncle"* → **he was going to visit his uncle**; *"I will visit"* → **he would visit**.
- Expect **going to** in the three forms: positive (*is going to attend*), negative (*am not going to travel*), question (*Are you going to visit…?*).
- Expect **participle** naming: present participles end in **-ing** (travelling, studying); past participles include **written, eaten, gone, seen**.
- Expect **adjective formation**: care → **careful**, interest → **interesting**, excitement → **exciting**.
- Expect **active voice** changes: *"The farm was visited by John."* → **John visited the farm.**
- Expect **If 3** gaps filled with **would** or **have**.
- Expect **vocabulary meanings**: commence = **to begin**, fare = **money paid for travelling**, bash = **an informal party**, chores = **regular small jobs**.
- Question words to expect: **Mention… State… Give… Make a sentence… Change… Complete… Add the correct tag… Identify…**
