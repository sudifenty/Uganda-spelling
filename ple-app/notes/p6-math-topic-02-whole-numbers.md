*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Six Curriculum, Set One, Mathematics Theme 2 Topic 1,

**What you already know:** You can read and write numbers and you have met Roman numerals.
In P.6 you work with numbers **up to 9,999,999** and Roman numerals **up to M (1,000)**.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- identify place values up to millions
- write numbers in expanded form
- read and write numbers in figures and in words up to 9,999,999
- read and write Roman numerals up to M and change them to Hindu-Arabic numbers
- give real-life examples of where Roman numerals are used

---

## 1. PLACE VALUE AND VALUE

### Place value chart

| Place | Millions | Hundred thousands | Ten thousands | Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Digit | 4 | 8 | 3 | 5 | 2 | 6 | 9 |

The number is **4,835,269**.

### Definitions

**Place value:** The position a digit holds in a number.

**Value:** The digit multiplied by its place value.

### Worked examples

1. *In 4,835,269, what is the place value of 8?* → **hundred thousands**
2. *What is the value of 8 in the same number?* → 8 × 100,000 = **800,000**
3. *What is the value of 3?* → 3 × 10,000 = **30,000**

**Careful:** *place value* is the **name of the position**; *value* is a **number**.

---

## 2. EXPANDED FORM

**Expanded form** shows a number as the sum of the values of its digits.

- 4,835,269 = **4,000,000 + 800,000 + 30,000 + 5,000 + 200 + 60 + 9**
- 507,043 = 500,000 + 7,000 + 40 + 3
- Using multiplication: 6,204 = (6 × 1,000) + (2 × 100) + (0 × 10) + (4 × 1)

**Going backwards:** *Write the number: 3,000,000 + 400,000 + 60 + 5* → **3,400,065**

---

## 3. READING AND WRITING NUMBERS UP TO 9,999,999

Group the digits in **threes from the right**: 4 | 835 | 269.

| Figures | Words |
|---|---|
| 4,835,269 | Four million, eight hundred thirty-five thousand, two hundred sixty-nine |
| 2,040,006 | Two million, forty thousand and six |
| 9,999,999 | Nine million, nine hundred ninety-nine thousand, nine hundred ninety-nine |
| 705,300 | Seven hundred five thousand, three hundred |

**Rules:** always write a **zero** where a place is empty; the largest 7-digit number is
**9,999,999** and the smallest is **1,000,000**.

## 4. ROMAN NUMERALS UP TO M

| Roman | I | V | X | L | C | D | M |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value | 1 | 5 | 10 | 50 | 100 | 500 | 1,000 |

### The rules

1. A symbol may be repeated up to **three** times: XXX = 30.
2. **V, L and D are never repeated.**
3. A smaller value **before** a bigger one is **subtracted**: IV = 4, IX = 9, XL = 40, XC = 90, CD = 400, CM = 900.
4. A smaller value **after** a bigger one is **added**: VI = 6, LXX = 70.
5. Only **I, X and C** are used for subtracting.
6. Roman numerals are always written in **capital letters**.

### Worked examples

| Number | Roman |
|---|---|
| 24 | **XXIV** |
| 49 | **XLIX** |
| 90 | **XC** |
| 444 | **CDXLIV** |
| 950 | **CML** |
| 1,000 | **M** |

*Change MCMXLV to Hindu-Arabic:* M = 1000, CM = 900, XL = 40, V = 5 → **1,945**

### Where Roman numerals are used in real life

On **clock faces** · to number the **pages of the introduction** of a book (i, ii, iii) ·
on **buildings and monuments** to show the year · in **chapter and topic numbers** ·
for the names of kings and popes (**Henry VIII, John Paul II**) · in outlines and
examination question parts (i), (ii), (iii).

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Place value | The position a digit holds in a number |
| Value | The digit multiplied by its place value |
| Expanded form | A number written as the sum of the values of its digits |
| Digit | A single figure from 0 to 9 |
| Roman numerals | The number system using I, V, X, L, C, D and M |
| Hindu-Arabic numerals | The ordinary numbers 0, 1, 2, 3 … |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Group digits in **threes** from the right when reading big numbers.
- The largest 7-digit number is **9,999,999**; the smallest is **1,000,000**.
- Value = digit × place value.
- **M = 1,000, D = 500, C = 100, L = 50, X = 10, V = 5, I = 1.**
- **V, L and D are never repeated** and are never used for subtracting.
- **CM = 900** and **CD = 400** — the commonest examination traps.
- Roman numerals are written in **capital letters**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Value of 8 in 4,835,269 → **800,000**.
- Place value of 3 in 4,835,269 → **ten thousands**.
- Write 2,040,006 in words → two million, forty thousand and six.
- 49 in Roman numerals → **XLIX**.
- MCMXLV → **1,945**.
- One place where Roman numerals are used → clock faces (or book pages, monuments).

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing IIII for 4 instead of **IV**, or VV for 10 instead of **X**.
- Confusing **place value** (a name) with **value** (a number).
- Leaving out zeros: writing 2,4,6 for two million, forty thousand and six.
- Writing Roman numerals in small letters.
- Writing "and" in the wrong place when reading numbers.

# P.6 QUICK REVISION

- Group in **threes** from the right · zeros hold empty places
- **Value = digit × place value**
