## 1. WEATHER AND CLIMATE

### Definitions

**Weather:** The condition of the atmosphere of a place at a particular time.

**Climate:** The average weather conditions of a place recorded over a long period of time, usually 30 years or more.

### Elements of weather

| Element | What it means | Instrument used |
|---|---|---|
| Rainfall | The amount of water falling from clouds | Rain gauge |
| Temperature | The degree of hotness or coldness of the air | Thermometer |
| Wind direction | Where the wind is blowing from | Wind vane |
| Wind speed | How fast the wind is moving | Anemometer |
| Humidity | The amount of water vapour in the air | Hygrometer |
| Air pressure | The weight of air pressing on the earth | Barometer |
| Sunshine | Hours of sunshine received | Sunshine recorder |

### Important Points

- Weather changes from hour to hour; climate stays much the same for many years.
- The elements of weather together decide the **type of climate** an area has.
- Climate is studied using **climate graphs** which show rainfall as bars and temperature as a line.

## 2. CLIMATIC REGIONS OF AFRICA

The syllabus names six climatic regions for Africa: **Equatorial, Tropical, Desert (hot and dry), Mediterranean, Temperate** and **Montane**.

### (a) Equatorial climate

- **Where:** Along the Equator — the Congo Basin, the coast of West Africa (Nigeria, Ghana, Liberia), parts of Uganda near Lake Victoria.
- **Characteristics:** Hot and wet all year; rainfall over 1,500 mm a year and falls almost daily, usually in the afternoon; temperatures about 25 °C–27 °C all year; high humidity; two rainfall peaks; heavy convectional rainfall; thick cloud cover.
- **Vegetation:** Tropical rain forest.

### (b) Tropical (savannah) climate

- **Where:** Most of Africa north and south of the equatorial belt — Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, Nigeria's middle belt, Zambia, Zimbabwe.
- **Characteristics:** A clear **wet season and dry season**; rainfall 750 mm–1,500 mm a year; hot for most of the year; the further from the Equator, the shorter the wet season.
- **Vegetation:** Savannah grassland with scattered trees.

### (c) Desert (hot and dry) climate

- **Where:** The Sahara (Egypt, Libya, Algeria, Niger, Chad, Sudan, Mali, Mauritania), the Kalahari (Botswana, Namibia) and the Namib (Namibia).
- **Characteristics:** Very little rainfall, below 250 mm a year; very hot during the day (over 40 °C) and very cold at night; clear skies; low humidity; strong dusty winds; a very big daily range of temperature.
- **Vegetation:** Desert scrub, cacti and thorny bushes.

### (d) Mediterranean climate

- **Where:** The north coast of Africa — Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya and northern Egypt — and the south-western tip of South Africa around Cape Town.
- **Characteristics:** **Hot dry summers and warm wet winters** — the rain comes in winter; moderate rainfall of 500 mm–900 mm; plenty of sunshine.
- **Vegetation:** Mediterranean vegetation — short evergreen trees and shrubs with thick bark, such as olives, cork oak, citrus and vines.

### (e) Temperate (warm temperate) climate

- **Where:** Parts of South Africa away from the Cape, such as the Natal coast and the high veld.
- **Characteristics:** Warm summers and cool winters; moderate rainfall spread over the year; four seasons are noticed — summer, autumn, winter and spring.
- **Vegetation:** Temperate grasslands called **velds**.

### (f) Montane (mountain) climate

- **Where:** On high mountains and highlands — Ethiopian Highlands, Mt Kilimanjaro, Mt Kenya, Rwenzori, the Atlas and Drakensberg mountains.
- **Characteristics:** Temperature falls as you climb higher; heavy relief rainfall on the windward slopes; cold nights; snow and ice on the very high peaks; the climate changes as you go up the mountain.
- **Vegetation:** Montane vegetation in bands — forest, bamboo, heath, moorland, then bare rock and ice.

## 3. FACTORS THAT INFLUENCE THE CLIMATE OF AFRICA

### (a) Latitude (distance from the Equator)

Places near the Equator receive the sun's rays almost overhead all year, so they are hot. As you move away from the Equator the rays become slanting and spread over a larger area, so it becomes cooler.

### (b) Altitude (height above sea level)

The higher the place, the cooler it is. Temperature falls by about **1 °C for every 150 metres** of height. That is why the Ethiopian Highlands are cool although Ethiopia lies in the tropics, and why there is ice on Kilimanjaro even though it stands close to the Equator.

### (c) Nearness to water bodies

Large lakes, seas and oceans cool the air near them during the day and warm it at night. Places near water bodies get **more rainfall** and have a **small range of temperature**. Areas far inland, like the middle of the Sahara, get little rain — this is called **continentality**.

### (d) Winds

Winds carry moisture from one place to another.

| Wind | What it does |
|---|---|
| South-east and north-east trade winds | Bring rain to the eastern coasts of Africa |
| Monsoon winds | Bring rain to the East African coast |
| **Harmattan** | A dry, dusty wind that blows from the Sahara over West Africa in the dry season |
| **Berg winds** | Hot dry winds that blow down the slopes in South Africa |
| Sea breeze / land breeze | Cool the coast by day and warm it by night |

### (e) Ocean currents

| Current | Type | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Benguela Current (west coast, Namibia) | Cold | Makes the Namib Desert dry |
| Canary Current (west coast, north) | Cold | Helps make the western Sahara dry |
| Mozambique / Agulhas Current (east coast) | Warm | Brings rain to the coast of Mozambique and eastern South Africa |
| Guinea Current (Gulf of Guinea) | Warm | Brings rain to the West African coast |

### (f) Relief and aspect

Mountains force moist air to rise. The air cools and drops its rain on the **windward side** of the mountain. The other side, called the **leeward side** or **rain shadow**, remains dry. This is how relief rainfall is formed.

### (g) Vegetation

Thick forests give off water vapour into the air (transpiration). This raises humidity and brings more rainfall. Where forests are cut down, rainfall reduces.

### (h) Human activities

People change climate by cutting down trees, burning bushes, draining swamps, overgrazing, mining and building factories.

### (i) Land mass (size of the continent)

Africa is a very large continent. Its interior lies far from the sea, so the middle of the Sahara receives almost no moist air and is extremely dry.

## 4. TYPES OF RAINFALL

| Type | How it is formed | Where it is common |
|---|---|---|
| **Convectional** | Hot ground heats the air, the air rises, cools, and forms rain in the afternoon | Equatorial regions, Lake Victoria basin |
| **Relief (orographic)** | Moist air is forced to rise over a mountain, cools and forms rain | Mountain areas — Rwenzori, Elgon, Atlas, Drakensberg |
| **Frontal (cyclonic)** | Warm air meets cold air; the warm air rises over the cold air and forms rain | Mediterranean and temperate areas of Africa |

## 5. INFLUENCE OF CLIMATE ON HUMAN ACTIVITIES

| Climatic region | Human activities carried out |
|---|---|
| **Hot deserts** | Irrigation farming, mining (oil in Libya and Algeria), nomadic pastoralism, tourism, trade in oases |
| **Mediterranean and temperate** | Growing citrus fruits, olives, grapes and wheat; trading; industry; tourism; fishing |
| **Equatorial** | Lumbering, growing cocoa, oil palm, rubber and coffee; hunting and gathering; tourism; mining; trading and transport |
| **Tropical (savannah)** | Livestock farming, growing cotton, maize, groundnuts, millet and sorghum; tourism in game parks; trading |
| **Montane** | Farming on volcanic soils (coffee, tea, bananas), tourism, lumbering, mining |

### Problems people face in different climatic regions

- **Deserts:** shortage of water and pasture, extreme heat, sand storms, poor transport, famine.
- **Equatorial areas:** thick forests are hard to clear, heavy rain destroys roads, pests and diseases such as malaria and sleeping sickness.
- **Savannah areas:** unreliable rainfall, long droughts, bush fires, pests such as locusts.
- **Mountain areas:** cold, steep slopes, soil erosion, landslides.

### Possible solutions

Irrigation and water harvesting, drought-resistant crops, afforestation, better roads, spraying against pests, terracing on steep slopes, and resettlement of people from dangerous slopes.

## 6. INFLUENCE OF HUMAN ACTIVITIES ON CLIMATE

### Activities that affect climate negatively

- **Deforestation** — cutting trees for timber, charcoal and farmland reduces rainfall.
- **Bush burning** — releases smoke and destroys vegetation cover.
- **Swamp reclamation** — draining wetlands reduces the water vapour in the air.
- **Overgrazing** — leaves the soil bare and leads to **desertification**.
- **Industries and vehicles** — release gases that cause **global warming**.
- **Mining and quarrying** — destroy the vegetation cover.

### Activities that affect climate positively

- **Afforestation** — planting trees where there were none.
- **Reafforestation** — replanting trees where they were cut down.
- **Agroforestry** — growing trees together with crops.
- **Protecting wetlands and forest reserves** by law.
- **Using clean energy** such as solar, wind and hydro-electric power.

### Key terms

**Global warming:** The rise in the average temperature of the earth caused mainly by gases released by human activities.

**Climate change:** A long-term change in the normal climate of an area, such as rains becoming shorter or less reliable.

**Desertification:** The spread of desert conditions into land that was not desert before.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Weather changes daily; **climate** is the average over many years.
- **Equatorial** — hot and wet all year. **Tropical** — wet and dry seasons.
- **Desert** — very hot by day, cold at night, under 250 mm of rain.
- **Mediterranean** — hot dry summers, **warm wet winters**.
- **Montane** — becomes colder as you go up.
- Temperature falls about **1 °C for every 150 m** of altitude.
- **Cold Benguela Current** — the reason the Namib Desert is dry.
- **Harmattan** — dry dusty wind of West Africa.
- Hot deserts of Africa: **Sahara, Kalahari, Namib**.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Weather is the condition of the atmosphere of a place at a particular time.
2. Climate is the average weather conditions of a place recorded over a long period of time.
3. Desertification is the spread of desert conditions into land that was not desert before.
4. A rain shadow is the dry leeward side of a mountain, which receives little rainfall because the moist air lost its water on the windward side.
5. Rainfall — rain gauge; temperature — thermometer; wind direction — wind vane; wind speed — anemometer. (Also humidity — hygrometer; air pressure — barometer.)
6. Equatorial, Tropical (savannah), Desert, Mediterranean, Temperate and Montane.
7. The Sahara, the Kalahari and the Namib.
8. Any three of: latitude, altitude, nearness to water bodies, winds, ocean currents, relief, vegetation, human activities, land mass.
9. The Mediterranean climate.
10. Any one of: Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Egypt or South Africa.
11. The cold Benguela Current.
12. Relief rainfall (orographic rainfall).
13. The Harmattan.
14. Because temperature falls as altitude increases — by about 1 °C for every 150 metres. Kilimanjaro is 5,895 m high, so the top is far colder than the plains below, cold enough for ice and snow.
15. Cutting down forests reduces rainfall; burning bushes and using vehicles and factories releases gases that warm the earth; draining swamps reduces the moisture that goes into the air. (Overgrazing and mining also expose the soil.)
16. Deserts have clear skies and no clouds or water vapour to hold the heat. During the day the ground heats quickly, but at night the heat escapes fast into the sky, so it becomes very cold.
17. **Causes:** cutting down of trees and swamp drainage in the area, and wider climate change caused by global warming. **Actions:** plant trees (afforestation and reafforestation), protect the remaining wetlands and forests, practise agroforestry, and harvest rain water for irrigation.
18. Because the desert receives less than 250 mm of rain a year, which is too little for crops. Irrigation brings water from rivers, oases or underground so that crops can grow.
19. 1,500 ÷ 150 = 10; 10 × 1 °C = 10 °C fall. 24 °C − 10 °C = **14 °C**.
20. *Learner's map.* The equatorial region should be shaded as a belt along the Equator, covering the Congo Basin and the coast of West Africa.
