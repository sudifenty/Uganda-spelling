## SUB-TOPIC 7A: MARRIAGE

### Types of marriage named in the syllabus

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Cultural (customary) marriage** | Conducted according to the customs of the people, with the introduction and bride price |
| **Religious marriage** | Conducted in a church or mosque by a religious leader |
| **State (civil) marriage** | Conducted before a registrar of marriages, in a government office |

### Vocabulary from the syllabus

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| introduction (kwanjula) | The ceremony where the man is formally introduced to the girl's family |
| engagement | A promise to marry, often marked by giving a ring |
| bride | The woman getting married |
| bridegroom | The man getting married |
| couple | The two people getting married |
| spouse | A husband or a wife |
| best man | The man who supports the bridegroom |
| maid / matron of honour | The young woman (maid) or married woman (matron) who supports the bride |
| bridesmaids | The girls who attend to the bride |
| bridal gown | The special dress worn by the bride |
| bouquet | The bunch of flowers the bride carries |
| fiancé / fiancée | A man / a woman who is engaged to be married |
| bride price / dowry | Property or money given in a customary marriage |
| certificate | The document proving the marriage took place |
| refreshments | Food and drinks served to guests |
| reception | The party held after the wedding |

### The order of events at a wedding

Engagement → introduction (kwanjula) → announcing the banns or giving notice →
the wedding service (vows, rings, signing the register) → photographs →
**reception** with speeches, cake cutting and refreshments → gifts → sending off the
couple.

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Relative pronouns** | *The woman **who** was the bride is my aunt.* |
| **Neither … nor …** | ***Neither** my aunt **nor** my uncle attended the burial.* |
| **Both … and …** | ***Both** the President **and** the Prime Minister attended the wedding.* |
| **… prefer …** | *Most couples **prefer having** a religious marriage **to** a state one.* |

**Two rules to remember**

- **Neither … nor** takes a **singular** verb when both parts are singular: *Neither Ann
  nor Sarah **was** present.* **Both … and** takes a **plural** verb: *Both Ann and Sarah
  **were** present.*
- After **prefer** use *-ing … to …* or *to … rather than …*: *I prefer **walking to**
  riding* / *I prefer **to walk rather than** ride.*

### Grammar focus 1: Relative pronouns (who, which, where)

| Pronoun | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | people | *The man **who** married my sister is a teacher.* |
| **whose** | possession | *The bride **whose** gown was white is my cousin.* |
| **which** | things | *The cake **which** was cut first was very big.* |
| **where** | places | *This is the church **where** they wedded.* |

### Grammar focus 2: Forming adjectives

| Noun | Adjective | Noun | Adjective |
|---|---|---|---|
| culture | cultural | religion | religious |
| tradition | traditional | ceremony | ceremonial |
| beauty | beautiful | nation | national |
| colour | colourful | custom | customary |

## SUB-TOPIC 7B: FUNERAL

*The syllabus warns the teacher to handle this sub-topic gently, because some learners have
lost people they love.*

### Vocabulary from the syllabus

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| burial | The placing of a dead body in a grave |
| funeral rites | The customs performed when somebody has died |
| widow | A woman whose husband has died |
| widower | A man whose wife has died |
| orphan | A child whose parent or parents have died |
| mourner | A person who attends a funeral to show sorrow |
| a will | A written statement saying who should get a person's property |
| condolences | Words expressing sorrow to the bereaved |
| pay respect | To honour the dead person, usually by viewing the body or attending |
| coffin | The box in which a dead body is placed |
| back cloth (barkcloth) | Cloth from a tree used in some cultures to wrap the body |
| shroud | The cloth in which a body is wrapped |
| state funeral | An official funeral organised by the government for an important person |
| heir / heiress | The male / female person chosen to take the dead person's place |
| ancestral grounds | The family land where relatives are traditionally buried |
| cremate | To burn a dead body to ashes |
| grave | The hole in the ground where a body is buried |

### The order of events at a funeral

Announcement of the death → gathering of relatives and neighbours → sending condolence
messages → preparing the body and the grave → the funeral service, with speeches and
prayers → burial → the last funeral rites and the naming of the **heir** in some cultures →
comforting the bereaved family afterwards.

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Despite the fact that …** | ***Despite the fact that** Abdul was a rich man, he was buried in two shrouds.* |
| **Emphatic pronouns** | *He **himself** read the condolence messages.* |
| **… often …** | *I **often** dream about dead relatives.* |

### Grammar focus 3: Emphatic (reflexive) pronouns

| Person | Pronoun |
|---|---|
| I | myself |
| you (one) | yourself |
| you (many) | yourselves |
| he | himself |
| she | herself |
| it | itself |
| we | ourselves |
| they | themselves |

- **Emphatic use** — for stress: *The Kabaka **himself** attended the funeral.*
- **Reflexive use** — the action returns to the doer: *She hurt **herself**.*

### Grammar focus 4: Adjectives of number and quantity

| With countable nouns | With uncountable nouns |
|---|---|
| few, a few, many, several, both | little, a little, much |

- ***A few** mourners arrived early* (some — positive).
- ***Few** mourners arrived early* (almost none — negative).
- *There was **little** food left.* *There was **a little** food left.*

### Grammar focus 5: Past perfect and future perfect

| Tense | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Past perfect** | had + past participle | *The mourners **had left** before the rain started.* |
| **Future perfect** | will/shall have + past participle | *By Sunday, they **will have completed** the funeral rites.* |

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ceremony | A formal event held to mark an important occasion |
| Introduction (kwanjula) | The ceremony where a man is presented to the girl's family |
| Bride / bridegroom | The woman / the man getting married |
| Best man | The man who supports the bridegroom |
| Bride price (dowry) | Property or money given in a customary marriage |
| Widow / widower | A woman whose husband has died / a man whose wife has died |
| Orphan | A child whose parent or parents have died |
| Condolences | Words expressing sorrow to the bereaved |
| Heir / heiress | The male / female person chosen to take the dead person's place |
| Emphatic pronoun | A pronoun such as *himself* used to give emphasis |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Three types of marriage: **cultural, religious and state (civil)**.
- **Neither … nor** with two singular subjects takes a **singular** verb; **both … and** takes a **plural** verb.
- After **prefer**: *prefer + -ing + to + -ing*, or *prefer to … rather than …*
- **who** for people, **which** for things, **where** for places, **whose** for possession.
- Emphatic pronouns end in **-self** (singular) and **-selves** (plural).
- **Few** = almost none; **a few** = some. **Little** = almost none; **a little** = some.
- Past perfect **had + p.p.**; future perfect **will have + p.p.**
- A woman whose husband has died is a **widow**; a man whose wife has died is a **widower**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Give one word for: a man whose wife has died → **widower**.
- Give one word for: a child whose parents have died → **orphan**.
- Give one word for: the box in which a body is placed → **coffin**.
- Give one word for: words expressing sorrow to the bereaved → **condolences**.
- *Neither Ann nor Sarah ____ (be) present.* → **was**
- *Both the bride and the bridegroom ____ (be) happy.* → **were**
- *The Kabaka ____ attended the ceremony.* → **himself**
- *By next Sunday, they ____ (complete) the rites.* → **will have completed**

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing *"either … nor"* or *"neither … or"* — it is **either … or** and **neither … nor**.
- Using a plural verb after *neither … nor* with two singular subjects.
- Writing *"I prefer to walk than ride"* — say *prefer walking **to** riding* or *prefer to walk **rather than** ride*.
- Writing *"theirselves"* or *"hisself"* — the correct forms are **themselves** and **himself**.
- Confusing *few* (almost none) with *a few* (some).
- Spelling: **ceremony, bridegroom, condolences, cemetery, bouquet**.

# REVISION QUESTIONS

2. Give the meaning of: introduction (kwanjula), bride price, best man, matron of honour.
3. Give one word for: the bunch of flowers carried by a bride.
4. Arrange these wedding events in the correct order: reception, engagement, wedding service, introduction.
5. Give one word for: a woman whose husband has died.
6. Give one word for: a person chosen to take the place of the dead person.
7. Give the meaning of: condolences, mourner, shroud, cremate.
8. Fill in: *Neither the bride nor the bridegroom ____ (be) late.*
9. Fill in: *Both the maid of honour and the best man ____ (be) present.*
10. Rewrite using *prefer*: *He likes a religious marriage more than a state one.*
11. Fill in the correct relative pronoun: *The church ____ they wedded is near the market.*
12. Fill in the correct relative pronoun: *The woman ____ was the bride is my aunt.*
13. Fill in the correct emphatic pronoun: *The President ____ attended the state funeral.*
14. Fill in: *____ mourners arrived early, so the service started late.* (Few / A few)
15. Rewrite in the past perfect: *The mourners left. Then the rain started.*
16. Rewrite in the future perfect: *They will complete the funeral rites by Sunday.*
17. Rewrite beginning *Despite the fact that*: *Although Abdul was a rich man, he was buried in two shrouds.*
18. Form adjectives from: culture, religion, tradition, ceremony.
19. Write a short dialogue (six lines) between two people planning to attend a wedding.
20. Write a short paragraph (about six sentences) describing a ceremony you have attended.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Cultural (customary) marriage, religious marriage and state (civil) marriage.**
2. **Introduction (kwanjula)** — the ceremony where the man is formally introduced to the girl's family; **bride price** — property or money given in a customary marriage; **best man** — the man who supports the bridegroom; **matron of honour** — the married woman who supports the bride.
3. A **bouquet**.
4. Engagement → introduction → wedding service → reception.
5. A **widow**.
6. An **heir** (a female one is an **heiress**).
7. **Condolences** — words expressing sorrow to the bereaved; **mourner** — a person who attends a funeral to show sorrow; **shroud** — the cloth in which a body is wrapped; **cremate** — to burn a dead body to ashes.
8. **was**
9. **were**
10. *He **prefers** a religious marriage **to** a state one.* (Also: *He prefers having a religious marriage to having a state one.*)
11. **where**
12. **who**
13. **himself**
14. **Few**

16. *By Sunday, they **will have completed** the funeral rites.*
