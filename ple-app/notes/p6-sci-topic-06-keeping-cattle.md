**What you already know:** You have seen cattle kept in your area. In P.6 you learn the
**types and breeds of cattle**, how they are **fed, watered, housed and cared for**, and
how a **livestock farm** is started and run.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- name the types and breeds of cattle
- name the external features of a cow and a bull
- describe grazing, watering, feeding and housing of cattle
- describe mating, the gestation period and calving
- describe milking and milk products
- name cattle pests and diseases and the practices that harm cattle
- explain how to start a livestock farm

## 1. TYPES OF CATTLE

| Type | Kept for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Dairy cattle** | **Milk** | Friesian, Jersey, Guernsey, Ayrshire |
| **Beef cattle** | **Meat** | Boran, Aberdeen Angus, Hereford, Charolais |
| **Dual-purpose** | Both milk and meat | Sahiwal, Red Poll |
| **Work (draught) type** | Ploughing and pulling carts | Ankole long-horned, Zebu oxen |

---

## 2. BREEDS OF CATTLE

| Group | Meaning | Examples | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indigenous (local)** | Breeds that have always been in the country | **Ankole long-horned**, Zebu, Nganda, Karamojong short-horned | Resistant to disease, heat and long walks; but give **little milk** |
| **Exotic** | Breeds brought from other countries | **Friesian, Jersey, Guernsey, Ayrshire, Boran** | Give **plenty of milk or meat**; but need good feeding, housing and treatment |
| **Cross-breeds** | Local crossed with exotic | Friesian × Ankole | Combine good yield with some local resistance |

**The Friesian** is black and white and gives the most milk; the **Jersey** is small and
brown and gives milk rich in butterfat; the **Ankole** has very long horns and is well
suited to Ugandan conditions.

---

## 3. EXTERNAL FEATURES OF A COW AND A BULL

**Common to both:** head, horns, eyes, ears, muzzle, neck, dewlap, hump (in zebu types),
back, ribs, flank, tail with a switch, four legs with hooves, hide.

| Cow (female) | Bull (male) |
|---|---|
| Has an **udder with four teats** | Has **no udder**; has male reproductive organs |
| Smaller head and thinner neck | **Larger head, thicker neck and shoulders** |
| Body more wedge-shaped in dairy cows | More muscular body |
| Gentler | Usually stronger and fiercer |

**The words to know:** a **cow** is an adult female that has calved · a **heifer** is a
young female that has not yet calved · a **bull** is an adult male · a **steer or ox** is a
castrated male · a **calf** is the young one.

## 4. CARING FOR CATTLE

### Grazing methods

| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| **Free range (open grazing)** | Animals move about freely over a large area |
| **Paddocking** | The land is divided into paddocks and animals are moved from one to another |
| **Tethering** | The animal is tied to a peg with a rope on a small piece of land |
| **Zero grazing** | The animal is kept in a shed and all feed and water are brought to it |
| **Strip grazing** | Animals graze a strip at a time behind a movable fence |

**Zero grazing** suits small plots of land and gives clean milk and easy collection of
manure, but the feed must be cut and carried every day.

### Feeding

Pasture and fodder grasses (elephant grass, Rhodes grass) · legumes (calliandra, lucerne,
desmodium) · crop residues (maize stover, banana peelings, potato vines) · concentrates
(dairy meal, bran, cotton seed cake) · **mineral salt licks** · plenty of **clean water**
every day.

### Housing

A good cattle shed is **spacious, dry, well ventilated and easy to clean**, with a
**concrete or sloping floor**, a good roof, separate places for feeding and watering, a
**calf pen**, a milking place and a **crush** for treatment. Its purposes are to protect
cattle from **rain, sun, cold, thieves and wild animals** and to make treatment and milking
easier.

### Other care

Regular **spraying or dipping** against ticks · **deworming** · **vaccination** · hoof and
horn trimming · brushing and cleaning · treating sickness early · keeping records · gentle
handling.

---

## 5. MATING, GESTATION AND CALVING

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mating (service)** | The joining of a bull and a cow so that the cow becomes pregnant |
| **Artificial insemination (A.I.)** | Placing semen from a good bull into the cow using an instrument |
| **Heat (oestrus)** | The period when a cow is ready to be served — she becomes restless, bellows and mounts others |
| **Gestation period** | The time the calf stays in the womb — about **nine months (280 days)** |
| **Calving** | The act of giving birth to a calf |
| **Colostrum** | The first thick yellow milk after calving, rich in antibodies for the calf |

**Care of an in-calf cow:** good feeding, clean water, no long walks or beating, treatment
when sick, and a clean, quiet place for calving. **The calf must get colostrum within the
first few hours.**

**Advantages of artificial insemination:** the farmer uses a very good bull without keeping
one · it prevents the spread of diseases passed on by mating · one bull can serve very many
cows · it improves the breed quickly.

## 6. MILKING AND MILK PRODUCTS

### Rules of clean milking

Milk at the **same time** each day · wash your **hands** and the cow's **udder** with clean
warm water and dry it · use **clean, dry containers** (aluminium or stainless steel, not
plastic) · milk quickly and completely, using the **full-hand method**, not pulling the
teats · keep the shed clean and free of dust · sieve and cool the milk immediately · a sick
milker or a sick cow must not supply milk for drinking.

### Milk products

Fresh milk · **ghee** · butter · **yoghurt** · cheese · sour milk (*bongo, eshabwe*) ·
cream · powdered milk · ice cream.

**Value of milk:** it is a complete food rich in **protein, calcium and vitamins**; it
builds bones and teeth; and selling milk and its products earns income.

## 7. PESTS, DISEASES AND HARMFUL PRACTICES

### Pests

| Pest | Harm |
|---|---|
| **Ticks** | Suck blood and spread East Coast fever, anaplasmosis and heart water |
| **Tsetse flies** | Spread **nagana** (trypanosomiasis) |
| **Worms** (round, tape, liver fluke) | Cause loss of weight, poor growth and anaemia |
| **Lice, mites and biting flies** | Irritation, wounds and loss of condition |

### Diseases

| Disease | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Foot and mouth disease** | Viral; sores in the mouth and on the hooves; causes quarantine |
| **East Coast fever** | Spread by ticks; fever and swollen glands |
| **Nagana** | Spread by tsetse flies |
| **Anthrax** | Sudden death; the carcass must not be opened; it can infect people |
| **Brucellosis** | Causes abortion in cows; can infect people through raw milk |
| **Mastitis** | Infection of the udder; the milk becomes clotted or bloody |
| **Rinderpest** | Once a great killer; controlled by vaccination |

**Control:** spraying or dipping · deworming · **vaccination** · clean housing · quarantine
of sick animals · boiling milk · calling a **veterinary officer** early.

### Practices that harm cattle

Beating and overworking them · over-milking or milking roughly · tethering on a short rope
with no shade or water · overstocking the land (overgrazing) · dehorning or castrating with
dirty instruments · burning grass so pasture is destroyed · selling or eating meat from an
animal that died of disease · using expired or wrong drugs.

---

## 8. STARTING A LIVESTOCK FARM

1. Choose a **suitable site** — enough land, water, pasture, a road and a market nearby.
2. Decide the **type of cattle** and the system (zero grazing, paddocking, free range).
3. Get **capital** and prepare a simple plan and budget.
4. Prepare the land — clear, plant pasture, fence, dig a water source.
5. Build the **shed, crush and store**.
6. Buy healthy **stock** from a trusted farm, with records.
7. Arrange **feeds, water and minerals**.
8. Register with the **veterinary officer** for vaccination and advice.
9. Keep **records** — breeding, milk yield, treatment, income and expenses.
10. Find a **market** and keep improving the breed.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Types: **dairy, beef, dual-purpose and work** cattle.
- **Friesian** gives the most milk; **Ankole long-horned** is the well-known local breed.
- Indigenous breeds resist disease and heat; **exotic breeds give more milk but need more care**.
- A cow has an **udder with four teats**; a bull has none.
- The **gestation period of a cow is about nine months (280 days)**.
- **Colostrum** is the first milk and protects the calf against disease.
- **Ticks** spread East Coast fever; **tsetse flies** spread nagana.
- **Mastitis** is the disease of the udder.
- Always **wash the udder and your hands** before milking, and use clean containers.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Breed that gives the most milk → Friesian.
- Local breed with very long horns → Ankole long-horned.
- Gestation period of a cow → about 9 months.
- First milk after calving → colostrum.
- Pest that spreads East Coast fever → the tick.
- Disease of the udder → mastitis.
- Keeping an animal in a shed and feeding it there → zero grazing.
- One advantage of artificial insemination → a very good bull can serve many cows / no disease spread by mating.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Dairy** — milk; **beef** — meat; **dual-purpose** — both milk and meat; **work (draught)** — ploughing and pulling carts.
2. **Indigenous** breeds have always been in the country, for example Ankole long-horned and Zebu; **exotic** breeds were brought from other countries, for example Friesian and Jersey.
3. **Indigenous:** resist diseases and heat, and can walk long distances for pasture and water. **Exotic:** give much more milk or meat, and mature faster.
4. Any six of: horns, eyes, ears, muzzle, dewlap, hump, udder with teats, tail, hooves, hide, back, flank.
5. A cow has an **udder with four teats** while a bull has none; a bull has a **larger head and thicker neck and shoulders**; a bull is usually stronger and fiercer, while the cow is gentler and produces milk.
6. **Heifer** — a young female that has not yet calved; **steer (ox)** — a castrated male; **calf** — the young one of cattle.
7. Any four of: free range, paddocking, tethering, zero grazing, strip grazing.
8. Any three of: it suits a small piece of land; the milk and the animal stay clean; manure is easily collected; the animal uses less energy so it gives more milk; it is easy to control diseases and feeding.
9. Any five of: pasture grasses, fodder such as elephant grass, legumes such as calliandra, crop residues, concentrates such as dairy meal, mineral salt licks, and clean water.
10. Any five of: spacious, dry, well ventilated, easy to clean, with a concrete or sloping floor, a good roof, feeding and watering places, a calf pen, a milking area and a crush.
11. The gestation period is about **nine months (280 days)**. **Calving** is the act of giving birth to a calf.
12. Colostrum is the **first thick yellow milk** produced after calving. The calf must get it because it is rich in **antibodies** that protect it from disease, and in nutrients for a good start.
13. Any three of: a farmer can use a very good bull without keeping one; it prevents diseases spread by mating; one bull can serve very many cows; it improves the breed quickly; it is cheaper than keeping a bull.
14. Any six of: milk at the same time daily; wash hands and the udder with clean warm water and dry them; use clean dry metal containers; use the full-hand method; milk quickly and completely; keep the shed clean; sieve and cool the milk at once; a sick person or sick cow must not supply milk.
15. Any five of: ghee, butter, yoghurt, cheese, sour milk, cream, powdered milk, ice cream.
16. Any four: **ticks** — suck blood and spread East Coast fever; **tsetse flies** — spread nagana; **worms** — cause anaemia and poor growth; **lice and mites** — cause irritation and wounds.
17. Any four, for example: **foot and mouth disease** — vaccination and quarantine; **East Coast fever** — spraying or dipping against ticks; **nagana** — control tsetse flies and clear bush; **mastitis** — clean milking and treatment; **anthrax** — vaccination and never opening the carcass.
18. Any five of: beating and overworking; rough or over-milking; tethering with no shade or water; overstocking and overgrazing; using dirty instruments for dehorning or castration; burning pasture; using wrong or expired drugs.
19. Any six of: choose a suitable site; decide the type of cattle and the system; get capital and plan; prepare land, pasture and fencing; build the shed and crush; buy healthy stock; arrange feeds and water; register with the veterinary officer; keep records; find a market.
20. Because records show which cows produce most milk, when each was served and is due to calve, what treatment was given, and how much money came in and went out — so the farmer can plan, breed better animals and know whether the farm is making a profit.
