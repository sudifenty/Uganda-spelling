## 1. WHAT PRIMARY HEALTH CARE IS

**Primary Health Care (PHC):** Essential health care that is made available to all people in the community, at a cost they can afford, with their full participation.

Its aim is to **prevent disease** and promote health, rather than only to treat people after
they fall sick. It is carried out **where people live** — in homes, villages and schools —
and not only in hospitals.

---

## 2. THE ELEMENTS OF PHC

| # | Element |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Health education** about common diseases and how to prevent them |
| 2 | **Proper nutrition** and enough food |
| 3 | **Safe water and basic sanitation** |
| 4 | **Maternal and child health**, including **family planning** |
| 5 | **Immunisation** against the major infectious diseases |
| 6 | **Prevention and control of local endemic diseases** such as malaria |
| 7 | **Appropriate treatment of common diseases and injuries** |
| 8 | **Provision of essential drugs** |
| 9 | **Control of HIV/AIDS** and other epidemics *(added later in Uganda)* |
| 10 | **Mental health and care of the disabled and the elderly** |

**A way to remember the first eight:** **E-N-W-M-I-P-T-D** — Education, Nutrition, Water and
sanitation, Maternal and child health, Immunisation, Prevention of endemic diseases,
Treatment, Drugs.

---

## 3. THE PRINCIPLES OF PHC

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Community participation** | The people themselves plan, work and take responsibility |
| **Equitable distribution** | Health services reach **everybody**, rich or poor, near or far |
| **Appropriate technology** | Using simple, cheap methods people can manage — pit latrines, drying racks, ORS |
| **Intersectoral collaboration** | Health, education, agriculture, water and local government working **together** |
| **Focus on prevention** | Preventing disease is better and cheaper than curing it |
| **Affordability** | Services must cost what the community can pay |

---

## 4. PHC ACTIVITIES THAT PROMOTE COMMUNITY HYGIENE

- **Rubbish and excreta disposal** — digging rubbish pits, using latrines, community clean-up days.
- **Protecting water sources** — fencing springs, building drainage channels, keeping animals and latrines away.
- **Draining stagnant water** and clearing bushes to control mosquitoes.
- Building and using **drying racks, bath shelters and hand-washing facilities (tippy taps)**.
- **Health education** through village health teams, radio, drama, songs and posters.
- **Immunisation days** and growth monitoring of children.
- **Kitchen gardens** for vegetables and fruit.
- **School health clubs**, latrine cleaning rotas and inspection of pupils' hygiene.

---

## 5. RESPONSIBILITIES IN HEALTH

| Who | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **The individual** | Keep clean; eat well; use the latrine and wash hands; sleep under a net; go for treatment early; avoid alcohol, smoking and drug abuse; keep the surroundings clean |
| **The family** | Build and maintain a latrine, bath shelter, rubbish pit and drying rack; boil drinking water; feed the family well; take children for immunisation; care for the sick, the elderly and the disabled |
| **The community** | Protect water sources; hold clean-up days; support village health teams; build and maintain health centres; report outbreaks; discourage harmful practices |
| **The government** | Train and pay health workers; supply drugs and vaccines; build health centres; make and enforce public health laws; run health education campaigns |

---

## 6. FAMILY CARE

**Family:** A group of people related by blood, marriage or adoption who live together and care for one another.

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Nuclear family** | Father, mother and their children |
| **Extended family** | Nuclear family together with other relatives |
| **Single-parent family** | One parent with the children |
| **Child-headed family** | Children living alone after losing their parents |

### Duties of family members

**Parents:** provide food, shelter, clothing, medical care and education; give love, guidance
and discipline; protect the children.
**Children:** obey and respect the parents; help with the chores; go to school and work hard;
care for younger children and the elderly; keep themselves clean; report anything that
frightens or hurts them.

### Care of special members of the family

**Babies and young children** — feeding, immunisation, warmth, love and play.
**The sick** — clean bedding, soft food, medicine given on time, and quick help.
**The elderly** — soft food, warmth, company, help with washing and moving, respect.
**People with disabilities** — support without pity, help to attend school and work, and
inclusion in family activities.

**A healthy home has:** a clean compound, a **latrine**, a **rubbish pit**, a **drying rack**,
a **bath shelter**, a **kitchen with a raised fireplace**, **clean water storage**, and a
**mosquito net** on every bed.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary Health Care | Essential health care available to all at an affordable cost |
| Health education | Teaching people how to keep healthy |
| Sanitation | Safe disposal of human waste and rubbish |
| Immunisation | Giving a vaccine to protect against disease |
| Endemic disease | A disease always present in an area, such as malaria |
| Community participation | People taking part in planning and running their own services |
| Appropriate technology | Simple, cheap methods the community can manage |
| Village health team | Trained community volunteers who promote health |
| Nuclear family | Father, mother and their children |
| Extended family | Nuclear family plus other relatives |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **PHC** is care that is **essential, affordable, accessible and shared by the community**.
- Its **elements** include health education, nutrition, **safe water and sanitation**, maternal and child health, **immunisation**, control of endemic diseases, treatment of common illnesses and **essential drugs**.
- Its **principles**: community participation, equitable distribution, appropriate technology, intersectoral collaboration and **prevention first**.
- **Prevention is better and cheaper than cure.**
- A healthy home has a **latrine, rubbish pit, drying rack, bath shelter and mosquito nets**.
- **Village health teams** take health services to the village.
- Family types: **nuclear, extended, single-parent, child-headed**.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Primary Health Care is **essential health care made available to all people in the community at a cost they can afford, with their full participation**, aimed at preventing disease and promoting health.
2. Any six of: health education; proper nutrition; safe water and basic sanitation; maternal and child health including family planning; immunisation; prevention and control of local endemic diseases; treatment of common diseases and injuries; provision of essential drugs; control of HIV/AIDS; care of the disabled and elderly.
3. Any four of: community participation, equitable distribution, appropriate technology, intersectoral collaboration, focus on prevention, affordability. *(Explain two, for example: **community participation** means the people themselves plan and run their health services; **appropriate technology** means using simple, cheap methods such as pit latrines and ORS that the community can manage.)*
4. Because preventing a disease **costs far less** than treating it, saves lives and suffering, keeps children in school and adults at work, and stops the disease from spreading to others.
5. Any six of: digging rubbish pits and using latrines; protecting water sources; draining stagnant water and clearing bushes; building drying racks, bath shelters and hand-washing facilities; health education; immunisation days; kitchen gardens; school health clubs and clean-up days.
6. Any five of: keep the body clean; eat a balanced diet; use the latrine and wash hands with soap; sleep under a treated net; seek treatment early; avoid alcohol, smoking and drug abuse; keep the surroundings clean.
7. Any five of: build and maintain a latrine, bath shelter, rubbish pit and drying rack; boil or treat drinking water; feed the family well; take children for immunisation; care for the sick, elderly and disabled; keep the home and compound clean.
8. **Community:** protect water sources; hold clean-up days; support village health teams; build and maintain health centres (also report outbreaks). **Government:** train and pay health workers; supply drugs and vaccines; build health centres; make and enforce public health laws (also run health campaigns).
9. A family is a group of people related by blood, marriage or adoption who live together and care for one another. Types: **nuclear, extended, single-parent and child-headed**.
10. **Parents:** provide food, shelter, clothing, medical care and education; give love, guidance and discipline; protect the children. **Children:** obey and respect parents; help with chores; go to school and work hard; care for younger ones and the elderly; keep clean; report anything that frightens or hurts them.
11. Give **clean bedding and a quiet, airy room**; provide soft, tasty food and plenty of fluids; give the **medicine on time and complete the dose**; keep the person clean and comfortable; watch for danger signs and **take them to a health worker quickly**.
12. **The elderly** need soft food, warmth, company, help with washing and moving, and **respect**. **People with disabilities** need support without pity, help to attend school and work, assistive aids where possible, and full **inclusion** in family activities.
13. Any six of: a clean compound; a latrine; a rubbish pit; a drying rack; a bath shelter; a kitchen with a raised fireplace; clean covered water storage; mosquito nets on the beds; a kitchen garden.
14. Village health teams are **trained community volunteers**. They teach families about health, encourage immunisation and use of latrines and nets, treat or refer simple illnesses, follow up sick people and report outbreaks.
15. Any four of: keeping the compound and latrines clean; running hand-washing points with soap; a school health club with drama, posters and talks; growing vegetables in a school garden; inspecting pupils' hygiene; inviting health workers; deworming and immunisation days.
