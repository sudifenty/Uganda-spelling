# TOPIC 3: CLIMATE OF UGANDA

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, Social Studies Topic 3
(`curriculum/P5-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, pages 195–197).*

**What you already know:** You know that some days are hot and others rainy. In P.5 you
learn what **climate** is, the **climatic regions of Uganda**, what influences them, and how
climate affects the way people live.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- explain the difference between weather and climate
- identify and describe the major climatic regions of Uganda
- explain the factors that influence the climate of Uganda
- explain how climate influences human activities
- identify problems associated with climate and suggest solutions

---

## 1. WEATHER AND CLIMATE

### Definitions

**Weather:** The condition of the atmosphere of a place at a particular time.

**Climate:** The average weather conditions of a place recorded over a long period, usually 30 years or more.

### Elements of weather and the instruments used

| Element | Instrument |
|---|---|
| Rainfall | **Rain gauge** |
| Temperature | **Thermometer** |
| Wind direction | **Wind vane** |
| Wind speed | **Anemometer** |
| Humidity | **Hygrometer** |
| Air pressure | **Barometer** |
| Sunshine | **Sunshine recorder** |

All these instruments are kept at a **weather station**, in a white wooden box called a
**Stevenson screen**, which is painted white to reflect heat and has louvres to let air pass.

---

## 2. THE CLIMATIC REGIONS OF UGANDA

The syllabus groups Uganda's climate into **hot, wet and dry** regions. In more detail:

| Region | Where | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Equatorial (wet)** | Around Lake Victoria, the south-west and the Rwenzori foothills — Entebbe, Kampala, Masaka, Kalangala | Rain **almost all year**, over 1,200 mm; two rainy seasons (March–May and September–November); warm, 20–28 °C; high humidity |
| **Tropical / savannah** | Most of central and northern Uganda — Lira, Gulu, Mbarara | A clear **wet season and dry season**; 750–1,200 mm of rain; hot by day |
| **Semi-arid (dry and hot)** | **Karamoja** and parts of Ankole and the rift valley floor | Under 750 mm of rain; long dry spells; very hot; unreliable rainfall |
| **Montane (mountain)** | Slopes of Rwenzori, Elgon, Muhavura | **Cool to cold**; heavy relief rainfall; snow on the Rwenzori peaks |

---

## 3. FACTORS THAT INFLUENCE THE CLIMATE OF UGANDA

| Factor | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Altitude (height)** | The higher the place, the **cooler** it is — about 1 °C for every 150 m. This is why Kabale and the Rwenzori are cold and the rift valley floor is hot |
| **Nearness to water bodies** | Places near Lake Victoria get **more rainfall** and a moderate temperature |
| **Relief (mountains)** | Mountains force air to rise, cool and give **relief rainfall** on the windward side; the leeward side stays dry (a **rain shadow**) |
| **Vegetation** | Forests give off water vapour, bringing more rain; when they are cut, rainfall reduces |
| **Winds** | Moist winds from the lakes and the Congo forest bring rain; dry winds from the north-east bring drought |
| **Latitude** | Uganda lies on the **Equator**, so it is warm all year |
| **Human activities** | Deforestation, swamp draining, bush burning and factory smoke change the climate |

---

## 4. HOW CLIMATE INFLUENCES HUMAN ACTIVITIES

| Region | Activities it favours |
|---|---|
| **Equatorial (wet)** | Growing coffee, bananas, sugar cane, tea and vanilla; fishing; lumbering; dense settlement |
| **Tropical (savannah)** | Growing cotton, maize, millet, sorghum, groundnuts and simsim; cattle keeping; tourism in game parks |
| **Semi-arid (Karamoja)** | **Nomadic pastoralism**; growing drought-resistant crops such as sorghum and millet; bee keeping |
| **Montane** | Growing coffee, tea, bananas, Irish potatoes and vegetables on volcanic soils; tourism and lumbering |

Climate also decides the **type of houses** people build, the **clothes** they wear, and
even the **times** when they work in the gardens.

---

## 5. PROBLEMS ASSOCIATED WITH CLIMATE, AND SOLUTIONS

| Problem | Where it is common | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| **Drought and famine** | Karamoja, Teso, Ankole | Irrigation, water harvesting, drought-resistant crops, food storage |
| **Floods** | Kasese, Butaleja, Kampala, Teso | Drainage channels, avoid building in wetlands, resettlement, planting trees |
| **Landslides** | Bududa, Kigezi | Terracing, tree planting, avoid farming steep slopes, resettlement |
| **Hailstorms and lightning** | Kigezi, Elgon area | Lightning conductors on buildings, insurance, sheltering safely |
| **Soil erosion** | Hilly areas | Terracing, contour ploughing, mulching, cover crops |
| **Unreliable rainfall** | Much of the country | Irrigation, weather forecasts from the meteorology department, early planting |
| **Crop pests and diseases** | Everywhere in wet seasons | Spraying, resistant varieties, extension advice |

### Climate change

Because of **deforestation, swamp draining, bush burning and smoke**, the rains in many
parts of Uganda now come **late, stop early or fail**, while storms have become heavier.
The cure is the same everywhere: **plant trees, protect wetlands and forests, and use
energy-saving stoves, biogas and solar** instead of firewood and charcoal.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Weather | The condition of the atmosphere of a place at a particular time |
| Climate | The average weather of a place over a long period |
| Altitude | The height of a place above sea level |
| Humidity | The amount of water vapour in the air |
| Relief rainfall | Rain formed when moist air is forced to rise over a mountain |
| Rain shadow | The dry leeward side of a mountain |
| Drought | A long period without rain |
| Nomadic pastoralism | Moving with animals in search of pasture and water |
| Weather station | The place where weather instruments are kept and readings taken |
| Stevenson screen | The white wooden box that holds weather instruments |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Weather** changes daily; **climate** is the average over many years.
- Rainfall → **rain gauge**; temperature → **thermometer**; wind direction → **wind vane**.
- The **Stevenson screen** is painted **white to reflect heat**.
- Temperature falls about **1 °C for every 150 metres** of altitude.
- Uganda's climatic regions: **equatorial (wet), tropical (savannah), semi-arid, montane**.
- **Karamoja** is the driest part of Uganda; the **Lake Victoria basin** is the wettest.
- Areas near **Lake Victoria** receive more rainfall.
- Deforestation is the main human cause of **climate change** in Uganda.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Instrument for measuring rainfall → the **rain gauge**.
- Why the Stevenson screen is white → to **reflect heat**.
- The driest region of Uganda → **Karamoja**.
- The main occupation in Karamoja → **nomadic pastoralism** (cattle keeping).
- Why Kabale is cold → it is at a **high altitude**.
- Rain formed when air rises over a mountain → **relief rainfall**.
- One effect of cutting down forests → **less rainfall** / climate change.
- One solution to drought → **irrigation** (or water harvesting, drought-resistant crops).

# REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Give the difference between weather and climate.
2. Name any five elements of weather and the instrument used to measure each.
3. What is a Stevenson screen, and why is it painted white?
4. Name the climatic regions of Uganda and describe any two.
5. Which part of Uganda is the wettest, and which is the driest?
6. Give any five factors that influence the climate of Uganda.
7. Why is it cold in Kabale and on the Rwenzori Mountains?
8. Explain how Lake Victoria influences the climate of the areas near it.
9. What is relief rainfall, and what is a rain shadow?
10. Give three activities favoured by the wet equatorial region.
11. Give two activities carried out in the semi-arid region of Karamoja and say why.
12. Give any five problems associated with climate in Uganda.
13. Give a solution to each of: drought, floods, landslides, soil erosion.
14. Give any four human activities that are changing the climate of Uganda.
15. Suggest any four ways of protecting the climate of Uganda.

---

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Weather** is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular time, while **climate** is the average weather of a place over a long period, usually 30 years or more.
2. Any five: rainfall — rain gauge; temperature — thermometer; wind direction — wind vane; wind speed — anemometer; humidity — hygrometer; air pressure — barometer; sunshine — sunshine recorder.
3. A Stevenson screen is the **white wooden box** in which weather instruments are kept at a weather station. It is painted white to **reflect heat**, so the readings are not affected by the sun.
4. Equatorial (wet), tropical (savannah), semi-arid and montane. *(Describe any two, for example: the **equatorial** region has rain almost all year, over 1,200 mm, and is warm and humid; the **semi-arid** region has under 750 mm of unreliable rain, long dry spells and very high temperatures.)*
5. The wettest is the **Lake Victoria basin and the south-west**; the driest is **Karamoja**.
6. Any five of: altitude, nearness to water bodies, relief, vegetation, winds, latitude, human activities.
7. Because they lie at a **high altitude**, and temperature falls by about 1 °C for every 150 metres you climb.
8. Water evaporates from the lake and forms clouds, so the areas near it receive **more rainfall**; the lake also cools the air by day and warms it at night, giving a **moderate temperature**.
9. **Relief rainfall** is rain formed when moist air is forced to rise over a mountain, cools and condenses. A **rain shadow** is the dry leeward side of the mountain, which receives little rain.
10. Any three of: growing coffee, bananas, sugar cane, tea or vanilla; fishing; lumbering.
11. **Nomadic pastoralism**, because the pasture and water are scattered and seasonal, so herders move to find them; and growing **drought-resistant crops** such as sorghum and millet, because the rainfall is too little for crops that need much water.
12. Any five of: drought and famine, floods, landslides, hailstorms and lightning, soil erosion, unreliable rainfall, crop pests and diseases.
13. **Drought** — irrigation and water harvesting; **floods** — drainage channels and not building in wetlands; **landslides** — terracing, planting trees and avoiding steep slopes; **soil erosion** — contour ploughing, mulching and cover crops.
14. Any four of: cutting down forests; draining swamps; bush burning; smoke from factories and vehicles; overgrazing; quarrying and mining.
15. Any four of: plant trees (afforestation and reafforestation); protect wetlands and forest reserves; use energy-saving stoves, biogas and solar; stop bush burning; teach people about the environment; enforce environmental laws.
