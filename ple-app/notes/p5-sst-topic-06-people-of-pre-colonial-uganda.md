## 1. THE MAJOR ETHNIC GROUPS OF UGANDA

**Ethnic group:** A large group of people who share the same origin, language family and customs.

| Ethnic group | Cradle land (place of origin) | Tribes in Uganda | Where they settled |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bantu** | **Central and West Africa** (the Congo–Cameroon region) | Baganda, Banyankole, Banyoro, Batooro, Bakiga, Basoga, Bagisu, Bagwere, Banyarwanda, Bakonzo, Batwa | The **south, west and east** — the fertile, well-watered areas |
| **Luo (River–Lake Nilotes)** | **Bahr-el-Ghazal, South Sudan** | Acholi, Langi, Alur, Japadhola, Jonam | The **north and parts of the east** |
| **Nilo-Hamites (Plain Nilotes)** | **Ethiopia** (the Ethiopian border area) | Karimojong, Iteso, Kakwa, Sebei, Kumam, Pokot | The **north-east and east** |
| **Hamites (Cushites)** | **North-East Africa** (the Ethiopian Highlands) | Bahima and related pastoral groups | The **west and south-west** grasslands |

**Note:** the **Bantu** are the largest group in Uganda. The **Batwa (Pygmies)** of the
south-western forests are among the earliest known inhabitants.

---

## 2. WHY AND HOW THEY MIGRATED

### Reasons for migration

Search for **fertile land** and pasture · **population pressure** in the cradle land ·
**wars and internal conflicts** · **drought and famine** · **diseases and pests** ·
the wish to **expand and control new land** · following their **cattle**.

### The routes and settlement

The **Bantu** came from the west and settled in the fertile, well-watered south and west.
The **Luo** followed the **Nile valley** southwards from Bahr-el-Ghazal into Acholi, Lango
and West Nile. The **Nilo-Hamites** moved south-west from the Ethiopian border into
Karamoja and Teso. The **Hamites** came from the north-east into the western grasslands
with their long-horned cattle.

### Problems they met on the way

**Resistance** from people already living there · **wild animals** and **diseases** ·
**thick forests, rivers and swamps** · shortage of **food and water** · long distances on
foot · language barriers.

### Effects of the migrations

Population **increased** · new **skills** spread — iron working, farming and cattle keeping ·
**intermarriage** and new communities · **kingdoms and chiefdoms** were formed · but there
were also **wars over land**, loss of land by earlier peoples, and the loss of some cultures.

## 3. SOCIAL ORGANISATION

- People lived in **clans** made of related families, each with a **totem** (an animal or
  plant the clan does not eat or kill).
- The **extended family** lived together and shared work and property.
- **Marriage** was arranged between clans, with **bride price**; a person could not marry
  from their own clan.
- **Traditional religion**: belief in a supreme god (**Katonda** in Buganda, **Ruhanga** in
  the west, **Jok** among the Luo), spirits and ancestors, with shrines and priests.
- **Customs and ceremonies**: birth and twin ceremonies, naming, **initiation** (the
  *imbalu* circumcision of the Bagisu), marriage and burial rites.
- Education was **informal** — children learnt by watching and helping, and through
  **folk tales, riddles, proverbs, songs and dances**.

---

## 4. POLITICAL ORGANISATION

| System | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Centralised** | Ruled by **one leader (a king)** with chiefs under him | **Buganda** — Kabaka; **Bunyoro** — Omukama; **Toro** — Omukama; **Ankole** — Omugabe; **Busoga** — Kyabazinga |
| **Decentralised** | No single ruler; led by **clan elders** | **Acholi**, **Karimojong**, **Iteso**, **Bagisu**, **Alur**, **Bakiga** |

### How a centralised kingdom worked (Buganda as an example)

The **Kabaka** was the head, helped by the **Katikkiro** (prime minister), the **Lukiiko**
(council), county chiefs (**Ssaza**), sub-county chiefs (**Ggombolola**) and parish chiefs
(**Muluka**). The chiefs collected **tribute and taxes**, kept order, settled cases and
raised an **army**. The Bachwezi and later the Babiito ruled the great western kingdoms.

### How a decentralised society worked

The **clan elders** and the **rwot** or council of elders settled disputes, allocated land,
led ceremonies and organised defence. Decisions were made by **discussion and agreement**,
and there was no single king over all the people.

## 5. ECONOMIC ORGANISATION

| Activity | Details |
|---|---|
| **Crop growing** | Bananas (matooke), millet, sorghum, sweet potatoes, cassava, beans, yams, simsim; grown with hoes |
| **Cattle keeping** | The Bahima, Karimojong and Banyoro kept long-horned cattle; cattle were wealth, bride price and food |
| **Iron working** | Making hoes, knives, spears and arrowheads; a highly respected skill |
| **Bark cloth making** | Especially in Buganda, from the mutuba tree |
| **Pottery and basketry** | Pots, plates and baskets from clay and reeds |
| **Salt making** | At **Lake Katwe** and Kibiro |
| **Fishing and hunting** | On the lakes, rivers and in the forests |
| **Barter trade** | Goods exchanged for goods — salt for iron tools, bark cloth for food |

**Trade before money:** people used **barter**. Later, **cowrie shells** were used as money
before the coming of coins and notes.

**Food and cash crops of the time:** the food crops above were grown for the family, while
the crops that later became **cash crops** — coffee, cotton, tea and tobacco — came mainly
with the foreigners, though **coffee (*emwanyi*)** grew wild in Buganda and was used in
blood-brotherhood ceremonies.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ethnic group | A large group sharing origin, language family and customs |
| Cradle land | The original home of a people |
| Migration | The movement of people to settle in another place |
| Clan | A group of related families with a common ancestor |
| Totem | An animal or plant respected by a clan and never eaten |
| Centralised society | A society ruled by one leader such as a king |
| Decentralised society | A society led by clan elders, with no single king |
| Tribute | Goods or labour given to a chief or king |
| Barter trade | Exchanging goods for goods without money |
| Initiation | A ceremony marking the passage from childhood to adulthood |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Bantu** — from **Central/West Africa**; the largest group; Baganda, Banyankole, Bagisu.
- **Luo** — from **Bahr-el-Ghazal, South Sudan**; Acholi, Langi, Alur, Japadhola.
- **Nilo-Hamites** — from **Ethiopia**; Karimojong, Iteso, Kakwa, Sebei.
- **Hamites** — from **North-East Africa**; the Bahima pastoralists.
- **Centralised:** Buganda (Kabaka), Bunyoro and Toro (Omukama), Ankole (Omugabe), Busoga (Kyabazinga).
- **Decentralised:** Acholi, Karimojong, Iteso, Bagisu.
- The Kabaka's prime minister was the **Katikkiro**; his council was the **Lukiiko**.
- Trade was by **barter**; **cowrie shells** were later used as money.
- **Imbalu** is the initiation ceremony of the Bagisu.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. An ethnic group is a large group of people who share the same origin, language family and customs. The four are the **Bantu, the Luo (River–Lake Nilotes), the Nilo-Hamites (Plain Nilotes) and the Hamites (Cushites)**.
2. Bantu — **Central and West Africa**; Luo — **Bahr-el-Ghazal, South Sudan**; Nilo-Hamites — **Ethiopia**; Hamites — **North-East Africa**.
3. **Bantu:** Baganda, Banyankole, Bagisu (also Banyoro, Batooro, Bakiga, Basoga). **Luo:** Acholi, Langi, Alur (also Japadhola).
4. Any five of: search for fertile land and pasture; population pressure; wars and conflicts; drought and famine; diseases and pests; the wish to expand; following their cattle.
5. Any four of: resistance from people already there; wild animals; diseases; thick forests, rivers and swamps; shortage of food and water; long distances; language barriers.
6. Any four of: population increased; new skills such as iron working and farming spread; intermarriage created new communities; kingdoms and chiefdoms were formed; wars broke out over land; some peoples lost their land and culture.
7. A **clan** is a group of related families with a common ancestor. A **totem** is an animal or plant respected by a clan, which members never eat or kill.
8. Any five of: people lived in clans with totems; the extended family lived and worked together; marriage was arranged between clans with bride price; they believed in a supreme god, spirits and ancestors; they held ceremonies such as naming, initiation and burial rites; education was informal, through work, folk tales and proverbs.
9. A **centralised** society is ruled by one leader such as a king, for example **Buganda and Bunyoro**; a **decentralised** society has no single ruler and is led by clan elders, for example the **Acholi and the Karimojong**.
10. Buganda — **Kabaka**; Bunyoro — **Omukama**; Ankole — **Omugabe**; Busoga — **Kyabazinga**.
11. The **Kabaka** was the head. He was helped by the **Katikkiro** (prime minister) and the **Lukiiko** (council). Below them were county (**Ssaza**), sub-county (**Ggombolola**) and parish (**Muluka**) chiefs, who collected tribute, kept order, settled cases and raised the army.
12. By **discussion and agreement among the clan elders**, who settled disputes, shared out land, led ceremonies and organised defence; there was no single king to decide for everyone.
13. Any six of: crop growing; cattle keeping; iron working; bark cloth making; pottery and basketry; salt making; fishing; hunting; barter trade.
14. **Barter trade** is exchanging goods for goods without using money. Before coins, people used **cowrie shells** as money.
15. *Learner's map.* The Bantu areas should cover the south, west and parts of the east; the Luo areas the north and north-west.
