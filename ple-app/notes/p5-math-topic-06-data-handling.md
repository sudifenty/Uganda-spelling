**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- collect data and record it in a table

- read and interpret information from a graph
- work out the average of a set of data

---

## 1. DATA AND HOW WE COLLECT IT

**Data:** facts or information that have been collected, for example the marks of a class or
the number of pupils born in each month.

We collect data by **counting, measuring, asking questions** or **observing**, and we record
it with **tally marks**.

| Colour of shirt | Tally | Number of pupils |
|---|---|---|
| White | \|\|\|\| \|\|\|\| | 9 |
| Blue | \|\|\|\| \|\|\| | 8 |
| Green | \|\|\|\| | 5 |
| Yellow | \|\|\| | 3 |

Tally marks are grouped in **fives** — four upright strokes and one across them.

---

## 2. THE AXES AND THE SCALE

Every graph has two lines called **axes**:

- the **horizontal axis** (across, also called the x-axis) — usually what we are counting
- the **vertical axis** (up, also called the y-axis) — usually how many

**Scale:** what one square or one space on the axis stands for. A scale must be written on
the graph, for example **"Scale: 1 square represents 5 pupils."**

```
Scale: 1 square = 5 pupils
1 square  →  5 pupils
3 squares →  15 pupils
7 squares →  35 pupils
```

**Choosing a scale:** if the biggest number is 40 and you have 8 squares, use
40 ÷ 8 = **5 pupils to a square**.

---

## 3. BAR GRAPHS

A **bar graph** uses bars of equal width. The **height** of the bar shows the number. The
bars do **not** touch each other.

Pupils in a class and the fruits they like best:

| Fruit | Mangoes | Oranges | Bananas | Pineapples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pupils | 12 | 8 | 15 | 5 |

```
Pupils
 16 |            ##
 14 |            ##
 12 | ##         ##
 10 | ##         ##
  8 | ##   ##    ##
  6 | ##   ##    ##
  4 | ##   ##    ##   ##
  2 | ##   ##    ##   ##
    ---------------------------
     Man   Ora   Ban   Pin
Scale: 1 space on the vertical axis = 2 pupils
```

### Reading a bar graph

- Which fruit is liked most? **Bananas (15 pupils)**
- Which is liked least? **Pineapples (5 pupils)**
- How many more like bananas than oranges? **15 − 8 = 7 pupils**
- How many pupils are in the class? **12 + 8 + 15 + 5 = 40 pupils**

---

## 4. LINE GRAPHS

A **line graph** shows how something **changes over time**. Points are plotted and then
joined with straight lines.

The number of pupils absent each day in one week:

| Day | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absent | 4 | 6 | 3 | 7 | 5 |

```
Pupils
  8 |
  7 |                   *
  6 |       *          / \
  5 |      / \        /   *
  4 | *   /   \      /
  3 |  \ /     *----/
  2 |
    ---------------------------
     Mon  Tue  Wed  Thu  Fri
```

- On which day were most pupils absent? **Thursday**
- On which day were fewest absent? **Wednesday**
- Between which days did absence fall most? **Tuesday to Wednesday (6 down to 3)**

## 5. AVERAGE (MEAN)

```
Average = Total ÷ Number of items
```

**Worked example 1.** Find the average of 12, 15, 18, 11 and 14.

```
Total  = 12 + 15 + 18 + 11 + 14 = 70
Number = 5
Average = 70 ÷ 5 = 14
```

**Worked example 2.** From the fruit bar graph above, find the average number of pupils per
fruit.

```
Total = 12 + 8 + 15 + 5 = 40
Number of fruits = 4
Average = 40 ÷ 4 = 10 pupils
```

**Worked example 3 — working backwards.** The average mass of 6 boys is 32 kg. Find their
total mass.

```
Total = Average × Number = 32 × 6 = 192 kg
```

**Worked example 4.** The average of four numbers is 15. Three of them are 12, 18 and 14.
Find the fourth number.

```
Total of all four = 15 × 4 = 60
Total of the three = 12 + 18 + 14 = 44
Fourth number = 60 − 44 = 16
Check: (12 + 18 + 14 + 16) ÷ 4 = 60 ÷ 4 = 15  ✓
```

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Data | Information that has been collected |
| Tally | A stroke used to record one item while counting |
| Axis | One of the two lines of a graph |
| Horizontal axis | The line going across (x-axis) |
| Vertical axis | The line going up (y-axis) |
| Scale | What one square or space on an axis stands for |
| Bar graph | A graph that uses separate bars of equal width |
| Line graph | A graph in which plotted points are joined by lines |
| Average (mean) | The total divided by the number of items |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Tally marks are grouped in **fives**.
- Every graph needs a **title**, **labelled axes** and a **scale**.
- In a bar graph the bars are of **equal width** and do **not touch**.
- A **line graph** is best for showing change over time.
- **Average = Total ÷ Number of items.**
- **Total = Average × Number of items.**
- The average always lies **between** the smallest and the largest value.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- If 1 square = 5 pupils, then 7 squares = **35 pupils**.
- The average of 12, 15, 18, 11 and 14 is **14**.
- If the average mass of 6 boys is 32 kg, the total mass is **192 kg**.
- Read the **scale first** before answering any question on a graph.
- "How many more?" means **subtract** the two bars.
- To find a missing value, work out the **total first**.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Reading a bar as "1 square = 1" when the scale says something else.
- Forgetting to label the axes or to write the scale.
- Dividing by the wrong number of items when finding an average.
- Adding the averages of two groups instead of working out the totals.
- Drawing bars of different widths.
