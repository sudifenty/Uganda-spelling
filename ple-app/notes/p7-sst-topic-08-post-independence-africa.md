## 1. THE ORGANISATION OF AFRICAN UNITY (OAU)

### Definition

**OAU:** The Organisation of African Unity — the first organisation that brought together independent African states.

### Formation

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Date formed | **25 May 1963** |
| Place | **Addis Ababa, Ethiopia** |
| Host | Emperor **Haile Selassie** of Ethiopia |
| Founder members | **32** independent African states |
| Headquarters | Addis Ababa, Ethiopia |
| First Secretary-General | Diallo Telli of Guinea |

**Africa Day** is celebrated every year on **25 May** to remember the formation of the OAU.

### Why the OAU was formed

- To **free the African countries that were still under colonial rule**.
- To promote **unity and solidarity** among African states.
- To defend the **sovereignty, territorial integrity and independence** of member states.
- To **settle disputes** between African countries peacefully.
- To promote **cooperation** in economic, social, cultural, education and health matters.
- To **fight apartheid and racial discrimination**.
- To speak with **one voice** in world affairs.

### Objectives of the OAU

1. To promote the unity and solidarity of African states.
2. To coordinate cooperation for a better life for the peoples of Africa.
3. To defend the independence and sovereignty of member states.
4. To eradicate all forms of colonialism from Africa.
5. To promote international cooperation with respect for the United Nations Charter.

### Functions of the OAU

- Held **meetings and summits** for heads of state.
- Ran a **Liberation Committee** which supported freedom fighters with money and training.
- **Mediated** in border and civil conflicts.
- Encouraged **trade and cooperation** among members.
- Represented Africa in **international organisations**.

### Achievements of the OAU

- It **ended colonialism in Africa** by supporting liberation movements — Angola, Mozambique, Zimbabwe and Namibia became free.
- It helped to **end the apartheid policy in South Africa**.
- It **solved some border conflicts**, for example between Morocco and Algeria.
- It kept African states **united** and gave Africa one voice in world affairs.
- It encouraged the formation of **regional economic groupings**.
- It **gave birth to the African Union**.

### Failures of the OAU

- It **failed to stop civil wars** in member states such as Sudan, Somalia, Liberia and Rwanda.
- It failed to stop **military coups** and dictatorship.
- It failed to remove **poverty and underdevelopment**.
- It had **limited funds** because members did not pay their contributions.
- It could not act inside a member country because of its rule of **non-interference**.
- Members remained **divided** by ideology and by language.
- It failed to form a **united African government**.

## 2. THE AFRICAN UNION (AU)

### Formation

The OAU was replaced by the **African Union**, which was launched in **2002 at Durban, South Africa**, after the Sirte Declaration made in Libya in 1999. Its headquarters remain in **Addis Ababa, Ethiopia**. It has **55 member states**.

### Objectives of the AU

- To achieve greater **unity and solidarity** among African countries and peoples.
- To defend the **sovereignty and independence** of member states.
- To speed up the **political and social-economic integration** of Africa.
- To promote **peace, security and stability** on the continent.
- To promote **democratic principles, good governance and human rights**.
- To promote **sustainable development** and raise living standards.
- To fight **diseases** and promote good health.

### Organs of the AU

| Organ | Work |
|---|---|
| **The Assembly of Heads of State and Government** | The top decision-making body; meets at summits |
| **The Executive Council** | Made up of foreign ministers; prepares the work of the Assembly |
| **The AU Commission** | The secretariat that runs the day-to-day work |
| **The Pan-African Parliament** | Represents the peoples of Africa; sits in Midrand, South Africa |
| **The Peace and Security Council** | Handles conflicts, peacekeeping and security |
| **The Permanent Representatives Committee** | Ambassadors who prepare Council work |
| **The African Court of Justice / Court on Human and Peoples' Rights** | Settles legal disputes and human rights cases |
| **ECOSOCC** | Gives civil society a voice in the AU |
| **Specialised Technical Committees** | Handle areas such as trade, agriculture and education |

### Challenges of the AU

- **Limited funds** to run all its affairs; it depends on donors.
- **Constant civil wars** and insecurity in member states.
- **Different political ideologies** among leaders.
- **Dependence on developed countries** for money and technical help.
- **Poor governance, coups and corruption** in some member states.
- Poor **transport and communication** between member states.
- Some members **do not honour AU decisions**.
- **Language differences** among members.

### Possible solutions to the challenges of the AU

- Members should **pay their contributions** on time so the AU funds itself.
- Strengthen the **Peace and Security Council** and the African standby force.
- Encourage **democracy, free elections and good governance**.
- Promote **trade among African countries** to reduce dependence.
- Improve **roads, railways, air links and internet** across the continent.
- Punish members who **break AU decisions** by suspending them.
- Encourage the **peaceful settlement** of disputes and dialogue.

## 3. REGIONAL ECONOMIC GROUPINGS

### Definition

**Regional economic grouping (common market):** A group of neighbouring countries that agree to work together to improve trade and development.

| Grouping | Full name | Formed | Headquarters | Some members |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EAC** | East African Community | 1967, collapsed 1977, revived in 2000 | Arusha, Tanzania | Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, D.R. Congo, Somalia |
| **COMESA** | Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa | 1994 | Lusaka, Zambia | Uganda, Kenya, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Egypt, Ethiopia, Sudan |
| **ECOWAS** | Economic Community of West African States | 1975 | Abuja, Nigeria | Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Mali, Liberia, Sierra Leone |
| **ECOMOG** | ECOWAS Monitoring Group | 1990 | — | The peacekeeping arm of ECOWAS |
| **SADC** | Southern African Development Community | 1992 | Gaborone, Botswana | South Africa, Zambia, Zimbabwe, Tanzania, Angola, Botswana |
| **IGAD** | Intergovernmental Authority on Development | 1986, revived 1996 | Djibouti | Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia, Somalia, Sudan, South Sudan, Djibouti, Eritrea |

### Why economic groupings were formed

- To **widen the market** for goods made in member countries.
- To **remove or reduce taxes** on goods traded between members.
- To attract **investors** to a bigger market.
- To help members **develop together** — roads, railways, power lines.
- To **keep peace** in the region.
- To speak with **one voice** when bargaining with other countries.

### Advantages of economic cooperation

- A **wider market** for members' goods.
- **Free movement** of people, goods, services and labour.
- **Cheaper goods** because taxes between members are removed.
- **Joint projects** — roads, railways, dams and power lines that one country could not afford alone.
- **Employment** opportunities across the region.
- **Peace and security** through cooperation.
- **Sharing of skills, ideas and technology**.
- Stronger **bargaining power** in world trade.

### Disadvantages of economic cooperation

- Member states may lose **revenue** from taxes on trade between them.
- Countries with **stronger industries** benefit more than weaker ones.
- Free movement may spread **crime, smuggling and diseases**.
- Local **infant industries** may be killed by competition.
- Members may lose some **independence** in making decisions.
- Members produce **similar goods**, so they compete instead of trading.
- **Political differences** and wars can break up the grouping — as happened to the EAC in 1977.

### Ways of solving the problems of economic groupings

- Sharing benefits **fairly** and helping weaker members.
- Encouraging members to produce **different goods** so they can trade.
- Settling **political disputes** peacefully and through dialogue.
- Improving **transport and communication** between member states.
- Members should **pay their contributions** and honour agreements.
- Building **joint industries and projects** owned by all the members.
- Fighting smuggling and improving **border management**.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| OAU | Organisation of African Unity, formed in 1963 |
| AU | African Union, launched in 2002 to replace the OAU |
| Sovereignty | The right of a country to rule itself without outside control |
| Apartheid | The policy of separating people by race, once used in South Africa |
| Common market | A group of countries that trade freely with one another |
| Economic cooperation | Countries working together to improve trade and development |
| Summit | A meeting of heads of state |
| Coup | The removal of a government by force, usually by the army |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- OAU formed **25 May 1963** in **Addis Ababa, Ethiopia**, by **32** founder members.
- **Africa Day** is 25 May.
- The OAU's greatest achievement — **ending colonialism and apartheid** in Africa.
- The OAU's greatest failure — **failing to stop civil wars and coups**.
- The AU was launched in **2002 at Durban, South Africa**; headquarters **Addis Ababa**; **55** members.
- The top organ of the AU is the **Assembly of Heads of State and Government**.
- **EAC** headquarters — Arusha; **COMESA** — Lusaka; **ECOWAS** — Abuja; **SADC** — Gaborone; **IGAD** — Djibouti.
- The EAC collapsed in **1977** and was revived in **2000**; it now has **eight** member states.
