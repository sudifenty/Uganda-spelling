## 1. FORMATION OF THE UNITED NATIONS

### Origin

- The **First World War (1914–1918)** killed millions of people. After it, the **League of Nations** was formed in 1919 to keep world peace, but it failed.
- The **Second World War (1939–1945)** was even worse. When it ended, the nations of the world agreed to try again.
- The **United Nations was formed on 24 October 1945**, when its Charter came into force. The charter had been drawn up at a conference in **San Francisco, USA**.
- **24 October** is celebrated every year as **United Nations Day**.
- The **headquarters** of the UN is in **New York, United States of America**.
- The UN today has **193 member states**. Uganda joined in **1962**, soon after independence.
- The head of the UN Secretariat is the **Secretary-General**. Two former Secretaries-General from Africa were **Boutros Boutros-Ghali** (Egypt) and **Kofi Annan** (Ghana).

### Objectives of the United Nations

1. To **maintain world peace and security**.
2. To develop **friendly relations** among nations.
3. To promote **cooperation** in solving economic, social, cultural and humanitarian problems.
4. To promote **respect for human rights and fundamental freedoms** for all.
5. To be a **centre for harmonising** the actions of nations.

### Functions of the UN

- Sends **peacekeeping forces** to areas of conflict.
- **Settles disputes** between countries peacefully.
- Gives **technical assistance**, loans, grants and donations to poor countries.
- Provides **relief aid** — food, medicine and shelter to victims of war and disaster.
- Protects and settles **refugees**.
- Promotes **education, health and children's welfare** through its agencies.
- Protects **human rights** and fights discrimination.
- Encourages **trade and development** among nations.

## 2. MEMBERSHIP AND ORGANS OF THE UN

### Membership

Any peace-loving state that accepts the UN Charter may apply. It is admitted by the **General Assembly** on the recommendation of the **Security Council**.

### The six main organs

| Organ | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The General Assembly** | New York | All member states meet here; each has one vote; it discusses world problems and passes the budget |
| **The Security Council** | New York | Keeps world peace and security; has **5 permanent members — USA, UK, France, Russia and China** — and 10 non-permanent members elected for two years |
| **The Secretariat** | New York | Runs the day-to-day work of the UN, headed by the **Secretary-General** |
| **The Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)** | New York | Handles economic, social, health, education and cultural matters |
| **The International Court of Justice** | **The Hague, Netherlands** | Settles legal disputes between countries |
| **The Trusteeship Council** | New York | Looked after territories that were not yet self-governing; its work is now finished and it was suspended in 1994 |

**Note:** only the five permanent members of the Security Council have the **veto power** — the power to block a decision.

### Importance of the United Nations

- **Cooperation** between countries.
- **Unity** and one meeting place for the world.
- **Technical assistance** — experts, training and equipment.
- Promotion of **trade** between nations.
- **Loans, grants and donations** for development.
- Keeping **peace** and stopping wars.
- Fighting **diseases, hunger and illiteracy**.
- Protecting **refugees** and children.

## 4. HUMAN RIGHTS IN THE UN CHARTER

### Definition

**Human rights:** The basic freedoms and entitlements that every person is born with, simply because they are human.

The **Universal Declaration of Human Rights** was adopted on **10 December 1948**. **10 December** is celebrated as **Human Rights Day**.

### Some of the rights declared

- All human beings are **born free and equal** in dignity and rights.
- Everyone is **entitled to all the rights and freedoms** in the declaration, without discrimination.
- Everyone has the right to **life, liberty and security of person**.
- **No one shall be subjected to torture** or to cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment.
- **No one shall be held in slavery**; the slave trade is prohibited in all its forms.
- Everyone has the right to a **nationality**.
- Everyone has the right to **education**, to work, and to a fair trial.

### Ways in which human rights are violated

- **Torture**, beating and inhuman punishment.
- **Detention without trial** and unlawful arrest.
- **Child labour**, child sacrifice, child marriage and child trafficking.
- **Denying children education** or health care.
- **Discrimination** by race, tribe, sex, religion or disability.
- **Domestic violence** and defilement.
- Denying people **freedom of speech, worship or association**.
- **Forced labour** and modern slavery.
- **Rigging elections** and denying people the right to vote.

### Ways of respecting human rights

- Making and enforcing **laws** that protect rights.
- **Educating** the public and children about their rights and responsibilities.
- Setting up **human rights commissions** and courts.
- Providing **free basic education** and health services.
- Fair trial in the **courts of law** for everyone.
- Treating all people **equally**, whatever their tribe, sex, religion or ability.
- **Reporting** abuse to police, local leaders or child protection offices.
- Respecting the rights of **others** while enjoying your own.

## 5. THE COMMONWEALTH OF NATIONS

### Definition

**The Commonwealth of Nations:** A free association of independent countries, most of which were once ruled by Britain, that work together for the good of their people.

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Membership | **56 member countries** |
| Head of the Commonwealth | The British monarch — currently **King Charles III** |
| Secretariat | **Marlborough House, London**, headed by a Secretary-General |
| Secretary-General | **Shirley Ayorkor Botchwey** of Ghana, who took office in 2025 |
| Main meeting | **CHOGM** — Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting, held every two years. Uganda hosted CHOGM in **Kampala in 2007** |
| Special note | Membership is voluntary. Four members — **Mozambique, Rwanda, Gabon and Togo** — were never British colonies |

### Functions of the Commonwealth

- Promotes **democracy, good governance, the rule of law and human rights** among members.
- Gives **technical assistance, scholarships and training** to member countries.
- Promotes **trade and investment** among members.
- Helps to **settle disputes** and observes elections in member states.
- Organises the **Commonwealth Games** every four years.
- Promotes **education and youth programmes**, including the Commonwealth Scholarship scheme.
- Supports **small and developing states** in world negotiations.
- May **suspend** a member whose government seizes power unlawfully.

### Comparing the UN and the Commonwealth

| Point | United Nations | Commonwealth |
|---|---|---|
| Membership | 193 states — almost the whole world | 56 states, mostly former British territories |
| Formed | 1945 | Developed from 1926/1949 |
| Headquarters | New York, USA | London, United Kingdom |
| Head | Secretary-General | Head of the Commonwealth (the British monarch); run by a Secretary-General |
| Peacekeeping | Has its own peacekeeping forces | Has no army; uses persuasion, observers and suspension |
| Both | Promote peace, human rights, development, education and cooperation | |

### Challenges of the UN and the Commonwealth

- **Insecurity and political instability** in member states.
- **Limited funding** — some members do not pay their contributions.
- **World dictators** and leaders who ignore decisions.
- **Conflicts among member states**.
- The **veto power** of the five permanent members blocks some UN decisions.
- Decisions are **not binding** in the Commonwealth; a country may simply ignore them or leave.
- **Big powers dominate** the smaller ones.
- **Corruption** and misuse of aid in some member countries.
