## 1. COMMUNICATION

### Definition

**Communication:** The sending and receiving of messages, information or ideas from one person or place to another.

### Traditional means of communication

| Means | How it was used |
|---|---|
| **Drums** | Different beats announced war, death, a meeting, a ceremony or danger |
| **Alarms and screaming** | To call for help when there was danger, a thief or a fire |
| **Horns and trumpets** | Blown to call people to a meeting, to hunt or to war |
| **Whistles** | Used by hunters, herdsmen and leaders |
| **Smoke signals** | Smoke from a fire on a hill warned of danger or called people |
| **Specialised messengers** | Trusted runners carried word from the chief to the people |
| **Rock pictures and signs** | Drawings and marks left to pass on messages, as at Nyero rock paintings |
| **Fire and torches at night** | Warned of danger or guided people |
| **Word of mouth and town criers** | Announcements made in the village |

### Challenges of traditional communication

- Messages could be **misunderstood** or wrongly interpreted.
- They covered a **short distance** only.
- They were **slow**.
- They needed a person who knew the **code** or the language.
- Smoke and drums could not be used in **bad weather** or in war time secrecy.
- The **enemy could hear** the drums and know the plan.
- **No record** was kept of the message.
- Messengers were attacked by wild animals or enemies, or got tired.

### Modern means of communication

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Postal services** | Letters, parcels, telegrams, post office boxes |
| **Telephones** | Mobile phones and landlines; mobile money |
| **Radio calls** | Used by police, health workers and organisations in remote places |
| **Broadcasting** | Radio and television |
| **Print media** | Newspapers, magazines, posters, notice boards, billboards |
| **Electronic media** | The internet, e-mail, websites, social media, video calls |
| **Others** | Fax machines, neon signs, sirens, church and school bells |

### Advantages of modern communication

It is **fast** — a message reaches another country in seconds · it covers **long
distances** · a **record** can be kept (letters, e-mails, recordings) · it carries **detail
and pictures** · it can reach **many people at once** through radio and television · it
creates **employment** and supports business.

### Challenges in modern communication

- It is **expensive** — phones, airtime, data and computers cost money.
- Many rural areas have **no electricity** and poor **network coverage**.
- **Illiteracy** — some people cannot read letters, newspapers or the internet.
- **Language barriers**.
- Poor **roads** delay the postal service.
- **Breakdowns**, power cuts and network failures.
- **Theft, fraud and con artists** using phones and the internet.
- Spread of **false information (rumours)** and harmful content.
- **Loss of privacy**.

### Solutions to the challenges

Extend **electricity and network masts** to rural areas · encourage **adult literacy** and
teach ICT in schools · make phones, data and airtime **cheaper** · improve **roads** for
postal services · use **solar power** where there is no mains electricity · train people to
check information before believing it · make and enforce **laws against fraud and cyber
crime** · use local languages on radio.

## 2. TRANSPORT

### Definition

**Transport:** The movement of people and goods from one place to another.

### Traditional means of transport

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Human** | Walking on foot, head porterage, carrying on the back, stretchers, hammocks |
| **Animals** | Donkeys, camels, oxen, horses; ox carts and sledges |
| **Water** | Canoes made from tree trunks, rafts, **dhows** on the coast |
| **Bridges** | Rope bridges, logs across streams, stepping stones |

**Challenges:** slow · only small loads · tiring · dangerous (wild animals, robbers,
drowning) · could not go far · goods such as milk and fish went bad on the way.

### Modern means of transport

| Type | Examples in East Africa |
|---|---|
| **Road** | Bicycles, motorcycles (boda boda), cars, buses, lorries, trailers |
| **Railway** | The old metre-gauge Kenya–Uganda railway (Mombasa–Kisumu, built 1896–1901), the Central Line in Tanzania (Dar es Salaam–Kigoma), **TAZARA** to Zambia, and the new **Standard Gauge Railway** in Kenya |
| **Air** | Entebbe International Airport, Jomo Kenyatta International Airport (Nairobi), Julius Nyerere International Airport (Dar es Salaam), Kigali International Airport |
| **Water** | Ferries and steamers on Lake Victoria (Port Bell, Jinja, Kisumu, Mwanza, Bukoba); ships at the ports of **Mombasa, Dar es Salaam and Tanga** |
| **Pipeline** | The Eldoret–Mombasa oil pipeline; the crude-oil pipeline from Hoima being built to Tanga |

### Advantages and disadvantages of each form of transport

| Form | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Road** | Reaches almost everywhere, door to door; flexible; cheap for short distances | Accidents; traffic jams; roads damaged by rain; expensive for heavy loads over long distances |
| **Railway** | Carries very heavy and bulky goods cheaply; few accidents; not affected by traffic jams | Very expensive to build; fixed routes only; slow; cannot reach every village |
| **Air** | The **fastest**; good for perishables such as fish, flowers and medicine; reaches remote places | The **most expensive**; carries small loads; affected by bad weather; few airports |
| **Water** | Cheapest for very heavy goods over long distances; carries bulky cargo | Very slow; only where there is water; storms and accidents; needs ports |
| **Pipeline** | Carries oil, gas and water safely and continuously; low running cost; no traffic | Very costly to lay; carries liquids and gases only; leaks pollute the environment |

### Importance of transport and communication

They carry **goods to markets** and raw materials to industries · they take people to
**school, work and hospital** · they help **trade** within East Africa and with the world ·
they create **employment** · they spread **news and knowledge** · they help government
services and **security** reach the people · they promote **unity and tourism** in the
region.

### Problems facing transport in East Africa

**Poor roads**, especially in rural areas and during the rains · **traffic jams** in big
towns · **accidents** caused by careless driving and bad vehicles · **high fuel costs** ·
**old vehicles and trains** · **insecurity** and road robbery · rivers, mountains and
swamps that make road building hard · being **landlocked**, as Uganda, Rwanda and Burundi
are · shortage of funds for building and repair.

### Solutions

Tarmac and repair the roads · widen roads and build by-passes in towns · enforce **traffic
laws** and vehicle inspection · rebuild and extend the **railway** · improve lake ports and
ferries · train drivers · strengthen regional cooperation through the **EAC** so that goods
cross borders quickly · build pipelines for fuel · attract investment in transport.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Communication | The sending and receiving of messages or information |
| Transport | The movement of people and goods from one place to another |
| Traditional means | Ways used long ago, using local materials and skills |
| Modern means | Ways that use present-day machines and technology |
| Head porterage | Carrying goods on the head |
| Dhow | A wooden sailing boat used on the Indian Ocean coast |
| Pipeline | A line of pipes for carrying oil, gas or water |
| Landlocked country | A country with no coastline of its own |
| Perishable goods | Goods that go bad quickly, such as fish, milk and flowers |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Traditional communication: **drums, horns, whistles, smoke, messengers, alarms, rock signs**.
- The biggest weakness of traditional communication is that it covered **only a short distance** and could be **misunderstood**.
- Modern communication is **fast, keeps records and reaches far**, but it is costly and needs power and network.
- The Kenya–Uganda railway was built from **Mombasa** and reached **Kisumu** in 1901.
- The main sea ports of East Africa are **Mombasa (Kenya)** and **Dar es Salaam (Tanzania)**.
- **Air transport** is the fastest but the most expensive; **water transport** is the cheapest for heavy goods but the slowest.
- **Uganda, Rwanda and Burundi are landlocked** and depend on Mombasa and Dar es Salaam.
- **Pipelines** carry only liquids and gases.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- One traditional means of communication — drums (or horns, smoke, messengers).
- One challenge of traditional communication — the message could be misunderstood.
- The fastest means of transport — air.
- The cheapest means of transport for heavy goods over a long distance — water.
- Main port used by Uganda — Mombasa.
- Country in East Africa with no coastline — Uganda (also Rwanda and Burundi).
- Means of transport used to carry oil — pipeline.
- One cause of road accidents — careless driving (or poor roads, old vehicles).
