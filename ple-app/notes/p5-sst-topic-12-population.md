# TOPIC 12: POPULATION, SIZE AND DISTRIBUTION

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, Social Studies Topic 12

**What you already know:** You have studied Uganda's land, climate, vegetation and
resources. In this last topic you study **the people themselves** — how many there are,
where they live and why, and what a fast-growing population means for the country.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- explain population, population growth, distribution and density
- explain what a census is and why it is carried out
- give the factors that influence where people settle
- name densely and sparsely populated areas of Uganda
- state the effects of rapid population growth and suggest solutions

---

## 1. THE WORDS YOU MUST KNOW

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Population** | The total number of people living in a place at a particular time |
| **Population growth** | The increase in the number of people over a period of time |
| **Population distribution** | The way people are spread over an area |
| **Population density** | The number of people living in one square kilometre |
| **Census** | The official counting of all the people in a country |
| **Birth rate** | The number of babies born per 1,000 people in a year |
| **Death rate** | The number of deaths per 1,000 people in a year |
| **Migration** | The movement of people from one place to another to settle |
| **Densely populated** | Having many people in a small area |
| **Sparsely populated** | Having few people in a large area |

**Population density = total population ÷ total area (km²)**

*Example:* a district with 480,000 people covering 6,000 km² has a density of
480,000 ÷ 6,000 = **80 people per km²**.

---

## 2. THE CENSUS

A **census** counts everybody in the country on one night — their number, age, sex,
occupation, education, housing and other details. In Uganda it is carried out by the
**Uganda Bureau of Statistics (UBOS)** about **every ten years**.

| Census year | Population counted |
|---|---|
| 1969 | about 9.5 million |
| 1991 | about 16.7 million |
| 2002 | about 24.2 million |
| 2014 | about 34.6 million |
| **2024** | about **45.9 million** |

*(The 2024 census was Uganda's first fully digital one.)*

### Why a census is important

To know the **number of people** and how fast they are growing · to plan for **schools,
hospitals, water, roads and housing** · to plan **food production and jobs** · to share
government **services and money** fairly among districts · to know how many children need
**immunisation** · to help researchers and investors plan.

---

## 3. FACTORS INFLUENCING POPULATION DISTRIBUTION

| Factor | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Relief** | Gentle, flat land attracts people; very steep or rocky land does not |
| **Climate** | Areas with reliable rainfall are crowded; very dry areas are empty |
| **Soils** | Fertile soils attract farmers |
| **Vegetation** | Thick forests and swamps discourage settlement |
| **Water supply** | People settle near lakes, rivers and springs |
| **Pests and diseases** | Tsetse flies and mosquitoes drive people away |
| **Transport** | People settle along roads, railways and lake ports |
| **Social services** | Schools, hospitals, electricity and water attract people |
| **Employment and industries** | Towns, factories, plantations and mines attract workers |
| **Security** | People leave areas of insecurity and cattle raids |
| **Government policy** | Resettlement schemes, national parks and forest reserves move people in or out |

---

## 4. WHERE PEOPLE LIVE IN UGANDA

| Densely populated | Why |
|---|---|
| The **Lake Victoria crescent** — Kampala, Wakiso, Mukono, Masaka | Fertile soils, reliable rainfall, industries, jobs, services |
| **Kigezi** — Kabale, Kisoro | Fertile volcanic soils and a cool climate |
| Slopes of **Mt Elgon** — Mbale, Sironko | Fertile volcanic soils, reliable relief rainfall |
| Big towns — Jinja, Mbarara, Gulu, Lira | Employment, trade and services |

| Sparsely populated | Why |
|---|---|
| **Karamoja** | Semi-arid, little water and pasture, insecurity |
| **Northern rift valley and Lake Albert flats** | Hot, dry and low-lying |
| **Thick forests and swamps** — Budongo, the Kyoga swamps | Hard to clear, pests, waterlogged |
| **National parks and game reserves** | People are not allowed to settle there |

## 5. POPULATION GROWTH

### Causes of rapid growth in Uganda

**High birth rate** · **early marriages** · **polygamy** · low use of **family planning** ·
**better health services** and immunisation, which lower the **death rate** · improved food
supply · cultural and religious beliefs that value many children · **immigration and
refugees**.

### Effects of rapid population growth

| Problems | Advantages |
|---|---|
| Shortage of land, and land conflicts | A large **labour force** |
| Unemployment and poverty | A bigger **market** for goods |
| Pressure on schools, hospitals and water | More **taxes** for government |
| Food shortage and famine | More ideas, skills and talent |
| Overcrowding and slums in towns | Cheaper labour attracts investment |
| Poor sanitation and fast spread of disease | Development of new areas |
| Destruction of forests and wetlands | |
| Traffic congestion and crime | |

### Solutions

**Family planning** education and services · **educating girls** and discouraging early
marriage · creating **jobs** and supporting small businesses · improving **farming methods**
so less land feeds more people · **resettling** people in less crowded areas · building more
schools, hospitals and houses · developing **rural areas** so fewer people move to towns ·
proper **town planning**.

### The effect of HIV/AIDS on population

HIV/AIDS **raised the death rate**, especially among young working adults, leaving many
**orphans** and **child-headed families**, reducing the labour force and adding heavy costs
to families and to government. Testing, treatment with **ARVs**, health education and
preventing mother-to-child transmission have greatly reduced deaths, and people living with
HIV now live long, healthy lives — and must never be discriminated against.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Density = population ÷ area.**
- A **census** is carried out by **UBOS** about **every ten years**.
- Uganda's **2024 census** counted about **45.9 million** people.
- The **Lake Victoria crescent, Kigezi and the Elgon slopes** are densely populated.
- **Karamoja, national parks, thick forests and swamps** are sparsely populated.
- The main cause of rapid growth is a **high birth rate** with a falling death rate.
- Rapid growth causes **land shortage, unemployment and pressure on services**.
- HIV/AIDS raised the **death rate** and left many **orphans**; ARVs have changed this.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- The official counting of people → a **census**.
- The body that carries out the census in Uganda → **UBOS**.
- Number of people per square kilometre → **population density**.
- One densely populated area of Uganda → the **Lake Victoria crescent** (or Kigezi, Elgon slopes).
- One sparsely populated area → **Karamoja** (or a national park, swamp, thick forest).
- Why Karamoja is sparsely populated → it is **semi-arid** with little water and pasture.
- One effect of rapid population growth → land shortage (or unemployment).
- One solution → family planning education (or creating jobs, educating girls).

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Population** — the number of people living in a place at a particular time; **population growth** — the increase in that number over time; **population distribution** — the way people are spread over an area; **population density** — the number of people per square kilometre.
2. **Density = total population ÷ total area.** 720,000 ÷ 9,000 = **80 people per km²**.
3. A census is the official counting of all the people in a country. It is carried out by the **Uganda Bureau of Statistics (UBOS)**, about **every ten years**.
4. About **45.9 million** people.
5. Any five of: to know the number of people and the growth rate; to plan schools, hospitals, water and roads; to plan food production and jobs; to share government services and money fairly; to plan immunisation and health services; to help investors and researchers.
6. Any six of: relief, climate, soils, vegetation, water supply, pests and diseases, transport, social services, employment and industries, security, government policy.
7. Any three, for example: the **Lake Victoria crescent** — fertile soils, rainfall and jobs; **Kigezi** — fertile volcanic soils and a cool climate; the **Elgon slopes** — volcanic soils and reliable relief rainfall.
8. Any three, for example: **Karamoja** — semi-arid with little water and insecurity; **national parks** — people are not allowed to settle there; **swamps and thick forests** — waterlogged or hard to clear, with pests.
9. Any five of: high birth rate; early marriages; polygamy; low use of family planning; better health services lowering the death rate; improved food supply; cultural and religious beliefs; immigration and refugees.
10. Any six of: land shortage and conflicts; unemployment and poverty; pressure on schools, hospitals and water; food shortage; overcrowding and slums; poor sanitation and disease; destruction of forests and wetlands; traffic congestion; crime.
11. Any four of: a large labour force; a bigger market for goods; more taxes for government; more ideas and skills; cheaper labour that attracts investment; development of new areas.
12. Any six of: family planning education and services; educating girls and discouraging early marriage; creating jobs; improving farming methods; resettling people; building more social services; developing rural areas; proper town planning.
13. **Rural–urban migration** is the movement of people from villages to towns. Young people move to look for **jobs and better pay**, and for **better schools, hospitals, electricity and town life**.
14. Any four of: it raised the death rate, especially among young working adults; it left many orphans and child-headed families; it reduced the labour force; it increased costs for families and government; it lowered the population growth rate for a time.
15. *Learner's map.* For example, the Lake Victoria crescent shaded as densely populated because of fertile soils and jobs, and Karamoja shaded as sparsely populated because it is semi-arid.
