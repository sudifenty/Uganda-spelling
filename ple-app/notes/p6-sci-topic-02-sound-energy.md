## 1. WHAT SOUND IS

**Sound:** A form of energy produced when objects **vibrate**.

**Vibration:** A fast to-and-fro movement.

If there is no vibration, there is no sound. Sound travels in **waves** and needs a
**medium** (a material) to travel through — it **cannot travel through a vacuum**.

### Sources of sound

| Natural sources | Artificial sources |
|---|---|
| Thunder, wind, flowing water, animal and bird calls, the human voice | Drums, radios, bells, vehicles, machines, guitars, whistles |

---

## 2. PITCH, FREQUENCY AND VOLUME

| Term | Meaning | What changes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Frequency** | The number of vibrations in one second | How fast the object vibrates |
| **Pitch** | How high or low a sound is | **High frequency → high pitch**; low frequency → low pitch |
| **Volume (loudness)** | How loud or soft a sound is | The **size of the vibration** (the energy used) |

**Examples:** a thin, tight string gives a **high-pitched** sound; a thick, loose string
gives a **low-pitched** one. Hitting a drum harder does not change the pitch — it makes the
sound **louder**.

**Echo:** A sound heard again after it has bounced off a hard surface such as a wall or cliff.

---

## 3. HOW SOUND TRAVELS

Sound travels **fastest in solids**, more slowly in liquids, and **slowest in gases**,
because the particles in a solid are close together and pass the vibration on quickly.

| Medium | Speed |
|---|---|
| Solid (iron, wood) | Fastest |
| Liquid (water) | Slower |
| Gas (air) | Slowest |
| **Vacuum** | **No sound at all** |

**Simple experiments:**

- Put your ear on a desk while a friend scratches the other end — the sound is louder and
  clearer than through the air, showing that **solids carry sound well**.
- Two tins joined by a tight string make a "telephone" — the sound travels along the
  **string**.
- Tap two stones together under water and listen with your ear in the water — sound travels
  in **liquids** too.

---

## 4. HOW SOUND IS PRODUCED, STORED AND REPRODUCED

| Stage | How |
|---|---|
| **Produced** | By vibrating objects — plucking a string, beating a drum, blowing a whistle, the vibration of the vocal cords in the voice box |
| **Stored (recorded)** | On tapes, CDs, DVDs, memory cards, computer files and phones |
| **Reproduced (played back)** | Through radios, tape recorders, CD and DVD players, computers, phones and loudspeakers |

**Musical instruments and how they make sound:**

| Type | How it makes sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **String** | Strings vibrate | Guitar, adungu, fiddle |
| **Wind** | A column of air vibrates | Flute, trumpet, whistle |
| **Percussion** | The whole instrument or a skin vibrates when struck | Drum, xylophone, rattle |

---

## 5. THE HUMAN EAR

### The three parts and their work

| Part | Contains | Work |
|---|---|---|
| **Outer ear** | Pinna, ear canal, ear drum | The **pinna** collects sound and directs it into the **ear canal**; the **ear drum** vibrates |
| **Middle ear** | Hammer, anvil and stirrup (the three small bones, or ossicles); Eustachian tube | The bones **carry and increase** the vibrations; the **Eustachian tube** balances air pressure with the throat |
| **Inner ear** | Cochlea, semi-circular canals, auditory nerve | The **cochlea** changes vibrations into messages; the **auditory nerve** carries them to the brain; the **semi-circular canals** keep the body's **balance** |

**The path of sound:** pinna → ear canal → ear drum → hammer, anvil, stirrup → cochlea →
auditory nerve → **brain**.

### Diseases and disorders of the ear

| Condition | Cause / sign |
|---|---|
| **Deafness** | Loss of hearing — from disease, injury, very loud noise or old age |
| **Otitis media (ear infection)** | Bacteria; pain and pus from the ear |
| **Perforated (burst) ear drum** | Poking the ear, a heavy blow or a very loud bang |
| **Ear wax blockage** | Too much wax hardening in the canal |
| **Foreign bodies** | Insects, seeds or beads pushed into the ear |
| **Boils in the ear canal** | Bacteria in a hair follicle |

### Care for the human ear

Never put anything sharp — sticks, pins or pens — into the ear · clean only the **outer**
part with a clean, damp cloth · avoid very loud noise and loud earphones · treat coughs,
colds and ear infections early · do not slap anyone on the ear · protect the ears from cold
and from very loud bangs · go for check-ups if hearing becomes poor · never let anyone hit
a child on the head or ears.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sound | A form of energy produced by vibrating objects |
| Vibration | A fast to-and-fro movement |
| Frequency | The number of vibrations in one second |
| Pitch | How high or low a sound is |
| Volume | How loud or soft a sound is |
| Echo | A sound heard again after bouncing off a hard surface |
| Medium | The material through which sound travels |
| Ear drum | The thin skin in the ear that vibrates when sound hits it |
| Cochlea | The part of the inner ear that changes vibrations into nerve messages |
| Eustachian tube | The tube that balances air pressure between the ear and the throat |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Sound is produced by **vibration** and travels in **waves**.
- Sound **cannot travel through a vacuum**.
- Sound travels **fastest in solids** and **slowest in gases**.
- **High frequency → high pitch.** Bigger vibration → **louder** sound.
- The three small bones of the middle ear are the **hammer, anvil and stirrup**.
- The **cochlea** changes vibrations into messages; the **auditory nerve** carries them to the brain.
- The **semi-circular canals** keep balance.
- Never put anything sharp into the ear.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Sound is produced by vibration.
- The material sound travels through fastest → a solid.
- Part of the ear that collects sound → the pinna.
- Part that vibrates when sound reaches it → the ear drum.
- Part that keeps the body balanced → the semi-circular canals.
- Nerve that carries sound messages to the brain → the auditory nerve.
- One cause of a burst ear drum → poking the ear (or a very loud bang).

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Sound is a form of energy produced when objects **vibrate**.
2. **Natural:** thunder, wind, animal calls (also flowing water, the human voice). **Artificial:** a drum, a radio, a bell (also vehicles and machines).
3. **Pitch** is how high or low a sound is and depends on frequency; **volume** is how loud or soft a sound is and depends on the size of the vibration.
4. Frequency is the number of vibrations in one second. The **higher** the frequency, the **higher** the pitch.
5. In a **solid**, because its particles are packed closely together and pass the vibrations on quickly.
6. Because a vacuum has **no particles (no medium)** to carry the vibrations.
7. Place your ear flat on a desk while a friend scratches or taps the far end of the desk. The sound heard through the wood is louder and clearer than through the air, showing that solids carry sound well.
8. An echo is a sound heard again after bouncing off a hard surface. It can be heard in valleys, near cliffs, in empty halls and near high walls.
9. The **outer ear, middle ear and inner ear**.
10. The **hammer, anvil and stirrup**.
11. **Pinna** — collects sound and directs it into the ear canal; **ear drum** — vibrates when sound hits it; **cochlea** — changes vibrations into nerve messages; **auditory nerve** — carries the messages to the brain.
12. The **semi-circular canals**.
13. Any four of: deafness, otitis media (ear infection), a perforated ear drum, ear wax blockage, foreign bodies in the ear, boils in the ear canal.
14. Any five of: never put sharp objects in the ear; clean only the outer ear with a damp cloth; avoid very loud noise and loud earphones; treat infections early; do not slap anyone on the ear; protect ears from cold; go for check-ups.
15. **String** — guitar or adungu; **wind** — flute or whistle; **percussion** — drum or xylophone.
