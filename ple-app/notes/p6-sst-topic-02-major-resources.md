## 1. WHAT RESOURCES ARE

### Definitions

**Resource:** Anything found in the environment that people use to satisfy their needs.

**Natural resource:** A resource provided by nature — land, water, minerals, vegetation, animals and climate.

The major resources of East Africa are **land, minerals, water, vegetation, wildlife,
climate and the people themselves**.

---

## 2. LAND AS A RESOURCE

### (a) Plants (vegetation)

| Vegetation type | Where in East Africa |
|---|---|
| Tropical rain forest | Mabira and Budongo (Uganda), Kakamega (Kenya), Kivu shores |
| Savannah grassland | Most of the region — the largest area |
| Montane vegetation | Slopes of Kilimanjaro, Mt Kenya, Elgon and Rwenzori |
| Semi-desert | Karamoja, northern Kenya, central Tanzania |
| Mangrove forest | The Indian Ocean coast of Kenya and Tanzania |
| Swamp vegetation | The Sudd edge, Lake Kyoga area, Kilombero |

**Uses:** timber, poles, firewood and charcoal · medicine · pasture · raw materials for
paper and furniture · protection of soil and water catchments · homes for wild animals ·
tourist attractions.

### (b) Animals — domestic and wild

**Domestic animals:** cattle, goats, sheep, pigs, poultry, donkeys. Breeds of cattle include the local **Ankole long-horned** and **Zebu**, and the exotic **Friesian, Jersey and Guernsey**.
They give meat, milk, hides and skins, manure, transport and money.

**Wild animals:** elephants, lions, buffaloes, giraffes, zebras, antelopes, rhinos, gorillas and chimpanzees. They attract **tourists**, are used for research and education, and are part of our heritage.

### (c) Cultivation — cash and food crops

| Crop type | Examples and where they are grown |
|---|---|
| **Cash crops** | Coffee (Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania), tea (Kericho in Kenya, Fort Portal in Uganda), cotton (eastern Uganda, Tanzania), sugar cane (Kakira, Kinyara, Mumias), sisal (Tanga in Tanzania), pyrethrum (Kenya highlands), tobacco (West Nile), cloves (**Zanzibar and Pemba**), cashew nuts (southern Tanzania), vanilla (Uganda) |
| **Food crops** | Matooke, maize, millet, sorghum, cassava, sweet potatoes, beans, groundnuts, rice, wheat |

**Factors favouring crop growing:** fertile soils · reliable rainfall · suitable climate ·
availability of land and labour · markets and transport.

### (d) Tourism

**Tourism:** Travelling to visit places of interest for pleasure, study or rest.

| Attraction | Examples |
|---|---|
| Game parks and reserves | **Serengeti, Ngorongoro** (Tanzania); **Maasai Mara, Tsavo, Amboseli** (Kenya); **Murchison Falls, Queen Elizabeth, Kidepo, Bwindi** (Uganda); **Akagera** (Rwanda); **Ruvubu** (Burundi) |
| Mountains | Kilimanjaro, Mt Kenya, Rwenzori, Elgon, Virunga volcanoes |
| Water features | Lake Victoria, the source of the Nile at Jinja, Murchison Falls, the Indian Ocean beaches |
| Culture and history | Kasubi Tombs, Fort Jesus in Mombasa, Bagamoyo, traditional dances, the Kigali Genocide Memorial |

**Importance of tourism:** foreign exchange · employment · government revenue · market for
crafts and food · development of roads, hotels and airports · international friendship.

**Problems of tourism:** poaching · insecurity and political unrest · poor roads to some
parks · high cost of travel · destruction of habitats · few skilled guides · animals raiding
gardens near parks.

## 3. MINERALS

### Definitions

**Mineral:** A useful substance obtained from the earth by mining.

**Mining:** The process of removing minerals from the earth.

### Minerals of East Africa and where they are found

| Country | Minerals |
|---|---|
| **Uganda** | Copper (**Kilembe**), cobalt, limestone (**Hima, Tororo**), gold (Karamoja, Busia), salt (**Katwe**), iron ore (Muko), phosphates (Sukulu), oil (**Albertine Graben**) |
| **Kenya** | Soda ash (**Lake Magadi**), fluorspar (Kerio Valley), limestone (Athi River), titanium (Kwale), gemstones, salt |
| **Tanzania** | Diamonds (**Mwadui**), gold (**Geita**), **tanzanite** (Mererani), natural gas (Songo Songo), coal (Kiwira), tin, phosphates |
| **Rwanda** | Cassiterite (tin), coltan, wolfram, gold |
| **Burundi** | Nickel, gold, cassiterite, peat |

### Types of mining

| Type | How it is done |
|---|---|
| **Traditional** | Simple tools — hoes, pangas, pans and baskets; panning gold in river sand; digging shallow pits by hand |
| **Modern (open cast)** | Removing the soil above and scooping the mineral with machines, as at Mwadui and Hima |
| **Modern (shaft / underground)** | Digging deep shafts and tunnels, as at Kilembe |
| **Drilling** | Boring deep holes for oil and natural gas |
| **Dredging / alluvial** | Scooping minerals from river or lake beds |

### Minerals as a source of income

They earn **foreign exchange** when exported · provide **employment** · give government
**taxes and royalties** · provide **raw materials** for industries such as cement and steel ·
lead to the growth of **towns and roads** where mines are opened.

### Problems of the mining industry

Shortage of **capital** and modern machinery · **low level of technology** and few skilled
people · minerals in **remote areas** with poor roads · **accidents** and mine collapses ·
**pollution** and land degradation · minerals in **small scattered deposits** · unstable
**world prices** · **smuggling** and illegal mining · displacement of people.

**Solutions:** attract investment, train people in mining skills, improve roads to mining
areas, enforce safety and environmental laws, add value by processing minerals at home,
and fight smuggling.

## 4. WATER AND DRAINAGE

### Definition

**Drainage:** The way water is held and carried in an area by lakes, rivers, swamps, seas and oceans.

### Lakes of East Africa

| Lake | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Lake Victoria** | The largest in Africa; shared by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania |
| **Lake Tanganyika** | The deepest in Africa; Tanzania, D.R. Congo, Burundi, Zambia |
| **Lake Albert, Edward, George, Kyoga, Wamala** | Uganda |
| **Lake Turkana, Naivasha, Nakuru, Baringo, Magadi** | Kenya |
| **Lake Natron, Eyasi, Manyara, Rukwa, Nyasa (Malawi)** | Tanzania |
| **Lake Kivu** | Rwanda and D.R. Congo |
| **Lake Rweru and Cohoha** | Burundi |

Lakes are of different kinds: **rift valley** lakes (Tanganyika, Albert, Turkana),
**depression** lakes (Victoria, Kyoga), **crater** lakes (Katwe, Nyamunuka) and
**man-made** lakes (behind dams).

### Rivers of East Africa

**River Nile** (from Lake Victoria at Jinja, flowing north) · **Kagera** (the longest feeder of Lake Victoria) · **Tana** and **Athi–Galana** (Kenya) · **Rufiji, Pangani, Ruvuma, Ruaha** (Tanzania) · **Katonga, Semliki, Aswa** (Uganda) · **Ruvubu and Ruzizi** (Burundi and Rwanda).

**Other water bodies:** the **Indian Ocean** on the eastern side, and swamps such as those
of Lake Kyoga, the Kilombero valley and Rwanda's Rugezi.

### Uses of water in East Africa

Drinking and domestic use · **irrigation** (Mubuku, Doho, Mwea in Kenya) · **hydro-electric
power** (Nalubaale, Kiira, Bujagali, Isimba, Karuma, Owen Falls; Kidatu in Tanzania) ·
**fishing** · **water transport** on lakes, rivers and the ocean · **tourism and sport** ·
industry · watering animals.

**Problems:** pollution from factories and towns · water hyacinth · silting · drying up of
lakes and wetlands · floods · water-borne diseases · water shortage in dry areas.

## 5. FISHING

### Definition

**Fishing:** The catching of fish and other water animals for food and for sale.

### Fishing grounds

Lake Victoria (the biggest) · Lakes Kyoga, Albert, Edward and George · Lake Tanganyika ·
Lake Turkana · the **Indian Ocean** coast · fish ponds on farms.

Common fish: **Nile perch (mputa), tilapia (ngege), silver fish (mukene), tuna, catfish, lungfish, sardines (dagaa)**.

### Fishing methods

| Traditional | Modern |
|---|---|
| Hooks and lines | Trawling with large nets pulled by boats |
| Baskets and traps | Purse seining |
| Spears and harpoons | Gill nets and long lines from motor boats |
| Weirs and fences across streams | Fish farming in ponds and cages |
| Poisoning and stunning (now illegal) | Use of echo sounders and refrigerated boats |

### Preservation of fish

| Traditional | Modern |
|---|---|
| Sun drying | Refrigeration and freezing |
| Smoking | Canning |
| Salting | Filleting and cold storage |
| Deep frying | Modern processing factories, as at Jinja and Kisumu |

### Importance of fishing to the people of East Africa

**Food** rich in protein · **employment** for fishermen, traders and factory workers ·
**income** for families · **foreign exchange** from exported Nile perch fillets · **raw
materials** for fish-processing and animal-feed industries · government **revenue** through
licences and taxes · growth of **landing sites and towns**.

### Problems of the fishing industry

- **Over-fishing** and use of **undersized nets**, which catch immature fish.
- Use of **poison and explosives**, which kill all life in the water.
- **Water hyacinth** blocking landing sites.
- **Water pollution** from factories, towns and farms.
- **Poor preservation and transport**, so fish goes bad.
- **Lack of capital** for modern boats and equipment.
- **Accidents and storms** on the lakes; many fishermen cannot swim.
- **Insecurity** and theft of nets and engines.
- **Diseases** such as bilharzia at landing sites.
- **Low prices** paid to the fishermen by middlemen.

### Solutions

Enforce laws on **net sizes and closed seasons** · ban poison and explosives · encourage
**fish farming** in ponds and cages · remove water hyacinth · build **cold rooms** and
improve roads to landing sites · give **loans and training** to fishermen · provide life
jackets and weather warnings · form **fishermen's cooperatives** so they get fair prices ·
patrol the lakes.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Copper is mined at **Kilembe** in Uganda; diamonds at **Mwadui** and gold at **Geita** in Tanzania; soda ash at **Lake Magadi** in Kenya.
- **Tanzanite** is found only in Tanzania.
- Cloves are the main cash crop of **Zanzibar and Pemba**; pyrethrum is grown in the **Kenya highlands**; sisal at **Tanga**.
- **Lake Victoria** is the largest lake in Africa; **Lake Tanganyika** is the deepest.
- The **River Nile** starts from Lake Victoria at Jinja and flows **north**.
- Main fish: **Nile perch, tilapia and mukene**.
- Traditional preservation: sun drying, smoking, salting. Modern: refrigeration and canning.
- The biggest problem of the fishing industry is **over-fishing and the use of wrong fishing gear**.

- Largest lake in East Africa — Lake Victoria.
- Longest river that feeds Lake Victoria — River Kagera.
- One modern method of fishing — trawling (or use of gill nets).
- One traditional way of preserving fish — smoking (or sun drying, salting).
