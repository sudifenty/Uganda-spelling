# PRIMARY 6 ENGLISH

# TOPIC 5: HOTELS

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Six Curriculum, Set One, English Topic 5, 35 periods
(`curriculum/P6-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, topic outline page 24 and pages 47–51).*

**What you already know:** You have seen hotels, restaurants and lodges in your town. In
P.6 you learn the **English used in a hotel** — the workers, the rooms, the meals, the
menu, and the polite structures used when asking for service.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- use hotel vocabulary correctly
- name the workers in a hotel and their duties
- read and write a **menu** and a hotel **advertisement**
- ask for service politely using *May I …?*
- use *may*, *no sooner … than*, adjectives and direct speech
- act out dialogues that take place in a hotel

---

## 1. HOTEL VOCABULARY

### The people

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **waiter / waitress** | The man / woman who serves food and drinks at table |
| **chef** | The person who cooks the food |
| **receptionist** | The person at the reception who receives guests and books rooms |
| **customer / guest** | The person who buys the service |
| **manager** | The person in charge of the hotel |
| **porter** | The person who carries guests' luggage |

### The places

**reception** · **restaurant** · **dining room** · **lounge** · **bar** · **pub** ·
**conference room** · **washrooms / restrooms** · **gym** · **sauna** · **swimming pool** ·
**balcony** · **counter** · **kitchen** · **bedroom with a shower**.

### The things and the service

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **menu** | The list of food and drinks a hotel offers, with prices |
| **order** | To ask for the food you want |
| **serve** | To bring the food to the customer |
| **bill** | The paper showing how much a customer must pay |
| **receipt** | The paper showing that payment has been made |
| **cutlery** | Knives, forks and spoons |
| **serviette / napkin** | The cloth or paper used to wipe the mouth and hands |
| **table mat** | The mat placed under a plate |
| **tooth pick** | The small stick used to remove food from between the teeth |
| **booking** | Arranging a room or table in advance |
| **check in / check out** | To arrive and register / to leave and pay |
| **air conditioner / fan** | Machines that cool a room |
| **table manners** | The correct way of behaving at table |

### The meals and the courses

| Meal | When it is taken |
|---|---|
| **Breakfast** | In the morning |
| **Lunch** | In the middle of the day |
| **Supper** | In the evening |
| **Dinner** | The main meal of the day, usually in the evening |

**The parts of a meal:** **soup** or **salad** first, then the **main course** with a
**sauce**, then **dessert** — **pudding**, **jelly** or **fruit**.

## 2. A MENU

A menu shows what is available and how much it costs, usually in groups.

| **KAMPALA VIEW HOTEL — LUNCH MENU** | **Price (UGX)** |
|---|---|
| **Soups** | |
| Vegetable soup | 4,000 |
| **Main dishes** | |
| Matooke with groundnut sauce | 10,000 |
| Rice with beef stew | 12,000 |
| Chicken with chips | 15,000 |
| **Desserts** | |
| Fresh fruit salad | 5,000 |
| Jelly | 4,000 |
| **Drinks** | |
| Soda | 2,500 |
| Bottled water | 1,500 |

**When you write a menu:** put the name of the hotel and the meal at the top · group the
items (soups, main dishes, desserts, drinks) · write the **price** against each item ·
spell the dishes correctly · keep it neat.

## 3. POLITE LANGUAGE IN A HOTEL

| Situation | What to say |
|---|---|
| Asking for the menu | *"**May I** have the menu, please?"* |
| Ordering | *"I would like rice and beef stew, please."* |
| Asking the price | *"How much is the chicken, please?"* |
| Calling the waiter | *"Excuse me, please."* |
| Asking for the bill | *"**May I** have the bill, please?"* |
| Thanking | *"Thank you very much."* |
| Booking a room | *"I would like to book a single room for two nights."* |

### Table manners

Wash your hands before eating · sit upright · put the **serviette** on your lap · use the
**cutlery** properly, starting from the outside · do not talk with your mouth full · chew
with your mouth closed · do not make noise with the cutlery · say *please* and *thank you* ·
ask to be excused before leaving the table.

---

## 4. LANGUAGE STRUCTURES

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **May I …?** | ***May I** have the menu, please?* |
| **… may …** | *Amuria Primary School **may** hold their end of year party in the hotel conference room.* |
| **… no sooner … than …** | ***No sooner had** the boss paid for his breakfast **than** he left the hotel.* |

**Note on *no sooner*:** when the sentence begins with **No sooner**, the verb comes
**before** the subject (*No sooner **had he** finished…*), and the second part uses
**than**, never "when".

## 5. GRAMMAR

### (a) Forming adjectives

| Noun / verb | Adjective |
|---|---|
| comfort | **comfortable** |
| enjoy | **enjoyable** |
| beauty | **beautiful** |
| care | **careful / careless** |
| attract | **attractive** |
| expense | **expensive** |
| taste | **tasty** |
| use | **useful / useless** |

*Example:* *The hotel has **comfortable** beds and an **attractive** garden, and the food is
**tasty** but rather **expensive**.*

### (b) Direct speech and its punctuation

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Put the exact words in **quotation marks** | *"May I have the menu, please?"* |
| Begin the spoken words with a **capital letter** | *The guest said, **"T**he food is delicious."* |
| Put a **comma** before the quotation when the saying part comes first | *He said, "Bring the bill."* |
| The **question mark or full stop goes inside** the quotation marks | *"Is the room ready?" he asked.* |
| Start a **new line** for each new speaker in a dialogue | |

**A short dialogue**

> **Waiter:** Good afternoon, madam. May I take your order?
> **Guest:** Yes, please. May I have the menu first?
> **Waiter:** Here it is, madam.
> **Guest:** I would like rice with beef stew and a bottle of water.
> **Waiter:** Very well, madam. Your order will be ready in ten minutes.

## 6. A HOTEL ADVERTISEMENT

An advertisement should have a **name and a catchy line**, the **services offered**, the
**price or special offer**, the **location**, and the **contact**.

> **RWENZORI GARDEN HOTEL**
> *Where every guest is family.*
> Clean self-contained rooms · free Wi-Fi · a swimming pool and a gym · a conference room
> for 200 people · tasty local and international dishes.
> **Rooms from 60,000/= a night. Book two nights and get breakfast free.**
> Opposite the main market, Fort Portal. Tel: 0700 000 000.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hotel | A place where travellers pay for meals and accommodation |
| Waiter / waitress | A person who serves food and drinks at table |
| Chef | The person who cooks the food |
| Receptionist | The person who receives guests and books rooms |
| Menu | The list of food and drinks with their prices |
| Bill | The paper showing how much a customer must pay |
| Receipt | The paper showing that payment has been made |
| Cutlery | Knives, forks and spoons |
| Booking | Arranging a room or table in advance |
| Table manners | The correct way of behaving at table |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Waiter** serves; **chef** cooks; **receptionist** books rooms and receives guests.
- A **menu** lists the food; a **bill** shows what you owe; a **receipt** proves you paid.
- **Check in** on arrival; **check out** when leaving.
- **May I …?** is the polite way to ask for anything.
- **No sooner … than …**, with the verb before the subject at the start of the sentence.
- Dessert comes **last**: pudding, jelly or fruit.
- Direct speech: quotation marks, capital letter, comma, and the full stop or question mark **inside**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- The person who cooks in a hotel → the **chef**.
- The list of food and prices → the **menu**.
- The paper showing what a customer must pay → the **bill**.
- Knives, forks and spoons → **cutlery**.
- Cloth used to wipe the mouth at table → a **serviette (napkin)**.
- *____ I have the menu, please?* → **May**
- *No sooner had he paid ____ he left.* → **than**
- Adjective from *comfort* → **comfortable**.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing *"No sooner had he paid **when** he left"* — it must be **than**.
- Confusing **bill** (what you owe) with **receipt** (proof of payment).
- Writing *"Can I have the menu"* in a formal test where **May I** is expected.
- Forgetting quotation marks and the comma in direct speech.
- Spelling: **restaurant, serviette, cutlery, receipt, conference**.

4. Name any six places found in a big hotel.
5. Name the four meals mentioned in the syllabus and say when each is taken.

7. Write a short lunch menu for a hotel, with at least five items and their prices.
8. Complete: *____ I have the bill, please?*
9. Complete: *No sooner had the guest finished eating ____ he asked for the bill.*
10. Complete: *The school ____ hold its party in the hotel conference room.*

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Any five: **waiter/waitress** — serves food and drinks at table; **chef** — cooks the food; **receptionist** — receives guests and books rooms; **manager** — is in charge of the hotel; **porter** — carries guests' luggage.
2. **Menu** — the list of food and drinks with prices; **bill** — the paper showing how much a customer must pay; **receipt** — the paper showing that payment has been made; **cutlery** — knives, forks and spoons; **serviette** — the cloth or paper used to wipe the mouth and hands.
3. **Checking in** is arriving at a hotel and registering for a room; **checking out** is leaving the hotel and paying the bill.
4. Any six of: reception, restaurant, dining room, lounge, bar, conference room, washrooms, gym, sauna, swimming pool, kitchen, balcony.
5. **Breakfast** — in the morning; **lunch** — in the middle of the day; **supper** — in the evening; **dinner** — the main meal, usually in the evening.
6. A **dessert** is the sweet dish taken at the end of a meal. Examples: fruit, jelly, pudding (also ice cream, cake).
7. *Learner's menu.* It should have the hotel name and meal at the top, at least five items grouped sensibly, and a price against each.
8. **May**
9. **than**
10. **may**
11. comfort → **comfortable**; enjoy → **enjoyable**; beauty → **beautiful**; expense → **expensive**; taste → **tasty**.
12. ***"May I have the menu, please?" said the guest.***
13. *Learner's dialogue.* It must name each speaker, start a new line for each, use quotation marks or the play format consistently, and use polite language such as *May I …, please*.
14. Any six of: wash hands before eating; sit upright; place the serviette on your lap; use the cutlery properly; do not talk with your mouth full; chew with your mouth closed; do not make noise with the cutlery; say please and thank you; ask to be excused before leaving the table.
15. *Learner's advertisement.* It should have the hotel name, a catchy line, the services, the price or an offer, the location and a contact.
