**What you already know:** You have seen that children grow and change. In P.6 you learn
about **growth and development, puberty and adolescence**, the **reproductive organs**,
how a baby is formed, **reproductive health** and **family planning**.

*This topic is about your body and your health. Learn it seriously and respectfully, and
ask your teacher, parent or a health worker when you do not understand something.*

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- describe growth and development in human beings
- explain puberty and adolescence and the changes that come with them
- name the primary and secondary sex characteristics

- explain fertilisation, conception and pregnancy
- explain reproductive health, problems in pregnancy and teenage pregnancy
- explain family planning, its importance and the myths about it

## 1. GROWTH AND DEVELOPMENT

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Growth** | An increase in size and weight of the body |
| **Development** | The changes that make the body able to do more, such as walking, talking and reproducing |

**Stages of human life:** infancy → childhood → **adolescence** → adulthood → old age.

---

## 2. PUBERTY AND ADOLESCENCE

### Definitions

**Puberty:** The stage at which the reproductive organs become mature and a young person becomes able to produce children. It usually begins at about **10–14 years in girls** and **12–16 years in boys**, though it differs from person to person.

**Adolescence:** The whole period of change between childhood and adulthood, in body, mind and feelings.

**Adolescent:** A young person who is going through adolescence.

### Physical changes at puberty

| In girls | In boys |
|---|---|
| Breasts develop | Voice deepens (breaks) |
| Hips widen | Shoulders and chest broaden |
| Menstruation (periods) begins | Wet dreams begin |
| Pubic and armpit hair grows | Pubic, armpit, chest and facial hair grows |
| Skin may become oily, with pimples | Skin may become oily, with pimples |
| Rapid growth in height | Rapid growth in height and muscles |
| Sweating increases | Sweating increases |

### Social and emotional changes

Wanting more **independence** · caring greatly what friends think (**peer pressure**) ·
quick changes of mood · shyness or awkwardness · becoming interested in the opposite sex ·
wanting privacy · sometimes arguing with parents · new interest in appearance and dress.

**Advice for this stage:** bathe daily and keep clean · girls should use clean sanitary
pads and change them regularly · eat well and rest · choose good friends · resist peer
pressure · **abstain from sex** — it is the only sure protection at this age · talk to a
parent, teacher, health worker or another trusted adult when worried · remember that
everybody develops at a different speed and there is nothing wrong with you.

## 3. PRIMARY AND SECONDARY SEX CHARACTERISTICS

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Features present at **birth** — the reproductive organs themselves | The penis and testes in a boy; the vagina, uterus and ovaries in a girl |
| **Secondary** | Features that appear at **puberty** | Breasts, wide hips and menstruation in girls; deep voice, broad chest, facial hair and wet dreams in boys; pubic and armpit hair in both |

## 4. REPRODUCTIVE ORGANS AND CELLS

### The male reproductive system

| Part | Work |
|---|---|
| **Testes** (in the scrotum) | Produce **sperm cells** and the male hormone |
| **Scrotum** | The bag holding the testes and keeping them slightly cooler than the body |
| **Sperm duct** | Carries sperms from the testes |
| **Urethra** | Passes urine and semen out of the body (at different times) |
| **Penis** | The organ through which semen and urine leave the body |

### The female reproductive system

| Part | Work |
|---|---|
| **Ovaries** (two) | Produce **egg cells (ova)** and the female hormones |
| **Fallopian tubes (oviducts)** | Carry the egg to the uterus; **fertilisation takes place here** |
| **Uterus (womb)** | Where the baby develops for nine months |
| **Cervix** | The neck of the uterus |
| **Vagina** | The passage leading from the uterus to outside the body |

### The two sex cells

| Cell | Made in | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Sperm cell** | The testes | Very small; can swim using a tail; made in millions |
| **Egg cell (ovum)** | The ovaries | Larger; usually **one is released each month** (ovulation) |

### The menstrual cycle

**Menstruation:** The monthly flow of blood and the lining of the uterus through the vagina, lasting about 3–7 days, when the egg has not been fertilised.

The cycle is about **28 days** on average, though it varies. **Ovulation** — the release of
the egg — happens about the middle of the cycle. A girl should keep a simple record of her
dates and always have clean sanitary materials ready.

## 5. FERTILISATION, CONCEPTION AND PREGNANCY

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fertilisation** | The joining of a **sperm cell and an egg cell**, which happens in the fallopian tube |
| **Zygote** | The single cell formed at fertilisation |
| **Conception** | The zygote attaching itself to the wall of the uterus and beginning to grow |
| **Embryo / foetus** | The developing baby in the uterus |
| **Pregnancy (gestation)** | The period the baby develops in the womb — about **nine months (40 weeks)** |
| **Placenta** | The organ through which the baby receives food and oxygen from the mother and passes out waste |
| **Umbilical cord** | The cord joining the baby to the placenta |
| **Amniotic fluid** | The liquid that surrounds and protects the baby |

**Signs of pregnancy:** the monthly periods stop · morning sickness · the breasts enlarge ·
the abdomen grows · tiredness and changes in appetite.

### Care of a pregnant mother (antenatal care)

Attend the **antenatal clinic** early and regularly · eat a **balanced diet** with plenty of
iron and vitamins · take the medicines and supplements given · sleep under a **treated
mosquito net** · rest enough and avoid heavy work · avoid **alcohol, smoking and self-medication** ·
keep clean · plan to deliver at a **health centre with a trained health worker**.

### Problems in pregnancy

Anaemia · malaria · high blood pressure · miscarriage · bleeding · swollen legs and hands ·
vomiting too much · obstructed labour · malnutrition · infections including HIV. Any of
these needs a **health worker at once**.

### Teenage pregnancy

**Teenage pregnancy** is pregnancy in a girl below 20 years, whose body is not yet ready.

**Effects:** danger to the health and life of the girl and the baby · obstructed labour and
fistula · low birth weight · dropping out of school · loss of future opportunities ·
poverty · rejection and stigma · unsafe abortion, which can kill.

**Prevention:** stay in school · **abstain from sex** · avoid bad company, alcohol and
drugs · avoid gifts and lifts from older people (sugar daddies and sugar mummies) · know
your rights and report abuse to a trusted adult · get correct information from parents,
teachers and health workers.

*Defilement — a sexual act with a person below 18 — is a serious crime in Uganda, and any
child who is abused should tell a trusted adult and report it immediately. It is never the
child's fault.*

## 6. CARE FOR THE REPRODUCTIVE ORGANS AND COMMON DISORDERS

### Care

Bathe daily and wash the private parts with clean water · wear **clean cotton underwear**
and change it daily · girls should wipe **from front to back** and use clean sanitary
materials, changing them regularly · boys should wash carefully and keep dry · never insert
anything into the private parts · avoid harmful traditional practices · treat any itching,
sore or discharge early · **abstain from sex** at your age · never allow anybody to touch
your private parts, and report it if they try.

### Common diseases and disorders

| Condition | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)** — syphilis, gonorrhoea, chlamydia | Spread mainly through sexual contact; cause discharge, sores and pain; can lead to infertility |
| **HIV/AIDS** | Spread through unprotected sex, infected blood, sharing sharp instruments, and mother to child |
| **Urinary tract infections** | Pain when passing urine; drink water and see a health worker |
| **Menstrual pain and irregular periods** | Common; rest, warmth and advice from a health worker |
| **Infertility** | Failure to produce children; may follow untreated infections |
| **Cancer of the cervix and of the prostate** | Screening and early treatment matter |

## 7. FAMILY PLANNING

### Definition

**Family planning:** Deciding **how many children to have and when to have them**, and using acceptable methods to achieve this.

**Child spacing** means leaving enough time — usually at least **two to three years** —
between one birth and the next.

### Importance of family planning

- Protects the **mother's health**, giving her body time to recover.
- Gives each child better **care, food, health and education**.
- Reduces **infant and maternal deaths**.
- Helps the family **plan its money** and avoid poverty.
- Reduces very rapid **population growth** and the pressure on land and services.
- Allows parents to work, study and rest.

### Methods

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Natural methods** | Abstinence · the calendar (rhythm) method · breastfeeding in the early months · withdrawal (unreliable) |
| **Artificial methods** | Condoms · pills · injections · implants · the loop (IUD) · sterilisation for adults who have finished having children |

*Couples choose a method with the advice of a health worker. Some methods also reduce the
risk of infection, but only **abstinence** is a complete protection for a young person of
your age.*

### Myths and misconceptions about family planning

| The myth | The truth |
|---|---|
| "Family planning makes a woman permanently barren." | Most methods are **reversible**; fertility returns when they are stopped. |
| "It is only for women." | It is the responsibility of **both** the man and the woman. |
| "It is against culture and religion." | Communities and faiths differ; **child spacing** itself is widely accepted. |
| "It encourages bad behaviour among young people." | The best protection for young people is **abstinence and good guidance**; family planning is for adults planning their families. |
| "It causes cancer and other diseases." | Methods are tested and given by trained health workers, who advise on any side effects. |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Puberty** = the body becomes able to reproduce; **adolescence** = the whole period of change.
- **Primary** characteristics are present at birth; **secondary** ones appear at puberty.
- Sperm cells are made in the **testes**; egg cells are made in the **ovaries**.
- **Fertilisation takes place in the fallopian tube**; the baby develops in the **uterus**.
- Pregnancy lasts about **nine months (40 weeks)**.
- The **placenta** supplies food and oxygen to the baby.
- The menstrual cycle is about **28 days**; ovulation is around the middle.
- **Teenage pregnancy** is dangerous for both the girl and the baby.
- **Abstinence** is the only complete protection at your age.
- Family planning protects the mother's health and gives each child better care.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Organ that produces sperm cells → the testes. Egg cells → the ovaries.
- Where fertilisation takes place → the fallopian tube (oviduct).
- Where the baby develops → the uterus (womb).
- The cell formed when a sperm joins an egg → a zygote.
- Organ that supplies food and oxygen to the unborn baby → the placenta.
- Monthly flow of blood in a girl → menstruation.
- One secondary sex characteristic in boys → a deep voice (or facial hair).
- Leaving enough time between births → child spacing.
- The safest choice for a P.6 learner → abstinence.
