## 1. WHAT DIGESTION IS

**Digestion:** The breaking down of food into a form the body can absorb and use.

**Digestive system:** The system of organs that breaks down food, absorbs it and removes the waste.

It is made of the **alimentary canal** — one long tube from the mouth to the anus — and the
**associated organs** that help it.

---

## 2. THE ALIMENTARY CANAL AND WHAT EACH PART DOES

| Part | What happens there |
|---|---|
| **Mouth** | Teeth **chew** the food; the tongue mixes it; **saliva** moistens it and begins to break down starch |
| **Oesophagus (gullet)** | A tube that pushes the food down to the stomach by squeezing movements called **peristalsis** |
| **Stomach** | Food is churned and mixed with **gastric juice** and **hydrochloric acid**, which kill germs and start digesting protein |
| **Small intestine** | **Most digestion is completed** here and the digested food is **absorbed** into the blood through **villi** |
| **Large intestine** | Absorbs **water and mineral salts** from the undigested food |
| **Rectum** | Stores the waste (faeces) |
| **Anus** | The waste is passed out — this is **egestion** |

### Associated organs

| Organ | Work |
|---|---|
| **Salivary glands** | Produce **saliva** |
| **Liver** | Produces **bile**, which breaks fats into small drops; it also stores food and removes poisons |
| **Gall bladder** | **Stores bile** |
| **Pancreas** | Produces **pancreatic juice** which digests starch, protein and fat |

### The teeth

| Tooth | Number in an adult | Work |
|---|---|---|
| **Incisors** | 8 | Cutting and biting |
| **Canines** | 4 | Tearing |
| **Premolars** | 8 | Crushing and grinding |
| **Molars** | 12 | Chewing and grinding |

An adult has **32 teeth**; a child has **20 milk teeth**.

---

## 3. WHAT HAPPENS TO THE FOOD

| Food type | Broken down into |
|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates (starch)** | **Simple sugars (glucose)** |
| **Proteins** | **Amino acids** |
| **Fats and oils** | **Fatty acids and glycerol** |

**Absorption** takes place mainly in the **small intestine**, through tiny finger-like
structures called **villi**, and the food is carried by the blood to all parts of the body.

---

## 4. DISEASES AND DISORDERS OF THE DIGESTIVE SYSTEM

| Condition | Cause | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| **Diarrhoea** | Germs in dirty food and water | Frequent watery stool, dehydration |
| **Dysentery** | Bacteria or amoeba from dirty food and water | Stool with **blood and mucus**, stomach pain |
| **Cholera** | Bacteria in contaminated water | Very watery stool, vomiting, rapid dehydration — an emergency |
| **Typhoid** | Bacteria in contaminated food and water | High fever, headache, stomach pain |
| **Intestinal worms** | Worm eggs from dirty hands, food or bare feet | Stomach pain, pot belly, loss of weight, anaemia |
| **Constipation** | Little water and roughage in the diet | Hard stool, difficulty passing it |
| **Ulcers** | Too much acid, irritation, stress, skipping meals | Burning pain in the upper stomach |
| **Tooth decay** | Sugary foods and poor brushing | Holes in the teeth, toothache |
| **Vomiting and indigestion** | Bad food, eating too fast, overeating | Discomfort, nausea |
| **Appendicitis** | Infection of the appendix | Sharp pain on the lower right side — needs surgery |

**First aid for diarrhoea:** give plenty of fluids and **ORS (oral rehydration salts)** —
or a home mixture of **one litre of boiled cooled water, eight level teaspoons of sugar and
half a teaspoon of salt** — and take the person to a health worker.

---

## 5. KEEPING THE DIGESTIVE SYSTEM HEALTHY

- Eat a **balanced diet** with plenty of **roughage** (fruits and vegetables).
- Drink **plenty of clean, boiled or treated water**.
- **Wash hands with soap** before eating and after using the latrine.
- Wash fruits and vegetables; cover food against flies.
- **Chew food well** and eat at regular times; do not overeat or rush.
- **Deworm** every six months.
- **Brush teeth** twice a day and avoid too many sweets.
- Use a **latrine** and keep it clean.
- Avoid alcohol, smoking and self-medication.
- Go for **treatment early** when sick.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Digestion | The breaking down of food into a form the body can absorb |
| Alimentary canal | The tube from the mouth to the anus |
| Peristalsis | The squeezing movement that pushes food along the canal |
| Saliva | The juice made in the mouth that moistens food and starts digesting starch |
| Bile | The juice made by the liver that breaks fats into small drops |
| Villi | Tiny finger-like structures in the small intestine that absorb food |
| Absorption | The passing of digested food into the blood |
| Egestion | The removal of undigested food from the body |
| ORS | Oral rehydration salts, given for diarrhoea |
| Roughage | Fibre in food that helps digestion and prevents constipation |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The alimentary canal: **mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum → anus**.
- **Saliva** starts digesting **starch**; the **stomach** starts digesting **protein**.
- **Bile** is made by the **liver** and stored in the **gall bladder**; it works on **fats**.
- **Most digestion and absorption happen in the small intestine**, through the **villi**.
- The **large intestine absorbs water**.
- An adult has **32 teeth**; a child has **20**.
- **Peristalsis** pushes food along the canal.
- **ORS** is the first treatment for diarrhoea.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Digestion** is the breaking down of food into a form the body can absorb and use. The **digestive system** is the system of organs that breaks down food, absorbs it and removes the waste.
2. **Mouth → oesophagus (gullet) → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum → anus.**
3. **Mouth** — chews the food and mixes it with saliva, which starts digesting starch; **stomach** — churns food with gastric juice and acid, which kill germs and start digesting protein; **small intestine** — completes digestion and absorbs the food into the blood; **large intestine** — absorbs water and mineral salts.
4. Peristalsis is the **squeezing, wave-like movement** of the walls of the alimentary canal that pushes food along.
5. **Salivary glands** — produce saliva; **liver** — produces bile, stores food and removes poisons; **gall bladder** — stores bile; **pancreas** — produces pancreatic juice.
6. The **liver** makes bile; it is stored in the **gall bladder**; bile **breaks fats into small drops** so they can be digested easily.
7. **Incisors** — cutting and biting; **canines** — tearing; **premolars** — crushing and grinding; **molars** — chewing and grinding.
8. An adult has **32**; a child has **20** milk teeth.
9. Starch → **simple sugars (glucose)**; protein → **amino acids**; fats → **fatty acids and glycerol**.
10. In the **small intestine**, through tiny finger-like structures called **villi**.
11. Any six, for example: **diarrhoea** — germs in dirty food and water; **dysentery** — bacteria or amoeba; **cholera** — bacteria in contaminated water; **typhoid** — bacteria in food and water; **worms** — worm eggs from dirt; **constipation** — little water and roughage; **ulcers** — too much acid and skipping meals; **tooth decay** — sugary foods and poor brushing.
12. **Dysentery:** stool with **blood and mucus**, stomach pain and fever. **Worms:** stomach pain, **pot belly**, loss of weight, tiredness and anaemia.
13. **ORS** is oral rehydration salts, given to replace water and salts lost in diarrhoea. A home mixture: **one litre of boiled, cooled water + eight level teaspoons of sugar + half a teaspoon of salt**, stirred until dissolved.
14. Any eight of: eat a balanced diet with roughage; drink clean boiled water; wash hands with soap; wash fruits and cover food; chew well and eat at regular times; deworm every six months; brush teeth twice a day; use a clean latrine; avoid alcohol and self-medication; seek treatment early.
15. Because germs and worm eggs from dirt, faeces and animals stay on the fingers. When we eat with dirty hands they enter the mouth and cause **diarrhoea, dysentery, typhoid, cholera and worms**. Soap removes them.
