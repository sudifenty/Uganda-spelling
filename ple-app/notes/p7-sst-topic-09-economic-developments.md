## 1. MAJOR ECONOMIC RESOURCES OF AFRICA

### Definitions

**Resource:** Anything that people can use to satisfy their needs.

**Natural resource:** A resource provided by nature, such as land, water, minerals, vegetation, animals and climate.

**Human resource:** The people of a country, together with their skills and labour.

### The seven resources named in the syllabus

| Resource | How it contributes to development |
|---|---|
| **Land and soils** | Used for farming, settlement, industries and roads; fertile soils grow food and cash crops for sale and export |
| **Minerals** | Mined and sold for foreign exchange; used in industries; create jobs and towns |
| **Animals** (wild and domestic) | Wild animals attract tourists; livestock give meat, milk, hides and manure |
| **People** | Provide labour and skills, pay taxes, and form the market that buys goods |
| **Vegetation** | Gives timber, firewood, medicine and raw materials; protects soil and attracts rain |
| **Climate** | Good climate supports farming and tourism; determines the crops that can be grown |
| **Water** | Used for drinking, irrigation, fishing, transport, industry and hydro-electric power |

### Problems faced in the utilisation of resources

- **Low level of technology** and few skilled people.
- **Shortage of capital (money)** to develop mines, dams and factories.
- **Poor transport and communication** to reach the resources.
- Some resources are in **remote or difficult places** — deserts, thick forests, deep underground.
- **Political instability and wars** in resource-rich areas.
- **Over-exploitation** — over-fishing, over-mining, over-cutting of forests.
- **Corruption and mismanagement**.
- **Pollution and environmental damage** caused by mining and industry.
- **Unreliable world market prices** for raw materials.
- **Land conflicts** and unclear land ownership.

### Caring for our resources

- **Afforestation and reafforestation**; agroforestry.
- **Crop rotation, mulching, terracing and contour ploughing** to save the soil.
- **Controlled grazing** to stop overgrazing.
- Protecting **wetlands, lakes and rivers** from pollution and encroachment.
- **Gazetting** national parks and forest reserves and enforcing laws.
- **Recycling and proper waste disposal**.
- **Educating the public** about conservation.
- **Adding value** to raw materials at home instead of exporting them raw.

## 2. CASE STUDY: NIGERIA (West Africa)

- **Climate:** Equatorial in the south (hot and wet), tropical savannah in the middle belt, semi-desert in the far north.
- **Oil drilling:** Nigeria is Africa's biggest oil producer. Oil is drilled in the **Niger Delta** around Port Harcourt and Warri. Oil earns most of Nigeria's foreign exchange.
- **Farming:** cocoa, oil palm, groundnuts, cotton, rubber, yams, cassava, millet and sorghum.
- **Nomadism:** the **Fulani** herders of the dry north move with their cattle looking for pasture and water.
- **Hydro-electric power:** the **Kainji Dam** on the River Niger.
- **Trade and transport:** the port of Lagos, roads, railways and airports; Nigeria is the largest market in Africa by population.
- **Problems:** oil pollution in the Delta, over-dependence on oil, insecurity, corruption.

---

## 3. CASE STUDY: LIBYA (North Africa)

- **Climate:** Mediterranean along the coast (warm wet winters); hot desert climate inland — most of Libya is in the Sahara.
- **Oil mining:** Libya's economy depends almost entirely on **oil and natural gas**, exported through coastal ports.
- **Irrigation farming:** water is pumped from underground in the desert; the **Great Man-Made River** project carries water in huge pipes from desert aquifers to the coastal farms and cities. Crops: dates, olives, citrus, wheat and barley.
- **Industrialisation:** oil refining, petrochemicals, cement and food processing.
- **Tourism:** ancient Roman ruins such as Leptis Magna, desert scenery and Mediterranean beaches.
- **Problems:** shortage of water, very little farmland, over-dependence on oil, political instability.

**Compare with Nigeria:** both depend on oil, but Nigeria has a large population and rich farmland, while Libya has a small population and must irrigate the desert to farm.

## 4. CASE STUDY: SUDAN (North-East Africa)

- **Climate:** desert in the north, semi-desert and savannah in the centre and south.
- **The Gezira Irrigation Scheme:** lies in the triangle between the **Blue Nile and the White Nile** south of Khartoum. Water from the **Sennar Dam** on the Blue Nile irrigates the land. It is one of the largest irrigation schemes in the world and mainly grows **cotton**, plus wheat, groundnuts and sorghum.
- **Hydro-electric power:** the Sennar, Roseires and Merowe dams on the Nile.
- **The Nile Valley:** the river is the lifeline of Sudan — settlement, farming, fishing and transport are concentrated along it.
- **Other activities:** livestock keeping, gum arabic production, oil, and trade through Port Sudan.
- **Problems:** long civil conflicts, drought and desertification, shortage of rainfall away from the Nile.

## 5. CASE STUDY: SOUTH AFRICA (Southern Africa)

- **Mining:** the richest mining country in Africa — **gold** at Witwatersrand near Johannesburg, **diamonds** at Kimberley, plus coal, platinum, chrome, iron ore and manganese.
- **Farming:** maize on the high veld, wheat, sugar cane in Natal, citrus, and **vines for wine** in the Mediterranean climate of the Cape.
- **Trade:** the busiest ports in Africa — Durban, Cape Town, Port Elizabeth and Richards Bay.
- **Tourism:** Kruger National Park, Table Mountain, Cape Town, the Drakensberg and historical sites.
- **Industrialisation:** the most industrialised country in Africa — iron and steel, motor vehicle assembly, chemicals, textiles and food processing.
- **Problems:** unemployment, inequality left by apartheid, crime, power shortages.

---

## 6. CASE STUDY: DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC OF CONGO (Central Africa)

- **Climate:** equatorial — hot and wet throughout the year in the Congo Basin.
- **Mining:** very rich in minerals — **copper and cobalt** in Katanga (Shaba), diamonds at Mbuji-Mayi, gold, tin and coltan.
- **Lumbering:** hardwood timber such as mahogany and ebony from the equatorial forest.
- **Farming:** coffee, palm oil, rubber, cocoa, cassava, maize and bananas.
- **Water:** the River Congo is used for transport and for the **Inga hydro-electric dams**, which have huge potential.
- **Problems:** long civil wars and insecurity in the east, poor transport in the thick forest, corruption and exploitation of minerals by armed groups.

## 7. CASE STUDY: UGANDA (East Africa)

- **Climate:** mainly tropical (savannah) with equatorial areas near Lake Victoria, and a semi-arid area in Karamoja; moderated by altitude and by the lakes.
- **Farming:** the backbone of the economy — **coffee** (the leading export crop), tea, cotton, sugar cane, tobacco, vanilla, cocoa; food crops: matooke, maize, beans, cassava, millet, sweet potatoes; fishing on Lakes Victoria, Kyoga and Albert.
- **Mining:** copper at **Kilembe**, cobalt, limestone for cement at Hima and Tororo, gold, salt at Katwe, and oil discovered in the **Albertine Graben**.
- **Tourism:** mountain gorillas in Bwindi, Murchison Falls, Queen Elizabeth and Kidepo national parks, the source of the Nile at Jinja, the Rwenzori Mountains.
- **Irrigation schemes:** Mubuku (Kasese), Doho (Butaleja — rice), Kibimba/Tilda (Bugiri — rice), Olweny (Lira), Agoro (Lamwo).
- **Pastoralism:** cattle keeping in Karamoja, Ankole and the cattle corridor.
- **Hydro-electric power:** Nalubaale and Kiira (Jinja), Bujagali, Isimba and Karuma on the River Nile.
- **Problems:** dependence on rain-fed agriculture, low value addition, poor rural roads, being landlocked, and a fast-growing population.

### Comparing Uganda with the other case-study countries

| Point of comparison | Uganda | Others |
|---|---|---|
| Main earner | Agriculture (coffee) | Oil in Nigeria and Libya; minerals in South Africa and D.R. Congo; cotton in Sudan |
| Access to the sea | **Landlocked** — uses Mombasa and Dar es Salaam | Nigeria, Libya, Sudan, South Africa and D.R. Congo all have ports |
| Rainfall | Reliable rainfall, so mostly rain-fed farming | Libya and Sudan must irrigate because they are dry |
| Industry | Small but growing | South Africa is the most industrialised in Africa |
| Tourism | Wildlife, gorillas, the Nile | Libya — Roman ruins; South Africa — Kruger and the Cape |

## 8. CHALLENGES AFFECTING ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT IN AFRICA

### Social challenges

Illiteracy · poverty · ignorance · diseases such as malaria and HIV/AIDS · high infant and maternal mortality · high fertility rate · teenage pregnancies and early marriages · low use of contraceptives · famine · rapid population growth.

### Economic challenges

Poor transport and communication · shortage of money and capital · low level of technology and skills · high dependency ratio · unemployment · corruption · collapse of some economies · **brain drain** (skilled people leaving for other countries) · dependence on the export of raw materials · unfair world market prices · heavy foreign debts.

### Political challenges

Wars, coups, conflicts and violation of human rights · political instability · large numbers of refugees · poor governance · foreign domination · riots and demonstrations · tribalism and nepotism.

### Possible solutions

- Provide **education for all**, including adult literacy and skills training.
- Improve **health services**, immunisation and family planning education.
- Improve **roads, railways, electricity and internet**.
- **Add value** to raw materials by processing them before export.
- Fight **corruption** and strengthen accountability.
- Encourage **investment**, small businesses and cooperatives.
- Promote **peace, democracy, free elections and good governance**.
- Encourage **trade within Africa** through the AU and the economic groupings.
- **Diversify** the economy so that it does not depend on one crop or mineral.
- Use **appropriate technology** and train more scientists and technicians.
- Encourage **saving and investment** and reduce dependence on foreign aid.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Africa's main economic resources: **land and soils, minerals, animals, people, vegetation, climate, water**.
- **Nigeria** — oil in the Niger Delta; Kainji Dam; Fulani nomads.
- **Libya** — oil and the **Great Man-Made River** irrigation project.
- **Sudan** — the **Gezira Scheme** between the Blue and White Nile, mainly cotton.
- **South Africa** — gold at Witwatersrand, diamonds at Kimberley; the most industrialised country in Africa.
- **D.R. Congo** — copper and cobalt in Katanga; equatorial forest lumbering; Inga dams.
- **Uganda** — coffee is the leading export crop; copper at Kilembe; HEP at Nalubaale, Kiira, Bujagali, Isimba and Karuma.

- **Brain drain** means skilled people leaving the country to work abroad.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

15. Because it has plenty of minerals such as gold, coal, iron ore and platinum to feed its factories; it has capital and skilled labour; it has good transport and busy ports; it has plenty of electricity and a large market; and industries were developed there over a long period.

17. Processing (roasting, grinding and packing) **adds value**, so the coffee sells at a much higher price than raw beans; it also creates jobs, saves the country from unstable raw-material prices and keeps the profit at home. To make this possible Uganda needs **processing factories and reliable electricity**, and **skilled workers, capital and markets** for the finished product.

19. **Reasons:** poor farming methods and low technology; lack of capital and credit; poor roads and no access to markets; small fragmented land holdings; crops sold raw and cheaply; lack of skills and education. **Solutions:** train farmers in modern methods; form cooperatives and provide credit; improve feeder roads and market information; add value by processing produce locally.
