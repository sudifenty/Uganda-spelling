# PRIMARY 4 SOCIAL STUDIES

**Theme: Living Together in Our District**

# TOPIC 4: PEOPLE IN OUR DISTRICT

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Four Social Studies Syllabus, Term II, Topic 4 (15 periods, pages 26–31).*

**How to use this topic.** The syllabus asks about the people of **your own district** —
their origin, why they settled there and the work they do. You fill in the tribes, clans and
jobs of your own district.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- explain the origin of the people in your district
- name the tribes and clans found in your district
- tell a legend of one of those people
- give the causes of immigration
- give the factors that make people settle in a place
- name the types of work people do and the problems they meet

---

## 1. What is the social environment?

The environment has two parts:

| Physical environment | Social environment |
|---|---|
| The natural things — land, water, air, plants, animals | **People and the things people make** — homes, schools, roads, markets |

This topic is about the **social environment**: the people.

---

## 2. What is a tribe? What is a clan? What is a totem?

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tribe** | A large group of people who share one language, one culture and one origin |
| **Clan** | A smaller group inside a tribe, whose members believe they came from one ancestor |
| **Ancestor** | A person from whom a family or clan came long ago |
| **Totem** | An animal or plant that a clan respects and does not eat |
| **Culture** | The whole way of life of a group of people |

A **tribe is made up of many clans**. Each clan usually has its own **totem**.

## 3. What is a legend? Name the three legends in the syllabus.

A **legend** is an **old story handed down by word of mouth**, telling how a group of people
began. Legends are told by elders. They are not history that can be proved, but they teach a
people about themselves.

| Legend | People it belongs to |
|---|---|
| The story of **Mundu and Sera** | The Bagisu, in Bugisu |
| The story of **Labong and Gipir** | The Luo-speaking people |
| The story of **Kintu and Nambi** | The Baganda |

> *The syllabus names these three legends but does not print the stories. Ask an elder, your
> teacher or the school library to tell you the one that belongs to your own people, and
> write it in your own words.*

## 4. What is migration? What do immigration and emigration mean?

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Migration** | The movement of people from one place to another to settle |
| **Immigration** | Moving **into** a place |
| **Emigration** | Moving **out of** a place |
| **Immigrant** | A person who moves into a place |
| **Emigrant** | A person who moves out of a place |

> **A way to remember: I**mmigration = coming **I**n.

---

## 5. What causes migration?

| Cause | Why people move |
|---|---|
| **Search for water, food and pasture** | To find enough water and grass for animals and food for the family |
| **Looking for jobs** | To find work and earn money |
| **Epidemics** | To escape a disease that is killing many people |
| **Droughts** | To escape a long time without rain |
| **Running away from conflicts** | To escape war and fighting |
| **Search for fertile soils** | To find good soil for growing crops |

---

## 6. What is a drought? What is an epidemic?

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Drought** | A long time without rain |
| **Epidemic** | A disease that attacks very many people at once |

---

## 7. What is a settlement pattern? What makes people settle in a place?

A **settlement pattern** is **the way people's homes are arranged in a place** — whether they
are close together or far apart.

| Factor | Why it attracts people |
|---|---|
| **Fertile soil** | Crops grow well, so there is food |
| **Enough and reliable rainfall** | Crops and animals do well |
| **Trade** | People can buy and sell and earn money |
| **Jobs** | There is work to do |
| **Enough food** | Nobody goes hungry |
| **Good security and peace** | People feel safe |
| **Social services** | Schools, water, good roads and health care are near |

**Why some places have few people**

Poor or rocky soil · too little rain · steep slopes · thick forests · big swamps ·
insecurity · pests and diseases · no roads, schools or water.

---

## 8. What types of work do people do? Why do people work?

| Work | What the person does |
|---|---|
| **Fishing** | Catches fish from lakes, rivers or ponds |
| **Farming** | Grows crops and keeps animals |
| **Pottery** | Makes pots and jars from clay |
| **Brick laying** | Makes bricks and builds with them |
| **Craft working** | Makes mats, baskets, carpets and carvings |
| **Trading** | Buys and sells goods |
| **Teaching** | Teaches learners in a school |
| **Office work** | Works in an office, writing and keeping records |

An **occupation** is the work a person does to earn a living.

**Why people work:** to get food, to get money, to pay school fees, to buy clothes and
medicine, to build a home, and to serve others.

---

## 9. What problems do people face in their work?

| Problem | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Insecurity and wars** | People cannot work in safety and property is destroyed |
| **Diseases of animals and birds** | Animals die, so the farmer loses money |
| **Poor people's health** | A sick person cannot work |
| **Poor transport** | Goods cannot reach the market, and they go bad |

**Other problems you should also know:** bad weather · poor prices · pests and crop diseases
· lack of money to start · lack of skills · too few tools · theft.

**Suggested solutions**

- Keep **peace and security** and report wrong acts
- **Vaccinate** and treat animals; spray against pests
- Go for treatment early and keep the home clean
- Repair the **roads** and use better transport
- **Save** money and join a savings group
- Learn **skills** at a technical school
- Store crops well and sell when the price is fair

---

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Mixing up **immigration** (in) with **emigration** (out).
- Saying a clan is bigger than a tribe — it is **smaller**.
- Treating a **legend** as proved history.
- Giving a **problem** when the question asks for a **solution**.
- Naming a tribe that is not found in your own district.
- Writing "people" as an answer for the type of work — name the actual work.
