## 1. PIE CHARTS

### Definition

**Pie chart:** A circle divided into sectors, where each sector shows a part of the whole. The whole circle is **360°**.

### The two key formulas

- **Angle of a sector = (part ÷ total) × 360°**
- **Part = (angle ÷ 360°) × total**

### Worked example 1

*A school of 720 pupils has: P.5 = 240, P.6 = 180, P.7 = 300. Find the angle for each class.*

| Class | Pupils | Working | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| P.5 | 240 | 240/720 × 360 | **120°** |
| P.6 | 180 | 180/720 × 360 | **90°** |
| P.7 | 300 | 300/720 × 360 | **150°** |
| Total | 720 | | **360°** ✓ |

### Worked example 2

*On a pie chart showing how a farmer uses 90 hectares, maize takes 80°. How many hectares are under maize?*

80/360 × 90 = **20 hectares**

### Worked example 3

*A pie chart shows a family budget. Food takes 150°, rent 90°, school fees 80° and savings the rest. If the income is 720,000 shillings, how much is saved?*

Savings angle = 360 − (150 + 90 + 80) = **40°**
Savings = 40/360 × 720,000 = **80,000 shillings**

## 2. TRAVEL GRAPHS

### Definition

**Travel graph (distance–time graph):** A graph showing how far someone has travelled as time goes on. Time is on the horizontal axis and distance on the vertical axis.

### How to read one

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| A sloping line going up | Moving away from the starting point |
| A steeper line | Moving **faster** |
| A flat (horizontal) line | **Resting** — the distance is not changing |
| A line coming down | Returning towards the starting point |

### The formulas

**Speed = distance ÷ time · Distance = speed × time · Time = distance ÷ speed**

### Worked example

*A motorist left Kampala at 8:00 a.m. and reached a town 180 km away at 11:00 a.m., resting for 30 minutes on the way.*

- Total time taken = 3 hours; time actually moving = 3 − ½ = 2½ hours
- Average speed for the whole journey = 180 ÷ 3 = **60 km/h**
- Speed while moving = 180 ÷ 2.5 = **72 km/h**

## 3. GRAPHS OF ORDERED PAIRS (COORDINATES)

### Definitions

**Coordinates:** A pair of numbers (x, y) giving the position of a point on a grid.

**x-axis:** The horizontal line. **y-axis:** The vertical line. **Origin:** The point (0, 0) where the axes cross.

### The rule

Always move **along (x) first, then up or down (y)** — "along the corridor, then up the stairs".

| Point | Meaning |
|---|---|
| (3, 2) | 3 right, 2 up |
| (−4, 1) | 4 left, 1 up |
| (2, −5) | 2 right, 5 down |
| (−3, −2) | 3 left, 2 down |

### Worked example

*Plot A(1, 1), B(5, 1), C(5, 4) and D(1, 4). What shape is formed, and what is its area?*

The shape is a **rectangle**. Length = 5 − 1 = 4 units, width = 4 − 1 = 3 units.
Area = 4 × 3 = **12 square units**.

## 4. MEAN, MEDIAN, MODE AND RANGE

### Definitions

**Mean (average):** The total of the values divided by how many there are.

**Median:** The middle value when the values are arranged in order.

**Mode:** The value that appears most often.

**Range:** The biggest value minus the smallest value.

### Worked example

*Find the mean, median, mode and range of: 4, 7, 10, 9, 10.*

- Arrange them: 4, 7, 9, 10, 10
- **Mean** = (4 + 7 + 9 + 10 + 10) ÷ 5 = 40 ÷ 5 = **8**
- **Median** = the middle value = **9**
- **Mode** = the value appearing most = **10**
- **Range** = 10 − 4 = **6**

### When there is an even number of values

*Find the median of 3, 5, 8, 12.*
The two middle values are 5 and 8. Median = (5 + 8) ÷ 2 = **6.5**

### Working backwards from the mean

*The mean age of 5 pupils is 12 years. Four of them are 10, 11, 13 and 14. Find the age of the fifth pupil.*

Total age = 12 × 5 = 60. Sum of the four = 10 + 11 + 13 + 14 = 48.
Fifth pupil = 60 − 48 = **12 years**

## 5. PROBABILITY OF NUMBERS

**Probability = (number of favourable outcomes) ÷ (total number of outcomes)**

### Worked examples

1. *A number is picked at random from 1 to 10. Find the probability that it is a prime number.*
   Primes: 2, 3, 5, 7 → 4 out of 10. P = 4/10 = **2/5**

2. *From the numbers 1 to 20, find the probability of picking a multiple of 5.*
   Multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20 → 4 out of 20. P = **1/5**

3. *A die is rolled. Find the probability of getting a number less than 3.*
   Numbers 1 and 2 → 2 out of 6. P = **1/3**

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The angles of a pie chart always add up to **360°**.
- Sector angle = part/total × 360°.
- On a travel graph, a **flat line means resting**; a **steeper line means faster**.
- Coordinates: **x first, then y**.
- Always **arrange the data in order** before finding the median.
- Range is a single number — it is a **subtraction**, not two numbers.
- Speed = distance ÷ time.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- 240 out of 720 gives an angle of 120°.
- 80° out of 360° of 90 hectares = 20 hectares.
- Mean of 4, 7, 9, 10, 10 = 8; median = 9; mode = 10; range = 6.
- Median of an even set = average of the **two** middle values.
- Total = mean × number of items (used for "find the missing value" questions).
- P(prime from 1 to 10) = 2/5.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Finding the median without arranging the numbers first.
- Using 100 instead of 360 in pie-chart angles.
- Plotting (y, x) instead of (x, y).
- Saying the range is "4 to 10" — it is the single number **6**.
- Reading a resting line on a travel graph as movement.

# REVISION QUESTIONS

1. What is the total of all the angles in a pie chart?
2. A pie chart shows 480 pupils. If 160 are in P.6, find the angle of the P.6 sector.
3. On a pie chart of 540 books, the history sector is 60°. How many history books are there?
4. A family budget of 900,000 shillings shows food 140°, rent 100°, fees 70° and the rest savings. Find the amount saved.
5. Find the mean of 12, 15, 18, 21, 24.
6. Find the median of 7, 3, 9, 11, 5.
7. Find the mode of 4, 6, 4, 8, 9, 4, 6.
8. Find the range of 23, 9, 45, 17, 31.
9. Find the median of 6, 10, 14, 18.
10. The mean of four numbers is 25. Three of them are 20, 27 and 31. Find the fourth number.
11. A motorist covers 240 km in 4 hours. Find the average speed.
12. A cyclist rides at 15 km/h for 2½ hours. How far does he go?
13. Give the coordinates of a point 3 units to the left of the origin and 4 units up.
14. Plot P(2, 1), Q(6, 1) and R(6, 5). What kind of triangle is PQR?
15. A number is picked at random from 1 to 12. Find the probability that it is an even number.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **360°**
2. 160/480 × 360 = **120°**
3. 60/360 × 540 = **90 books**
4. Savings angle = 360 − (140 + 100 + 70) = 50°. Savings = 50/360 × 900,000 = **125,000 shillings**
5. (12 + 15 + 18 + 21 + 24) ÷ 5 = 90 ÷ 5 = **18**
6. In order: 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 → median = **7**
7. **4** (it appears three times)
8. 45 − 9 = **36**
9. The two middle values are 10 and 14 → (10 + 14) ÷ 2 = **12**
10. Total = 25 × 4 = 100. 100 − (20 + 27 + 31) = 100 − 78 = **22**
11. 240 ÷ 4 = **60 km/h**
12. 15 × 2.5 = **37.5 km**
13. **(−3, 4)**
14. It is a **right-angled triangle** (the angle at Q is 90°).
15. Even numbers from 1 to 12: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 → 6 out of 12. P = **1/2**

# P.7 QUICK REVISION

- Pie chart: angle = part/total × **360°**
- Travel graph: flat = **rest**, steeper = **faster**; speed = distance ÷ time
- Coordinates: **x then y** · origin = (0, 0)
- Mean = total ÷ number · Median = middle (in order) · Mode = most often · Range = biggest − smallest
- Total = mean × number of items
- Probability = wanted ÷ total
