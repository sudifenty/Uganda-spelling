## 1. LIGHT AS A FORM OF ENERGY

### Definitions

**Light:** A form of energy that enables us to see.

**Luminous body:** An object that produces its own light (the sun, a candle, a bulb).

**Non-luminous body:** An object that does not produce light but can be seen when light falls on it (the moon, a book, a wall).

### Sources of light

| Natural sources | Artificial sources |
|---|---|
| The sun, stars, lightning, fireflies, burning wood | Electric bulbs, candles, torches, lanterns, gas lamps |

### How light travels

Light travels in **straight lines** — this is called **rectilinear propagation** — and it
travels very fast (about 300,000 km per second). Light can travel through a vacuum, unlike
sound.

**Simple proof:** line up three cards with holes in the same position; you can see a candle
through them only when the holes are in a straight line.

### Beams of light

| Beam | Description |
|---|---|
| **Parallel** | Rays travel side by side, as in a torch beam |
| **Converging** | Rays come together at a point, as through a convex lens |
| **Diverging** | Rays spread out from a point, as from a bulb |

### Importance of light

Seeing · plants use it for **photosynthesis** · it gives warmth · it is used in
photography, mirrors, microscopes and telescopes · solar energy · it kills some germs and
dries clothes and food · it enables signalling and communication (optical fibres).

## 2. EFFECT OF DIFFERENT MATERIALS ON LIGHT

| Material | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Transparent** | Lets nearly all light pass through; you can see clearly through it | Clear glass, clean water, air |
| **Translucent** | Lets only some light through; you cannot see clearly | Frosted glass, oiled paper, thin cloth, muddy water |
| **Opaque** | Does not let light through at all | Wood, metal, stone, cardboard, the human body |

## 3. SHADOWS AND ECLIPSES

### Definition

**Shadow:** A dark area formed when an opaque object blocks light.

A shadow needs three things: a **source of light**, an **opaque object** and a **screen**.

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Umbra** | The dark, full shadow in the middle |
| **Penumbra** | The lighter shadow around the umbra |

**Note:** the nearer the object is to the light, the **bigger** the shadow.

### Eclipses

**Eclipse:** What happens when one heavenly body blocks light from reaching another.

| Eclipse | What happens | Order |
|---|---|---|
| **Solar eclipse** (eclipse of the sun) | The moon comes between the sun and the earth | Sun – **Moon** – Earth |
| **Lunar eclipse** (eclipse of the moon) | The earth comes between the sun and the moon | Sun – **Earth** – Moon |

**Warning:** never look directly at the sun during a solar eclipse; it can damage the eyes.

### The pin-hole camera

A closed box, blackened inside, with a tiny hole at the front and a screen of translucent
paper at the back. Light from the object travels in straight lines through the hole and
forms an image on the screen.

**The image is: real, inverted (upside down), laterally inverted and smaller** (it becomes
bigger if the camera is moved closer to the object or the box is made longer). Making the
hole bigger makes the image brighter but blurred.

## 4. REFLECTION AND MIRRORS

### Definitions

**Reflection:** The bouncing back of light when it hits a surface.

**Regular reflection:** Reflection from a smooth, shiny surface, giving a clear image.

**Diffuse (irregular) reflection:** Reflection from a rough surface, scattering the light.

### Laws of reflection

1. The **angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection**.
2. The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane.

### Images formed by a plane mirror

- **Virtual** (cannot be caught on a screen)
- **Upright** (the same way up)
- **The same size** as the object
- **Laterally inverted** (left appears as right — that is why words look reversed)
- As **far behind** the mirror as the object is in front

### The periscope

A long box with **two plane mirrors** fixed at **45°**, facing each other. Light from the
object hits the top mirror, is reflected down to the bottom mirror, and then into the eye.
It is used to see over walls and crowds, and in submarines.

---

## 5. REFRACTION

### Definition

**Refraction:** The bending of light as it passes from one transparent material into another of different density.

### Effects of refraction

- A stick in water looks **bent** at the surface.
- A swimming pool or river looks **shallower** than it really is.
- Coins in water appear **raised**.
- The sun is seen for a short time **before it rises and after it sets**.
- Stars appear to twinkle.
- **Dispersion** and the rainbow.

### Dispersion and the rainbow

**Dispersion:** The splitting of white light into its seven colours as it passes through a prism or raindrops.

The seven colours in order: **red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet** —
remembered as **ROY G BIV**.

A **rainbow** forms when sunlight is refracted, reflected and dispersed inside raindrops.
It is seen when the sun is behind you and rain is falling in front of you.

## 6. LENSES AND THE LENS CAMERA

### Definitions

**Lens:** A piece of transparent glass or plastic with curved surfaces that bends light.

| Lens | Shape | What it does | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Convex (converging)** | Thicker in the middle | Brings rays together | Magnifying glass, camera, spectacles for long sight, human eye |
| **Concave (diverging)** | Thinner in the middle | Spreads rays out | Spectacles for short sight, peep holes |

### The magnifying glass

A convex lens held close to an object makes the object appear **bigger, upright and
virtual**. It is used for reading small print and examining insects.

### The lens camera

| Part | Work |
|---|---|
| **Convex lens** | Focuses light onto the film or sensor |
| **Diaphragm and aperture** | Control how much light enters |
| **Shutter** | Controls how long light enters |
| **Film / sensor** | Records the image |
| **Light-proof box** | Keeps out unwanted light |

The image formed is **real, inverted and smaller** than the object.

## 7. THE HUMAN EYE

### Structure and functions

| Part | Work |
|---|---|
| **Cornea** | The clear front part; lets light in and begins to bend it |
| **Sclera** | The tough white outer coat that protects the eye |
| **Iris** | The coloured part; controls the size of the pupil |
| **Pupil** | The hole that lets light into the eye |
| **Lens** | Focuses light onto the retina |
| **Ciliary muscles** | Change the shape of the lens (accommodation) |
| **Retina** | The light-sensitive screen where the image forms |
| **Optic nerve** | Carries the message to the brain |
| **Aqueous humour** | Watery fluid in front of the lens |
| **Vitreous humour** | Jelly-like fluid behind the lens; keeps the eye's shape |
| **Choroid** | The dark layer that prevents reflection inside the eye |

The image formed on the retina is **real, inverted and smaller** — the **brain** turns it
the right way up.

### Comparing the eye with a lens camera

| Eye | Camera |
|---|---|
| Iris and pupil | Diaphragm and aperture |
| Lens (changes shape) | Lens (moves in and out) |
| Retina | Film or sensor |
| Eyelid | Shutter |
| Choroid | Black inside of the camera box |

### Eye defects and their correction

| Defect | Problem | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| **Short sight (myopia)** | Can see near objects but not distant ones | **Concave** lenses |
| **Long sight (hypermetropia)** | Can see distant objects but not near ones | **Convex** lenses |
| **Astigmatism** | Blurred vision from an uneven cornea | Cylindrical lenses |
| **Presbyopia (old sight)** | The lens hardens with age | Reading (convex) glasses |
| **Colour blindness** | Cannot tell some colours apart | No cure; the person is advised on suitable work |

### Diseases and disorders of the eye

| Disease | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Conjunctivitis (red eye)** | Infection; red, itching, watering eyes; very contagious |
| **Trachoma** | Bacterial infection spread by flies and dirt; can cause blindness |
| **Cataract** | The lens becomes cloudy; treated by surgery |
| **Night blindness** | Caused by **lack of vitamin A** |
| **Glaucoma** | Pressure builds up inside the eye and damages the optic nerve |
| **Squint** | The eyes do not look in the same direction |

### Care of the eye

Eat foods rich in **vitamin A** (carrots, pawpaw, mangoes, green vegetables, liver, eggs) ·
wash the face and eyes daily · never share towels or handkerchiefs · read in good light and
do not read in a moving vehicle · never look directly at the sun or a welding flame · avoid
rubbing the eyes with dirty hands · wear protective glasses when working with dust or
chemicals · treat eye infections early and go for eye check-ups.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Light travels in **straight lines** at about 300,000 km/s.
- **Solar eclipse:** sun – moon – earth. **Lunar eclipse:** sun – earth – moon.
- A pin-hole camera image is **real, inverted and smaller**.
- A plane mirror image is **virtual, upright, the same size and laterally inverted**.
- A periscope uses **two plane mirrors at 45°**.
- Seven colours: **ROY G BIV**.
- **Convex** lens converges light; **concave** lens diverges it.
- The retina is the screen of the eye; the **brain** turns the image the right way up.
- **Short sight is corrected with concave lenses; long sight with convex lenses.**
- **Night blindness** comes from lack of **vitamin A**.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Light is a form of energy that enables us to see. Natural: the sun, lightning (also stars, fireflies). Artificial: electric bulb, candle (also torch, lantern).
2. A luminous body produces its own light, for example the sun; a non-luminous body does not produce light but is seen when light falls on it, for example the moon.
3. Light travels in **straight lines**. Take three cards with holes punched at the same height, line them up with a candle behind them; the candle is seen only when the three holes are in a straight line. When one card is moved, the light is blocked.
4. Transparent lets nearly all light through (clear glass); translucent lets only some light through (frosted glass or oiled paper); opaque lets no light through (wood).
5. A source of light, an opaque object and a screen.
6. In a **solar eclipse** the moon comes between the sun and the earth. In a **lunar eclipse** the earth comes between the sun and the moon.
7. It is **real, inverted (upside down), laterally inverted and smaller** than the object.
8. (i) The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. (ii) The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane.
9. It is virtual, upright, the same size as the object, laterally inverted, and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front (any four).
10. It has two plane mirrors fixed at 45° facing each other. Light from the object strikes the top mirror and is reflected down to the lower mirror, which reflects it into the eye of the observer.
11. Refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one transparent material to another. Effects: a stick in water looks bent; a pool looks shallower than it is; a coin in water appears raised; dispersion and the rainbow (any three).
12. Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
13. **Convex (converging)** — used in a magnifying glass and cameras; **concave (diverging)** — used in spectacles for short sight.
14. Any five, for example: cornea — lets light in and starts bending it; iris — controls the size of the pupil; pupil — lets light into the eye; lens — focuses light on the retina; retina — where the image is formed; optic nerve — carries the message to the brain.
15. **Short sight** is corrected with **concave** lenses; **long sight** is corrected with **convex** lenses.
