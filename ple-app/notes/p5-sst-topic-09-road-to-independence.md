# TOPIC 9: THE ROAD TO INDEPENDENCE

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, Social Studies Topic 9
(`curriculum/P5-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, pages 214–217).*

**What you already know:** In Topic 8 you saw how Uganda was made one Protectorate under
British rule. In this topic you learn how Ugandans **struggled and won back their
independence on 9th October 1962**.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- give the reasons why Ugandans wanted independence
- describe the methods they used in the struggle
- name the political parties and their leaders
- name the leading nationalists and what each did
- describe the events that led to independence and the problems faced

---

## 1. WHY UGANDANS WANTED INDEPENDENCE

- To **rule themselves** and choose their own leaders.
- To stop **forced labour (kasanvu)** and **heavy taxes**.
- To end **racial discrimination** — Africans were paid less and given the poorest jobs, schools and hospitals.
- To **control their own resources**, land and cash crops instead of selling only to the colonial buyers.
- To get **better prices** for cotton and coffee, which Asians and Europeans controlled.
- To restore the **dignity and culture** of Africans.
- To get **better education, health and social services**.
- Because of the **example of other countries** — India (1947) and **Ghana (1957)**.
- Because **ex-soldiers** who fought in the Second World War came back with new ideas.
- Because of the growth of **education**, which produced leaders who could argue their case.

---

## 2. METHODS USED IN THE STRUGGLE

| Method | Example |
|---|---|
| **Forming political parties** | UNC, DP, UPC, KY |
| **Forming cooperative societies** | Farmers organised to sell their own cotton and coffee |
| **Trade unions and strikes** | Workers' strikes of **1945** and the riots of **1949** |
| **Boycotts** | The 1959–60 **boycott of Asian and European shops and buses** |
| **Newspapers and the press** | *Uganda Eyogera*, *Munno*, *Uganda Argus* letters and articles |
| **Petitions and delegations** | Sending representatives to the Governor and to London |
| **Demonstrations** | Peaceful marches and meetings |
| **Constitutional conferences** | The **Lancaster House conferences in London, 1961 and 1962** |
| **Elections** | The 1958, 1961 and 1962 elections |

---

## 3. THE POLITICAL PARTIES

| Party | Formed | Founder / leader | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Uganda National Congress (UNC)** | **1952** | **Ignatius Kangave Musaazi** | The **first political party** in Uganda |
| **Democratic Party (DP)** | **1954** | Matayo Mugwanya; later led by **Benedicto Kiwanuka** | Strong among Catholics |
| **Uganda People's Congress (UPC)** | **1960** | **Apollo Milton Obote** | Formed from the UNC and the UPU |
| **Kabaka Yekka (KY)** | **1961** | Supporters of the Kabaka | Meant "the Kabaka only"; allied with UPC in 1962 |
| **Uganda National Movement (UNM)** | 1959 | Augustine Kamya | Organised the trade boycott |

---

## 4. THE LEADING NATIONALISTS

| Person | What he did |
|---|---|
| **Ignatius K. Musaazi** | Founded the **first party (UNC)** in 1952 and organised farmers' cooperatives |
| **Benedicto Kiwanuka** | Leader of the **DP**; became the **first Chief Minister (1961) and first Prime Minister (March 1962)** |
| **Apollo Milton Obote** | Founded the **UPC**; became **Prime Minister at independence** in 1962 |
| **Sir Edward Muteesa II** | The **Kabaka of Buganda**, exiled in 1953–1955 for opposing British plans; became the **first President** in 1963 |
| **Augustine Kamya** | Led the **trade boycott** of 1959–60 |
| **Others in the LEGCO** | Kawalya Kaggwa, Petero Nyangabyaki and Yekonia Zirabamuzaale — among the **first Africans in LEGCO (1945)** |

**The 1953–55 Kabaka crisis:** Governor **Sir Andrew Cohen** deported **Kabaka Muteesa II**
to Britain after he demanded a timetable for independence and opposed an East African
federation. Ugandans protested strongly, and the Kabaka returned in **1955** a national
hero — which greatly strengthened the demand for independence.

---

## 5. THE STEPS TO INDEPENDENCE

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1921** | LEGCO formed — no Africans |
| **1945** | **First Africans join LEGCO** |
| **1952** | **UNC**, the first political party, is formed |
| **1953–55** | The **Kabaka is deported** and returns |
| **1958** | The first **direct elections** to LEGCO in some districts |
| **1961** | **March elections** — DP wins; **Benedicto Kiwanuka becomes Chief Minister**; **Lancaster House Conference (September)** |
| **1962** | **April elections** — the **UPC–KY alliance** wins; **Obote becomes Prime Minister** |
| **1 March 1962** | Uganda gains **internal self-government** |
| **9 October 1962** | **Uganda becomes independent**; the Union Jack is lowered at **Kololo** and the Uganda flag raised |
| **1963** | Uganda becomes a **republic** within the Commonwealth; **Sir Edward Muteesa II becomes the first President**, with Obote as Prime Minister |

---

## 6. PROBLEMS FACED IN THE STRUGGLE

- **Arrest, imprisonment and detention** of leaders.
- **Deportation**, as with the Kabaka in 1953.
- **Banning** of parties, meetings and newspapers.
- **Lack of funds** and of transport for campaigns.
- **Disunity** — parties were divided by **religion and tribe**.
- **Illiteracy**, so many people did not understand the message.
- **Colonial laws** that made meetings and strikes difficult.
- **Fear** among people who did not want to lose their jobs.
- The **Buganda question** — Buganda wanted a special position, which delayed agreement.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Independence | The freedom of a country to govern itself |
| Nationalist | A person who fights for the independence of his or her country |
| Political party | An organised group of people who want to form the government |
| Boycott | Refusing to buy from or deal with somebody as a protest |
| Cooperative society | A group of farmers who market their produce together |
| Petition | A written request to those in authority |
| Internal self-government | Governing one's own internal affairs before full independence |
| Republic | A country whose head of state is a president, not a king |
| Deportation | Sending a person out of the country by force |
| LEGCO | The Legislative Council of the Protectorate |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The **first political party** in Uganda was the **UNC (1952)**, founded by **Ignatius Musaazi**.
- **DP 1954** (Benedicto Kiwanuka) · **UPC 1960** (Milton Obote) · **KY 1961**.
- The **first Chief Minister and first Prime Minister** was **Benedicto Kiwanuka**.
- **Kabaka Muteesa II was deported in 1953** and returned in **1955**.
- Internal self-government came on **1 March 1962**.
- **Uganda became independent on 9 October 1962**, with **Obote as Prime Minister**.
- Uganda became a **republic in 1963**, with **Muteesa II as first President**.
- The independence ceremony was held at **Kololo**.
