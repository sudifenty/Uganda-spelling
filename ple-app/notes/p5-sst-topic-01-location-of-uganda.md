## 1. THE DISTRICTS THAT FORM UGANDA

Uganda is divided into **districts** for easier administration. Each district is divided
into counties, sub-counties, parishes and villages. Uganda has **over 140 districts**, and
the number **keeps changing** as new districts are created, so always use the number your
teacher gives for the current year. **Kampala** is the capital city and is administered by
the **Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA)**.

The country is often grouped into four regions: **Central, Eastern, Northern and Western**.

---

## 2. COMPASS DIRECTION

**Compass:** An instrument used to find direction.

| Cardinal points | Semi-cardinal points |
|---|---|
| North (N), East (E), South (S), West (W) | North-East (NE), South-East (SE), South-West (SW), North-West (NW) |

**A way to remember the cardinals clockwise:** **N**ever **E**at **S**limy **W**orms.

### Uganda's neighbours

| Neighbour | Direction from Uganda |
|---|---|
| **Kenya** | East |
| **South Sudan** | North |
| **Democratic Republic of Congo** | West |
| **Rwanda** | South-West |
| **Tanzania** | South |

Uganda is **landlocked** — it has no coastline of its own, so it uses the ports of
**Mombasa** (Kenya) and **Dar es Salaam** (Tanzania).

---

## 3. LINES OF LATITUDE AND LONGITUDE

### Definitions

**Lines of latitude:** Imaginary lines running from **west to east** on the globe, showing how far a place is north or south of the Equator.

**Lines of longitude:** Imaginary lines running from the **North Pole to the South Pole**, showing how far a place is east or west of the Prime Meridian.

### Uganda's position

- Uganda lies between about **4°N and 1°S** of the Equator.
- It lies between about **29°E and 35°E** of the Prime Meridian.
- **The Equator (0°) passes through Uganda**, near Kayabwe on the Kampala–Masaka road, so part of Uganda is in the Northern Hemisphere and part in the Southern Hemisphere.

**Important:** the **Equator** is the most important line of latitude, at **0°**; the
**Prime (Greenwich) Meridian** is the most important line of longitude, at **0°**.

---

## 4. THE ELEMENTS OF A MAP

**Map:** A drawing of the earth's surface, or part of it, as seen from above.

| Element | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Tells what the map shows |
| **Key (legend)** | Explains the symbols used |
| **Scale** | Shows the relationship between distance on the map and on the ground |
| **Compass (direction)** | Shows where north is |
| **Frame (margin)** | Encloses the map neatly |
| **Grid** | Lines that help locate places exactly |

**A way to remember:** **T K S C F** — Title, Key, Scale, Compass, Frame.

### Map symbols to know

Church ✝ · mosque ☾ · school · hospital · railway line · **permanent lake** (solid blue) ·
**seasonal lake** (broken blue) · swamp · quarry · dam · bridge · airport · contour ·
mountain peak · factory · port.

### Scale and grid reference

**Scale:** For example, *1 : 50,000* means 1 cm on the map is 50,000 cm (½ km) on the ground.
So 6 cm on such a map = 3 km on the ground.

**Grid reference:** The numbered lines on a map. Read the **eastings (across) first**, then
the **northings (up)** — "along the corridor, then up the stairs".

## 5. THE SIZE AND POSITION OF UGANDA

- Uganda covers about **241,000 square kilometres** (about 241,550 km² including its open
  water and swamps).
- About **one fifth** of the country is water and swamp — Lake Victoria alone is huge.
- Uganda lies in **East Africa**, on the plateau between the two arms of the **Great Rift
  Valley**.
- Its capital is **Kampala**; **Entebbe** has the international airport.
- The **highest point** is Margherita Peak on Mt Rwenzori (5,109 m); the lowest area is the
  Albert Nile region.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Uganda's neighbours: **Kenya (east), South Sudan (north), D.R. Congo (west), Rwanda (south-west), Tanzania (south)**.
- Uganda is **landlocked** and uses **Mombasa and Dar es Salaam**.
- The **Equator crosses Uganda**; Uganda lies between about **4°N and 1°S**.
- Uganda's area is about **241,000 km²**; the capital is **Kampala**.
- Elements of a map: **Title, Key, Scale, Compass, Frame**.
- On a grid, read **eastings first, then northings**.
- Latitude runs **west to east**; longitude runs **north to south**.

# MAP WORK

On a map of East Africa you should be able to:

- draw Uganda and shade it, marking **Kampala**
- name all **five neighbours** in their correct directions
- draw the **Equator** through Uganda
- show **Lake Victoria** in the south-east

# REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Name the five countries that neighbour Uganda and give the direction of each.
2. What is a compass used for? Name the four cardinal and four semi-cardinal points.
3. Define lines of latitude and lines of longitude.
4. Which line of latitude passes through Uganda, and what does it do to the country?
5. Between which lines of latitude and longitude does Uganda lie?
6. Why is Uganda called a landlocked country, and which two ports does it use?
7. Name the five elements of a map.
8. Give the use of a key and of a scale on a map.
9. On a map of scale 1 : 50,000, two towns are 8 cm apart. Find the real distance.
10. How is a grid reference read?
11. What is the capital city of Uganda, and where is the international airport?
12. About how big is Uganda, and about how much of it is water and swamp?
13. Name the four regions of Uganda.
14. Give one reason why the number of districts in Uganda keeps changing.
15. Draw the map of Uganda showing the Equator, Kampala and the neighbouring countries.

---

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Kenya** — east; **South Sudan** — north; **D.R. Congo** — west; **Rwanda** — south-west; **Tanzania** — south.
2. A compass is used to find **direction**. Cardinal: North, East, South, West. Semi-cardinal: North-East, South-East, South-West, North-West.
3. **Lines of latitude** are imaginary lines running from west to east, showing distance north or south of the Equator. **Lines of longitude** run from the North Pole to the South Pole, showing distance east or west of the Prime Meridian.
4. The **Equator**. It divides Uganda so that part of the country lies in the **Northern Hemisphere** and part in the **Southern Hemisphere**.
5. Between about **4°N and 1°S**, and between about **29°E and 35°E**.
6. Because it has **no coastline of its own**. It uses the ports of **Mombasa** in Kenya and **Dar es Salaam** in Tanzania.
7. Title, key, scale, compass (direction) and frame.
8. The **key** explains the symbols used on the map; the **scale** shows how distance on the map compares with distance on the ground.
9. 8 × 50,000 = 400,000 cm = 400,000 ÷ 100,000 = **4 km**.
10. Read the **eastings (the lines going across) first**, then the **northings (the lines going up)**.
11. The capital is **Kampala**; the international airport is at **Entebbe**.
12. About **241,000 km²**, and about **one fifth** of it is open water and swamp.
13. Central, Eastern, Northern and Western.
14. Because government **creates new districts** by splitting the old ones, to bring services nearer to the people.
15. *Learner's map.* It should show Uganda with the Equator crossing the south, Kampala marked near Lake Victoria, and the five neighbours named in their correct positions.
