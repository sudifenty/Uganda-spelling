## 1. COMMON SICKNESSES IN A HOME

| Sickness | Cause | How it is spread | Control and prevention |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Malaria** | A parasite carried by the female anopheles mosquito | Mosquito bites | Sleep under a treated net, drain stagnant water, clear bushes, take treatment early |
| **Diarrhoea** | Germs in dirty food and water | Dirty hands, unsafe water, flies | Boil drinking water, wash hands with soap, cover food, use a latrine; give ORS |
| **Typhoid** | Bacteria in contaminated food and water | Dirty water and food | Boil water, wash hands, proper waste disposal, immunisation |
| **Cholera** | Bacteria | Dirty water and food | Boil water, use latrines, wash hands, report cases at once |
| **Intestinal worms** | Worms and their eggs | Walking barefoot, dirty food, dirty hands | Wear shoes, wash hands and food, deworm regularly, use latrines |
| **Cough, flu and colds** | Viruses | Coughing and sneezing | Cover the mouth, keep rooms airy, keep warm, treat early |
| **Tuberculosis (TB)** | Bacteria | Coughing and sneezing in crowded rooms | BCG immunisation, ventilation, complete the treatment |
| **Skin diseases** (scabies, ringworm, jiggers) | Mites, fungi, fleas | Dirt and sharing clothing | Bathe daily, wash clothes and beddings, do not share towels, wear shoes |
| **Eye infections** (conjunctivitis, trachoma) | Bacteria and viruses spread by flies and dirt | Sharing towels, flies | Wash the face daily, keep the home clean, treat early |
| **Malnutrition** (kwashiorkor, marasmus) | Poor feeding | — | Eat a balanced diet; feed babies well |

### General ways of controlling sickness in a home

Keep the home and compound **clean** · use a **latrine** and wash hands with soap · **boil
or treat** drinking water · cover and store food well · sleep under a **treated mosquito
net** · drain stagnant water · take children for **immunisation** and growth monitoring ·
eat a **balanced diet** · go to a health centre early instead of using untested remedies ·
keep animals away from the house.

## 2. COMMUNITY HEALTH AND SOCIAL PROBLEMS AMONG YOUNG PEOPLE

| Problem | Effects |
|---|---|
| **Early pregnancy and early marriage** | Dropping out of school, birth complications, poverty |
| **Drug and substance abuse** (alcohol, tobacco, marijuana, aviation fuel, khat) | Poor health, poor performance at school, crime, addiction |
| **Sexually transmitted infections and HIV/AIDS** | Long illness, stigma, death |
| **Defilement and sexual abuse** | Injury, disease, pregnancy, fear and trauma; it is a **crime** |
| **Poor personal hygiene** | Skin disease, worms, bad smell, low self-esteem |
| **Child labour and school dropout** | Loss of education, injury, exploitation |
| **Peer pressure** | Leads young people into risky behaviour |
| **Poor nutrition** | Anaemia, poor growth, tiredness in class |

### Anti-social behaviour

**Anti-social behaviour:** Behaviour that harms other people or the community.

Examples: stealing · bullying · fighting and violence · drug and alcohol abuse ·
truancy (staying away from school) · vandalism (destroying property) · abusive language ·
gambling · rape and defilement · prostitution · cheating in examinations.

**Effects:** it destroys peace and trust, causes injury and disease, leads to punishment
and imprisonment, spoils a person's future, and brings shame and loss to families.

**Prevention:** good guidance and counselling at home and school · useful use of free time
in sports, clubs and hobbies · choosing friends wisely and resisting **peer pressure** ·
following the rules of the school and the country · religious and moral teaching ·
reporting wrongdoing to parents, teachers or the police.

### Sex deviations

The syllabus lists, under the heading **"sex deviations"**, the following: *bestiality,
incest, homosexuality, lesbianism, oral sex and masturbation.*

At P.7 level the teaching aim is **protection, not detail**. What every learner should know:

- Your body belongs to you. **No one** — a relative, a neighbour, an older pupil or an
  adult — has the right to touch your private parts.
- Any sexual act involving a child is **abuse** and is against the law in Uganda.
- Some sexual practices carry a high risk of **infection**, including HIV and other
  sexually transmitted infections.
- If someone touches you wrongly, or tries to, **say NO, get away and tell a trusted adult
  at once** — a parent, teacher, health worker, religious leader or the police. It is never
  the child's fault, and it must never be kept secret.
- Avoid pornography, dangerous friendships and places where you are alone with someone who
  makes you uncomfortable.

## 3. ACTIVITIES TO ADDRESS HEALTH CONCERNS

### Health education

Teaching people how to keep healthy — through talks, radio programmes, posters, drama,
songs, health clubs and community meetings.

### A health survey

**Health survey:** Going round a community to collect information about health problems so that they can be solved.

**Steps in carrying out a simple health survey:**

1. Choose the health problem to study (for example, malaria in the village).
2. Prepare simple questions or a checklist.
3. Get permission from the head teacher or the local leaders.
4. Visit the homes and **observe, ask and record** (Is there a latrine? A rubbish pit? Mosquito nets? Stagnant water?).
5. Organise the findings in tables, tally charts and graphs.
6. Discuss what the findings mean.
7. **Report** to the community and the health workers, and suggest what should be done.
8. Take action — clean-up days, drama, posters — then check whether things improve.

### Other activities

Immunisation days · clean-up campaigns · digging rubbish pits and latrines · protecting
water sources · distributing mosquito nets · growing vegetables and fruit for good
nutrition · sports and physical fitness · guidance and counselling.

## 4. INFORMATION AND DATA ON HUMAN POPULATION

### Definitions

**Population:** The total number of people living in a place at a particular time.

**Census:** The official counting of all the people in a country. In Uganda, a national census is carried out about every ten years.

### Ways of collecting population information

By **census** · from **birth and death registers** (NIRA) · from **health centre records** ·
from **school registers** · through **surveys and questionnaires** · from **local council
records**.

### Why population information is needed

- To plan for **schools, hospitals, water, roads and houses**.
- To know the **growth rate** of the population.
- To plan for **food production and jobs**.
- To share **government services and money** fairly.
- To fight disease — for example, to know how many children need immunisation.

### Effects of a rapidly growing population

| Problems | Advantages |
|---|---|
| Overcrowding, slums and poor sanitation | A large labour force |
| Pressure on schools, hospitals and water | A bigger market for goods |
| Unemployment and poverty | More ideas, skills and taxes |
| Food shortage and famine | Better use of land in areas with few people |
| Land shortage and land conflicts | Faster development of services |
| Destruction of forests and wetlands | |
| Fast spread of diseases | |

### Ways of controlling rapid population growth

Educating people, especially girls, so that they marry later · **family planning** services
and education · discouraging early marriage and teenage pregnancy · improving child
survival so that families do not need many children · creating jobs and improving incomes.

## 5. HEALTH CLUBS

### Definition

**Health club:** A group of learners in a school who work together to promote good health in the school and the community.

### Activities of a health club

- Keeping the **compound, classrooms and latrines clean**.
- Checking **personal hygiene** — nails, hair, uniforms, handkerchiefs.
- Running **hand-washing points** with soap and clean water.
- Making and displaying **posters and health messages**.
- Presenting **drama, poems, songs and debates** about health.
- Organising **talks** by health workers.
- Growing **vegetables and fruit trees** for good nutrition.
- Carrying out **health surveys** and clean-up campaigns.
- Teaching about malaria, HIV/AIDS, worms and personal safety.
- Keeping a **first-aid box** and reporting sick learners.

### Importance of health clubs

They reduce disease in the school · they teach practical skills and responsibility · they
spread health messages to families · they build confidence and leadership · they make the
school clean and pleasant.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Malaria is spread by the **female anopheles mosquito**; the best home protection is a **treated mosquito net**.
- Diarrhoea is treated at home with **ORS** (oral rehydration salts) while getting help.
- Most home sicknesses are prevented by **clean water, latrines, hand washing with soap and a balanced diet**.
- Sexual abuse of a child is a **crime**; the child must **tell a trusted adult at once**, and it is never the child's fault.
- A **census** counts everybody in the country; Uganda holds one about every ten years.
- Rapid population growth causes **unemployment, land shortage and pressure on services**.
- Health clubs promote hygiene, nutrition and health education in schools.
