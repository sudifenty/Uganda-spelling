## 1. GOATS AND SHEEP

### External parts

**Goat:** head, horns, **beard**, eyes, ears, nostrils, mouth, neck, back, **udder** (female)
or scrotum (male), legs with **hooves**, short tail that points **upwards**, hair coat.

**Sheep:** head, horns (in some breeds), eyes, ears, muzzle, neck, back, udder, legs with
hooves, tail that hangs **downwards**, and a **woolly fleece**.

| Goat | Sheep |
|---|---|
| Has a **beard**; tail points **up** | No beard; tail hangs **down** |
| Coat of **hair** | Coat of **wool (fleece)** |
| A **browser** — eats leaves and shrubs | A **grazer** — eats grass |
| Very active, climbs and jumps | Calm, moves in a flock |
| Gives meat, milk and skins | Gives meat, **wool** and skins |

### Breeds

| Animal | Local (indigenous) | Exotic |
|---|---|---|
| **Goats** | Small East African, Mubende | **Toggenburg, Saanen, Alpine** (milk); **Boer** (meat) |
| **Sheep** | East African Blackhead, Masai | **Merino, Dorper, Corriedale** (wool and meat) |

### Uses

**Meat** (mutton from sheep, chevon from goats) · **milk**, especially from dairy goats ·
**skins and hides** · **wool** from sheep · **manure** · **money** and school fees ·
cultural and religious ceremonies · they are easy to keep on small land.

---

## 2. PIGS

### External parts

**Snout** (the flat nose used for rooting) · eyes · ears · **bristles** (stiff hairs) ·
back · **hooves (cloven feet)** · **curly tail** · udder with teats (sow) · flank.

### Breeds

**Local pigs** — small, hardy, dark; and **exotic breeds**: **Large White**, **Landrace**,
**Duroc**, **Hampshire** and **Camborough** crosses, which grow fast and give more meat.

**Terms to know:** a **boar** is a male pig; a **sow** is a female that has produced; a
**gilt** is a young female; a **piglet** is a young pig; **farrowing** is giving birth; the
**gestation period is about 114 days** — "three months, three weeks and three days".

---

## 3. HOUSING AND MANAGEMENT

### A good house for goats, sheep or pigs

**Raised and well-drained** floor so it stays dry · **well ventilated** but not draughty ·
strong walls and a leak-proof roof · easy to **clean**, with a slope and a drainage channel ·
separate pens for the young and for sick animals · **feeding troughs and drinkers** ·
enough space so they are not crowded · a **wallow or shade** for pigs, which cannot sweat.

### Management

Give **clean water always** and a **balanced feed** — pasture and browse, crop residues,
kitchen leftovers (for pigs), concentrates and **mineral licks** · **deworm** and
**vaccinate** regularly · spray or dip against external parasites · **clean the house
daily** · trim hooves · handle them gently · isolate the sick · keep **records** of births,
sales, feeds and treatments · castrate males not needed for breeding.

## 5. PARASITES AND DISEASES

### Parasites

| Type | Examples | Harm |
|---|---|---|
| **External** | Ticks, lice, mites (mange), fleas, tsetse flies | Suck blood, cause itching, wounds and spread disease |
| **Internal** | Round worms, tape worms, liver flukes, lung worms | Cause loss of weight, pot belly, anaemia, poor growth |

### Common diseases

| Disease | Animal | Signs | Prevention and control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pneumonia** | Goats, sheep, pigs | Coughing, fast breathing, fever | Warm, dry, ventilated housing; treatment |
| **Foot and mouth disease** | Goats, sheep, pigs | Sores in the mouth and on the hooves, lameness | Vaccination, quarantine |
| **Mange** | All | Itching, hair loss, thick skin | Spraying or dipping; treatment |
| **Worm infestation** | All | Thin body, pot belly, diarrhoea | Regular deworming, clean pasture |
| **Orf** | Goats and sheep | Scabs on the lips and muzzle | Isolate and treat; vaccination |
| **African swine fever** | Pigs | High fever, red skin, sudden death — **no cure and no vaccine** | Strict hygiene, quarantine, no swill from infected areas, no visitors in the pig house |
| **Swine erysipelas** | Pigs | Diamond-shaped skin marks, fever | Vaccination and treatment |
| **Pig mange and worms** | Pigs | Itching, poor growth | Spraying and deworming |

**Golden rules:** keep the house **clean and dry** · **deworm and vaccinate** on time ·
**isolate** sick animals at once · call the **veterinary officer** early · never eat or sell
meat from an animal that died of disease.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Browser | An animal that feeds on leaves and shrubs |
| Grazer | An animal that feeds on grass |
| Fleece | The woolly coat of a sheep |
| Boar | A male pig |
| Sow | A female pig that has produced |
| Farrowing | The act of a pig giving birth |
| Gestation period | The time the young stays in the womb |
| Wallow | A muddy or watery place where pigs cool themselves |
| Mange | A skin disease caused by mites |
| Quarantine | Keeping animals apart to stop disease spreading |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- A **goat has a beard and its tail points up**; a **sheep has wool and its tail hangs down**.
- Goats are **browsers**; sheep are **grazers**.
- Sheep give **wool**; goats give **milk and meat**.
- A male pig is a **boar**, a female is a **sow**, giving birth is **farrowing**, and gestation is about **114 days**.
- Pigs **cannot sweat**, so they need shade and a **wallow**.
- **African swine fever has no cure and no vaccine** — only hygiene and quarantine control it.
- Houses must be **raised, dry, clean and well ventilated**.
- **Deworm, vaccinate, spray and keep records.**

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- The animal with a beard and an upward tail → the **goat**.
- The coat of a sheep → the **fleece (wool)**.
- An animal that feeds on leaves and shrubs → a **browser**.
- A male pig → a **boar**; a female that has produced → a **sow**.
- Giving birth in pigs → **farrowing**; gestation → about **114 days**.
- Why pigs wallow in mud → they **cannot sweat**, so they cool themselves.
- The pig disease with no cure and no vaccine → **African swine fever**.
- One external parasite of goats → **ticks** (or lice, mites).

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Any six of: head, horns, beard, eyes, ears, nostrils, mouth, neck, back, udder or scrotum, legs, hooves, tail.
2. A **goat** has a beard, a tail that points up, a coat of hair, and it browses on leaves; a **sheep** has no beard, a tail that hangs down, a woolly fleece, and it grazes on grass.
3. **Local:** Small East African and Mubende. **Exotic:** Toggenburg and Saanen (also Alpine, Boer).
4. **Local:** East African Blackhead and Masai. **Exotic:** Merino and Dorper (also Corriedale).
5. Any five of: meat; milk; skins and hides; wool from sheep; manure; income and school fees; ceremonies; easy to keep on small land.
6. Any six of: snout, eyes, ears, bristles, back, hooves (cloven feet), curly tail, udder with teats, flank.
7. Any three of: **Large White, Landrace, Duroc, Hampshire**.
8. **Boar** — a male pig; **sow** — a female pig that has produced; **gilt** — a young female pig; **farrowing** — a pig giving birth.
9. About **114 days** — three months, three weeks and three days.
10. Any six of: raised and well-drained floor; well ventilated but not draughty; strong walls and a leak-proof roof; easy to clean with a slope and drainage; separate pens for the young and the sick; feeding troughs and drinkers; enough space; shade or a wallow for pigs.
11. Because pigs **cannot sweat**, so they cannot cool themselves in the usual way. Shade and a wallow keep them cool and also help control external parasites.
12. Any six of: give clean water always; feed a balanced ration with mineral licks; deworm and vaccinate; spray or dip against parasites; clean the house daily; trim hooves; handle them gently; isolate sick animals; keep records; castrate males not needed for breeding.
13. **Goats:** meat, milk, skins, manure, income. **Sheep:** meat, **wool**, skins, manure. **Pigs:** pork, bacon, ham, sausages, lard, bristles, manure.
14. **External:** ticks, lice, mites, fleas (also tsetse flies). **Internal:** round worms, tape worms, liver flukes (also lung worms).
15. Any five, for example: **pneumonia** — coughing and fever, prevented by warm dry housing and treated early; **foot and mouth** — mouth and hoof sores, controlled by vaccination and quarantine; **mange** — itching and hair loss, controlled by spraying; **worms** — thin body and pot belly, controlled by deworming; **African swine fever** — high fever and sudden death, controlled only by hygiene and quarantine, as there is no cure.
