*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, Mathematics Term III,

**What you already know:** You know Uganda's notes and coins and you can add and subtract

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- work out the cost of several items and the change due
- explain what profit and loss mean
- calculate simple profit and loss
- solve everyday money problems

## 1. UGANDA'S CURRENCY

Uganda's money is the **Uganda shilling** (written **UGX** or **shs**).

| Coins in use | Notes in use |
|---|---|
| 50, 100, 200, 500 shillings | 1,000 · 2,000 · 5,000 · 10,000 · 20,000 · 50,000 shillings |

> **A note that is newer than the syllabus:** the syllabus was written in 2010 and simply
> says "Uganda currency". The list of notes and coins above is what is in everyday use in
> Uganda today. Older coins of smaller value are no longer used in ordinary trade.

## 2. BUYING — WORKING OUT THE COST

**Cost = price of one item × number of items.**

**Example 1.** Sugar costs 4,500 shillings a kilogram. How much do 3 kg cost?

```
3 × 4,500 = 13,500 shillings
```

**Example 2.** A pupil buys 4 exercise books at 1,200 shillings each and 2 pens at 500
shillings each. What is the total cost?

```
Books: 4 × 1,200 = 4,800
Pens:  2 × 500   = 1,000
Total: 4,800 + 1,000 = 5,800 shillings
```

### Change

**Change = money given − total cost.**

```
If the pupil gives a 10,000 note:
Change = 10,000 − 5,800 = 4,200 shillings
```

### Finding the price of one item

```
5 kg of rice cost 17,500 shillings.
Price of 1 kg = 17,500 ÷ 5 = 3,500 shillings
```

---

## 3. COST PRICE AND SELLING PRICE

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cost price (C.P.)** | The money a trader pays to **buy** goods |
| **Selling price (S.P.)** | The money a trader receives when he **sells** goods |

---

## 4. PROFIT

A trader makes a **profit** when the selling price is **more than** the cost price.

```
Profit = Selling price − Cost price
```

**Example.** A trader buys a bag of charcoal at 25,000 shillings and sells it at 32,000
shillings. Find his profit.

```
Profit = 32,000 − 25,000 = 7,000 shillings
```

**Finding the selling price when the profit is known:**

```
Selling price = Cost price + Profit
A shirt bought at 15,000 is sold at a profit of 4,000.
S.P. = 15,000 + 4,000 = 19,000 shillings
```

---

## 5. LOSS

A trader makes a **loss** when the selling price is **less than** the cost price.

```
Loss = Cost price − Selling price
```

**Example.** A trader buys a goat at 180,000 shillings and sells it at 165,000 shillings.

```
Loss = 180,000 − 165,000 = 15,000 shillings
```

**Finding the cost price when the loss is known:**

```
Cost price = Selling price + Loss
A radio sold at 45,000 gave a loss of 8,000.
C.P. = 45,000 + 8,000 = 53,000 shillings
```

---

## 6. WHY PROFIT AND LOSS HAPPEN

| Leads to profit | Leads to loss |
|---|---|
| Buying cheaply and selling at a good price | Goods going bad or expiring |
| Selling many goods quickly | Theft or fire |
| Keeping costs such as transport low | Selling below the cost price to get customers |
| Good record keeping | Prices falling after the goods were bought |

## 7. MIXED WORD PROBLEMS

**Example 1.** A shopkeeper bought 20 loaves of bread at 3,500 shillings each and sold all of
them at 4,200 shillings each. Find the total profit.

```
Cost price   = 20 × 3,500 = 70,000 shillings
Selling price = 20 × 4,200 = 84,000 shillings
Profit = 84,000 − 70,000 = 14,000 shillings
Check: profit on one loaf = 700; 700 × 20 = 14,000  ✓
```

**Example 2.** Mother's shopping list: 2 kg of beans at 4,000, 3 kg of sugar at 4,500 and 1
litre of oil at 9,000. She pays with two 20,000 notes. Find her change.

```
Beans: 2 × 4,000 = 8,000
Sugar: 3 × 4,500 = 13,500
Oil:                9,000
Total: 8,000 + 13,500 + 9,000 = 30,500
Money given = 2 × 20,000 = 40,000
Change = 40,000 − 30,500 = 9,500 shillings
```

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Currency | The money used in a country |
| Cost price | The money paid to buy goods |
| Selling price | The money received when goods are sold |
| Profit | The money gained when the selling price is higher |
| Loss | The money lost when the selling price is lower |
| Change | The money given back after paying more than the cost |
| Discount | Money taken off the usual price |
| Balance | The money that remains |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Profit = S.P. − C.P.** and **Loss = C.P. − S.P.**
- **S.P. = C.P. + Profit** and **C.P. = S.P. + Loss**
- **Change = money given − total cost.**
- Always write **shillings** in your answer.
- If the selling price equals the cost price, there is **neither profit nor loss**.
- Work out the cost of **all** the items before finding the change.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Bought at 25,000, sold at 32,000 → **profit 7,000 shillings**.
- Bought at 180,000, sold at 165,000 → **loss 15,000 shillings**.
- 3 kg of sugar at 4,500 → **13,500 shillings**.
- Change from 10,000 after spending 5,800 → **4,200 shillings**.
- 20 loaves bought at 3,500 and sold at 4,200 → **profit 14,000 shillings**.
- Check a total profit by finding the profit on **one item** and multiplying.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Subtracting the wrong way round and calling a loss a profit.
- Forgetting to multiply by the **number of items** before comparing.
- Leaving out one item when adding up a shopping list.
- Writing the answer without the word **shillings**.
- Confusing the cost price with the selling price in a word problem.

# REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Name any three notes used in Uganda today.
2. Sugar costs 4,500 shillings a kilogram. Find the cost of 3 kg.
3. Find the cost of 4 exercise books at 1,200 shillings each.
4. 5 kg of rice cost 17,500 shillings. Find the cost of 1 kg.
5. A pupil spends 5,800 shillings and gives a 10,000 note. Find the change.
6. A trader buys a bag of charcoal at 25,000 and sells it at 32,000. Find the profit.
7. A goat bought at 180,000 was sold at 165,000. Find the loss.
8. A shirt bought at 15,000 is sold at a profit of 4,000. Find the selling price.
9. A radio sold at 45,000 gave a loss of 8,000. Find the cost price.
10. A trader buys 20 loaves at 3,500 each and sells them at 4,200 each. Find the total profit.
11. Find the total cost of 2 kg of beans at 4,000, 3 kg of sugar at 4,500 and 1 litre of oil at 9,000.
12. Using question 11, find the change from two 20,000 shilling notes.
13. A book costs 8,500 shillings. How many such books can be bought with 60,000 shillings, and what will remain?
14. A trader bought 50 eggs at 400 shillings each and sold them all at 550 shillings each. Find the profit.
15. If a bicycle is bought at 250,000 and sold at 250,000, what has the trader made?

---

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Any three of **1,000 · 2,000 · 5,000 · 10,000 · 20,000 · 50,000 shillings**
2. 3 × 4,500 = **13,500 shillings**
3. 4 × 1,200 = **4,800 shillings**
4. 17,500 ÷ 5 = **3,500 shillings**
5. 10,000 − 5,800 = **4,200 shillings**
6. 32,000 − 25,000 = **7,000 shillings profit**
7. 180,000 − 165,000 = **15,000 shillings loss**
8. 15,000 + 4,000 = **19,000 shillings**
9. 45,000 + 8,000 = **53,000 shillings**
10. C.P. = 70,000; S.P. = 84,000; profit = **14,000 shillings**
11. 8,000 + 13,500 + 9,000 = **30,500 shillings**
12. 40,000 − 30,500 = **9,500 shillings**
13. 60,000 ÷ 8,500 = 7 books (7 × 8,500 = 59,500), remainder **500 shillings** → **7 books and 500 shillings left**
14. C.P. = 50 × 400 = 20,000; S.P. = 50 × 550 = 27,500; profit = **7,500 shillings**
15. **Neither profit nor loss** — the selling price equals the cost price.
