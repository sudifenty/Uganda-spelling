## 1. WHAT AN ETHNIC GROUP IS

### Definitions

**Ethnic group:** A large group of people who share the same origin, language family, customs and way of life.

**Tribe:** A smaller group within an ethnic group whose members share one language and culture.

**Migration:** The movement of people from one place to another to settle there.

**Cradle land:** The original home of a group of people before they migrated.

**Indigenous people:** The people who were already living in an area before others came.

### Important Points

- Africa has very many tribes, but they are grouped into a few large **ethnic groups**.
- People of one ethnic group often speak related languages, even when they live far apart.
- Uganda alone has over 50 tribes belonging to the Bantu, Nilotic and Cushitic groups.

---

## 2. THE MAIN ETHNIC GROUPS OF AFRICA

The syllabus names these groups: **Berbers, Negroes (who gave rise to the Bantu), Cushites, Arabs, and Caucasians (Hamites and Semites)**.

| Ethnic group | Cradle land / origin | Where they are found today | Examples of tribes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bantu** (from the Negro group) | The Congo–Cameroon highlands of West/Central Africa | Central, East and Southern Africa | Baganda, Banyankole, Bagisu, Bakiga (Uganda); Kikuyu (Kenya); Chagga (Tanzania); Zulu, Xhosa (South Africa) |
| **Nilotics (Nilotes)** | The Bahr-el-Ghazal region of southern Sudan, along the Nile | South Sudan, northern Uganda, western Kenya | Acholi, Langi, Alur, Japadhola (Uganda); Luo (Kenya); Dinka, Nuer (South Sudan) |
| **Nilo-Hamites (Plain Nilotes)** | Ethiopian border area / south-eastern Sudan | North-eastern Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania | Karimojong, Iteso, Kakwa, Sebei (Uganda); Maasai, Turkana (Kenya) |
| **Cushites (Hamites)** | The Ethiopian Highlands and the Horn of Africa | Ethiopia, Somalia, Eritrea, Djibouti, northern Kenya | Somali, Oromo, Afar, Borana, Bahima (related pastoral groups) |
| **Semites (Arabs)** | The Arabian Peninsula (Asia) | North Africa, the East African coast, Sudan | Arabs of Egypt, Libya, Sudan; Swahili people of the coast |
| **Berbers** | North Africa itself — the oldest known people of the region | Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya | Tuareg, Kabyle, Rif |
| **Pygmies (Twa)** | The equatorial forests of Central Africa | D.R. Congo, Rwanda, Burundi, south-western Uganda | Batwa, Mbuti |
| **Bushmen (San) and Khoikhoi** | Southern Africa | Botswana, Namibia, South Africa | San, Nama |

### Important Points

- The **Bantu** are the largest ethnic group in Africa. The word *Bantu* means "people".
- The **Pygmies (Batwa)** and the **Bushmen (San)** are among the **oldest** inhabitants of Africa; they lived by hunting and gathering.
- Arabs came into Africa from Asia mainly through the Red Sea, the Isthmus of Suez and by sea to the East African coast.

---

## 3. MOVEMENTS OF ETHNIC GROUPS INTO AND WITHIN AFRICA

### The main movements

- **The Bantu migration** — from the Congo–Cameroon region the Bantu spread east and south from about A.D. 1000, reaching East Africa and later Southern Africa. Some entered Uganda through the west and settled in the fertile areas near Lake Victoria.
- **The Nilotic migration** — the Luo-speaking peoples moved south along the Nile from Bahr-el-Ghazal into northern Uganda and western Kenya.
- **The Nilo-Hamitic (Plain Nilote) movement** — moved south-west from the Ethiopian border into Karamoja, Teso and the Rift Valley of Kenya.
- **The Cushitic movement** — came from Ethiopia southwards into northern Kenya and parts of Uganda.
- **The Arab movement** — came from Arabia into North Africa and along the East African coast, mainly for trade and to spread Islam.

### Migration routes

Most movements followed **river valleys, grasslands and easy passes** because these gave water and pasture. Groups avoided very thick forests, deserts and steep mountains.

---

## 4. SETTLEMENT PATTERNS

### Definition

**Settlement pattern:** The way in which homes and villages are arranged in an area.

| Pattern | Description | Where it is common |
|---|---|---|
| **Nucleated (clustered)** | Houses built close together in one place, often around a market, well or chief's home | Trading centres, areas that needed defence, Karamoja *manyattas* |
| **Dispersed (scattered)** | Homes built far apart, each on its own farmland | Farming areas of Buganda, Kigezi, Bugisu |
| **Linear** | Homes built in a line along a road, river or lake shore | Along main roads, the Nile, the coast |

### Factors that influenced where people settled

- Availability of **water** — rivers, lakes and springs.
- **Fertile soils** for growing crops.
- **Good climate** — reliable rainfall and moderate temperatures.
- **Pasture** for their animals.
- **Security** — hills and forests for defence against enemies.
- **Relief** — gentle slopes and well-drained land.
- Absence of **pests and diseases** such as tsetse flies and mosquitoes.
- Later: **trade routes, roads, minerals and towns**.

---

## 5. REASONS FOR THE MIGRATION AND SETTLEMENT OF ETHNIC GROUPS

The syllabus gives these reasons:

- **Internal conflicts** among families, clans and leaders.
- **Wars** with neighbours and attacks from stronger groups.
- **Population increase** in their cradle land.
- **Lack of enough land and water** for people and animals.
- **Harsh climate** — long droughts and famine.
- **Expansionism** — the desire to find and control new land.

Other reasons include the search for **fertile soils and pasture**, the spread of **diseases and pests**, and the **spirit of adventure**.

---

## 6. PROBLEMS MET DURING MOVEMENT AND SETTLEMENT

- **Resistance** from the indigenous people they found on the way and in the places they settled.
- **Difficulty in movement** — they walked long distances on foot, carrying their property.
- **Wild animals** attacked them, and **diseases** such as malaria and sleeping sickness killed many.
- **Difficult terrain** — thick forests, mountains, swamps, rivers and deserts.
- **Food and water shortage** along the way.
- **Harsh climate** — very hot or very cold areas.
- **Language barriers** with the people they met.

---

## 7. EFFECTS OF ETHNIC MIGRATIONS

### Positive effects

- **Population increased** in the areas where they settled.
- **New crops and skills** were introduced — iron working, new farming methods, cattle keeping.
- **Intermarriages** took place and new communities were formed.
- **Trade** developed between the newcomers and the local people.
- New **kingdoms and chiefdoms** were formed, such as Buganda and Bunyoro.
- **Languages spread** — Bantu languages and later Kiswahili.

### Negative effects

- **Loss of land** by the indigenous people, who were pushed away.
- **Wars and conflicts** over land, water and pasture.
- **Cultures changed** and some small cultures disappeared.
- **Diseases spread** from one group to another.
- **Loss of life and property** during the movements and fights.

### Migration in Africa today

People still move, but for different reasons:

- **Rural–urban migration** — moving from villages to towns to look for jobs and services.
- **Rural–rural migration** — moving to areas with better land, as when people move to fertile or resettlement areas.
- **Urban–rural migration** — returning to the village after retirement or when town life becomes too expensive.
- **International migration and refugees** — moving to other countries because of war, insecurity or work.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The **Bantu** are the largest ethnic group in Africa; their cradle land was the Congo–Cameroon region.
- The **Nilotics** came from Bahr-el-Ghazal in southern Sudan.
- The **Nilo-Hamites (Plain Nilotes)** came from the Ethiopian border area.
- The **Cushites** came from the Ethiopian Highlands.
- The **Arabs (Semites)** came from Arabia in Asia.
- The **Berbers** are the oldest known people of North Africa.
- **Batwa (Pygmies)** and **San (Bushmen)** were hunters and gatherers and are among the oldest peoples of Africa.
- Three settlement patterns: **nucleated, dispersed, linear**.

15. **Positive:** population increased; new skills and crops such as iron working and farming spread; intermarriages took place and new communities formed; trade developed; kingdoms were formed; languages spread. **Negative:** the indigenous people lost land; wars and conflicts broke out; cultures were changed or lost; diseases spread; lives and property were lost. (Any three and any two.)
16. Because thick forests and deserts were difficult to travel through, had little food or pasture, and carried dangers such as wild animals, pests and shortage of water. Grasslands and river valleys offered water, pasture and easy movement.
