# TOPIC 8: HOW UGANDA BECAME A NATION

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, Social Studies Topic 8
(`curriculum/P5-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, pages 211–214).*

**What you already know:** In Topic 7 you saw how the foreigners came. In this topic you
learn how the many kingdoms and clan societies were joined into **one country called
Uganda**, and how it was governed as a Protectorate.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- explain how the name "Uganda" came about
- describe the steps by which Uganda became one nation
- name the agreements signed and what they contained
- describe the colonial system of administration
- state the effects of joining the different peoples into one country

---

## 1. THE NAME "UGANDA"

The Arab and Swahili traders from the coast called the kingdom of **Buganda** by its
Kiswahili form, **"Uganda"**. When the British arrived they used the same name, and it was
later given to the **whole Protectorate**, not just Buganda.

---

## 2. THE STEPS TO ONE NATION

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| **1888** | The **Imperial British East Africa Company (IBEACo)** was given a royal charter to trade and administer the area |
| **1890** | **Captain Frederick Lugard** arrived in Buganda for the Company and signed a treaty with **Kabaka Mwanga** |
| **1893** | The Company ran short of money and pulled out; the British Government took over |
| **1894** | Britain declared a **Protectorate over Buganda** |
| **1896** | The Protectorate was extended to **Bunyoro, Toro, Ankole and Busoga** |
| **1900** | The **Buganda Agreement** was signed |
| **1900–1901** | The **Toro Agreement (1900)** and the **Ankole Agreement (1901)** were signed |
| **1902** | The **Eastern Province** (up to Naivasha) was transferred to Kenya; Uganda's eastern boundary took shape |
| **1914** | The last areas, including Karamoja and West Nile, were brought under the Protectorate |
| **1926** | Uganda's boundaries were finally fixed |
| **1933** | The **Bunyoro Agreement** was signed |

### Semei Kakungulu

**Semei Kakungulu**, a Muganda chief, was used by the British to bring **Bukedi, Bugisu,
Teso and Lango** under colonial rule between about 1899 and 1913. He set up chiefs and a
system of administration modelled on Buganda's, and he founded the town of **Mbale**.

---

## 3. THE AGREEMENTS

### The Buganda Agreement of 1900

Signed between **Sir Harry Johnston** for Britain and the **regents of the young Kabaka
Daudi Chwa** for Buganda.

**What it contained:**

- Buganda accepted **British protection** and became a province of the Protectorate.
- Land was divided into **mailo land** (given to the Kabaka, chiefs and notables in square
  miles) and **Crown land** for the government.
- **Taxes** were introduced — hut tax and gun tax.
- The **Lukiiko** was recognised, with the Kabaka ruling under the advice of the British.
- Buganda's boundaries were fixed, taking in some counties from Bunyoro — the **"lost
  counties"**, which caused a lasting quarrel.

### The other agreements

The **Toro Agreement (1900)** and the **Ankole Agreement (1901)** recognised those kingdoms
and their rulers under British protection, with taxes and chiefs' allowances. The
**Bunyoro Agreement (1933)** came much later, after Bunyoro had resisted for a long time
under **Omukama Kabalega**, who was captured in 1899 and exiled.

---

## 4. THE COLONIAL SYSTEM OF ADMINISTRATION

Britain ruled Uganda through **indirect rule** — governing through the existing kings and
chiefs.

| Level | Officer |
|---|---|
| Top | **The Governor**, representing the British Crown |
| Province | **Provincial Commissioner** |
| District | **District Commissioner** |
| County (Ssaza) | **County chief** |
| Sub-county (Ggombolola) | **Sub-county chief** |
| Parish (Muluka) | **Parish chief** |
| Village | **Village chief** |

The **Legislative Council (LEGCO)** was set up in **1921** to make laws, but **no African
was on it until 1945**.

### The work of the chiefs

Collecting **taxes** · keeping law and order · settling minor cases · organising **forced
labour (kasanvu)** on roads · making people grow **cash crops** · carrying out the orders of
the District Commissioner.

---

## 5. EFFECTS OF BECOMING ONE NATION

### Positive

- The different peoples were joined into **one country with one government**.
- **Peace** was enforced between groups that used to raid one another.
- **Roads, the railway, schools and hospitals** were built.
- **Cash crops, trade and money** spread.
- A common **language of administration** (English) and a written law came in.
- The foundation of modern **Uganda** — its boundaries, districts and public service — was laid.

### Negative

- Uganda **lost its independence**.
- Kingdoms lost their power; **Kabalega and Mwanga were exiled** in 1899.
- **Land was given away** under the agreements, and Bunyoro lost the **"lost counties"**.
- **Forced labour and heavy taxes**.
- **Tribal and religious divisions** were deepened, some created by the way people were used against each other.
- Uganda's resources were used mainly for **Britain's benefit**.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nation | A large group of people living in one country under one government |
| Protectorate | A territory protected and controlled by a stronger country |
| Charter | An official document giving a company or body the right to act |
| Agreement | A written understanding signed by two or more sides |
| Mailo land | Land given out in square miles under the 1900 Buganda Agreement |
| Indirect rule | Ruling through the existing local chiefs |
| Governor | The officer who represented the British Crown in a colony |
| LEGCO | The Legislative Council, which made laws in the Protectorate |
| Lost counties | The counties taken from Bunyoro and given to Buganda |
| Kasanvu | The forced labour system used in colonial Uganda |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The name **Uganda** is the Kiswahili form of **Buganda**.
- **IBEACo 1888** · **Lugard 1890** · **Protectorate 1894** · **Buganda Agreement 1900**.
- The Buganda Agreement was signed by **Sir Harry Johnston** and the **regents of Kabaka Daudi Chwa**.
- It created **mailo land** and introduced **hut and gun taxes**.
- **Semei Kakungulu** helped bring the east and north under colonial rule and founded **Mbale**.
- **Omukama Kabalega** of Bunyoro resisted and was **captured and exiled in 1899**, together with **Kabaka Mwanga**.
- **LEGCO was formed in 1921**; the first Africans joined in **1945**.
- Britain used **indirect rule**, through the Governor, commissioners and chiefs.
