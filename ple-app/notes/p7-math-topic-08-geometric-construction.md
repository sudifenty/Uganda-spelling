## 1. LINES AND ANGLES

### Definitions

**Line segment:** A straight line with two end points and a definite length.

**Parallel lines:** Lines in the same plane that never meet, however far they are drawn. They are always the same distance apart.

**Intersecting lines:** Lines that cross each other at a point.

**Perpendicular lines:** Lines that meet at 90°.

**Skew lines:** Lines that are **not** in the same plane — they never meet and they are not parallel. Think of a road passing under a bridge: the road and the bridge never meet, but they are not parallel either.

### Types of angles

| Angle | Size |
|---|---|
| Acute | Less than 90° |
| Right | Exactly 90° |
| Obtuse | Between 90° and 180° |
| Straight | Exactly 180° |
| Reflex | Between 180° and 360° |

### Angles made by a line crossing two parallel lines

| Name | Where they are | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertically opposite angles** | Opposite each other where two lines cross | **Equal** |
| **Corresponding angles** | In matching corners (they form the letter **F**) | **Equal** |
| **Alternate angles** | On opposite sides of the crossing line (letter **Z**) | **Equal** |
| **Co-interior (allied) angles** | Between the parallel lines on the same side (letter **C** or **U**) | **Add up to 180°** |

### Worked example

*Two parallel lines are crossed by another line. One angle is 65°. Find the co-interior angle and the alternate angle.*

- Alternate angle = **65°** (alternate angles are equal)
- Co-interior angle = 180 − 65 = **115°**

## 2. BASIC CONSTRUCTIONS

Use only a ruler and a pair of compasses. **Never rub out your construction arcs** — they show the examiner how you worked.

### (a) Bisecting a line segment (perpendicular bisector)

1. Open the compasses to more than half the line.
2. With the point at one end, draw arcs above and below the line.
3. Repeat from the other end with the same radius.
4. Join the two points where the arcs cross. This line cuts the segment into two equal parts **and** meets it at 90°.

### (b) Bisecting an angle

1. With the point at the corner, draw an arc cutting both arms.
2. From each of those two points draw arcs that cross each other.
3. Join the corner to the crossing point.

### (c) Constructing angles

| Angle | How |
|---|---|
| **60°** | Draw an arc from the end of the line; from where it cuts the line, draw another arc of the same radius; join |
| **120°** | Two 60° arcs, one after the other |
| **90°** | Construct a perpendicular, or bisect the 180° straight angle |
| **30°** | Bisect a 60° angle |
| **45°** | Bisect a 90° angle |
| **75°** | Bisect the angle between 60° and 90° |

## 3. POLYGONS

### Definition

**Polygon:** A closed shape with three or more straight sides.

**Regular polygon:** A polygon whose sides are all equal **and** whose angles are all equal.

| Sides | Name | Sum of interior angles | Each interior angle (regular) | Each exterior angle (regular) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Triangle | 180° | 60° | 120° |
| 4 | Quadrilateral | 360° | 90° | 90° |
| 5 | Pentagon | 540° | 108° | 72° |
| 6 | Hexagon | 720° | 120° | 60° |
| 7 | Heptagon | 900° | ≈128.6° | ≈51.4° |
| 8 | Octagon | 1,080° | 135° | 45° |
| 9 | Nonagon | 1,260° | 140° | 40° |
| 10 | Decagon | 1,440° | 144° | 36° |

### The formulas

- **Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°**
- **Sum of exterior angles = 360°** (for every polygon)
- **Each exterior angle of a regular polygon = 360° ÷ n**
- **Each interior angle of a regular polygon = (n − 2) × 180° ÷ n**
- Interior angle + exterior angle = **180°**

### Worked examples

1. *Find the sum of the interior angles of a hexagon.*
   (6 − 2) × 180 = 4 × 180 = **720°**

2. *Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 24°. How many sides has it?*
   n = 360 ÷ 24 = **15 sides**

3. *Find each interior angle of a regular octagon.*
   Exterior = 360 ÷ 8 = 45°, so interior = 180 − 45 = **135°**

### Properties of some shapes

- **Square:** 4 equal sides, 4 right angles, diagonals equal and bisect at 90°.
- **Rectangle:** opposite sides equal, 4 right angles, diagonals equal.
- **Rhombus:** 4 equal sides, opposite angles equal, diagonals cross at 90°.
- **Parallelogram:** opposite sides equal and parallel, opposite angles equal.
- **Trapezium:** only one pair of parallel sides.

## 4. BEARING AND SCALE DRAWING

### Definitions

**Bearing:** The direction of one place from another, measured **clockwise from the north** and written with **three figures**.

**Scale:** The relationship between a distance on a drawing and the real distance on the ground.

### The compass and bearings

| Direction | Bearing |
|---|---|
| North | 000° or 360° |
| North-East | 045° |
| East | 090° |
| South-East | 135° |
| South | 180° |
| South-West | 225° |
| West | 270° |
| North-West | 315° |

**Compass bearing to three-figure bearing:** N 40° E = **040°**; S 30° W = 180 + 30 = **210°**; N 50° W = 360 − 50 = **310°**.

### Back bearings

If the bearing of B from A is less than 180°, **add 180°** to get the bearing of A from B. If it is 180° or more, **subtract 180°**.

*The bearing of B from A is 070°. The bearing of A from B is 70 + 180 = **250°**.*

### Scale drawing

*A map has a scale of 1 : 50,000. Two towns are 6 cm apart on the map. Find the real distance.*

6 × 50,000 = 300,000 cm = 300,000 ÷ 100,000 = **3 km**

*(Remember: 100 cm = 1 m and 1,000 m = 1 km, so 100,000 cm = 1 km.)*

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Line segment | A straight line with two end points |
| Parallel lines | Lines that never meet and stay the same distance apart |
| Skew lines | Lines in different planes that never meet and are not parallel |
| Perpendicular | Meeting at 90° |
| Bisect | To cut into two equal parts |
| Polygon | A closed shape with three or more straight sides |
| Regular polygon | A polygon with all sides and all angles equal |
| Interior angle | An angle inside a polygon |
| Exterior angle | The angle formed outside when a side is extended |
| Bearing | Direction measured clockwise from north, in three figures |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Vertically opposite, corresponding and alternate angles are **equal**; co-interior angles add up to **180°**.
- Sum of interior angles = **(n − 2) × 180°**.
- Sum of exterior angles of **any** polygon = **360°**.
- 60° is constructed with arcs of the **same radius**; 30° is 60° bisected; 45° is 90° bisected.
- Bearings are always written with **three figures** and measured **clockwise from north**.
- 100,000 cm = 1 km.
- Never rub out construction arcs.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Sum of the interior angles of a pentagon = 540°; each angle of a regular pentagon = 108°.
- Each exterior angle of a regular polygon = 360 ÷ n; if it is 24°, the polygon has 15 sides.
- Bearing of due west = 270°.
- N 40° E written as a three-figure bearing = 040°.
- Back bearing: add 180° (or subtract 180° if the bearing is 180° or more).
- Scale 1 : 50,000 → 1 cm on the map is 0.5 km on the ground.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing a bearing as 40° instead of **040°**.
- Measuring a bearing anti-clockwise, or from the south.
- Using (n − 2) × 180 to find **one** angle of a polygon without dividing by n.
- Rubbing out construction arcs.
- Confusing skew lines with parallel lines — skew lines are in **different planes**.

# REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Define parallel lines and skew lines.
2. Name the four types of angles formed when a line crosses two parallel lines.
3. Two parallel lines are crossed by a line. One of the co-interior angles is 105°. Find the other.
4. Describe briefly how to construct an angle of 60°.
5. How is an angle of 45° constructed?
6. Find the sum of the interior angles of an octagon.
7. Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.
8. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 30°. Find the number of sides.
9. The sum of the interior angles of a polygon is 1,080°. How many sides has it?
10. State any three properties of a rhombus.
11. Write N 25° E as a three-figure bearing.
12. Write S 45° W as a three-figure bearing.
13. The bearing of Q from P is 130°. Find the bearing of P from Q.
14. On a map with scale 1 : 100,000 two towns are 8.5 cm apart. Find the real distance in kilometres.
15. Find the sum of the exterior angles of a decagon.

---

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Parallel lines are lines in the same plane that never meet and are always the same distance apart. Skew lines are lines in different planes that never meet and are not parallel.
2. Vertically opposite angles, corresponding angles, alternate angles and co-interior (allied) angles.
3. 180 − 105 = **75°**
4. Draw a straight line. Put the compass point at one end and draw an arc cutting the line. Without changing the radius, put the point where the arc cuts the line and draw another arc cutting the first. Join the end of the line to the point where the arcs cross.
5. First construct a right angle (90°), then bisect it.
6. (8 − 2) × 180 = **1,080°**
7. Exterior = 360 ÷ 6 = 60°, so interior = 180 − 60 = **120°**
8. n = 360 ÷ 30 = **12 sides**
9. (n − 2) × 180 = 1,080 → n − 2 = 6 → **n = 8 sides**
10. All four sides are equal; opposite angles are equal; the diagonals cross at 90° (and opposite sides are parallel).
11. **025°**
12. 180 + 45 = **225°**
13. 130 + 180 = **310°**
14. 8.5 × 100,000 = 850,000 cm = 850,000 ÷ 100,000 = **8.5 km**
15. **360°** — the exterior angles of every polygon add up to 360°.

# P.7 QUICK REVISION

- **F** = corresponding · **Z** = alternate · **C/U** = co-interior (add to 180°)
- Interior sum = **(n − 2) × 180** · Exterior sum = **360** always
- Regular polygon: each exterior = 360 ÷ n; interior = 180 − exterior
- 60° from equal arcs · 30° = half of 60° · 45° = half of 90°
- Bearings: **three figures, clockwise from north** · back bearing ± 180°
- 1 km = 100,000 cm
