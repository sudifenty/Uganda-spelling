## 1. THE NATIONAL SYMBOLS OF UGANDA

| Symbol | Description and meaning |
|---|---|
| **The national flag** | Six stripes — **black, yellow, red, black, yellow, red** — with the **crested crane** in a white circle. **Black** = the African people; **yellow** = the sunshine; **red** = the blood that unites all people |
| **The crested crane** | The **national bird**, standing on one leg, showing that Uganda is always moving forward |
| **The coat of arms** | A shield and spears (defence), a **lion** and a **crested crane** (courage and the nation), the **waves** (lakes and rivers), the **sun** (sunshine), the **drum** (culture), and **coffee and cotton** (agriculture) |
| **The national anthem** | *"Oh Uganda, may God uphold thee"*, written and composed by **George Wilberforce Kakoma** |
| **The national motto** | **"For God and My Country"** |
| **The Constitution** | The 1995 Constitution — the supreme law of Uganda |
| **The Mace and the Speaker's chair** | Symbols of the authority of Parliament |

---

## 2. EVENTS SINCE INDEPENDENCE

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| **1962** | Independence, 9 October. Obote is Prime Minister; the Queen is still Head of State |
| **1963** | Uganda becomes a **republic**; **Sir Edward Muteesa II** becomes the first President |
| **1964** | The **referendum on the "lost counties"** returns Buyaga and Bugangaizi to Bunyoro |
| **1966** | The constitutional crisis; the Kabaka flees into exile |
| **1967** | A new Constitution makes Uganda a republic with an executive President and **abolishes kingdoms** |
| **1971** | **Idi Amin** takes power in a military coup |
| **1972** | The **Asians are expelled**, and the economy is badly damaged |
| **1979** | Amin is overthrown in the liberation war; short governments follow (Lule, Binaisa, the Military Commission) |
| **1980** | Elections; **Obote returns** to power; the bush war begins |
| **1986** | The **NRM/NRA takes over** on 26 January; **Yoweri Museveni** becomes President |
| **1993** | **Cultural leaders (kingdoms) are restored** as cultural, not political, institutions |
| **1995** | The **new Constitution** is promulgated on 8 October |
| **2005** | Uganda returns to a **multiparty** system of government |

---

## 3. LEADERS UGANDA HAS HAD

**Sir Edward Muteesa II** (first President, 1963) · **Apollo Milton Obote** (Prime Minister
1962, President 1966–71 and 1980–85) · **Idi Amin Dada** (1971–79) · **Yusuf Lule**,
**Godfrey Binaisa** and **Paulo Muwanga** (1979–80) · **Tito Okello Lutwa** (1985–86) ·
**Yoweri Kaguta Museveni** (since 1986).

---

## 4. ACHIEVEMENTS OF INDEPENDENT UGANDA

- A **written Constitution (1995)** and regular **elections** with an Electoral Commission.
- **Universal Primary Education (1997)** and **Universal Secondary Education (2007)**.
- More **schools, universities, hospitals and health centres**.
- Better **roads, electricity, water and telephone** services; hydro-electric dams at Bujagali, Isimba and Karuma.
- **Peace and security** in most of the country.
- Growth of **industries, trade and tourism**; discovery of **oil**.
- **Cultural institutions restored** in 1993.
- Uganda plays its part in the **East African Community**, the **African Union**, the **Commonwealth** and the **United Nations**, and sends peace-keeping soldiers abroad.
- Progress in the fight against **HIV/AIDS** and in **immunisation**.

## 5. CHALLENGES AND SOLUTIONS

| Challenge | Possible solution |
|---|---|
| **Poverty and unemployment** | Skills training, small business support, agriculture as a business |
| **Rapid population growth** | Education, especially of girls; family planning services |
| **Corruption** | Strong laws, accountability, punishing offenders, civic education |
| **Poor roads and few industries** | Investment, better planning, adding value to raw materials |
| **Diseases** — malaria, HIV/AIDS | Health education, immunisation, mosquito nets, testing and treatment |
| **Tribalism and political conflict** | National unity, civic education, dialogue, obeying the Constitution |
| **Insecurity and cattle rustling** | Police, army, community policing, disarmament programmes |
| **Environmental destruction** | Tree planting, protecting wetlands, enforcing NEMA laws |
| **Illiteracy** | Adult literacy classes, UPE and USE |
| **Dependence on foreign aid and raw exports** | Industrialisation, saving, widening the tax base |

## 6. NATIONAL DAYS OF UGANDA

| Day | Date | What is remembered |
|---|---|---|
| **Independence Day** | **9 October** | Uganda's independence, 1962 |
| **NRM Liberation Day** | **26 January** | The NRM takeover of 1986 |
| **Martyrs' Day** | **3 June** | The Uganda Martyrs of Namugongo |
| **National Heroes' Day** | **9 June** | Those who served the nation |
| **Labour Day** | **1 May** | Workers |
| **Women's Day** | **8 March** | Women |
| **Africa Day** | **25 May** | The founding of the OAU |

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Independence | The freedom of a country to govern itself |
| Republic | A country whose head of state is an elected president |
| National symbol | Something that stands for a country and identifies it |
| Coat of arms | The official emblem of a country |
| Motto | A short saying expressing a country's beliefs |
| Constitution | The supreme law of a country |
| Coup | The removal of a government by force |
| Referendum | A vote by all the people on one question |
| Cultural institution | A traditional kingdom or chiefdom kept for culture, not politics |
| Multiparty system | A system in which many parties compete for power |

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Independence: **9 October 1962**. Republic: **1963**, first President **Sir Edward Muteesa II**.
- The flag colours: **black (the people), yellow (sunshine), red (brotherhood)**, with the **crested crane**.
- The **crested crane** is the national bird; the motto is **"For God and My Country"**.
- The national anthem was composed by **George Wilberforce Kakoma**.
- **Kingdoms were abolished in 1967 and restored as cultural institutions in 1993**.
- The present Constitution was promulgated on **8 October 1995**.
- **UPE started in 1997**; **USE in 2007**.
- Uganda returned to **multiparty politics in 2005**.
- **Liberation Day 26 January** · **Independence Day 9 October** · **Martyrs' Day 3 June** · **Heroes' Day 9 June**.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Any five of: the national flag, the crested crane, the coat of arms, the national anthem, the national motto, the Constitution, the Mace.
2. It has **six stripes — black, yellow, red, black, yellow, red — with the crested crane in a white circle**. **Black** stands for the African people, **yellow** for the sunshine, and **red** for the blood that unites all people.
3. The **crested crane**. It stands on one leg, showing that Uganda is always **moving forward**, and it is a gentle, peaceful bird.
4. Any six: the **shield and spears** — defence of the country; the **lion** — courage; the **crested crane** — the nation; the **waves** — the lakes and rivers; the **sun** — sunshine; the **drum** — culture and tradition; **coffee and cotton** — agriculture.
5. **George Wilberforce Kakoma**; the motto is **"For God and My Country"**.
6. In **1963**; **Sir Edward Muteesa II** became the first President.
7. In **1967** a new Constitution **abolished the kingdoms**; in **1993** they were **restored as cultural institutions**, without political power.
8. Any five of: Sir Edward Muteesa II, Milton Obote, Idi Amin, Yusuf Lule, Godfrey Binaisa, Paulo Muwanga, Tito Okello Lutwa, Yoweri Museveni.
9. In **1986**, remembered on **26 January** as Liberation Day.
10. On **8 October 1995**.
11. Any six of: a written Constitution and regular elections; UPE and USE; more schools, hospitals and universities; better roads, electricity and water; peace and security in most areas; growth of industry, trade and tourism; restoration of cultural institutions; membership of the EAC, AU, Commonwealth and UN; progress against HIV/AIDS.
12. Any six of: poverty and unemployment; rapid population growth; corruption; poor roads and few industries; diseases such as malaria and HIV/AIDS; tribalism and political conflict; insecurity and cattle rustling; environmental destruction; illiteracy; dependence on foreign aid.
13. **Poverty** — skills training and support for small businesses; **corruption** — strong laws, accountability and punishing offenders; **malaria** — mosquito nets, draining stagnant water and early treatment; **environmental destruction** — planting trees, protecting wetlands and enforcing NEMA laws.
14. Any five: **Independence Day 9 October** — independence in 1962; **Liberation Day 26 January** — the NRM takeover; **Martyrs' Day 3 June** — the Uganda Martyrs; **Heroes' Day 9 June** — those who served the nation; **Labour Day 1 May** — workers; **Women's Day 8 March** — women.
15. Because it is the day Uganda **stopped being ruled by the British and began to govern itself**. It reminds every Ugandan of those who struggled for freedom and of the duty to keep the country united, peaceful and developing.
