# TOPIC 5: NATURAL RESOURCES IN UGANDA

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, Social Studies Topic 5
(`curriculum/P5-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, pages 200–203).*

**What you already know:** You have studied Uganda's physical features, climate and
vegetation. In P.5 you bring them together as **resources** — what people use to earn a
living — and learn how to care for them.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- explain what natural resources are and name the types found in Uganda
- locate the main natural resources on the map of Uganda
- explain how they are used and how they help the economy
- state the problems faced in developing them
- explain bad practices that destroy resources and how to care for them

---

## 1. WHAT NATURAL RESOURCES ARE

**Natural resources:** Useful things provided by nature which people use to satisfy their needs.

| Resource | Examples in Uganda |
|---|---|
| **Land and soil** | Fertile soils of the Lake Victoria crescent, Kigezi and the mountain slopes |
| **Water** | Lakes Victoria, Kyoga, Albert, Edward, George; rivers Nile, Katonga, Kafu; springs and swamps |
| **Minerals** | Copper, cobalt, limestone, gold, salt, iron ore, phosphates, oil, sand, clay, wolfram, tin, marble |
| **Vegetation and forests** | Mabira, Budongo, Bwindi, Kibale; papyrus swamps; grasslands |
| **Wildlife** | Elephants, lions, gorillas, chimpanzees, birds, fish |
| **Climate** | Reliable rainfall and warm sunshine for farming |
| **People** | The population that supplies labour, skills and a market |

---

## 2. MINERALS AND WHERE THEY ARE FOUND

| Mineral | Where | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Copper** | **Kilembe** (Kasese) | Electrical wires, coins |
| **Cobalt** | Kasese | Alloys and batteries |
| **Limestone** | **Hima** (Kasese) and **Tororo** | Cement |
| **Gold** | Busia, Karamoja, Mubende | Jewellery, foreign exchange |
| **Salt** | **Lake Katwe** (Kasese) | Cooking, animal licks |
| **Iron ore** | Muko (Kabale), Sukulu | Steel and iron sheets |
| **Phosphates** | Sukulu (Tororo) | Fertilisers |
| **Wolfram and tin** | Kabale, Ntungamo, Kisoro | Industry and export |
| **Oil (petroleum)** | The **Albertine Graben** (Hoima, Buliisa) | Fuel and money |
| **Sand, clay, stones, marble** | Many districts | Building, bricks, pottery, tiles |

**Types of mining:** **open cast** (Hima limestone), **shaft (underground)** at Kilembe,
**drilling** for oil, **panning** and small-scale digging for gold and salt.

---

## 3. USES OF NATURAL RESOURCES AND THEIR ECONOMIC VALUE

- **Land and soil** — growing food and cash crops, grazing, building.
- **Water** — drinking, domestic use, irrigation, fishing, transport, **hydro-electric power** (Nalubaale, Kiira, Bujagali, Isimba, Karuma), tourism.
- **Minerals** — raw materials for industries such as cement, steel and fertiliser; exported for **foreign exchange**.
- **Forests** — timber, poles, firewood, charcoal, medicine, paper.
- **Wildlife and scenery** — **tourism**, which is one of Uganda's biggest earners.
- **Climate** — supports agriculture, the backbone of Uganda's economy.
- **People** — labour, skills, taxes and a market for goods.

**How they help the economy:** they earn **foreign exchange**, create **employment**, give
government **taxes and royalties**, supply **raw materials** for industries, lead to the
building of **roads, towns and schools** near the resource, and raise people's incomes.

---

## 4. PROBLEMS FACED IN DEVELOPING NATURAL RESOURCES

- Shortage of **capital** and modern machinery.
- **Low technology** and few skilled people.
- **Poor transport** to remote areas where resources are found.
- Some deposits are **small and scattered**, so mining is expensive.
- **Unstable world prices** for minerals and crops.
- **Pollution and environmental damage** from mining and industry.
- **Land disputes** and displacement of people.
- **Corruption and smuggling**.
- **Pests, diseases and drought** affecting land and animal resources.
- **Over-exploitation** — over-fishing, over-cutting of forests, over-grazing.

---

## 5. BAD PRACTICES THAT DESTROY NATURAL RESOURCES

**Deforestation** · **bush burning** · **swamp draining and building in wetlands** ·
**over-fishing** and using poison, explosives or undersized nets · **poaching** ·
**overgrazing** · **over-cultivation** without resting or manuring the soil ·
**quarrying and mining without restoring the land** · **pouring waste into lakes and
rivers** · **careless use of chemicals** in gardens.

---

## 6. CARING FOR NATURAL RESOURCES

| Resource | How to care for it |
|---|---|
| **Soil** | Crop rotation, mulching, terracing, contour ploughing, manure, cover crops, fallowing |
| **Water** | Protect springs and wetlands, avoid pollution, harvest rain water, treat factory waste |
| **Forests** | Afforestation and reafforestation, agroforestry, energy-saving stoves, forest reserves |
| **Wildlife** | National parks and game reserves, laws against poaching, employing rangers |
| **Fish** | Correct net sizes, closed seasons, fish farming, no poison or explosives |
| **Minerals** | Careful mining, adding value at home, restoring mined land, fighting smuggling |

**Who helps:** **NEMA** (environment), the **National Forestry Authority**, the **Uganda
Wildlife Authority (UWA)**, the fisheries department, local governments and school
environment clubs.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Natural resource | A useful thing provided by nature |
| Mineral | A useful substance obtained from the earth |
| Mining | The removal of minerals from the earth |
| Open cast mining | Mining by removing the soil above the mineral |
| Shaft mining | Mining through deep underground shafts |
| Foreign exchange | Money earned from other countries through exports |
| Over-exploitation | Using a resource faster than it can be replaced |
| Conservation | Protecting resources so they are not destroyed |
| Poaching | The illegal killing of wild animals |
| Hydro-electric power | Electricity produced by falling water |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Uganda's natural resources: **land and soil, water, minerals, forests, wildlife, climate and people**.
- **Copper — Kilembe** · **limestone — Hima and Tororo** · **salt — Lake Katwe** · **gold — Busia and Karamoja** · **oil — the Albertine Graben**.
- **Kilembe** uses **shaft (underground)** mining; **Hima** uses **open cast**.
- Tourism and agriculture are among Uganda's biggest earners.
- The main problems are **lack of capital, low technology and poor transport**.
- **Over-fishing, deforestation and poaching** are the worst bad practices.
- **NEMA, NFA and UWA** protect Uganda's resources.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Natural resources are useful things provided by nature which people use to satisfy their needs. Any six of: land and soil, water, minerals, forests and vegetation, wildlife, climate, people.
2. Kilembe — **copper**; Hima — **limestone**; Katwe — **salt**; Busia — **gold**.
3. In the **Albertine Graben** (around Hoima and Buliisa). It is used to make **fuel** such as petrol, diesel and paraffin, and it earns the country money.
4. **Open cast** mining removes the soil above the mineral and scoops it out, for example limestone at **Hima**; **shaft mining** sinks deep shafts and tunnels underground, for example copper at **Kilembe**.
5. Any five of: drinking and domestic use; irrigation; fishing; transport; hydro-electric power; tourism; watering animals; industry.
6. Any four of: **Nalubaale**, **Kiira**, **Bujagali**, **Isimba**, **Karuma**.
7. Any five of: they earn foreign exchange; they create employment; they give government taxes and royalties; they supply raw materials for industries; they lead to building of roads and towns; they raise people's incomes.
8. Because the people supply the **labour and skills** needed to use every other resource, they pay **taxes**, and they form the **market** that buys the goods produced.
9. Any six of: shortage of capital; low technology and few skilled people; poor transport; small scattered deposits; unstable world prices; pollution; land disputes; corruption and smuggling; pests, diseases and drought; over-exploitation.
10. Any six of: deforestation; bush burning; swamp draining; over-fishing and use of poison or wrong nets; poaching; overgrazing; over-cultivation; mining without restoring land; pouring waste into water; careless use of chemicals.
11. Any five of: crop rotation, mulching, terracing, contour ploughing, applying manure, planting cover crops, fallowing, controlled grazing.
12. Any four of: using the recommended net sizes; observing closed seasons; banning poison and explosives; encouraging fish farming; patrolling the lakes; removing water hyacinth.
13. **NEMA** (environment), the **National Forestry Authority (NFA)** and the **Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA)**.
14. Because processing **adds value**, so the goods sell at a much higher price; it also creates **jobs** at home, supplies local industries, and saves the country from depending on unstable raw-material prices.
15. *Learner's map.* Kilembe in the west near the Rwenzori, Tororo in the east, and Lake Katwe in the western rift valley.
