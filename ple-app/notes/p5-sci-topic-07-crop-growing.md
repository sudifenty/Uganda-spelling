**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- describe methods of controlling them

---

## 1. TUBER CROPS

**Tuber:** A swollen underground part of a plant in which food is stored.

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Root tubers** | Swollen **roots** that store food | **Sweet potatoes, cassava, carrots, yams (some), beetroot** |
| **Stem tubers** | Swollen **underground stems** with buds ("eyes") | **Irish potatoes**, some yams |

**How to tell them apart:** a **stem tuber has buds or "eyes"** from which new shoots grow;
a root tuber has no buds — it grows from a vine cutting or from the crown.

---

## 2. GROWING TUBER CROPS

| Crop | How it is planted | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweet potatoes** | **Vine cuttings** planted on ridges or mounds | Ready in 3–4 months |
| **Cassava** | **Stem cuttings** planted at a slant on ridges | Ready in 9–18 months; drought resistant |
| **Irish potatoes** | Whole **seed tubers with eyes** planted in furrows | Needs a cool climate — Kabale, Mt Elgon |
| **Carrots** | **Seeds** sown in well-worked loose soil | Needs deep, loose loam soil |

### Steps in growing a tuber crop

1. **Choose a suitable site** — fertile, well-drained land.
2. **Prepare the land** — clear, dig and make **ridges or mounds** so the tubers have loose soil and good drainage.
3. Select **healthy, clean planting materials** from a disease-free garden.
4. **Plant at the right spacing** and at the start of the rains.
5. **Weed** regularly, especially in the first weeks.
6. **Earth up** (heap soil around the plants) to cover the tubers and stop weevils.
7. **Apply manure** and practise **crop rotation**.
8. **Control pests and diseases** early.
9. **Harvest** at the right time, and **store** properly.

---

## 3. PESTS AND DISEASES OF TUBER CROPS

| Pest or disease | Crop | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweet potato weevil** | Sweet potatoes | Bores into the tubers, which become bitter and rot |
| **Moles and rats** | All tubers | Eat and destroy the tubers underground |
| **Termites** | Cassava, potatoes | Eat the stems and roots |
| **Aphids and whiteflies** | Cassava | Suck sap and **spread virus diseases** |
| **Potato blight** | Irish potatoes | Leaves turn black and the plant dies |
| **Cassava mosaic** | Cassava | Leaves become yellow, twisted and small; yields fall |
| **Cassava brown streak** | Cassava | The tuber rots inside with brown streaks |
| **Bacterial wilt** | Irish potatoes | The plant wilts suddenly and dies |
| **Nematodes (eelworms)** | Potatoes and carrots | Galls on roots, stunted plants |

---

## 4. CONTROLLING PESTS AND DISEASES

- Plant **clean, certified or disease-free** planting materials.
- Plant **resistant varieties** — such as mosaic-resistant cassava (NASE varieties).
- Practise **crop rotation** — do not plant the same crop in the same place every season.
- **Earth up** and keep the tubers covered.
- **Weed** and keep the garden clean; remove and burn **diseased plants** (roguing).
- **Harvest in time** — leaving tubers too long invites weevils and rots.
- Use **traps** for rats and moles; use **ash and other local remedies**.
- Use **pesticides correctly**, following instructions and observing the waiting period.
- Practise **field hygiene** — clean tools, and do not carry cuttings from a diseased garden.
- Get advice from the **agricultural extension officer**.

---

## 5. HARVESTING AND STORING

**Harvesting:** dig carefully with a hoe or fork so the tubers are not cut or bruised;
harvest sweet potatoes **piecemeal** as needed; harvest cassava when mature; lift Irish
potatoes when the leaves turn yellow and dry.

**Storing:** keep tubers in a **cool, dry, dark, airy** place; sort out damaged ones first;
store Irish potatoes in **crates or on racks**, not in heaps; cassava may be **dried and
made into flour**; sweet potatoes may be **sliced and dried**; protect the store from rats.

**Uses of tuber crops:** food rich in **carbohydrates** · income when sold · animal feed
(peelings and vines) · raw material for **flour, starch and chips** · planting material for
the next season.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Root tubers:** sweet potatoes, cassava, carrots. **Stem tuber:** Irish potatoes.
- A **stem tuber has eyes (buds)**; a root tuber does not.
- Sweet potatoes are planted from **vine cuttings**; cassava from **stem cuttings**; Irish potatoes from **seed tubers**.
- Tubers are planted on **ridges or mounds** for loose soil and good drainage.
- The **sweet potato weevil** is the worst pest of sweet potatoes; **earthing up** and timely harvesting control it.
- **Cassava mosaic** is spread by **whiteflies**; use **resistant varieties**.
- Irish potatoes suffer from **blight** and **bacterial wilt**.
- Store tubers in a **cool, dry, airy, dark** place.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. A tuber is a swollen underground part of a plant in which food is stored. **Root tubers** — sweet potatoes and cassava; **stem tubers** — Irish potatoes and some yams.
2. A **stem tuber has buds or "eyes"** from which new shoots grow; a **root tuber has no eyes** and is grown from cuttings or the crown.
3. Sweet potatoes — **vine cuttings**; cassava — **stem cuttings**; Irish potatoes — **seed tubers with eyes**; carrots — **seeds**.
4. Choose a suitable site → prepare the land into ridges or mounds → select clean planting materials → plant at the right spacing at the start of the rains → weed regularly → earth up → apply manure and rotate crops → control pests and diseases → harvest and store properly.
5. Because ridges and mounds give **loose, deep soil** for the tubers to swell in, and they **drain well** so the tubers do not rot.
6. Earthing up is **heaping soil around the base of the plants**. It covers the tubers so they do not become green or be attacked by weevils, supports the plant and controls weeds.
7. Any five: **sweet potato weevil** — bores into tubers, making them bitter; **moles and rats** — eat tubers underground; **termites** — eat stems and roots; **aphids and whiteflies** — suck sap and spread viruses; **nematodes** — cause galls and stunted plants.
8. Any four: **cassava mosaic** — yellow, twisted, small leaves; **cassava brown streak** — brown rot inside the tuber; **potato blight** — leaves turn black and the plant dies; **bacterial wilt** — sudden wilting and death.
9. The **whitefly**.
10. Any six of: plant clean, disease-free materials; use resistant varieties; practise crop rotation; earth up; weed and keep the garden clean; remove and burn diseased plants; harvest in time; trap rats and moles; use pesticides correctly; seek advice from the extension officer.
11. Because **diseases such as cassava mosaic and brown streak are carried in the cuttings themselves**. Planting infected material spreads the disease to the whole garden, however well the farmer weeds or sprays.
12. Any four of: dig carefully with a hoe or fork so the tubers are not cut or bruised; harvest when mature; harvest sweet potatoes piecemeal as needed; lift Irish potatoes when the leaves turn yellow; avoid harvesting in very wet conditions; keep damaged tubers separate.
13. Any five of: keep them in a cool, dry, dark, airy place; sort out damaged ones first; store Irish potatoes in crates or on racks, not heaps; dry cassava and make flour; slice and dry sweet potatoes; protect the store from rats and termites.
14. Any five of: food rich in carbohydrates; income when sold; animal feed from peelings and vines; raw material for flour, starch and chips; planting material for the next season.
15. **Irish potatoes** grow best in cool areas such as Kabale; **cassava** resists drought best.
