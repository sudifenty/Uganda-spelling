# SUB-TOPIC 6A: NATIONALITIES

## 1. WHAT CULTURE AND NATIONALITY MEAN

**Culture:** the way of life of a group of people — their language, food, dress, music,
dances, greetings, beliefs and customs.

**Nationality:** the country a person belongs to.

## 2. COUNTRIES AND THEIR PEOPLE

| Country | The people | Country | The people |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uganda | **Ugandans** | Germany | **Germans** |
| Kenya | **Kenyans** | France | **The French** |
| Tanzania | **Tanzanians** | England | **The English** |
| Rwanda | **Rwandese** (Rwandans) | Scotland | **The Scottish** (Scots) |
| Sudan | **Sudanese** | America | **Americans** |
| Egypt | **Egyptians** | Brazil | **Brazilians** |
| Nigeria | **Nigerians** | China | **Chinese** |
| Congo | **Congolese** | India | **Indians** |
| Spain | **Spaniards** | Japan | **Japanese** |

> **Newer than the syllabus:** the syllabus was written in **2010**, before **South Sudan
> became an independent country in July 2011**. Today a person may be **Sudanese** (from
> Sudan) or **South Sudanese** (from South Sudan).

## 3. GRAMMAR: SAYING WHERE PEOPLE COME FROM

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| I am … | **I am Ugandan.** / **I am a Ugandan.** |
| … not a … but … | He is **not a** Ugandan **but** a Kenyan. |
| … come from … | **Egyptians come from Egypt.** |
| … lives in … | A **Tanzanian lives in** Tanzania. |

> **A note on one printed example:** the syllabus prints *"I am a Chinese."* In good English
> we say **"I am Chinese"** or **"I am a Chinese person."** The words *Chinese, Japanese,
> Sudanese, Congolese* and *Rwandese* are used as **adjectives**, so they do not take *a* on
> their own. The syllabus is quoted here so you know what it says, but the corrected forms
> are the ones to use.

## 4. GRAMMAR: ADJECTIVES AND PREPOSITIONS

An **adjective** describes a noun. Words of nationality are often adjectives.

```
a Ugandan teacher       a Kenyan song       Chinese food       French bread
```

**Order of adjectives:** opinion → size → age → colour → origin → material.

```
a beautiful long red Ugandan cloth
```

Common **prepositions** used with places and people:

| Preposition | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **from** | Where a person comes from | She comes **from** Rwanda. |
| **in** | Inside a country or town | They live **in** Kampala. |
| **at** | An exact place | We met **at** the border. |
| **to** | Movement | He travelled **to** Tanzania. |
| **between** | Two things | Uganda lies **between** Kenya and the Congo. |
| **among** | More than two | Kiswahili is spoken **among** East Africans. |

## 6. GRAMMAR: USING "BECAUSE"

**Because** gives the **reason**.

```
She speaks Lumasaba because she comes from Bugisu.
He speaks Kinyarwanda because he was born in Rwanda.
We learn English because it is our official language.
```

## 7. GRAMMAR: THE PASSIVE VOICE

In the **active voice** the doer comes first. In the **passive voice** the thing done comes
first.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| People in England speak English. | **English is spoken in England.** |
| The Baganda speak Luganda. | **Luganda is spoken by the Baganda.** |
| They sang a Kenyan song. | **A Kenyan song was sung.** |
| Farmers grow coffee in Uganda. | **Coffee is grown in Uganda.** |

**How to change to the passive:**

```
1. Put the object first.
2. Use is / are (present) or was / were (past) with the past participle.
3. Add "by …" only if the doer matters.
```

## 8. GRAMMAR: IF … WOULD (THE SECOND CONDITIONAL)

This is used for something **imagined**, not real.

```
if + past tense  ,  would + plain verb
```

| Example |
|---|
| **If I were** a Nigerian, I **would** speak Igbo. |
| Muyobo **would** speak Spanish **if she were** from Spain. |
| **If** we **lived** in Rwanda, we **would** learn Kinyarwanda. |

> We say **"If I were"**, not *"If I was"*, in this kind of imagined sentence.

## 9. GRAMMAR: THE "AS … SO …" COMPARISON

```
Kinyarwanda is to the Rwandese as Lingala is to the Congolese.
Luganda is to the Baganda as Ateso is to the Iteso.
```

This shows that two pairs match in the same way.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Culture | The whole way of life of a group of people |
| Nationality | The country a person belongs to |
| Custom | Something a people has done for a long time |
| Adjective | A word that describes a noun |
| Preposition | A word showing position or relation, such as *in*, *from* |
| Active voice | The doer of the action comes first |
| Passive voice | The thing acted upon comes first |
| Second conditional | An *if … would* sentence about something imagined |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Chinese, Japanese, Sudanese, Congolese, Rwandese** stay the same in the plural.
- We say *the French*, *the English*, *the Scottish* — not *Frenches* or *Englishes*.
- **Because** gives a reason.
- The passive uses **is / are / was / were + past participle**.
- The second conditional is **if + past tense, would + plain verb**.
- Use **"If I were"**, not *if I was*, for something imagined.
- **Kiswahili** is an official language of Uganda alongside **English**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- He is **not a** Ugandan **but** a Kenyan.
- **Egyptians come from Egypt.**
- She speaks Lumasaba **because** she comes from Bugisu.
- *People in England speak English* becomes **English is spoken in England**.
- **If I were** a Nigerian, I **would** speak Igbo.
- Kinyarwanda is to the Rwandese **as** Lingala is to the Congolese.
- The language of the Iteso is **Ateso**.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing *Chineses* or *Japaneses* — these words do not add **-s**.
- Saying *"I am a Chinese"* instead of **"I am Chinese"**.
- Forgetting *is* or *are* in a passive sentence.
- Writing *"If I was a Nigerian, I will be…"* — it is **were … would**.
- Confusing the **people** (Iteso) with their **language** (Ateso).
- Using *because* twice in one sentence: *Because she is Ugandan, so she speaks Luganda.*
