*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Seven Curriculum, Set One, Integrated Science Topic 7,

**What you already know:** You know that plants and animals live around us. In P.7 you
learn how every living and non-living thing **depends on the others**, and how
**agroforestry** helps people to farm and keep trees at the same time.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- name the components of the environment
- explain how plants and animals depend on each other
- explain how living things depend on non-living things and the other way round
- describe agroforestry, its types and its importance
- describe how trees are cared for and harvested properly
- explain how to manage a school or home wood project

---

## 1. COMPONENTS OF THE ENVIRONMENT

### Definitions

**Environment:** Everything that surrounds a living thing — living and non-living.

**Interdependence:** The way living and non-living things depend on one another for survival.

| Component | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Living (biotic)** | Things that have life | Plants, animals, human beings, bacteria, fungi |
| **Non-living (abiotic)** | Things that have no life | Air, water, soil, sunlight, rocks, temperature |

---

## 2. INTERDEPENDENCE OF PLANTS AND ANIMALS

### How animals depend on plants

- Plants give animals **food** (fruits, leaves, grass, grains, roots).
- Plants give out **oxygen** for breathing.
- Plants provide **shelter and habitat** — nests, hiding places, shade.
- Plants provide **materials** for building nests and homes.
- Plants provide **medicine** for people and some animals.

### How animals depend on other animals

- Some animals **eat other animals** — a lion eats a zebra (predator and prey).
- Animals get **protection** from others (a hen protects her chicks).
- Some animals **clean** others, for example the oxpecker bird eating ticks off a cow.
- People keep animals for **food, transport and work**.
- Some animals depend on others for **transport** of seeds and germs.

### How plants depend on animals

- Animals give out **carbon dioxide**, which plants use in photosynthesis.
- Animal droppings and dead bodies **make the soil fertile** (manure).
- Animals **pollinate** flowers — bees, butterflies and birds.
- Animals **disperse seeds** by carrying and dropping them.
- People **plant, weed, water and protect** crops and trees.
- Earthworms and burrowing animals **aerate** the soil.

### How plants depend on other plants

- Big trees give **shade** to small plants and shelter them from wind and heavy rain.
- Climbing plants use others for **support**.
- Fallen leaves rot and form **humus** that feeds other plants.
- Some plants live on others (parasites, such as dodder), and some, like beans and other
  legumes, **add nitrogen to the soil** for other plants.

### Food chains and food webs

**Food chain:** The path along which food energy passes from one living thing to another.

*Example:* grass → grasshopper → lizard → hawk

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Producer** | A green plant that makes its own food |
| **Consumer** | An animal that eats plants or other animals |
| **Herbivore** | An animal that eats only plants |
| **Carnivore** | An animal that eats only meat |
| **Omnivore** | An animal that eats both plants and meat |
| **Decomposer** | Bacteria and fungi that rot dead matter and return nutrients to the soil |

**Food web:** Many food chains joined together, showing that most animals eat more than one kind of food.

## 3. INTERDEPENDENCE OF LIVING AND NON-LIVING THINGS

### How animals depend on non-living things

- **Air** — for oxygen to breathe.
- **Water** — for drinking, cooling the body and as a home for fish.
- **Soil** — a home for worms and ants; it holds the plants they eat.
- **Sunlight** — for warmth and vitamin D.

### How plants depend on non-living things

- **Soil** — for anchorage, water and mineral salts.
- **Water** — for photosynthesis and transport of food.
- **Air** — carbon dioxide for photosynthesis and oxygen for respiration.
- **Sunlight** — the energy for photosynthesis.

### How non-living things benefit from living things

- Dead plants and animals **add humus** and make the soil fertile.
- Plant roots **hold the soil** and prevent erosion.
- Plants **release oxygen and water vapour** into the air, purifying it.
- Plants and animals help form **rocks and fossils** over a very long time.
- Wetland plants **filter and clean water**.

## 4. AGROFORESTRY

### Definition

**Agroforestry:** The growing of trees together with crops and/or animals on the same piece of land.

### Types of agroforestry

| Type | What is done together |
|---|---|
| **Agri-silviculture** | Growing **crops and trees** together |
| **Silvo-pastoral** | Rearing **animals and growing trees** on the same farm |
| **Agro-silvo-pastoral** | Growing **crops, rearing animals and growing trees** all on the same farm |

### Importance of agroforestry

- Trees provide **firewood, timber, poles and charcoal** without clearing forests.
- Trees give **shade** and act as **windbreaks** for crops and animals.
- Fallen leaves make **manure** and improve the soil.
- Tree roots **control soil erosion** and hold water.
- Some trees (leguminous, such as calliandra, sesbania and leucaena) **add nitrogen** to the soil.
- Trees provide **fodder** for animals and **fruits and medicine** for people.
- They provide **extra income** from fruits, timber and honey.
- They **attract rainfall** and keep the environment cool.
- Land is used **more productively** — the farmer gets crops, animals and trees from the same land.

### Care for trees in agroforestry

Water young seedlings · weed and mulch around them · **prune** the branches so that they
do not shade the crops too much · protect them from animals using fences or tree guards ·
apply manure · spray or remove pests and diseased parts · thin out where they are too close
together.

### Proper harvesting of trees

| Method | What is done | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Pollarding** | Cutting off the top branches of a tree, leaving the trunk standing | New shoots grow from the top; gives firewood and lets light reach crops |
| **Coppicing** | Cutting the tree close to the ground | New shoots grow from the stump, giving poles and firewood again |
| **Lopping** | Cutting off selected side branches | Gives firewood and fodder while the tree keeps growing |
| **Pruning** | Removing lower or unwanted branches | Improves growth and reduces shading |
| **Thinning** | Removing some whole trees from a crowded stand | Leaves room for the rest to grow big |

**Important:** never clear-cut all the trees at once. Harvest a few at a time and always
**replant**.

## 5. MANAGING A SCHOOL OR HOME WOOD PROJECT

### Steps

1. **Choose a suitable site** — good soil, near water if possible, and safe from animals.
2. **Prepare a nursery bed** — fine soil, shade and regular watering; sow the seeds.
3. **Prepare the land** — clear, dig and mark planting holes at the right **spacing**.
4. **Transplant** the seedlings, preferably at the start of the rains and in the evening.
5. **Water and mulch** the young trees.
6. **Weed** around them regularly.
7. **Protect** them from animals, fire and people using a fence or a live hedge.
8. **Prune and thin** as the trees grow.
9. **Control pests and diseases.**
10. **Keep records** of the number planted, the dates and the costs.
11. **Harvest properly** — pollarding, coppicing or lopping — and **replant** what is cut.

### Importance of a school wood project

It supplies firewood and poles for the school · it teaches learners practical skills ·
it earns the school money · it beautifies the compound and gives shade · it protects the
soil · it teaches learners to care for the environment.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The environment has **living (biotic)** and **non-living (abiotic)** components.
- A food chain **always begins with a producer** (a green plant).
- Animals give plants **carbon dioxide** and manure; plants give animals **oxygen** and food.
- **Decomposers** are bacteria and fungi.
- Three types of agroforestry: **agri-silviculture, silvo-pastoral, agro-silvo-pastoral**.
- **Pollarding** = cut the top; **coppicing** = cut near the ground; **lopping** = cut side branches.
- Leguminous trees add **nitrogen** to the soil.
- Always **replant** after harvesting trees.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Living things in the environment — biotic components.
- The first organism in every food chain — a green plant (producer).
- Organism that breaks down dead matter — a decomposer.
- Growing crops and trees on the same land — agri-silviculture.
- Cutting the top branches of a tree — pollarding.
- Two gases exchanged between plants and animals — oxygen and carbon dioxide.
- One importance of agroforestry — it controls soil erosion (or provides firewood without clearing forests).
