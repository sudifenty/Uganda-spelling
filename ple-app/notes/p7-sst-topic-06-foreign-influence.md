## 1. THE FOREIGN GROUPS THAT CAME TO AFRICA

| Group | Where they came from | Why they came |
|---|---|---|
| **Arab traders** | Arabia (Asia) | Trade in ivory, gold and slaves; to spread Islam |
| **Persian (Shirazi) traders** | Persia (Iran) | Trade along the East African coast; some settled |
| **Indian traders** | India | Trade in cloth, beads and metal goods; later shop keeping |
| **European explorers** | Europe | To find the sources of rivers, map the interior and satisfy curiosity |
| **European traders** | Europe | To buy raw materials and sell manufactured goods |
| **Missionaries** | Europe | To spread Christianity, stop the slave trade, start schools and hospitals |
| **Colonialists / administrators** | Europe | To take control of African land and rule it |
| **Settlers** | Europe | To live permanently in Africa and own farms |

### Important Points

- Arabs reached North Africa and the East African coast long before the Europeans.
- The **Portuguese** were the first Europeans to sail round Africa, looking for a sea route to India.
- The coming of foreigners changed Africa's politics, economy, religion, language and culture for ever.

---

## 2. EUROPEAN EXPLORERS

### Definition

**Explorer:** A person who travels to an unknown place in order to find out about it.

### Some famous explorers

| Explorer | What he is known for |
|---|---|
| **Prince Henry the Navigator** (Portugal) | Started a school of navigation and sent out sailors to explore the African coast |
| **Bartholomew Diaz** (Portugal) | Reached the southern tip of Africa — the Cape of Good Hope, in 1488 |
| **Vasco da Gama** (Portugal) | Sailed round Africa and reached India in 1498, passing the East African coast |
| **Mungo Park** (Scotland) | Explored the River Niger in West Africa |
| **The Lander brothers** (Britain) | Followed the River Niger to its mouth |
| **Dr David Livingstone** (Scotland) | Explored Central Africa, saw Victoria Falls, fought against the slave trade |
| **H. M. Stanley** (Britain/America) | Searched for Livingstone; explored the Congo; wrote to invite missionaries to Buganda |
| **Speke and Grant** (Britain) | Reached the source of the Nile at Jinja in 1862 |

### Reasons for the coming of European explorers

- To **find the sources of the great rivers** — the Nile, the Niger and the Congo.
- To **find a sea route to India** for the spice trade.
- To **map** the interior of Africa, which Europeans called "the dark continent".
- To look for **raw materials and markets** for their industries.
- To find land for **settlement**.
- Because of the **spirit of adventure** and the wish to become famous.
- To **spread Christianity** and to see how the slave trade could be stopped.
- Some were sent by their **governments and geographical societies**.

### Problems faced by European explorers

- **Tropical diseases** — malaria, sleeping sickness, yellow fever.
- **Hostile tribes** who attacked them.
- **Wild animals**.
- **Difficult terrain** — thick forests, swamps, rapids, mountains and deserts.
- **Language barriers**.
- **Shortage of food and clean water**.
- **Poor transport** — they walked or used porters and canoes.
- **Desertion** by their porters and guides.
- **Harsh climate** — too hot, too wet or too dry.

### Effects of European explorers

- Africa's interior was **mapped** and made known to Europe.
- Their reports **attracted missionaries, traders and colonialists**.
- They exposed the horrors of the **slave trade**, which helped bring it to an end.
- Some **routes and stations** they opened later became towns.
- Their reports increased European interest in African land, leading to the **scramble for Africa**.

## 3. EUROPEAN TRADERS AND THE TRIANGULAR TRADE

### Definition

**Triangular (Trans-Atlantic) Trade:** The three-sided trade between Europe, West Africa and the Americas, in which enslaved Africans were carried across the Atlantic Ocean.

### How the triangle worked

1. **Europe → Africa:** guns, gunpowder, cloth, beads, mirrors, alcohol and metal goods were brought to the West African coast.
2. **Africa → America:** enslaved Africans were carried across the Atlantic in terrible conditions — this crossing was called the **Middle Passage**.
3. **America → Europe:** sugar, cotton, tobacco, coffee and rum from the plantations were taken back to Europe.

### Reasons why traders came to Africa

- To get **raw materials** — ivory, gold, palm oil, rubber, timber.
- To get **cheap labour** in the form of slaves for the plantations of America and the West Indies.
- To find **markets** for goods made in European factories.
- To make **profit**.
- Because of the **industrial revolution** in Europe, which needed both raw materials and markets.

### Effects of European traders and the slave trade

**Negative:**
- Millions of strong young Africans were taken away — **loss of manpower**.
- **Depopulation** of many areas of West and Central Africa.
- **Wars and raids** between African communities as they hunted for captives to sell.
- **Insecurity**, famine and destruction of villages.
- **Guns** spread and increased violence.
- Some African industries died because of **imported goods**.

**Positive:**
- New **goods and crops** were introduced — maize, cassava, sweet potatoes.
- Some **coastal towns** grew into trading centres.
- New **trade routes** were opened.

### The end of the slave trade

Britain abolished the slave trade in **1807** and slavery in its empire in **1833**. The work of people such as **William Wilberforce** and **Dr David Livingstone**, the growth of industry which needed workers in Africa rather than in America, and pressure from missionaries all helped to bring it to an end.

## 4. MISSIONARIES IN AFRICA

### Why missionaries came

- To **spread Christianity** and win converts.
- To **stop the slave trade**, which they said was against God's will.
- To start **schools** and teach reading, writing and new skills.
- To start **hospitals** and treat the sick.
- To teach new **farming methods** and crafts.
- Some were invited — for example, **Kabaka Muteesa I** of Buganda, after Stanley's letter of 1875.

### Effects of missionary work

- **Christianity** spread and many churches were built.
- **Formal schooling** began; reading and writing spread.
- **Hospitals and health services** were started.
- African **languages were written down** and the Bible translated.
- New **crops and skills** were introduced.
- Some **African customs** were discouraged and weakened.
- Religious **divisions** appeared in some communities.
- Missionaries prepared the way for **colonial rule**.

## 5. COLONIALISTS: THE SCRAMBLE AND PARTITION OF AFRICA

### Definitions

**Colony:** A country or area ruled by a foreign power.

**The Scramble for Africa:** The rush by European powers to take control of parts of Africa in the late 1800s.

**Partition:** The dividing up of Africa among the European powers.

### Causes of the Scramble and Partition

- The need for **raw materials** for European industries.
- The need for **markets** for European manufactured goods.
- The need for **land for settlement** because of population increase in Europe.
- The desire for **prestige** — a country with colonies was thought to be great.
- **Strategic reasons** — control of the Suez Canal and the source of the Nile.
- **Rivalry** among European powers, especially Britain, France and Germany.
- Reports from **explorers and missionaries** about Africa's wealth.
- To **stop the slave trade** and "civilise" Africans (the reason they gave publicly).

### The Berlin Conference (1884–1885)

European powers met in **Berlin, Germany**, called by **Otto von Bismarck**, to agree on how to share Africa peacefully among themselves. **No African was invited.** The conference agreed that a country claiming an area had to occupy it effectively, and it fixed borders using straight lines on the map.

| Colonial power | Some of its colonies |
|---|---|
| **Britain** | Uganda, Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana (Gold Coast), Egypt, South Africa, Zimbabwe |
| **France** | Senegal, Mali, Niger, Algeria, Ivory Coast, Chad |
| **Portugal** | Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau |
| **Belgium** | Congo (D.R. Congo), Rwanda, Burundi |
| **Germany** | Tanganyika, Namibia (South West Africa), Cameroon, Togo |
| **Italy** | Libya, Somalia, Eritrea |
| **Spain** | Western Sahara, Equatorial Guinea |

### Methods used to acquire colonies

- **Signing treaties** with African chiefs, who often did not understand what they signed.
- **Use of violence and force** — war and superior weapons.
- Taking advantage of **existing misunderstandings** between African groups and helping one side.
- Using **chartered companies** such as the Imperial British East Africa Company.
- **Gifts and bribes** to chiefs.
- **Divide and rule**.
- Buying land or claiming it as "protection".

### Methods used to administer the colonies

| Method | Meaning | Used mainly by |
|---|---|---|
| **Indirect rule** | Ruling through the existing African chiefs and traditional systems | The British |
| **Direct rule** | Ruling with their own European officers, replacing African leaders | The Germans, Belgians and Portuguese |
| **Assimilation** | Turning Africans into citizens of the mother country by making them adopt its language and culture | The French |

## 6. EUROPEAN SETTLERS AND THE GREAT TREK

### European settlers

Some Europeans came to stay. They took the best land, especially in **South Africa**, **Zimbabwe** (then Rhodesia) and the **White Highlands of Kenya**. The Dutch (later called **Boers** or Afrikaners) first settled at the Cape in **1652** under Jan van Riebeeck. The British took over the Cape in the early 1800s.

### The Great Trek

**The Great Trek** was the movement of the Boers from the Cape Colony into the interior of South Africa from about **1835**.

**Causes of the Great Trek:**

- The Boers disliked **British rule** at the Cape.
- The British **abolished slavery in 1833**, and the Boers depended on slave labour.
- The Boers were not happy with the **compensation** paid for their freed slaves.
- **English** was made the official language instead of Dutch.
- The Boers wanted **more land** for farming and grazing.
- British **laws and courts** treated Africans and Boers as equals, which the Boers rejected.
- The Boers wanted **freedom to rule themselves**.

**Effects of the Great Trek:**

- The Boers founded new republics — **Transvaal**, **Orange Free State** and **Natal**.
- **Wars with African peoples** followed, such as the Zulu (the Battle of Blood River, 1838); Africans lost land and lives.
- African communities were **displaced** and scattered.
- **Gold and diamonds** were later discovered in the Boer areas, attracting more Europeans.
- Conflict between the Boers and the British led to the **Anglo-Boer Wars**.
- The racist attitudes of the trekkers helped to lay the foundation of **apartheid**.

## 7. EFFECTS OF FOREIGN INFLUENCE ON AFRICA

### Political effects

- Africans **lost their independence** and were ruled by foreigners.
- Traditional leaders and kingdoms **lost their power**.
- **New boundaries** were drawn that split tribes and joined enemies — the cause of many later conflicts.
- New systems of **administration, courts and laws** were introduced.
- **Apartheid** (racial separation) was introduced in South Africa.

### Economic effects

- **Exploitation of Africa's raw materials** — minerals, timber, cash crops taken to Europe.
- **Infrastructure** was developed — roads, railways, ports and towns, mainly to move raw materials.
- **New crops** were introduced — coffee, tea, cotton, cocoa, sisal.
- **Money economy and taxation** were introduced; Africans had to work for wages.
- Africans lost fertile **land** to settlers.
- African **local industries and crafts** declined.

### Social effects

- **Christianity and Islam** were introduced and spread.
- **Formal schooling** began; hospitals were built.
- **African culture was distorted** — dress, names, music, marriage customs changed.
- **Foreign languages** were introduced: English, French, Portuguese, Spanish, German.
- New languages such as **Kiswahili** developed and spread.
- **Intermarriages** took place.
- New diseases spread, but medicine also improved.

## 8. HOW AFRICANS REACTED TO COLONIAL RULE

### Collaboration

Some African leaders **cooperated** with the Europeans, hoping to gain guns, protection against enemies, trade goods or a stronger position. Examples include Kabaka Muteesa I and Semei Kakungulu in Uganda, and Lenana of the Maasai.

### Resistance

Others **fought back**:

- The **Maji Maji Rebellion** in Tanganyika (against the Germans).
- The **Nandi Resistance** in Kenya under Koitalel.
- **Samouri Toure** of the Mandinka empire (against the French in West Africa).
- The **Zulu** under Cetshwayo in South Africa.
- **Ethiopia** defeated Italy at the **Battle of Adowa in 1896** and kept its independence.
- **Kabalega** of Bunyoro and **Mwanga** of Buganda in Uganda.

Most resistances failed because Africans had **weaker weapons**, were **disunited**, and suffered from famine and disease. Ethiopia succeeded because it was united, well armed and led by a strong emperor, **Menelik II**.

- **Bartholomew Diaz** reached the Cape of Good Hope in **1488**; **Vasco da Gama** reached India in **1498**.
- **Mungo Park** explored the **River Niger**; **Livingstone** explored Central Africa and fought the slave trade.
- **Speke and Grant** reached the **source of the Nile at Jinja** in 1862.
- The **Berlin Conference (1884–85)** partitioned Africa; it was called by **Bismarck**; no African attended.
- **Britain — indirect rule. France — assimilation. Germany, Belgium, Portugal — direct rule.**
- The **Great Trek** began in **1835** after Britain abolished slavery in **1833**.
- **Ethiopia** defeated Italy at **Adowa (1896)** and was never colonised.
- The **Triangular Trade**: guns and cloth to Africa, slaves to America, sugar and cotton to Europe.
