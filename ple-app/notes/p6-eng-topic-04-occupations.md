**What you already know:** You know the work different people in your community do —
the farmer, the teacher, the trader. In P.6 you learn what an **occupation** is,
the main occupations of Uganda with what each provides, and how people choose one.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- say what an occupation is and give examples
- describe the main types of occupations and their importance
- say the factors to consider when choosing an occupation

## MEANING OF AN OCCUPATION

An **occupation** is the work that a person does regularly to earn a living or
provide a service.

Examples of occupations include:

- Farming
- Teaching
- Fishing
- Trading
- Carpentry
- Nursing
- Driving
- Building
- Tailoring
- Banking

## TYPES OF OCCUPATIONS

### 1. Farming

**Farming** is the growing of crops and keeping of animals for food, raw materials
and income.

Examples of farmers:

- Crop farmers
- Poultry farmers
- Dairy farmers
- Fish farmers
- Pig farmers

Importance of farming:

- Provides food.
- Provides employment.
- Provides raw materials for industries.
- Provides income.
- Provides goods for export.
- Supports other businesses.

### 2. Fishing

**Fishing** is the catching or rearing of fish for food and income.

Importance of fishing:

- Provides food.
- Provides employment.
- Provides income.
- Provides fish for export.
- Provides raw materials for some industries.

### 3. Trading

**Trading** is the buying and selling of goods and services.

Examples of traders:

- Shopkeepers
- Wholesalers
- Retailers
- Market vendors

Importance of trading:

- Provides goods and services.
- Creates employment.
- Provides income.
- Connects producers with consumers.
- Generates government revenue through taxes.

### 4. Teaching

**Teaching** is the process of helping people acquire knowledge, skills and good
behaviour.

A person who teaches is called a **teacher**.

Importance of teaching:

- Provides education.
- Develops people's skills.
- Prepares people for different occupations.
- Promotes good behaviour.
- Contributes to national development.

### 5. Medicine and Nursing

A **doctor** is a trained person who diagnoses and treats diseases.

A **nurse** is a trained health worker who cares for sick and injured people.

Importance:

- Treats sick people.
- Prevents diseases.
- Saves lives.
- Promotes good health.
- Provides health education.

### 6. Carpentry

**Carpentry** is the occupation of making and repairing things mainly from wood.

A person who does carpentry is called a **carpenter**.

Examples of products made by carpenters:

- Chairs
- Tables
- Beds
- Doors
- Cupboards

### 7. Building

**Building** is the occupation of constructing houses and other structures.

People involved may include:

- Builders
- Bricklayers
- Plumbers
- Electricians
- Engineers
- Architects

Importance:

- Provides houses and other buildings.
- Creates employment.
- Provides income.
- Supports economic development.

### 8. Transport

**Transport** involves moving people and goods from one place to another.

Examples of transport occupations:

- Driver
- Pilot
- Sailor
- Mechanic
- Motorcycle rider

## FACTORS TO CONSIDER WHEN CHOOSING AN OCCUPATION

A person may consider:

1. Ability and talent.
2. Level of education.
3. Personal interests.
4. Skills.
5. Availability of employment.
6. Income expected.
7. Working conditions.
8. Availability of training.
9. Demand for the occupation.
10. Health and physical ability.

## IMPORTANCE OF OCCUPATIONS

Occupations are important because they:

- Provide income.
- Provide employment.
- Provide goods and services.
- Improve people's standards of living.
- Promote economic development.
- Help people meet their basic needs.
- Generate government revenue.
- Promote self-reliance.
- Develop people's skills.
- Contribute to national development.

## IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- An occupation is done **regularly** — to **earn a living** or **provide a service**.
- Farmers come in types: **crop, poultry, dairy, fish and pig** farmers.
- Traders include **shopkeepers, wholesalers, retailers and market vendors**.
- Carpenters make things **mainly from wood**: chairs, tables, beds, doors, cupboards.
- Building brings together **builders, bricklayers, plumbers, electricians, engineers and architects**.
- Transport occupations include **driver, pilot, sailor, mechanic and motorcycle rider**.
- When choosing an occupation, consider **ability, education, interests, skills, employment, income, working conditions, training, demand and health**.
- All occupations **provide income and employment, goods and services**, and help **economic and national development**.
