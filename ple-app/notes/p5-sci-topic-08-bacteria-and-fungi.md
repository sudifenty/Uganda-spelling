## 1. BACTERIA

**Bacteria:** Very tiny living things (micro-organisms) which can only be seen with a **microscope**.

### Characteristics of bacteria

They are **very small** — invisible to the naked eye · they are **living things** that feed,
grow and **multiply very fast**, by splitting into two · they have **different shapes** —
round (cocci), rod-shaped (bacilli), spiral and comma-shaped · most need **warmth, moisture,
food and darkness** to grow · some need air, others do not · some form **spores** that
survive drying and heat.

### Where they are found and where they breed

**Everywhere** — in the air, water, soil, food, on the skin, in the mouth and inside the
intestines. They breed best in **warm, moist, dirty places**: rubbish heaps, stagnant water,
uncovered food, dirty latrines, wounds, and the human body itself.

### Useful bacteria

| Use | How |
|---|---|
| **Making food** | Turning milk into **yoghurt, sour milk (bongo) and cheese** |
| **Decay** | Rotting dead plants and animals, returning nutrients to the soil |
| **Making manure and compost** | Breaking down rubbish and dung |
| **Soil fertility** | **Nitrogen-fixing bacteria** in the root nodules of legumes |
| **Digestion** | Bacteria in the intestines help digestion and make vitamins |
| **Industry and medicine** | Making vinegar, some medicines and **antibiotics**; treating sewage |
| **Biogas** | Breaking down waste to produce gas |

### Harmful bacteria and the diseases they cause

| Disease | How it is spread |
|---|---|
| **Cholera** | Dirty water and food |
| **Typhoid** | Contaminated food and water |
| **Dysentery** (bacillary) | Dirty hands, food and flies |
| **Tuberculosis (TB)** | Coughing and sneezing |
| **Whooping cough, diphtheria, tetanus** | Air, or through wounds (tetanus) |
| **Pneumonia** | Air-borne droplets |
| **Boils, wound infections** | Dirt entering broken skin |
| **Food poisoning** | Eating food in which bacteria have multiplied |

## 2. FUNGI

**Fungi:** Living things that have **no chlorophyll**, so they cannot make their own food; they feed on dead or living matter. (One is a **fungus**; many are **fungi**.)

**Examples:** mushrooms · **yeast** · moulds on bread, fruit and leather · the fungi that
cause ringworm and athlete's foot.

### Characteristics of fungi

They have **no chlorophyll** and no true roots, stems or leaves · they reproduce by
**spores** · they grow in **warm, damp, dark places** · some are **saprophytes** (feeding on
dead matter) and some are **parasites** (feeding on living things).

### Useful (harmless) fungi

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| **Food** | Edible **mushrooms** |
| **Baking and brewing** | **Yeast** makes dough rise and is used in brewing |
| **Medicine** | **Penicillin**, an antibiotic made from a mould |
| **Decay** | Rotting dead matter and enriching the soil |
| **Cheese making** | Some moulds give cheese its flavour |

### Harmful fungi

| Harm | Example |
|---|---|
| **Human diseases** | **Ringworm**, **athlete's foot**, thrush, dandruff |
| **Crop diseases** | Potato **blight**, coffee leaf rust, groundnut rosette moulds, smut in maize |
| **Spoiling food and property** | Moulds on bread, fruit, leather, clothes and books |
| **Poisoning** | **Poisonous mushrooms**, which can kill; **aflatoxins** in badly dried groundnuts and maize |

## 3. PREVENTING AND CONTROLLING BACTERIAL AND FUNGAL DISEASES

| Way | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Personal hygiene** | Bathe daily, wash hands with soap, cut nails, wear clean dry clothes |
| **Food hygiene** | Wash, cover and cook food well; do not eat food that has gone bad |
| **Safe water** | Boil, filter or treat drinking water |
| **Proper waste disposal** | Use latrines, dig rubbish pits, drain stagnant water |
| **Keeping things dry** | Fungi need damp — dry between the toes, dry clothes in the sun, iron them |
| **Do not share** | Towels, combs, clothes, beddings or razor blades |
| **Sterilising** | Boil instruments; use antiseptic on wounds |
| **Immunisation** | Against TB, whooping cough, diphtheria and tetanus |
| **Treatment** | **Antibiotics** for bacterial diseases and **antifungal** medicines for fungal ones, prescribed by a health worker and **completed** |
| **Storing food dry** | Dry grain and groundnuts well to stop moulds and aflatoxins |

**A caution:** never buy or use antibiotics without advice, and always **finish the course**
given — stopping early makes the bacteria resistant and the medicine stops working.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bacteria | Very tiny living things seen only with a microscope |
| Micro-organism | A living thing too small to see with the naked eye |
| Fungi | Living things without chlorophyll that feed on dead or living matter |
| Spore | A tiny reproductive body of fungi and some bacteria |
| Saprophyte | An organism feeding on dead matter |
| Parasite | An organism feeding on a living host |
| Antibiotic | A medicine that kills or stops bacteria |
| Antiseptic | A substance that kills germs on the skin or a wound |
| Sterilising | Killing all germs, for example by boiling |
| Aflatoxin | A poison produced by moulds on badly dried food |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Bacteria are seen only with a **microscope** and multiply by **splitting into two**.
- They breed in **warm, moist, dirty** places.
- Useful bacteria make **yoghurt and sour milk**, cause **decay**, and **fix nitrogen** in legume nodules.
- Bacterial diseases: **cholera, typhoid, dysentery, TB, tetanus, whooping cough, pneumonia**.
- Fungi have **no chlorophyll** and reproduce by **spores**.
- Useful fungi: **mushrooms, yeast, penicillin**.
- Harmful fungi cause **ringworm, athlete's foot, blight** and mouldy food.
- **Antibiotics** treat bacterial diseases — always **complete the course**.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- The instrument used to see bacteria → the **microscope**.
- Bacteria that add nitrogen to the soil live in → the **root nodules of legumes**.
- The fungus used in baking → **yeast**.
- The medicine made from a mould → **penicillin**.
- Two skin diseases caused by fungi → **ringworm** and **athlete's foot**.
- One disease caused by bacteria → cholera (or typhoid, TB).
- Poison formed by moulds on badly dried groundnuts → **aflatoxin**.
- Why fungi grow between the toes → the place is **warm and damp**.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Bacteria are very tiny living things (micro-organisms) that can only be seen with a **microscope**.
2. Any five of: they are very small; they are living and multiply very fast by splitting into two; they have different shapes — round, rod, spiral, comma; most need warmth, moisture, food and darkness; some form spores; some need air and others do not.
3. They are found **everywhere** — in air, water, soil, food, on the skin and in the body. They breed best in **warm, moist, dirty places** such as rubbish heaps, stagnant water, uncovered food and dirty latrines.
4. Any five of: making yoghurt, sour milk and cheese; causing decay that returns nutrients to the soil; making manure and compost; fixing nitrogen in legume root nodules; helping digestion in the intestines; making vinegar, medicines and antibiotics; treating sewage; producing biogas.
5. Any six: **cholera** — dirty water and food; **typhoid** — contaminated food and water; **dysentery** — dirty hands and flies; **TB** — coughing and sneezing; **whooping cough and diphtheria** — air; **tetanus** — dirt in wounds; **pneumonia** — droplets in air.
6. Fungi are living things that have **no chlorophyll**, so they cannot make their own food, and feed on dead or living matter. Examples: mushrooms, yeast, moulds, the ringworm fungus.
7. Any four of: they have no chlorophyll; they have no true roots, stems or leaves; they reproduce by **spores**; they grow in warm, damp, dark places; some are saprophytes and some parasites.
8. A **saprophyte** feeds on **dead** matter, for example the mould on bread; a **parasite** feeds on a **living** host, for example the ringworm fungus on human skin.
9. Any four of: edible mushrooms as food; **yeast** for baking and brewing; **penicillin** and other medicines; causing decay that enriches the soil; flavouring cheese.
10. Any four of: they cause human diseases such as ringworm; they cause crop diseases such as blight; they spoil food, leather, clothes and books; some mushrooms are poisonous; moulds produce aflatoxins.
11. **Human:** ringworm and athlete's foot (also thrush). **Crops:** potato blight and coffee leaf rust (also maize smut).
12. Aflatoxins are **poisons produced by moulds** growing on badly dried food such as groundnuts and maize. They are prevented by **drying the produce properly**, storing it in a dry, airy place, and throwing away mouldy or discoloured grain.
13. Any eight of: bathe daily and wash hands with soap; wash, cover and cook food well; boil or treat drinking water; use latrines and dispose of rubbish properly; keep the body and clothes dry; do not share towels, combs or razor blades; sterilise instruments and use antiseptic on wounds; immunise children; take prescribed medicines correctly; dry and store food well.
14. Because stopping early leaves some bacteria **alive**. They then multiply and become **resistant**, so the same medicine no longer works and the illness returns and is harder to treat.
15. Because the fungi and their spores, and parasites such as jiggers and lice, live in **damp cloth**. The **sun's heat and the hot iron kill them**, so the clothes are safe to wear.

# P.5 QUICK REVISION

- **Bacteria** — need a **microscope**, multiply by splitting, breed in **warm, moist, dirty** places
- Useful: **yoghurt, decay, nitrogen fixing, digestion, antibiotics**
- Harmful: **cholera, typhoid, dysentery, TB, tetanus**
- **Fungi** — no chlorophyll, reproduce by **spores**, love warm damp dark places
- Useful fungi: **mushrooms, yeast, penicillin** · Harmful: **ringworm, athlete's foot, blight, aflatoxins**
- Prevent: hygiene · safe water · dry clothes · no sharing · immunisation · **finish the antibiotics**
