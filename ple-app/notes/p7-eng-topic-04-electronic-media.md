## SUB-TOPIC 4A: RADIO AND TELEVISION

### Vocabulary from the syllabus

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| programme | A show or item broadcast at a set time |
| channel | A station you tune to on radio or television |
| knob | The small round handle used to turn something on or adjust it |
| volume | The loudness of the sound |
| speaker | The part from which the sound comes out |
| aerial | The rod or wire that receives the signal |
| presenter | The person who introduces and runs a programme |
| studio | The room from which programmes are made or broadcast |
| advert | A short message paid for, telling people about goods or services |
| broadcast | To send out a programme by radio or television |
| talk show | A programme where people discuss a subject |
| line-up | The list of programmes to be shown or heard |
| forecast | A statement of what is expected, for example the weather |
| tune | To set a radio or television to a station |
| station | A broadcasting organisation, for example UBC or NBS |

### Kinds of programmes

News · sports · music (gospel, pop, traditional) · drama · documentaries · talk shows ·
farming programmes · children's programmes · religious programmes · adverts and
announcements · the weather forecast.

### Reading and writing media items

**An announcement** must answer: **what, who, when, where** and **who to contact**.

> *All P.7 candidates of Kigoowa Primary School are informed that briefing for the
> examinations will take place on Monday, 5th October 2026, at 9:00 a.m. in the school
> hall. For more information, contact the head teacher.*

**An advert** should have an attention-catching first line, the **name of the product or
service**, what makes it good, the **price or offer**, and **where to find it**.

**A news item** answers **who, what, when, where, why** and **how**, with the most
important fact first.

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **If + present simple + will/shall + verb (If 1)** | *If you tune in at five o'clock, you **will** hear the soccer news.* |
| **… and so …** | *Joan is a good presenter, **and so** is Amina.* |
| **I like …** | *I **like listening** to gospel music.* |
| **I don't like …** | *I **don't like** the new programme.* |

**Note on "and so":** the verb comes **before** the subject — *and so **is** Amina*, *and so
**do** I*, *and so **can** he*. Use *neither / nor* for negatives: *Joan does not like
adverts, and **neither does** Amina.*

### Grammar focus: If 1 and If 2

| Conditional | Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **If 1** (real, likely) | If + **present simple**, **will/shall** + verb | Something that can really happen | *If it **rains**, we **shall** watch television indoors.* |
| **If 2** (unreal, present) | If + **past simple**, **would/could** + verb | Something imagined or unlikely now | *If I **had** a television, I **would** watch the news.* |

More examples:

- *If you **press** that knob, the volume **will** increase.* (If 1)
- *If she **were** a presenter, she **would** run a talk show.* (If 2 — use *were* for all persons)
- *If I **knew** his station, I **would** tune in.* (If 2)

## SUB-TOPIC 4B: OTHER ELECTRONIC MEDIA

### Vocabulary from the syllabus

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| eject | To push out, for example a disc from a player |
| CD | Compact disc — a disc that stores music or data |
| CD-ROM | A compact disc that only reads stored information |
| DVD | Digital versatile disc — stores films and large files |
| player | A machine used to play discs or tapes |
| recorder | A machine used to record sound or pictures |
| tape | A ribbon that stores sound or pictures |
| the Internet | A worldwide network of computers linked together |
| extension cable | A cable that carries power further from the socket |
| connect | To join one thing to another |
| compact | Small and neatly packed together |

### Uses of other electronic media

Getting information and news · learning through videos and e-learning · sending e-mail and
messages · storing pictures, music and school work · entertainment · business and mobile
money · keeping in touch with people far away.

### Good and bad sides of electronic media

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Fast information from everywhere | Costly: gadgets, data and electricity |
| Learning and research | Bad content, cheating and time wasting |
| Entertainment and relaxation | Eye strain and lack of exercise |
| Communication over long distances | Fraud and cheating on the internet |
| Keeps records of pictures and sound | Spread of false news and rumours |

### How to care for electronic gadgets

Keep them **dry and free from dust** · handle discs by the **edges** · **eject** discs
properly before switching off · keep them out of strong sunlight and away from heat ·
switch off during storms and unplug · use the right **cables and voltage** · do not
overload one socket · keep out of the reach of small children.

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **If + past simple + would/could + verb (If 2)** | *If I **had** a lot of money, I **would record** all the gospel music on one DVD.* |
| **… needn't have …** | *You **needn't have switched off** the DVD, because I was watching a nice film.* |
| **… and so …** | *A CD should be kept well, **and so should** a DVD.* |

**Note:** *needn't have + past participle* means the action was done but it **was not
necessary**.

### Grammar focus: reported speech with media language

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| *"I am watching the news," he said.* | *He said that **he was watching** the news.* |
| *"We shall broadcast at eight," the presenter said.* | *The presenter said that **they would broadcast** at eight.* |
| *"Eject the disc," she told me.* | *She told me **to eject** the disc.* |
| *"Do you like this station?" he asked.* | *He asked **whether I liked** that station.* |

Remember: the tense moves **one step back**, and commands become **to + verb**.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **If 1** = real and likely: *If + present simple, will + verb.*
- **If 2** = imagined now: *If + past simple, would + verb* (use **were** for all persons).
- After *and so* the verb comes **before** the subject: *and so **is** Amina.*
- Use **neither / nor** for the negative: *and **neither does** she.*
- *needn't have + past participle* = it was done but was not necessary.
- In reported speech, commands become **to + verb**.
- Electronic media include radio, television, the internet, CDs, DVDs, players and recorders.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- *If you tune in at five, you ____ (hear) the news.* → **will hear**
- *If I ____ (be) a presenter, I would host a talk show.* → **were**
- *Joan is a good presenter and so ____ Amina.* → **is**
- *Musa doesn't like adverts and ____ does Peter.* → **neither**
- Give one word for: the person who introduces a programme → **presenter**.
- Give one word for: the room from which programmes are broadcast → **studio**.
- Change to reported speech: *"Eject the disc," she said.* → *She told him **to eject** the disc.*

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing *"If it will rain, we shall stay indoors"* — after **if** use the **present simple**.
- Writing *"and so Amina is"* instead of *and so **is** Amina*.
- Using *and so* with a negative sentence; use **neither/nor**.
- Confusing *needn't have* (it was unnecessary) with *didn't need to* (it was not done).
- Spelling: **aerial**, **advertisement**, **programme**, **studio**.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Electronic media are means of communication that use electricity or electronic devices. Examples: radio, television, the internet, DVD and CD players (any four).
2. **Presenter** — the person who introduces and runs a programme; **studio** — the room from which programmes are made or broadcast; **aerial** — the rod or wire that receives the signal; **eject** — to push out, for example a disc from a player.
3. CD — **compact disc**; DVD — **digital versatile disc**; CD-ROM — **compact disc read-only memory**.
4. **tune**
5. **had**
6. **would not buy**
7. **is**
8. **neither**
9. **You needn't have switched off the television.**
10. Father said that **he was watching the news**.
11. The teacher told us **to connect the extension cable**.
12. Any four of: news, sports, music, drama, documentaries, talk shows, farming programmes, children's programmes, religious programmes, adverts.
13. **Who, what, when, where, why** (and how).
14. *Learner's answer,* for example: *All P.7 pupils are informed that a briefing about the examinations will take place on Monday at 9:00 a.m. in the school hall. Every candidate must attend with a pen and a notebook. For more information, see the head teacher.*
15. *Learner's answer,* for example: *Are you looking for a strong school bag? Visit Mukwano Bag Shop, opposite the market, for strong bags from only 25,000 shillings. Hurry while stocks last!*
16. **Advantages:** fast information; learning and research; entertainment; communication over long distances (any four). **Disadvantages:** high cost of gadgets and data; bad content and time wasting; eye strain and little exercise; fraud; false news (any four).
17. Any five of: keep them dry and dust-free; hold discs by the edges; eject discs before switching off; keep away from strong sunlight and heat; switch off and unplug during storms; use the right cables and voltage; do not overload a socket; keep away from small children.
18. *Learner's sentences,* for example: *I **like** watching the weather forecast.* / *I **don't like** long adverts.*
19. **If 1** is about something real and likely and uses *If + present simple, will + verb*: *If it rains, we shall stay indoors.* **If 2** is about something imagined or unlikely now and uses *If + past simple, would + verb*: *If I had a television, I would watch the news.*
20. *Learner's dialogue.* It should have two speakers named, a new line for each speaker, correct punctuation and natural language, for example:
    *Ann: Which programme do you like most?*
    *Ben: I like the sports news. And you?*
