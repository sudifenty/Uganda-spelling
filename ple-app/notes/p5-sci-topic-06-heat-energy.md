## 1. MATTER

**Matter:** Anything that has weight (mass) and occupies space.

### Properties of matter

It **has mass or weight** · it **occupies space** · it **exerts pressure** · it can be seen,
felt or measured.

### The three states of matter

| State | Shape | Volume | Movement of particles | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Solid** | Fixed | Fixed | Particles packed tightly, only vibrate | Stone, ice, wood, iron |
| **Liquid** | Takes the shape of the container | Fixed | Particles move about but stay close | Water, milk, oil, paraffin |
| **Gas** | No fixed shape | Fills the whole container | Particles move freely and far apart | Air, oxygen, steam, smoke |

### Changes of state

| Change | Name |
|---|---|
| Solid → liquid | **Melting** |
| Liquid → solid | **Freezing (solidification)** |
| Liquid → gas | **Evaporation / boiling** |
| Gas → liquid | **Condensation** |
| Solid → gas directly | **Sublimation** |

---

## 2. SOLUTES, SOLVENTS AND MIXTURES

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Solute** | The substance that dissolves | Salt, sugar |
| **Solvent** | The liquid in which it dissolves | Water, paraffin |
| **Solution** | The mixture formed when a solute dissolves in a solvent | Salty water |
| **Soluble** | Able to dissolve | Salt, sugar |
| **Insoluble** | Not able to dissolve | Sand, stones, cooking oil in water |
| **Mixture** | Two or more substances put together without joining chemically | Sand and salt, air, soil |

### Separating mixtures

| Method | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hand picking** | Large pieces | Stones from beans |
| **Winnowing** | Light from heavy | Chaff from millet |
| **Sieving** | Small from large particles | Sand from gravel |
| **Filtering** | Insoluble solid from a liquid | Mud from water |
| **Decanting** | Letting solids settle, then pouring off | Clear water from muddy water |
| **Evaporation** | Getting a dissolved solid back | Salt from salty water |
| **Distillation** | Getting the pure liquid back | Pure water from salty water |
| **Magnetic separation** | Magnetic from non-magnetic | Iron filings from sand |

---

## 3. HEAT ENERGY

**Heat:** A form of energy that makes things hot, felt as warmth.

**Temperature:** The degree of hotness or coldness of a body, measured with a **thermometer** in **degrees Celsius (°C)**.

### Sources of heat

**The sun** — the main source · fire from **firewood, charcoal and paraffin** · **electricity**
· **friction** (rubbing hands, matchsticks) · **chemical reactions** · geothermal heat from
inside the earth · the human body.

---

## 4. HOW HEAT TRAVELS

| Method | How it works | Happens mainly in | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conduction** | Heat passes from particle to particle through the material | **Solids** | A metal spoon becomes hot in hot tea |
| **Convection** | Hot fluid rises and cold fluid sinks, forming currents | **Liquids and gases** | Boiling water; wind; smoke rising |
| **Radiation** | Heat travels in rays, needing no material | Through empty space | The heat of the sun; the heat you feel near a fire |

**Simple experiments:** hold a metal rod with wax and pins along it in a flame — the pins
fall one by one, showing **conduction**. Drop a coloured crystal into water being heated —
the colour rises and circles, showing **convection**. Stand near a fire and feel its heat —
that is **radiation**.

### Conductors and insulators

| Good conductors of heat | Poor conductors (insulators) |
|---|---|
| Copper, aluminium, iron, silver — all metals | Wood, plastic, rubber, glass, dry cloth, air, water, paper |

**Uses:** saucepans and irons are made of **metal** (conductors), but their handles are
**wood or plastic** (insulators); a **flask** keeps drinks hot by stopping conduction,
convection and radiation; blankets and sweaters trap **air**, which is a poor conductor.

---

## 5. EFFECTS OF HEAT ON MATTER

- **Expansion** — most things get bigger when heated and **contract** when cooled. This is
  why gaps are left in railway lines and bridges, and why a tight metal lid loosens when
  heated.
- **Change of state** — ice melts, water boils, steam condenses.
- **Burning** — heat changes wood into ash, smoke and gases (a **chemical change**).
- **Drying** — heat evaporates water from clothes and food.
- **Killing germs** — boiling makes water safe.
- **Cooking** — heat makes food soft, tasty and safe.

---

## 6. USES AND DANGERS OF HEAT

**Uses:** cooking · boiling water to kill germs · warming the body and the house · drying
clothes, grain and fish · ironing · welding and smelting metals · generating electricity ·
lighting · making bricks, pots and cement.

**Dangers and safety:** **burns and scalds** · **fires** in homes and bush · **smoke**
causing chest disease · **suffocation** from a charcoal stove in a closed room.
**Safety:** keep children from fire; turn saucepan handles inwards; put out fires properly;
never leave a charcoal stove burning in a closed bedroom; store paraffin and matches away
from children; cool a burn with **clean cool water**.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Matter | Anything that has mass and occupies space |
| Solute | The substance that dissolves in a liquid |
| Solvent | The liquid in which a solute dissolves |
| Solution | The mixture formed when a solute dissolves in a solvent |
| Heat | A form of energy that makes things hot |
| Temperature | The degree of hotness or coldness, measured in °C |
| Conduction | Heat passing from particle to particle in a solid |
| Convection | Heat carried by rising and sinking currents in liquids and gases |
| Radiation | Heat travelling in rays without a material |
| Expansion | The increase in size of a substance when heated |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Matter has **mass, occupies space and exerts pressure**; its states are **solid, liquid and gas**.
- Solid → liquid = **melting**; liquid → gas = **evaporation**; gas → liquid = **condensation**.
- **Solute + solvent = solution.**
- Salt is recovered from salty water by **evaporation**; pure water by **distillation**.
- The **sun** is the main source of heat.
- **Conduction** in solids · **convection** in liquids and gases · **radiation** through space.
- **Metals are good conductors**; wood, plastic and air are **insulators**.
- Heat causes **expansion**; cooling causes **contraction**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Anything with mass that occupies space → **matter**.
- Change from liquid to gas → **evaporation**; from gas to liquid → **condensation**.
- The substance that dissolves → the **solute**.
- Separating salt from salty water → **evaporation**.
- Separating iron filings from sand → **a magnet**.
- Heat travelling through a metal spoon → **conduction**.
- Heat from the sun reaching the earth → **radiation**.
- Why saucepan handles are wooden → wood is a **poor conductor (insulator)** of heat.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Matter is anything that has mass (weight) and occupies space. Properties: it **has mass**, it **occupies space** and it **exerts pressure**.
2. **Solid** — stone, ice; **liquid** — water, milk; **gas** — air, steam.
3. Ice to water — **melting**; water to steam — **evaporation (boiling)**; steam to water — **condensation**; water to ice — **freezing**.
4. **Solute** — the substance that dissolves, e.g. salt; **solvent** — the liquid it dissolves in, e.g. water; **solution** — the mixture formed, e.g. salty water.
5. **Soluble** substances dissolve, for example salt and sugar; **insoluble** ones do not, for example sand and stones.
6. Any six: hand picking — large pieces; winnowing — light from heavy; sieving — small from large; filtering — solids from liquids; decanting — settled solids from a liquid; evaporation — a dissolved solid; distillation — a pure liquid; magnetic separation — magnetic materials.
7. Salt from salty water — **evaporation**; iron filings from sand — **a magnet**; chaff from millet — **winnowing**; mud from water — **filtering** (or decanting first).
8. Heat is a form of energy that makes things hot. Sources: the **sun**, fire from firewood or charcoal, electricity, friction, chemical reactions (also geothermal heat and the body).
9. **Conduction** — mainly in solids; **convection** — in liquids and gases; **radiation** — through empty space, needing no material.
10. Take a **metal rod** and stick pins along it with wax. Heat one end in a flame. The wax melts and the **pins fall one after another from the hot end outwards**, showing that heat travelled along the solid by conduction.
11. Heat water gently in a clear glass container and drop in a **coloured crystal** (or sawdust). The colour **rises from the heated part, spreads at the top, sinks at the sides and circles**, showing convection currents.
12. **Good conductors:** copper, aluminium, iron, silver. **Poor conductors:** wood, plastic, rubber, glass (also dry cloth, air, paper).
13. The **metal body conducts heat quickly** to the food so it cooks fast, while the **wooden or plastic handle is a poor conductor**, so it stays cool and the cook is not burnt.
14. Any five of: expansion when heated and contraction when cooled; change of state; burning; drying; killing germs; cooking food.
15. **Uses (any five):** cooking; boiling water to kill germs; warming the body and house; drying clothes and food; ironing; welding; generating electricity; making bricks and pots. **Dangers (any four):** burns and scalds; fires; smoke causing chest disease; suffocation from a charcoal stove in a closed room.
