---

## 1. BREASTFEEDING

**Breastfeeding:** Feeding a baby on milk from the mother's breast.

A baby should be **breastfed exclusively for the first six months** — no water, no porridge —
and then breastfed alongside other foods up to two years or beyond.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **To the baby:** breast milk is complete food; **colostrum** gives antibodies that fight disease; it is clean, warm and always ready; it builds love between mother and baby; it is easy to digest | The mother must always be near the baby |
| **To the mother:** it costs nothing; it helps the womb return to normal; it delays another pregnancy; it lowers the risk of some cancers | It can be tiring; a sick mother may find it hard |
| **To the family:** it saves money, and the baby falls sick less often | A working mother needs to express milk or leave the baby |

---

## 2. BOTTLE FEEDING

**Bottle feeding:** Feeding a baby on milk (usually cow's milk or formula) from a bottle.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Anybody can feed the baby, so the mother can work | **Easily contaminated** — it causes diarrhoea if bottles are not sterilised |
| The amount taken can be measured | Formula and bottles are **expensive** |
| It helps when the mother is very ill, has died, or cannot produce enough milk | It has **no antibodies**, so the baby falls sick more often |
| Others in the family can share the work | It weakens the bond between mother and baby; it may be over-diluted, causing malnutrition |

**If bottle feeding must be used:** **sterilise** the bottle and teat by boiling, use
**boiled water**, mix exactly as instructed, feed at once, and never keep left-over milk.
A **cup and spoon** are safer than a bottle because they are easier to clean.

---

## 3. VULNERABLE GROUPS

**Vulnerable people:** People who are most easily harmed by poor feeding and disease.

| Group | Why they are vulnerable | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| **Babies and young children (0–5)** | Growing fast; weak immunity | Breast milk, then soft energy- and protein-rich foods; vitamin A; deworming |
| **Expectant mothers** | Feeding two; risk of anaemia | Iron-rich foods, protein, fruits, folic acid, plenty of water |
| **Breastfeeding mothers** | Producing milk | Extra food and fluids |
| **The sick, including people with HIV** | Appetite low; the body fights infection | Soft, tasty, frequent meals; plenty of fluids |
| **The elderly** | Weak teeth and digestion | Soft foods, calcium, fruits and vegetables |
| **People with disabilities** | May need help to eat | Support and a balanced diet |

---

## 4. FOOD VALUES AND A BALANCED DIET

| Food value | Work in the body | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** | Give **energy** | Matooke, cassava, potatoes, rice, maize, millet, bread |
| **Proteins** | **Body building** and repair | Beans, groundnuts, soya, meat, fish, eggs, milk |
| **Fats and oils** | Energy and warmth | Cooking oil, ghee, avocado, simsim, groundnuts |
| **Vitamins** | **Protect** the body from disease | Fruits and vegetables |
| **Mineral salts** | Build bones and teeth, make blood | Milk, fish with bones, greens, salt |
| **Water** | Digestion, transport, cooling | Clean drinking water, fruits, soup |
| **Roughage (fibre)** | Prevents constipation | Vegetables, fruit skins, whole grains |

**A balanced diet** contains all the food values in the right amounts. A simple Ugandan
example: **matooke + beans + greens + a fruit + water**.

---

## 5. DEFICIENCY DISEASES

| Disease | Cause | Signs | Prevention |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kwashiorkor** | Lack of **protein** | Swollen legs and face, thin hair, pot belly, misery | Give beans, groundnuts, eggs, milk, fish |
| **Marasmus** | Lack of **energy and protein** (general starvation) | Very thin, "old man's face", loose skin | Feed often with energy- and protein-rich foods |
| **Anaemia** | Lack of **iron** | Pale eyes and palms, tiredness, dizziness | Dodo, nakati, liver, beans, meat, plus vitamin C foods; deworm |
| **Rickets** | Lack of **vitamin D and calcium** | Bowed legs, soft bones | Milk, fish, eggs, morning sunshine |
| **Night blindness / xerophthalmia** | Lack of **vitamin A** | Failure to see in dim light; dry eyes | Carrots, pawpaw, mangoes, green vegetables, liver |
| **Scurvy** | Lack of **vitamin C** | Bleeding gums, slow healing | Oranges, lemons, guavas, tomatoes |
| **Goitre** | Lack of **iodine** | Swelling in the neck | Use **iodised salt**; eat fish |
| **Beriberi** | Lack of **vitamin B1** | Weak muscles, swollen legs | Whole grains, groundnuts, beans |
| **Obesity** | Too much fat and sugar with little exercise | Overweight, breathlessness | Balanced diet, exercise, less sugar and fried food |

## 6. FOOD HYGIENE, PREPARATION AND STORAGE

**Hygiene:** wash hands with soap before handling food and before eating · wash fruits and
vegetables · use clean water and clean utensils · **cover food** against flies and dust ·
keep pets and animals out of the kitchen · a person with a wound or diarrhoea should not
cook for others.

**Preparation to keep the food value:** do not overcook vegetables; **steam rather than
boil**, and use the cooking water in the sauce; cut vegetables just before cooking; cook
with the lid on; avoid too much oil and salt.

**Storage:** dry grains and legumes well before storing · store in **clean, dry, airy**
places, in sacks on pallets, or in granaries · protect from rats, weevils and moulds ·
keep milk, meat and cooked food covered and cool, and reheat well before eating · **never
eat mouldy or swollen tinned food** — mouldy groundnuts and maize carry **aflatoxins**.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Breastfeeding | Feeding a baby on milk from the mother's breast |
| Colostrum | The first thick yellow breast milk, rich in antibodies |
| Bottle feeding | Feeding a baby milk from a bottle |
| Sterilising | Killing germs, usually by boiling |
| Vulnerable group | People most easily harmed by poor feeding and disease |
| Balanced diet | A meal containing all food values in the right amounts |
| Deficiency disease | A disease caused by lack of a food value |
| Malnutrition | Poor health caused by a poor or unbalanced diet |
| Roughage | Fibre that helps digestion |
| Aflatoxin | A poison from moulds on badly dried food |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Babies should be **breastfed only, for the first six months**.
- **Colostrum** is the first milk and protects the baby from disease.
- Bottle feeding is risky because bottles are **easily contaminated**; a **cup and spoon** are safer.
- Vulnerable groups: **babies, expectant and breastfeeding mothers, the sick, the elderly**.
- **Kwashiorkor** — lack of **protein**; **marasmus** — lack of **energy and protein**.
- **Anaemia** — lack of iron; **rickets** — vitamin D; **night blindness** — vitamin A; **scurvy** — vitamin C; **goitre** — iodine.
- Use **iodised salt** to prevent goitre.
- Do not overcook vegetables; **cover food** and store it dry.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Breastfeeding is feeding a baby on milk from the mother's breast. A baby should be breastfed **exclusively for the first six months**, then breastfed with other foods up to two years or beyond.
2. **To the baby (any four):** breast milk is complete food; colostrum gives antibodies; it is clean, warm and ready; it is easy to digest; it builds love between mother and baby. **To the mother:** it costs nothing; it helps the womb return to normal (also delays another pregnancy).
3. Colostrum is the **first thick yellow milk** produced after birth. It is important because it is rich in **antibodies** that protect the baby from disease, and in nutrients for a good start.
4. Bottle feeding is feeding a baby milk from a bottle. **Advantages:** anybody can feed the baby; the amount can be measured. **Disadvantages:** bottles are easily contaminated and cause diarrhoea; formula and bottles are expensive; there are no antibodies; the bond with the mother is weaker; over-dilution causes malnutrition.
5. **Sterilise** the bottle and teat by boiling; use **boiled water**; mix exactly as instructed; feed the milk **at once** and never keep left-overs (using a cup and spoon is safer).
6. Vulnerable people are those most easily harmed by poor feeding and disease. Any five of: babies and young children; expectant mothers; breastfeeding mothers; the sick, including people with HIV; the elderly; people with disabilities.
7. She needs **iron-rich foods** (dodo, liver, beans), **protein**, **fruits and vegetables** and plenty of water, because she is feeding herself and the growing baby and is at risk of **anaemia**.
8. **Carbohydrates** — matooke; **proteins** — beans; **fats and oils** — cooking oil; **vitamins** — fruits; **mineral salts** — milk; **water** — clean drinking water; **roughage** — vegetables.
9. A balanced diet is a meal containing all the food values in the right amounts. Example: **matooke with beans, greens, a fruit and water**.
10. Any six: **kwashiorkor** — protein; **marasmus** — energy and protein; **anaemia** — iron; **rickets** — vitamin D and calcium; **night blindness** — vitamin A; **scurvy** — vitamin C; **goitre** — iodine; **beriberi** — vitamin B1.
11. **Kwashiorkor:** swollen legs and face, thin light hair, pot belly, misery and loss of appetite. **Marasmus:** very thin body, "old man's face", loose skin, ribs showing.
12. By eating **iron-rich foods** such as dodo, nakati, liver, beans and meat, together with **vitamin C** fruits to absorb the iron; by **deworming** regularly; by sleeping under a **treated mosquito net** to avoid malaria; and by treating bleeding early.
13. Any six of: wash hands with soap before handling food and eating; wash fruits and vegetables; use clean water and utensils; cover food against flies; keep animals out of the kitchen; do not let a person with diarrhoea or a wound cook for others; wash the cooking place daily.
14. Any four of: do not overcook vegetables; steam rather than boil; use the cooking water in the sauce; cut vegetables just before cooking; cook with the lid on; avoid too much oil and salt.
15. Any five of: dry grains and legumes well; store in clean, dry, airy places; use sacks on pallets or granaries; protect from rats, weevils and moulds; keep milk, meat and cooked food covered and cool and reheat well. **Mouldy groundnuts must never be eaten** because the moulds produce **aflatoxins**, poisons that damage the liver and can cause cancer.
