## SUB-TOPIC 5A: CHILDREN'S RIGHTS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

### Definitions

**Right:** Something every child is entitled to and which others must give or allow.

**Responsibility:** Something a child is expected to do — a duty.

**Child abuse:** Any act that harms a child physically, emotionally or sexually, or neglects the child's needs.

### Rights and the matching responsibilities

| Right | Matching responsibility |
|---|---|
| The right to **education** | Attending school regularly and doing your homework |
| The right to **food** | Eating what is provided and helping with the chores |
| The right to **shelter and clothing** | Keeping the home and your clothes clean and tidy |
| The right to **medical care** | Keeping yourself clean and reporting when you are unwell |
| The right to **play and rest** | Playing safely and returning home in good time |
| The right to be **listened to** | Listening to others and speaking respectfully |
| The right to **protection from abuse** | Reporting abuse and avoiding dangerous places and company |
| The right to a **name and nationality** | Respecting the laws of the country |

### Vocabulary from the syllabus

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| abuse | Cruel or harmful treatment of a person |
| mistreatment | Treating somebody badly |
| early marriage | Marrying while still a child |
| dropout | A learner who leaves school before finishing |
| child labour | Making a child do heavy or harmful work |
| bad touches | Touching a child's private parts or in a way that is not right |
| sugar-daddy / sugar-mummy | An older person who gives a young person gifts or money in exchange for a sexual relationship |
| convict | To find somebody guilty in a court of law |
| imprison | To put somebody in prison |
| innocent | Not guilty; having done no wrong |
| chores | Small jobs done at home |
| obedient | Willing to do what you are told by those in authority |

### Staying safe

Move in **groups**, especially at night · refuse gifts and lifts from strangers · say
**NO** loudly and get away if someone tries to touch you wrongly · never keep abuse a
secret — **tell a trusted adult at once**: a parent, teacher, religious leader, police
officer or the child helpline · remember that abuse is **never the child's fault** · avoid
dangerous places and bad company.

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **more interested in … than …** | *Girls are **more interested in** cooking **than** playing.* |
| **… ought to …** | *All children **ought to** go to school.* |
| **… in order to …** | *You should always move in groups **in order to** avoid child abuse.* |
| **either … or …** | *You **either** provide food to your children **or** you risk going to prison.* |
| **… is likely to …** | *Making wrong friends **is likely to** affect your studies.* |

**Note:** *ought to* means a strong duty, like *should*. Its negative is *ought not to*.

### Grammar focus 1: Relative pronouns

Relative pronouns join two sentences and tell us more about a noun.

| Pronoun | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | people (subject) | *The girl **who** reported the abuse was brave.* |
| **whom** | people (object) | *The man **whom** the police arrested is a neighbour.* |
| **whose** | possession | *The child **whose** parents died lives with an aunt.* |
| **which** | things and animals | *The school **which** helps orphans is nearby.* |
| **that** | people or things | *This is the book **that** I lost.* |
| **where** | places | *This is the home **where** she was rescued.* |

### Grammar focus 2: Future continuous tense

**Form:** will be + verb + **-ing** — an action that will be going on at a certain time in
the future.

- *This time tomorrow we **shall be discussing** children's rights.*
- *The pupils **will be presenting** a play about child abuse next week.*

## SUB-TOPIC 5B: ANIMAL NEEDS AND FREEDOMS

### Why animals matter

The syllabus explains that animal freedom is taught so that learners can **compare their
own rights with those of animals**, and grow up ready to protect animals.

### The needs and freedoms of animals

| Freedom | What it means |
|---|---|
| Freedom from **hunger and thirst** | Enough food and clean water |
| Freedom from **discomfort** | Proper shelter and a comfortable resting place |
| Freedom from **pain, injury and disease** | Prevention, quick treatment, no beating |
| Freedom to express **natural behaviour** | Enough space, company of their own kind |
| Freedom from **fear and distress** | Kind handling, no frightening or overworking |

### Vocabulary from the syllabus

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| capture | To catch and hold an animal |
| poach | To kill or catch wild animals illegally |
| poacher | A person who kills or catches animals illegally |
| captivity | Being kept in a cage or enclosure, not free |
| sanctuary | A protected place where animals are safe |
| insemination | Putting semen into a female animal so that it can produce young |
| discomfort | A feeling of not being comfortable |
| distress | Great worry, pain or suffering |
| hindrance | Something that stops or delays something |
| secure | Safe and protected |

### Ways people abuse animals

Overloading and overworking · beating and stoning · tying legs of chickens and goats
tightly or hanging them on bicycles · starving them or giving dirty water · keeping them
in dirty, tight places · not treating sickness · **poaching** · burning bush where wild
animals live · abandoning pets.

### How to care for animals

Give enough **clean water and feed** · build a clean, dry **shelter** · **treat and
vaccinate** them · handle them **gently** · give them **space** to move · protect them
from thieves and wild animals · report poaching and cruelty to the authorities.

### Acronyms about animal welfare (from the syllabus)

| Acronym | Full meaning |
|---|---|
| **WSPA** | World Society for the Protection of Animals |
| **USPA** | Uganda Society for the Protection of Animals |
| **UWEC** | Uganda Wildlife Education Centre |
| **UWA** | Uganda Wildlife Authority |
| **WCU** | Wildlife Clubs of Uganda |
| **ABS** | Animal Behaviour Society |
| **ADI** | Animal Defenders International |
| **AVAR** | Association of Veterinarians for Animal Rights |
| **ASAB** | Association for the Study of Animal Behaviour |
| **CAPS** | Captive Animals Protection Society |

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Much as …** | ***Much as** a cow is an animal, it also needs protection.* |
| **… need not have …** | *Obbo **need not have tied** his goat on the motorcycle carrier.* |
| **Whereas …** | ***Whereas** people want their rights, they do not respect animal freedoms.* |

### Grammar focus 3: Forming nouns from verbs

| Verb | Noun | Verb | Noun |
|---|---|---|---|
| protect | protection | inseminate | insemination |
| capture | captivity / capture | free | freedom |
| abuse | abuse / abuser | educate | education |
| behave | behaviour | responsible *(adj.)* | responsibility |
| decide | decision | care | care / carer |

### Grammar focus 4: Past perfect tense

**Form:** had + past participle — the action that happened **first** of two past actions.

- *The poacher **had escaped** before the rangers arrived.*
- *We **had fed** the animals before it rained.*

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Right | Something a person is entitled to |
| Responsibility | A duty; something one is expected to do |
| Child abuse | Any act that harms or neglects a child |
| Child labour | Making a child do heavy or harmful work |
| Poaching | Killing or catching wild animals illegally |
| Captivity | Being kept in a cage or enclosure |
| Sanctuary | A protected place where animals are safe |
| Relative pronoun | A word such as who, which or whose that joins and describes |
| Future continuous | will/shall be + verb-ing — an action going on in the future |
| Past perfect | had + past participle — the earlier of two past actions |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Every **right** has a matching **responsibility**.
- **ought to** = a strong duty (like *should*); negative *ought not to*.
- **who** for people, **which** for things and animals, **whose** for possession, **where** for places.
- Future continuous: **will/shall be + verb-ing**. Past perfect: **had + past participle**.
- *need not have + past participle* = the action was done but was not necessary.
- Animals have five freedoms: from **hunger and thirst, discomfort, pain, fear**, and freedom to show **natural behaviour**.
- **UWA** = Uganda Wildlife Authority; **UWEC** = Uganda Wildlife Education Centre.
- Abuse is **never the child's fault** — report it to a trusted adult at once.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- *The girl ____ reported the case was brave.* → **who**
- *The dog ____ leg was broken was treated.* → **whose**
- *All children ____ go to school.* → **ought to**
- *You should move in groups ____ avoid abuse.* → **in order to**
- Form the noun from *protect* → **protection**; from *behave* → **behaviour**.
- Give the full meaning of **UWA** → Uganda Wildlife Authority.
- Give one word for: a person who kills wild animals illegally → **poacher**.
- Give one word for: a protected place where animals are safe → **sanctuary**.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Confusing a right with a responsibility.
- Writing *"ought to going"* — after *ought to* use the **plain verb**: *ought to go*.
- Using *which* for people or *who* for things.
- Writing *"either … nor"* — it is **either … or**, and **neither … nor**.
- Mixing the past perfect with the simple past: *He had escaped before the rangers **arrived*** (not "had arrived" twice).
- Spelling: **responsibility, protection, sanctuary, captivity**.

# REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Give the difference between a right and a responsibility, with one example of each.
2. Name any five rights of a child and the responsibility that goes with each.
3. Give the meaning of: child labour, dropout, early marriage, bad touches.
4. What should a child do if somebody tries to touch him or her wrongly?
5. Complete: *All children ____ ____ go to school.* (two words)
6. Complete: *You should move in groups ____ ____ ____ avoid child abuse.* (three words)
7. Complete: *You ____ provide food to your children ____ you risk going to prison.*
8. Fill in the correct relative pronoun: *The teacher ____ helped the orphan was rewarded.*
9. Fill in the correct relative pronoun: *The goat ____ leg was broken has been treated.*
10. Fill in the correct relative pronoun: *This is the sanctuary ____ the animals are kept.*
11. Rewrite in the future continuous: *We shall discuss children's rights this time tomorrow.*
12. Rewrite using the past perfect: *The poacher escaped. Then the rangers arrived.*
13. Name the five freedoms of animals.
14. Give any five ways in which people abuse animals.
15. Give any five ways of caring for animals.
16. Give the full meaning of: UWA, UWEC, WSPA, USPA, WCU.
17. Form nouns from these verbs: protect, behave, decide, educate, capture.
18. Rewrite using *Much as*: *Although a cow is an animal, it also needs protection.*
19. Rewrite using *need not have*: *Obbo tied his goat on the motorcycle carrier, but it was not necessary.*
20. Write a short paragraph (about six sentences) explaining why children should protect animals.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. A **right** is something a child is entitled to, for example the right to education; a **responsibility** is a duty the child is expected to perform, for example attending school regularly and doing homework.
2. Any five pairs from the table, for example: the right to food — helping with the chores; the right to education — attending school regularly; the right to medical care — keeping clean and reporting illness; the right to play — playing safely; the right to protection — reporting abuse.
3. **Child labour** — making a child do heavy or harmful work; **dropout** — a learner who leaves school before finishing; **early marriage** — marrying while still a child; **bad touches** — touching a child's private parts or in a way that is not right.
4. The child should **say NO loudly, get away from the person, and tell a trusted adult at once** — a parent, teacher, religious leader, police officer or child helpline. The child must not keep it a secret, and it is never the child's fault.
5. **ought to**
6. **in order to**
7. *You **either** provide food to your children **or** you risk going to prison.*
8. **who**
9. **whose**
10. **where**
11. *This time tomorrow, we **shall be discussing** children's rights.*
12. *The poacher **had escaped** before the rangers arrived.*
13. Freedom from hunger and thirst; freedom from discomfort; freedom from pain, injury and disease; freedom to express natural behaviour; freedom from fear and distress.
14. Any five of: overloading and overworking; beating and stoning; tying legs tightly or hanging animals on bicycles; starving them or giving dirty water; keeping them in dirty, tight places; not treating sickness; poaching; burning bush; abandoning pets.
15. Any five of: give clean water and enough feed; build a clean dry shelter; treat and vaccinate them; handle them gently; give them space; protect them from thieves and wild animals; report cruelty and poaching.
16. **UWA** — Uganda Wildlife Authority; **UWEC** — Uganda Wildlife Education Centre; **WSPA** — World Society for the Protection of Animals; **USPA** — Uganda Society for the Protection of Animals; **WCU** — Wildlife Clubs of Uganda.
17. protect → **protection**; behave → **behaviour**; decide → **decision**; educate → **education**; capture → **captivity** (or capture).
18. ***Much as** a cow is an animal, it also needs protection.*
19. *Obbo **need not have tied** his goat on the motorcycle carrier.*
20. *Learner's paragraph.* It should be one paragraph with clear reasons, such as: animals also feel pain; they give us food, work and money; wild animals bring tourists and income; caring for animals shows kindness; the law protects them; and animals cannot speak for themselves.
