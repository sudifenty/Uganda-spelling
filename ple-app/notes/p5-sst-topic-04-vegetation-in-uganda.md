*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Five Curriculum, Set One, Social Studies Topic 4

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- say what vegetation is and name its types

- state the importance of vegetation and how to conserve it

---

## 1. WHAT VEGETATION IS

**Vegetation:** The general plant cover of an area.

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural vegetation** | Vegetation that grows on its own without being planted | Forests, swamps, shrubs, grass |
| **Planted vegetation** | Vegetation planted and cared for by people | Plantations, woodlots, crops, gardens, flowers |

---

## 2. TYPES OF NATURAL VEGETATION IN UGANDA

| Type | Where it is found | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Tropical rain forest** | Mabira (Buikwe), Budongo (Masindi), Bwindi (Kanungu), Kibale, Mpanga; the Lake Victoria crescent | Tall evergreen hardwood trees growing close together in layers; little grass below |
| **Savannah grassland** | Most of central, northern and eastern Uganda | Tall or short grass with scattered trees such as acacia and shea butter; deciduous trees |
| **Semi-desert / dry thorn** | Karamoja, parts of Ankole and the rift valley floor | Short tough grass, thorn bushes, cactus, euphorbia |
| **Montane vegetation** | Slopes of Rwenzori, Elgon and the Virunga mountains | Changes with height: forest → bamboo → heath → moorland → bare rock and ice |
| **Swamp (wetland) vegetation** | Around Lakes Kyoga and Victoria and along rivers | Papyrus, reeds, water lilies, sedges |

### Planted vegetation

Tea and sugar-cane **plantations** (Kakira, Lugazi, Kinyara, Fort Portal) · **forest
plantations** of pine and eucalyptus (Mafuga, Katugo) · **woodlots** at homes and schools ·
gardens of food and cash crops · flowers and shade trees in towns.

---

## 3. FACTORS THAT INFLUENCE VEGETATION DISTRIBUTION

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Rainfall (climate)** | Heavy, reliable rain grows forests; little rain grows short grass and thorn bushes |
| **Temperature** | High mountains are cold, so only heath and moorland grow at the top |
| **Soil type** | Deep, fertile soils grow big trees; sandy, rocky or salty soils grow scattered plants |
| **Relief / altitude** | Vegetation changes as you climb a mountain; steep slopes lose soil |
| **Drainage** | Waterlogged land grows papyrus and reeds; well-drained land grows forest |
| **Human activities** | Clearing for farms, settlement, firewood, charcoal, grazing and burning changes vegetation everywhere |

---

## 4. IMPORTANCE OF VEGETATION

- Provides **timber, poles, firewood and charcoal**.
- Provides **herbal medicine**, fruits, honey and wild foods.
- Provides **pasture** for animals.
- **Attracts rainfall** and cools the air.
- **Prevents soil erosion**; fallen leaves make the soil fertile.
- Is a **home for wild animals and birds**.
- Attracts **tourists** — Bwindi's gorillas, Mabira's birds, Kibale's chimpanzees.
- Supplies **raw materials** for industries: paper, furniture, mats and baskets from papyrus.
- Wetlands **filter water** and hold floods.
- Gives **shade, beauty and windbreaks**.

---

## 5. VEGETATION AND POPULATION DISTRIBUTION

| Vegetation zone | Population | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Savannah and the Lake Victoria crescent | **Dense** | Good rainfall, fertile soils, easy to clear, good for farming |
| Mountain slopes (lower parts) | **Dense** | Fertile volcanic soils and reliable relief rainfall |
| Thick forest | **Sparse** | Hard to clear, poor leached soils, pests and diseases |
| Semi-desert (Karamoja) | **Sparse** | Too dry, little pasture and water |
| Swamps | **Sparse** | Waterlogged, mosquitoes, hard to build on |

### Effects of population on vegetation

**Deforestation** for farmland, firewood, charcoal, building and settlement · **swamp
reclamation** for rice, factories and houses · **overgrazing** in the cattle corridor ·
**bush burning** for hunting and grazing · **soil erosion and loss of fertility** ·
loss of **wildlife habitats** and of some plant species.

---

## 6. CONSERVATION OF VEGETATION

**Conservation:** Protecting and using vegetation carefully so that it is not destroyed.

- **Afforestation** (planting trees where there were none) and **reafforestation** (replanting).
- **Agroforestry** — growing trees together with crops and animals.
- Using **energy-saving stoves, biogas and solar** instead of firewood and charcoal.
- **Gazetting** forest reserves and national parks — Mabira, Budongo, Bwindi, Mt Elgon.
- Protecting **wetlands and river banks** by law.
- **Controlled grazing** and controlled bush burning.
- **Educating** the community and forming school environment clubs.
- Enforcing laws through **NEMA** and the **National Forestry Authority (NFA)**.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Vegetation | The general plant cover of an area |
| Natural vegetation | Vegetation that grows on its own |
| Planted vegetation | Vegetation planted and cared for by people |
| Deforestation | The cutting down of trees over a wide area |
| Afforestation | Planting trees where there were none before |
| Reafforestation | Replanting trees where they were cut down |
| Agroforestry | Growing trees together with crops and animals |
| Gazetted area | An area protected by government order |
| Swamp reclamation | Draining a swamp so the land can be used |
| Conservation | Protecting resources so they are not destroyed |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Natural** vegetation grows on its own; **planted** vegetation is put there by people.
- Uganda's main types: **tropical rain forest, savannah, semi-desert, montane, swamp**.
- Big forests: **Mabira, Budongo, Bwindi, Kibale**.
- Vegetation is decided mostly by **rainfall**, then soil, relief, drainage and people.
- Montane vegetation changes in bands: **forest → bamboo → heath → moorland → rock and ice**.
- **Savannah** areas are densely populated; forests, swamps and Karamoja are sparsely populated.
- **Afforestation** = new trees; **reafforestation** = replanting.
- **NFA** and **NEMA** protect Uganda's forests and environment.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Vegetation is the general plant cover of an area. **Natural vegetation** grows on its own — forests and swamps; **planted vegetation** is planted by people — plantations and woodlots.
2. Any four: tropical rain forest — Mabira, Budongo, Bwindi; savannah — central and northern Uganda; semi-desert — Karamoja; montane — Rwenzori and Elgon slopes; swamp vegetation — around Lakes Kyoga and Victoria.
3. Any four: **Mabira** — Buikwe; **Budongo** — Masindi; **Bwindi** — Kanungu; **Kibale** — Kabarole; Mpanga — Mpigi.
4. It changes in bands: **forest** at the bottom, then **bamboo**, then **heath**, then **moorland**, and finally **bare rock, ice and snow** at the top, because it becomes colder as you climb.
5. Any five of: rainfall, temperature, soil type, relief and altitude, drainage, human activities.
6. Because the area around **Lake Victoria receives heavy, reliable rainfall** and has deep fertile soils, which support tall trees, while **Karamoja is semi-arid**, with under 750 mm of unreliable rain, so only short grass and thorn bushes can survive.
7. Any six of: timber, poles, firewood and charcoal; herbal medicine and wild foods; pasture; attracting rainfall; preventing soil erosion; a home for wild animals; attracting tourists; raw materials for industry; filtering water in wetlands; shade and beauty.
8. Making **mats, baskets and hats**; **thatching** and fencing; and making **crafts for sale** (papyrus also filters water and provides mulch).
9. The **savannah, the Lake Victoria crescent and the lower mountain slopes**, because they have good rainfall, fertile soils and land that is easy to clear and farm.
10. **Thick forests** (hard to clear, poor leached soils, pests), **semi-desert Karamoja** (too dry) and **swamps** (waterlogged, mosquitoes, hard to build on).
11. Any five of: deforestation for farmland and firewood; swamp reclamation; overgrazing; bush burning; soil erosion and loss of fertility; loss of wildlife habitats; disappearance of some plant species.
12. **Afforestation** is planting trees in an area where there were none before; **reafforestation** is replanting trees where they were cut down.
13. Any six of: afforestation and reafforestation; agroforestry; energy-saving stoves, biogas and solar; gazetting forest reserves and parks; protecting wetlands and river banks; controlled grazing and burning; public education; enforcing laws.
14. The **National Forestry Authority (NFA)** and the **National Environment Management Authority (NEMA)**.
15. *Learner's map.* Mabira in the centre near Jinja, Karamoja in the north-east, and Bwindi in the far south-west.
