---

## 1. THE TWO MAIN GROUPS

| Group | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Flowering plants** | Plants that produce **flowers and seeds** | Maize, beans, mango, bean, sunflower, grass |
| **Non-flowering plants** | Plants that **do not produce flowers** | Ferns, mosses, mushrooms, pines, algae |

---

## 2. FLOWERING PLANTS: LEGUMES AND CEREALS

| Group | Characteristics | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Legumes** | Seeds grow in **pods**; the seed has **two seed leaves** (dicotyledon); roots carry **nodules** with bacteria that add **nitrogen** to the soil; rich in **protein** | Beans, peas, groundnuts, soya beans, cowpeas |
| **Cereals** | Grasses grown for their **grain**; the seed has **one seed leaf** (monocotyledon); fibrous roots; rich in **carbohydrates** | Maize, rice, millet, sorghum, wheat, barley |

**Why legumes matter to a farmer:** their root nodules put **nitrogen** back into the soil,
so planting beans or groundnuts after maize makes the soil fertile again. This is why
**crop rotation** works.

### Parts of a flower

**Sepals** protect the bud · **petals** attract insects · **stamen** (anther and filament)
is the **male** part making pollen · **pistil** (stigma, style and ovary) is the **female**
part · **pollination** is the transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma ·
**fertilisation** follows, and the ovary becomes the **fruit** while the ovules become
**seeds**.

---

## 3. NON-FLOWERING PLANTS

| Group | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Spore-bearing plants** | Reproduce by **spores**, not seeds; like damp shady places | Ferns, mosses, mushrooms, moulds |
| **Conifers** | Bear **cones** instead of flowers; have needle-shaped leaves; stay green all year | Pine, cypress, cedar, fir |
| **Algae** | Simple plants living in water or damp places; no true roots, stems or leaves | Pond scum, seaweed, spirogyra |

---

## 4. SEED DISPERSAL

### Definition

**Seed dispersal:** The scattering of seeds away from the parent plant.

### Why dispersal is necessary

To avoid **overcrowding** · to reduce **competition** for water, light and food ·
to spread the plant to **new places** · to reduce the spread of **pests and diseases** ·
to help the species survive.

### Agents of dispersal and their seed features

| Agent | Features of the seed or fruit | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Wind** | Light, with wings, hairs or parachutes | Cotton, jacaranda, dandelion, tridax |
| **Water** | Light, spongy or with a waterproof cover so it floats | Coconut, water lily |
| **Animals and birds** | Sweet fleshy fruits that are eaten, or hooks that stick to fur and clothes | Mango, guava, black jack (**hooks**), tick berry |
| **Explosive (self) mechanism** | Dry pods that split open suddenly and throw seeds out | Beans, castor oil, pride of Barbados |
| **People** | Carried and planted deliberately, or dropped | Maize, beans, seedlings |

---

## 5. PLANT PROPAGATION

### Definition

**Plant propagation:** The way new plants are produced from old ones.

| Type | How | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Sexual propagation** | By **seeds**, after pollination and fertilisation | Maize, beans, tomatoes, trees |
| **Vegetative (asexual) propagation** | From a **part of the parent plant** — no seed | See below |

### Methods of vegetative propagation

| Method | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Cuttings** | A piece of stem is planted | Cassava, sugar cane, sweet potato vines |
| **Suckers** | Young shoots from the base of the parent | Banana, pineapple, sisal |
| **Tubers** | Swollen underground stems or roots | Irish potato (stem tuber), sweet potato (root tuber) |
| **Bulbs** | Underground buds with fleshy leaves | Onion, garlic |
| **Rhizomes** | Underground horizontal stems | Ginger, couch grass |
| **Runners (stolons)** | Stems that creep on the ground and root | Grass, strawberry |
| **Grafting and budding** | Joining a shoot or bud of a good plant onto another plant's root | Mango, orange, apple |
| **Layering** | Bending a branch to the ground until it roots | Guava, coffee |

**Advantages of vegetative propagation:** the new plant is **exactly like the parent** ·
it **matures faster** · it is useful for plants whose seeds do not grow well.
**Disadvantages:** diseases pass easily from parent to offspring; there is **no variety**;
plants may be weaker over time.

---

## 6. ECONOMIC VALUE OF PLANTS

- **Food** — cereals, legumes, fruits and vegetables.
- **Cash and employment** — coffee, tea, cotton, sugar cane, vanilla.
- **Timber and poles** for building and furniture.
- **Firewood and charcoal**.
- **Medicine** — both herbal and modern.
- **Raw materials** for industries — paper, textiles, oils, sugar.
- **Fodder** for animals.
- **Shade, windbreaks and beauty**.
- **Protection of the environment** — trees hold soil, attract rain and give **oxygen**.
- **Tourism** in forests and botanical gardens.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Flowering plant | A plant that produces flowers and seeds |
| Non-flowering plant | A plant that does not produce flowers |
| Legume | A plant whose seeds grow in pods and whose roots have nitrogen nodules |
| Cereal | A grass grown for its grain |
| Spore | A tiny reproductive body of ferns, mosses and fungi |
| Conifer | A non-flowering plant that bears cones |
| Seed dispersal | The scattering of seeds away from the parent plant |
| Propagation | The way new plants are produced |
| Vegetative propagation | Producing new plants from a part of the parent plant |
| Grafting | Joining a shoot of one plant onto the root system of another |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Flowering plants** produce flowers and seeds; **non-flowering plants** do not.
- Non-flowering groups: **spore bearers, conifers and algae**.
- **Legumes** have pods, two seed leaves and **nitrogen nodules**; **cereals** have grain and one seed leaf.
- Dispersal agents: **wind, water, animals, explosive mechanism and people**.
- Light seeds with hairs or wings → **wind**; hooked seeds → **animals**; floating seeds → **water**.
- Vegetative propagation gives a plant **exactly like the parent** and it matures faster.
- Cassava is grown from **cuttings**, bananas from **suckers**, onions from **bulbs**, ginger from **rhizomes**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Group of plants that bear cones → conifers.
- Plants that reproduce by spores → ferns, mosses and mushrooms.
- Crop that adds nitrogen to the soil → a legume such as beans or groundnuts.
- Seed dispersed by wind → cotton (light with hairs).
- Seed dispersed by animals → black jack (hooks) or mango (fleshy fruit).
- Plant grown from cuttings → cassava. From suckers → banana.
- One reason for seed dispersal → to avoid overcrowding and competition.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Flowering plants** produce flowers and seeds, for example maize and mango; **non-flowering plants** do not produce flowers, for example ferns and pine.
2. Spore-bearing plants — fern; conifers — pine; algae — spirogyra (or seaweed).
3. **Legumes:** seeds in pods, two seed leaves, root nodules that add nitrogen, rich in protein. **Cereals:** grasses grown for grain, one seed leaf, fibrous roots, rich in carbohydrates. (Any three pairs.)
4. Because their root **nodules contain bacteria that add nitrogen** to the soil, making it fertile again for the next crop.
5. The **stamen** (anther and filament) is the male part and makes **pollen**; the **pistil** (stigma, style and ovary) is the female part and receives pollen and holds the ovules.
6. **Pollination** is the transfer of pollen from the anther to the stigma. After fertilisation the **ovary becomes the fruit** and the ovules become **seeds**.
7. Seed dispersal is the scattering of seeds away from the parent plant. Reasons: to avoid overcrowding; to reduce competition for water, light and food; to spread the plant to new areas; to reduce the spread of pests and diseases (any four).
8. Wind, water, animals (and birds), explosive mechanism, and people.
9. **Wind:** the seed is light and has wings, hairs or a parachute. **Animals:** the fruit is sweet and fleshy so it is eaten, or the seed has hooks that stick to fur and clothes.
10. Plant propagation is the way new plants are produced from old ones. Its two types are **sexual (by seeds)** and **vegetative (asexual)**.
11. Any six: cuttings — cassava; suckers — banana; tubers — Irish potato; bulbs — onion; rhizomes — ginger; runners — grass; grafting — mango; layering — guava.
12. **Advantages:** the new plant is exactly like the parent and it matures faster. **Disadvantages:** diseases are passed on easily and there is no variety among the plants.
13. Any six of: food; cash crops and employment; timber and poles; firewood and charcoal; medicine; raw materials for industries; fodder for animals; shade and windbreaks; protection of the environment and supply of oxygen; tourism.
14. Because the beans are a **legume** whose root nodules **add nitrogen** to the soil, restoring the fertility that the maize used up. This is crop rotation.
15. Cuttings — **cassava**; suckers — **banana**; bulbs — **onion**; rhizomes — **ginger**; tubers — **Irish potato** (or sweet potato).
