---

## 1. MASS AND WEIGHT

| Term | Meaning | Unit | Instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mass** | The **amount of matter** in an object | gramme (g), **kilogramme (kg)**, tonne | **Beam balance**, weighing scale |
| **Weight** | The **pull of gravity** on an object | **newton (N)** | **Spring balance** |

**The difference:** mass **never changes** wherever the object is taken; weight **changes**
because gravity is different in different places — the same object weighs less on the moon.

**Conversions:** 1 kg = 1,000 g · 1 tonne = 1,000 kg

---

## 2. CAPACITY AND VOLUME

| Term | Meaning | Unit | Instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capacity** | How much a container can **hold** | litre (l), millilitre (ml) | Measuring jug, graduated cylinder |
| **Volume** | The **space** a substance occupies | cubic centimetre (cm³), cubic metre (m³), litre | Measuring cylinder, ruler for regular solids |

**The link:** **1 litre = 1,000 cm³** · 1 m³ = 1,000 litres

**Finding volume of a regular solid:** volume of a cuboid = **length × width × height**.
A box 6 cm × 4 cm × 5 cm has a volume of **120 cm³**.

---

## 3. WHY WE NEED STANDARD MEASURES

Before standard units, people used the hand span, the foot, the cubit and the calabash —
but **these differ from person to person**. Standard measures (the kilogramme, the litre,
the metre) are **the same everywhere**, so trade is fair, medicine doses are correct,
buildings fit together and science experiments can be repeated.

---

## 4. FLOATING AND SINKING

**Floating:** Staying on top of a liquid. **Sinking:** Going down to the bottom.

### What decides it

An object **floats** if it is **less dense** than the liquid, and **sinks** if it is **more
dense**. Shape also matters: a flat sheet of iron sinks, but the same iron shaped as a boat
floats because it now holds air and pushes aside more water.

| Float in water | Sink in water |
|---|---|
| Wood, dry paper, plastic bottle with air, ice, oil, cork, a boat | Stone, nail, metal spoon, sand, glass, wet clay |

**Simple experiment:** put a stone, a piece of wood, a nail, a plastic bottle and a cork in
a basin of water. Record which float and which sink, and explain each result.

**Salt water:** an egg sinks in fresh water but **floats in very salty water**, because
adding salt makes the water **denser**.

---

## 5. THE DISPLACEMENT METHOD

Used to find the **volume of an irregular object** such as a stone.

1. Pour water into a measuring cylinder and read the level, for example **50 cm³**.
2. Tie the stone on a string and lower it gently until it is fully covered.
3. Read the new level, for example **68 cm³**.
4. **Volume of the stone = new level − first level = 68 − 50 = 18 cm³.**

The water pushed aside (**displaced**) is equal to the volume of the object.

---

## 6. DENSITY

**Density:** The mass of a substance in a given volume.

**Density = mass ÷ volume** — measured in **g/cm³** (or kg/m³).

### Worked examples

1. *An object has a mass of 90 g and a volume of 30 cm³.*
   Density = 90 ÷ 30 = **3 g/cm³**
2. *A stone of mass 180 g raises water in a cylinder from 40 cm³ to 100 cm³.*
   Volume = 60 cm³ → density = 180 ÷ 60 = **3 g/cm³**
3. *A block has a density of 2 g/cm³ and a volume of 25 cm³. Find its mass.*
   Mass = density × volume = **50 g**

**The rule to remember:** the density of **water is 1 g/cm³**. Anything with a density
**less than 1 floats** in water; anything **more than 1 sinks**.

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Mass** is measured with a **beam balance** in **kg**; **weight** with a **spring balance** in **newtons**.
- Mass never changes; **weight changes with gravity**.
- **1 litre = 1,000 cm³** and 1 kg = 1,000 g.
- **Density = mass ÷ volume**, in **g/cm³**.
- **Water has a density of 1 g/cm³**; less than 1 floats, more than 1 sinks.
- The **displacement method** finds the volume of irregular objects.
- An egg floats in **salty** water because salt makes the water denser.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Instrument for measuring mass → **beam balance**; for weight → **spring balance**.
- The unit of weight → the **newton**.
- 1 litre = **1,000 cm³**.
- Method used to find the volume of a stone → **displacement**.
- Formula for density → **mass ÷ volume**.
- Why an iron sheet sinks but an iron boat floats → the boat's shape holds air, so it displaces more water and is **less dense** overall.
- Why an egg floats in salty water → salt makes the water **denser**.

9. Why does an iron sheet sink while a boat made of iron floats?
10. Describe an experiment to show that an egg floats in salty water.
11. Describe the displacement method for finding the volume of a stone.
12. A stone raises the water level in a cylinder from 30 cm³ to 75 cm³. Find its volume.

14. An object of mass 240 g has a volume of 60 cm³. Find its density.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Mass** is the amount of matter in an object and never changes; **weight** is the pull of gravity on the object and changes from place to place.
2. **Mass** — beam balance, in kilogrammes; **weight** — spring balance, in newtons; **volume of a liquid** — measuring cylinder or jug, in litres or millilitres.
3. **Capacity** is how much a container can hold; **volume** is the space a substance occupies.
4. 3 kg = **3,000 g**; 2.5 litres = **2,500 ml**; 4,000 cm³ = **4 litres**.
5. 8 × 5 × 4 = **160 cm³**
6. Because standard measures are **the same everywhere and never change**, while a hand span or calabash differs from person to person, so trade would be unfair and measurements could not be repeated.
7. An object **floats** if it is **less dense** than the liquid and **sinks** if it is **more dense**. Its **shape** also matters, because a shape that holds air pushes aside more water.
8. **Float:** wood, cork, a plastic bottle with air (also ice, oil). **Sink:** a stone, a nail, a metal spoon (also sand, glass).
9. The flat sheet is **denser than water** so it sinks; the boat shape **holds air and pushes aside much more water**, so the boat as a whole is less dense than water and floats.
10. Put an egg in a glass of **fresh water** — it sinks. Add salt slowly, stirring, and keep watching. As more salt dissolves, the water becomes **denser**, and at some point the **egg rises and floats**. This shows that a liquid's density affects floating.
11. Pour water into a measuring cylinder and **read the level**. Lower the stone gently on a string until it is fully covered. **Read the new level.** Subtract the first reading from the second — the difference is the **volume of the stone**.
12. 75 − 30 = **45 cm³**
13. Density is the mass of a substance in a given volume. **Density = mass ÷ volume**, measured in **g/cm³**.
14. 240 ÷ 60 = **4 g/cm³**
15. Mass = density × volume = 2.5 × 40 = **100 g**

# P.5 QUICK REVISION

- **Mass** (kg, beam balance) ≠ **Weight** (newtons, spring balance)
- **1 litre = 1,000 cm³** · cuboid volume = l × w × h
- Float if **less dense** than water, sink if **more dense** · water = **1 g/cm³**
- **Displacement**: new level − first level = volume
