## 1. WHAT SANITATION IS

**Sanitation:** Keeping the surroundings clean and disposing of human waste and rubbish safely so that people stay healthy.

**Poor sanitation causes:** cholera, typhoid, dysentery, diarrhoea, worms, bilharzia,
hepatitis, jiggers, and flies and bad smells everywhere.

---

## 2. TYPES OF TOILETS AND LATRINES

### (a) The ordinary (conventional) pit latrine

A pit dug in the ground with a **slab** and a **squat hole** with a cover, walls and a roof.

**Advantages:** cheap and easy to build with local materials; needs no water.
**Disadvantages:** smells; attracts flies; fills up and must be replaced; can pollute wells
if too close or too shallow; may collapse in sandy soil or when flooded.

### (b) The VIP latrine

**VIP** stands for **Ventilated Improved Pit** latrine.

**Its special parts:** a **vent pipe** running from the pit up above the roof, covered at
the top with a **fly screen**; a **dark interior**; and a squat hole that is **left
uncovered** so air can flow.

**How it works:** the sun heats the vent pipe, air rises up it and pulls the smell out of
the pit, while fresh air comes in through the door. **Flies** in the dark pit fly towards
the light at the top of the pipe and are trapped by the **screen**.

**Advantages:** almost **no smell**; controls flies; more comfortable to use.
**Disadvantage:** costs more to build than an ordinary latrine.

### (c) The Ecosan toilet

**Ecosan** means **ecological sanitation** — a toilet that turns human waste into manure.

Urine and faeces are **separated**: urine goes into a container through a separate opening,
and faeces fall into a sealed vault where **ash or dry soil** is added after every use.
When one vault is full it is closed and the second is used; after several months the dried
material is safe to use as **manure**.

**Advantages:** no pit to dig deep, so it suits **rocky, sandy, waterlogged or crowded**
areas; provides manure; does not pollute the ground water; no smell if ash is used.
**Disadvantages:** costs more; needs careful use and training; ash must always be
available; some people dislike handling the material.

### (d) The potty

A small **portable container** used by babies and small children who cannot use the
latrine. It must be **emptied into the latrine at once** and washed with clean water and
soap, then dried in the sun. Never empty it in the compound or the bathroom.

### (e) The water closet (flush toilet)

A toilet with a **cistern, a bowl with a water seal, and a handle or button** that flushes
the waste with water into a **septic tank or sewer line**.

**Advantages:** clean, no smell, comfortable, indoors, no flies.
**Disadvantages:** needs **plenty of piped water**; expensive to build and maintain; blocks
easily if wrong things are thrown in; useless where there is no water supply.

---

## 3. IMPORTANCE OF USING A TOILET OR LATRINE

- It stops the spread of diseases such as **cholera, typhoid, dysentery, diarrhoea and worms**.
- It keeps the compound, gardens and water sources **free from human waste**.
- It controls **flies and bad smells**.
- It protects **water sources** from contamination.
- It gives **privacy and dignity**.
- It keeps the home **clean and pleasant** and free from shame.
- It is required by **public health law**; every home should have a latrine.

**Remember the disease path:** faeces → **flies, fingers, food, fluids and fields** → mouth.
Using a latrine and washing hands **breaks that path**.

---

## 4. PROPER USE AND MAINTENANCE

### Proper use

Use the latrine **every time** — never the bush or the compound · **aim into the hole** and
keep the slab clean · **cover the hole** after use in an ordinary latrine (but a VIP hole is
left open) · put used paper in the pit, not on the floor · **add ash** after each use in an
Ecosan toilet · **flush** the water closet after use · **wash your hands with soap and
clean water** afterwards · teach small children to use the potty and empty it into the
latrine.

### Maintenance

**Sweep and scrub** the floor and slab daily · pour water or disinfectant where needed ·
keep a **hand-washing facility with soap** at the door · repair a cracked slab, a broken
door or a leaking roof at once · keep the **vent pipe screen** in good order on a VIP ·
keep the door closed so animals do not enter · **do not throw rubbish, sanitary pads,
polythene or stones into the pit** — they fill it up and block flush toilets · plant grass
or trees around it to reduce dust · when the pit is about **half a metre from full**, close
it and dig a new one.

### Where to site a latrine

At least **30 metres from a water source** and **downhill** from it · at least 6 metres from
the house, downwind · on firm, well-drained ground · away from where children play · with a
clear path so people can use it at night.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sanitation | Keeping surroundings clean and disposing of waste safely |
| Latrine | A pit toilet for disposing of human waste |
| VIP latrine | Ventilated Improved Pit latrine, with a vent pipe and fly screen |
| Ecosan toilet | An ecological toilet that turns waste into manure |
| Vent pipe | The pipe that removes smell from a VIP latrine |
| Fly screen | The mesh at the top of a vent pipe that traps flies |
| Potty | A small portable container used by babies |
| Water closet | A flush toilet that uses water |
| Septic tank | An underground tank where waste from a flush toilet is collected |
| Contamination | The making of something dirty or unsafe |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **VIP = Ventilated Improved Pit** latrine; its vent pipe removes smell and its **fly screen** traps flies.
- A VIP latrine's squat hole is left **uncovered**; an ordinary latrine's is **covered**.
- **Ecosan** separates urine from faeces and uses **ash**, turning waste into **manure**.
- A **water closet** needs plenty of water; a pit latrine needs none.
- A latrine should be at least **30 m from a water source** and downhill from it.
- Always **wash hands with soap** after using the toilet.
- Never throw **polythene, pads or rubbish** into the pit.
- Diseases spread by poor sanitation: **cholera, typhoid, dysentery, diarrhoea, worms**.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Sanitation is keeping the surroundings clean and disposing of human waste and rubbish safely so that people stay healthy.
2. Any five of: cholera, typhoid, dysentery, diarrhoea, worms, bilharzia, hepatitis, jiggers.
3. **Ventilated Improved Pit** latrine.
4. Features: a **vent pipe** from the pit to above the roof, a **fly screen** at the top of the pipe, and a **dark interior** with an uncovered squat hole. The sun heats the vent pipe so the air inside rises and carries the smell out, while fresh air enters through the door.
5. Flies in the dark pit fly towards the **light at the top of the vent pipe** and are trapped by the **fly screen**, where they die.
6. **Advantage:** it is cheap and easy to build with local materials and needs no water. **Disadvantage:** it smells and attracts flies (it also fills up and must be replaced).
7. An Ecosan (ecological sanitation) toilet is one that **separates urine from faeces** and turns the waste into **manure**. **Ash or dry soil** is added after each use.
8. Any three of: it provides manure; it suits rocky, sandy, waterlogged or crowded areas; it does not pollute ground water; there is no smell when ash is used; no deep pit has to be dug.
9. In **rocky, sandy, waterlogged or very crowded** areas, because a deep pit cannot be dug safely there and a pit would collapse or pollute the ground water.
10. A potty is a small portable container used by babies and small children who cannot use the latrine. It must be **emptied into the latrine immediately**, washed with clean water and soap, and dried in the sun — never emptied in the compound.
11. A water closet is a flush toilet with a cistern and a water seal that carries waste to a septic tank or sewer. **Advantages:** it is clean and has no smell; it is comfortable and free from flies. **Disadvantages:** it needs plenty of piped water; it is expensive and blocks easily.
12. Any six of: it prevents diseases such as cholera and worms; it keeps the compound and gardens free of human waste; it controls flies and bad smells; it protects water sources; it gives privacy and dignity; it keeps the home clean; it is required by public health law.
13. Faeces left in the open are carried to food and the mouth by **flies, fingers, food, fluids and fields** — the "five Fs". Using a latrine keeps the faeces **sealed in a pit** so flies and feet cannot carry them, and washing hands with soap removes what is on the fingers, so the path is broken.
14. Any six of: use the latrine every time; aim into the hole and keep the slab clean; cover the hole after use in an ordinary latrine; put used paper in the pit; add ash in an Ecosan toilet; flush a water closet; wash hands with soap afterwards; teach children to use the potty properly.
15. Any six of: sweep and scrub the floor and slab daily; use water or disinfectant; keep soap and water at the door; repair cracks, doors and roofs; keep the vent screen in good order; keep the door closed; do not throw rubbish into the pit; plant grass around it; dig a new pit when it is nearly full.
16. Because they **do not rot**, so they fill the pit quickly and shorten its life, and in a flush toilet they cause **blockages**.
17. At least **30 metres from a water source and downhill from it**, and about 6 metres from the house, downwind, on firm well-drained ground.
18. In a **VIP latrine the squat hole is left uncovered** so that air can flow through the pit and up the vent pipe; in an **ordinary pit latrine the hole is covered** after use to keep out flies and reduce the smell.
19. Because germs from faeces remain on the fingers, and hands touch food, water and other people. **Washing with soap** removes those germs and prevents diseases such as diarrhoea, cholera and worms.
20. It should be **closed and covered with soil** and a **new pit dug** elsewhere; the slab can be moved to the new pit, and the old site should be marked and, in time, planted with trees.
