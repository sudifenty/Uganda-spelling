## 1. ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION

### Definitions

**Environment:** Everything that surrounds us — land, water, air, plants, animals and people.

**Environmental protection:** Taking care of the environment so that it is not damaged.

**Sustainable use:** Using resources carefully today in a way that leaves enough for the people of tomorrow.

**Conservation:** Protecting and looking after natural resources so that they are not used up or destroyed.

### Sustainable use of the environment

**(a) Afforestation and reafforestation**

- **Afforestation** — planting trees in an area where there were no trees before.
- **Reafforestation** — replanting trees in an area where trees were cut down.
- **Agroforestry** — growing trees together with crops and animals on the same land.

**(b) Proper methods of farming**

| Method | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Crop rotation** | Changing the crop grown on a plot each season so the soil is not exhausted |
| **Mulching** | Covering the soil with dry grass or leaves to hold moisture and stop erosion |
| **Terracing** | Cutting steps on a hillside to slow running water |
| **Contour ploughing** | Ploughing across the slope instead of up and down |
| **Strip cropping** | Planting crops in strips along the contour |
| **Applying manure and compost** | Returning fertility to the soil naturally |
| **Controlled grazing (paddocking)** | Preventing overgrazing and bare ground |
| **Fallowing** | Resting the land for a season to recover |
| **Planting cover crops** | Protecting the soil surface from rain and sun |

**(c) Alternative energy use**

Instead of cutting trees for firewood and charcoal, people can use **solar energy,
biogas, hydro-electric power, wind energy, energy-saving stoves and briquettes** made from
waste. These save trees, produce less smoke and are cheaper in the long run.

**(d) Other conservation measures**

Gazetting **forest reserves, national parks and wetlands** · protecting **water catchment
areas** · fighting **poaching** · controlling **bush burning** · educating the community ·
making and enforcing **environmental laws** · planting trees on public land and at school.

## 2. WASTE MANAGEMENT

### Definition

**Waste management:** The collecting, sorting, treating and disposing of rubbish and other waste in a safe way.

### Types of waste

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Biodegradable (rots)** | Food remains, peelings, leaves, paper, animal dung |
| **Non-biodegradable (does not rot)** | Polythene bags (buveera), plastic bottles, glass, metal, batteries |
| **Liquid waste** | Sewage, waste water from homes and factories |
| **Industrial and medical waste** | Chemicals, oils, used syringes and drugs |

### The good ways of handling waste

- **Reduce** — buy and use less of what becomes rubbish, especially polythene.
- **Reuse** — use containers, bottles, sacks and jerrycans again.
- **Recycle** — turn waste into new things: plastic into basins, paper into new paper, metal into tools, old tyres into sandals.
- **Composting** — rot food waste and peelings into manure for the garden.
- **Sorting** — separate waste that rots from waste that does not.
- **Sanitary landfills** — bury waste in a prepared pit far from homes and water.
- **Incineration** — burn medical and dangerous waste in a proper incinerator.
- **Sewage treatment** — treat sewage in ponds or plants before it reaches rivers and lakes.
- **Using latrines and toilets** properly.
- **Community clean-up days** and public rubbish skips.

### Dangers of poor waste disposal

Blocked drainage and **flooding** · **diseases** such as cholera, typhoid, dysentery and
malaria · **pollution** of soil, water and air · bad smells · injuries from broken glass
and metal · death of animals and fish that swallow polythene · ugly surroundings that
drive away tourists.

## 3. THE NATIONAL ENVIRONMENT MANAGEMENT AUTHORITY (NEMA)

### What it is

**NEMA** is the Ugandan government body responsible for the management of the environment.
It was set up under the National Environment Act and started work in **1995**, with its
offices in Kampala. Other East African countries have similar bodies — Kenya has **NEMA**
too, and Tanzania has **NEMC**.

### Functions of NEMA

- **Coordinating and supervising** all environmental activities in the country.
- Making and enforcing **environmental standards and regulations**.
- Carrying out **environmental impact assessments** before big projects such as factories, dams and roads are built.
- **Monitoring** pollution of air, water and land.
- Protecting **wetlands, forests, river banks and lake shores**.
- **Educating the public** about the environment.
- **Advising government** on environmental policy.
- **Punishing offenders** who break environmental laws.

## 4. CLIMATE CHANGE

### Definition

**Climate change:** A long-term change in the normal climate of an area, such as rain becoming less reliable and temperatures rising.

### Causes

**Deforestation** · **bush burning** · **swamp reclamation** · smoke from **factories and
vehicles** · **overgrazing** · mining and quarrying · burning of fossil fuels · poor
disposal of waste.

### Signs and effects in East Africa

- Rains that come **late, stop early or fail** altogether.
- **Longer droughts**, especially in Karamoja and northern Kenya.
- **Heavier storms and floods**, as in Kasese, Bududa and Kampala.
- **Melting of the ice** on Mt Kilimanjaro and Rwenzori.
- Lakes and rivers **drying up**; falling water levels affect hydro-electric power.
- **Famine** and loss of pasture; conflicts over water and grazing land.
- More **crop pests and diseases**.

---

## 5. CONSEQUENCES OF IRRESPONSIBLE LIVING IN THE EAST AFRICAN ENVIRONMENT

| Consequence | How it happens |
|---|---|
| **Floods** | Wetlands are filled in and drainage channels are blocked with rubbish |
| **Mud and landslides** | Farming on steep slopes after clearing the trees, as at Bududa |
| **Desertification** | Overgrazing, tree cutting and bush burning turn land into semi-desert, as in parts of Karamoja |
| **Pollution of land, water and air** | Factory waste, sewage, polythene, chemicals and smoke |
| **Diseases** | Cholera and typhoid from dirty water; malaria from stagnant water; coughs from smoke and dust |
| **Resource depletion** | Over-fishing, over-mining and over-cutting of forests leave nothing for the future |
| **Over production of children** | A very fast-growing population puts great pressure on land, forests, water and services |
| **Loss of wildlife** | Poaching and destruction of habitats |
| **Soil erosion and loss of fertility** | Bare soil is washed away by rain |
| **Silting of lakes and rivers** | Soil washed from gardens fills up the water bodies |

## 6. SOLUTIONS TO THE ENVIRONMENTAL PROBLEMS

- **Afforestation and reafforestation**; every school and home planting trees.
- Using **energy-saving stoves, biogas and solar** instead of firewood and charcoal.
- **Proper farming methods** — terracing, mulching, crop rotation, contour ploughing.
- **Protecting wetlands, river banks and lake shores** and stopping building in them.
- **Proper waste management** — reduce, reuse, recycle, compost, and use sanitary landfills.
- **Treating sewage and factory waste** before releasing it.
- **Enforcing environmental laws** through NEMA, local councils and the police.
- **Environmental education** in schools, on radio and in the community.
- **Family planning education** to slow very rapid population growth.
- **Resettling people** from dangerous slopes and flood-prone areas.
- **Controlled grazing** and better livestock management.
- Joint action across East Africa through the **EAC**, since air, lakes and rivers are shared.

### What a P.6 learner can do

Plant and water a tree · use both sides of paper · pick up and sort rubbish · never burn
polythene in the compound · use a latrine · switch off lights and use water sparingly ·
join the **school environment club** · tell others what you have learnt.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Afforestation** = new trees where there were none; **reafforestation** = replanting where trees were cut.
- The three Rs of waste: **Reduce, Reuse, Recycle**.
- **Polythene (buveera)** does not rot — it blocks drainage, kills animals and spoils soil.
- **NEMA** manages the environment in Uganda and started work in **1995**.
- NEMA carries out an **environmental impact assessment** before big projects are built.
- Causes of climate change: **deforestation, bush burning, swamp drainage, factory and vehicle smoke**.
- Landslides at **Bududa** came from farming on steep, cleared slopes.
- Wetlands are **filters and sponges** — they clean water and hold floods.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. The environment is everything that surrounds us — land, water, air, plants, animals and people. Conservation is protecting and looking after natural resources so that they are not used up or destroyed.
2. **Afforestation** is planting trees where there were none before, while **reafforestation** is replanting trees in an area where trees were cut down.
3. Any five of: crop rotation, mulching, terracing, contour ploughing, strip cropping, applying manure, controlled grazing, fallowing, planting cover crops.
4. Any four of: solar energy, biogas, hydro-electric power, wind energy, energy-saving stoves, briquettes.
5. Waste management is the collecting, sorting, treating and disposing of rubbish and other waste in a safe way.
6. **Biodegradable** waste rots naturally, for example food remains and leaves. **Non-biodegradable** waste does not rot, for example polythene bags and glass.
7. **Reduce** — use less of what becomes rubbish; **Reuse** — use items such as bottles and sacks again; **Recycle** — make waste into new useful things such as plastic basins from old plastic.
8. Any four of: blocked drainage and flooding; diseases such as cholera and malaria; pollution of soil, water and air; bad smells; injuries from broken glass; death of animals that swallow polythene; ugly surroundings.
9. **National Environment Management Authority**; it started work in **1995**.
10. Any five of: coordinating environmental activities; making and enforcing standards; carrying out environmental impact assessments; monitoring pollution; protecting wetlands and forests; educating the public; advising government; punishing offenders.
11. Climate change is a long-term change in the normal climate of an area. Causes: deforestation, bush burning, swamp reclamation, smoke from factories and vehicles, overgrazing (any four).
12. Any four of: unreliable rainfall; longer droughts; heavier storms and floods; melting of ice on Kilimanjaro and Rwenzori; drying lakes and rivers; famine; more crop pests.
13. Any five of: floods, landslides, desertification, pollution of land, water and air, diseases, resource depletion, pressure from a very fast-growing population, loss of wildlife, soil erosion, silting of lakes.
14. Because a wetland holds water like a sponge. When it is filled in and built on, the water has nowhere to go, so the area **floods**; the houses stand on weak, waterlogged ground; the wetland can no longer filter water or provide fish, papyrus and water for the community.
15. **Causes:** cutting trees and farming on steep slopes; heavy prolonged rain on loose soil (also digging and building on steep ground). **Prevention:** plant trees and grass on steep slopes; make terraces and dig contour trenches; avoid cultivating and settling on very steep land; resettle people from dangerous slopes.
16. Desertification is the spread of desert conditions into land that was not desert before. It affects **Karamoja in Uganda and northern Kenya** most (also parts of central Tanzania).
17. Any six of: afforestation and reafforestation; energy-saving stoves, biogas and solar; proper farming methods; protecting wetlands and river banks; proper waste management; treating sewage and factory waste; enforcing laws through NEMA; environmental education; family planning education; resettling people from dangerous areas; joint EAC action.
18. Any four of: plant and water a tree; use both sides of paper; pick up and sort rubbish; never burn polythene; use a latrine; save water and switch off lights; join the school environment club; teach others.
19. Because the environment is **shared** — Lake Victoria, the River Nile, the air and the migrating wildlife cross borders. Pollution or deforestation in one country affects the others, so laws, research and clean-up efforts work best when the countries act together through the EAC.
