## 1. COLLECTING AND TABULATING DATA

### Definitions

**Data:** Information that has been collected.

**Tally:** A mark used when counting; every fifth mark crosses the previous four.

### Sources of data

Asking people (interviews and questionnaires) · counting and measuring · school registers ·
records and reports · observation.

### A frequency table

*Number of pupils and their favourite fruits in a class of 30:*

| Fruit | Tally | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Mango | ⑤ ⑤ | 10 |
| Orange | ⑤ ⏐⏐⏐ | 8 |
| Banana | ⑤ ⏐⏐ | 7 |
| Pineapple | ⑤ | 5 |
| **Total** | | **30** |

## 2. PIE CHARTS

### Definition

**Pie chart:** A circle divided into sectors, each showing part of the whole. The whole circle is **360°**.

**Angle of a sector = (part ÷ total) × 360°**
**Part = (angle ÷ 360°) × total**

### Worked example 1

Using the fruit table above, find the angle for each fruit.

| Fruit | Working | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Mango | 10/30 × 360 | **120°** |
| Orange | 8/30 × 360 | **96°** |
| Banana | 7/30 × 360 | **84°** |
| Pineapple | 5/30 × 360 | **60°** |
| **Total** | | **360°** ✓ |

### Worked example 2

*A pie chart shows how a farmer uses 72 hectares. Maize takes 150°, beans 90°, coffee 80°
and the rest is grazing land. Find the area under maize and the angle for grazing.*

- Maize = 150/360 × 72 = **30 hectares**
- Grazing angle = 360 − (150 + 90 + 80) = **40°**

## 3. LINE GRAPHS

### Definition

**Line graph:** A graph in which points are plotted and joined with straight lines to show how something changes.

### How to draw one

Draw the two axes and label them · choose a suitable **scale** and state it · plot the
points carefully · join them with straight lines · give the graph a **title**.

### Reading a line graph

*A line graph shows a shop's sales: Monday 20 bags, Tuesday 35, Wednesday 15, Thursday 40, Friday 30.*

- Highest sales: **Thursday** (40 bags)
- Lowest sales: **Wednesday** (15 bags)
- Total for the week: 20 + 35 + 15 + 40 + 30 = **140 bags**
- Mean per day: 140 ÷ 5 = **28 bags**
- Increase from Wednesday to Thursday: 40 − 15 = **25 bags**

## 4. SIMPLE STATISTICS

| Word | Meaning | How to find it |
|---|---|---|
| **Mean (average)** | The typical value | Total ÷ number of values |
| **Median** | The middle value | Arrange in order, take the middle |
| **Mode** | The commonest value | The one appearing most often |
| **Range** | The spread | Biggest − smallest |

### Worked example

*Find the mean, median, mode and range of: 12, 15, 11, 15, 17.*

- In order: 11, 12, 15, 15, 17
- **Mean** = (12 + 15 + 11 + 15 + 17) ÷ 5 = 70 ÷ 5 = **14**
- **Median** = **15** (the middle value)
- **Mode** = **15** (appears twice)
- **Range** = 17 − 11 = **6**

### When there is an even number of values

*Median of 4, 8, 10, 14* → (8 + 10) ÷ 2 = **9**

### Working backwards

*The mean mark of 6 pupils is 55. Five of them scored 50, 60, 45, 70 and 52. Find the sixth mark.*
Total = 55 × 6 = 330. Sum of the five = 277. Sixth = 330 − 277 = **53**

## 5. PROBABILITY

**Probability = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total number of outcomes**

- *A bag has 5 red and 7 yellow beads. P(red) = **5/12***
- *A die is thrown once. P(a number greater than 4) = 2/6 = **1/3***
- *From the fruit table, P(a pupil likes mangoes) = 10/30 = **1/3***

Probability is always between **0** (impossible) and **1** (certain).

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Data | Information that has been collected |
| Tally | A mark used when counting |
| Frequency | How many times something occurs |
| Pie chart | A circle divided into sectors showing parts of a whole |
| Line graph | A graph of plotted points joined by lines |
| Scale | What one unit on the graph stands for |
| Mean | The total divided by the number of values |
| Median | The middle value when data are arranged in order |
| Mode | The value that occurs most often |
| Range | The biggest value minus the smallest |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The angles of a pie chart add up to **360°**.
- Sector angle = part/total × 360°.
- Always **arrange the data in order** before finding the median.
- The **range is one number** — a subtraction.
- Total = mean × number of items.
- A line graph must have a **title, labelled axes and a stated scale**.
- Probability lies between 0 and 1.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- 10 out of 30 on a pie chart → 120°.
- If a sector is 150° out of 72 hectares, the part = 30 hectares.
- Mean of 12, 15, 11, 15, 17 = 14; median 15; mode 15; range 6.
- Median of an even set = average of the **two** middle values.
- P(red) from 5 red and 7 yellow = 5/12.
- The missing value = (mean × number) − sum of the others.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Using 100 instead of **360** for pie-chart angles.
- Finding the median without arranging the data first.
- Saying the range is "11 to 17" instead of **6**.
- Forgetting to label the axes or state the scale on a graph.
- Adding the frequencies wrongly so the total is not the whole.

# REVISION QUESTIONS

1. What is data, and give any three sources of data?
2. What is the total of all the angles on a pie chart?
3. In a class of 40 pupils, 15 like football. Find the angle of the football sector on a pie chart.
4. A pie chart shows 90 hectares of land. If maize takes 120°, find the area under maize.
5. On a pie chart of a 720,000-shilling budget, food takes 160°, rent 100°, fees 60° and savings the rest. Find the amount saved.
6. Find the mean of 14, 18, 22, 26, 30.
7. Find the median of 9, 4, 7, 12, 5.
8. Find the mode of 3, 7, 3, 8, 9, 3, 7.
9. Find the range of 45, 12, 38, 27, 50.
10. Find the median of 8, 12, 16, 20.
11. The mean of five numbers is 32. Four of them are 28, 35, 30 and 40. Find the fifth.
12. A shop sold 20, 35, 15, 40 and 30 bags from Monday to Friday. Find the total and the mean daily sales.
13. Using question 12, on which day were sales highest, and by how much did sales rise from Wednesday to Thursday?
14. A bag has 6 blue and 9 white beads. Find the probability of picking a blue bead.
15. A number is picked at random from 1 to 20. Find the probability that it is a multiple of 4.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Data is information that has been collected. Sources: interviews and questionnaires, counting and measuring, school registers, records and reports, observation (any three).
2. **360°**
3. 15/40 × 360 = **135°**
4. 120/360 × 90 = **30 hectares**
5. Savings angle = 360 − (160 + 100 + 60) = 40°. Savings = 40/360 × 720,000 = **80,000 shillings**
6. (14 + 18 + 22 + 26 + 30) ÷ 5 = 110 ÷ 5 = **22**
7. In order: 4, 5, 7, 9, 12 → median = **7**
8. **3** (it appears three times)
9. 50 − 12 = **38**
10. (12 + 16) ÷ 2 = **14**
11. Total = 32 × 5 = 160. 160 − (28 + 35 + 30 + 40) = 160 − 133 = **27**
12. Total = **140 bags**; mean = 140 ÷ 5 = **28 bags**
13. Highest on **Thursday**; the rise from Wednesday to Thursday was 40 − 15 = **25 bags**
14. Total = 15. P(blue) = 6/15 = **2/5**
15. Multiples of 4 from 1 to 20: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 → 5 out of 20. P = **1/4**

# P.6 QUICK REVISION

- Pie chart: angle = part/total × **360°**
- Line graph: title · labelled axes · stated scale
- Mean = total ÷ number · Median = middle (**in order**) · Mode = most often · Range = biggest − smallest
- Total = **mean × number of items**
- Probability = wanted ÷ total, between 0 and 1
