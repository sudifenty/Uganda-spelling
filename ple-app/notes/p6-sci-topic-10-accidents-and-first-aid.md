## 1. FIRST AID

### Definitions

**Accident:** An unexpected happening that causes injury or damage.

**First aid:** The immediate help given to an injured or suddenly sick person before a health worker arrives or before the person reaches hospital.

**First aider:** The person who gives first aid.

### Aims of first aid

To **save life** · to **prevent the injury getting worse** · to **relieve pain** · to
**promote recovery** · to get the person to proper medical care quickly.

### Qualities of a good first aider

Calm and confident · quick but careful · **clean** hands and materials · kind and
comforting · observant · knows his or her limits and does not try what they cannot do ·
sends for help early.

### A first-aid kit should contain

Cotton wool · **bandages and gauze** · plaster · a pair of scissors · **antiseptic** (such
as iodine or Dettol) · gloves · a thermometer · safety pins · clean water · a small torch ·
**ORS sachets** · pain tablets kept out of children's reach · a splint · a notebook and pen.

## 2. BURNS AND SCALDS

| Term | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Burn** | Dry heat — fire, a hot iron, hot metal, electricity, chemicals, the sun |
| **Scald** | **Moist heat** — hot water, hot porridge, steam, hot oil |

### First aid

1. Remove the person from the source of heat, and **switch off electricity** first if it is an electrical burn.
2. **Cool the burnt part with clean cool running water for about 10–20 minutes.**
3. Remove tight clothing and jewellery near the burn **before it swells**, but never pull off cloth that is stuck to the skin.
4. Cover with a **clean, dry, non-fluffy cloth** or a clean polythene sheet.
5. Give the person plenty of fluids to drink if they are fully awake.
6. Take them to a health centre — always for a large, deep, electrical or chemical burn, or a burn on the face, hands or private parts.

**Never** apply **cow dung, engine oil, paraffin, mud, butter, toothpaste or ash** · never
**burst blisters** · never use ice · never delay in taking a serious case to hospital.

### Preventing burns and scalds

Keep small children away from the **kitchen and fireplace** · turn saucepan handles inwards ·
put out fires properly · keep paraffin, petrol and matches locked away · do not overload
electric sockets · put out candles before sleeping · keep hot drinks away from the edge of
the table.

## 3. FEVER AND CONVULSIONS

### Fever

**Fever:** A body temperature higher than normal (normal is about **37 °C**).

**Causes:** malaria, flu, measles, typhoid, ear or throat infection, pneumonia.

**First aid:** move the person to a **cool, airy place** · remove heavy clothing ·
**sponge the body with a cloth and lukewarm water** (not cold water) · give plenty of
**fluids** · give the prescribed dose of fever medicine if available · **take the person
for a malaria test** and treatment quickly, especially a child.

### Convulsions (fits)

**Convulsions:** Sudden uncontrollable jerking of the body, often caused by very high fever in a child, epilepsy, or an infection such as cerebral malaria or meningitis.

**First aid:**

1. Stay calm and **call for help**.
2. Lay the person on the **floor on their side** in a safe place, away from fire, water and sharp objects.
3. **Loosen tight clothing** around the neck.
4. Put something soft under the head; do **not** hold the person down.
5. **Do not put anything into the mouth** — no spoon, no cloth, no fingers.
6. Cool a feverish child by sponging with lukewarm water once the jerking stops.
7. When the fit ends, keep the person on their side, cover them, and take them to a health centre **at once**.

**Never:** never pour cold water on the person, never force medicine or drink into the
mouth during a fit, never use traditional practices that hurt the child, never leave them
alone.

## 4. DROWNING AND NEAR DROWNING

**Drowning:** Death caused by water filling the lungs so that a person cannot breathe.

### First aid

1. **Do not jump in unless you are a strong swimmer** — many rescuers drown too.
2. Instead **shout for help** and reach out with a long stick, a rope, a jerrycan, a floating branch or a tyre.
3. Once the person is out, lay them on their **side** so water and vomit can drain.
4. Check whether they are **breathing**. If they are, keep them warm and comfortable and turn them on their side.
5. If they are not breathing, start rescue breathing/**chest compressions** if you have been trained, and keep sending for help.
6. Remove wet clothes, cover them, and take them to hospital **even if they seem well** — water in the lungs can cause problems hours later.

### Prevention

Learn to **swim** · never swim alone or in flooded rivers · do not overload boats and always
wear a **life jacket** · never play near wells, ponds or water tanks · **cover wells and
tanks** · watch small children near any water, even a basin or a jerrycan · do not swim
after a heavy meal or when it is stormy.

## 5. FAINTING

**Fainting:** A short loss of consciousness caused by too little blood reaching the brain.

**Causes:** standing too long in the sun or at assembly, hunger, tiredness, shock, fear,
pain, heat, illness, sudden bad news.

**Signs before fainting:** dizziness, pale skin, sweating, blurred vision, weakness.

### First aid

1. Lay the person **flat on their back** in a cool, airy place.
2. **Raise the legs** slightly above the level of the head so blood flows to the brain.
3. **Loosen tight clothing** at the neck and waist.
4. Give **fresh air** — ask the crowd to move back and open windows.
5. Sponge the face with cool water.
6. When they wake, let them rest and then give **water or a sweet drink** slowly — never
   force anything into the mouth while they are unconscious.
7. Take them to a health centre if they do not recover quickly or if fainting is repeated.

## 6. FOREIGN BODIES IN PASSAGES

**Foreign body:** Anything that enters the body where it does not belong.

| Passage | Examples | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Ear** | Insect, seed, bead | For an insect, pour a little clean cooking oil or warm water to float it out. For a solid object, **do not poke** — take the person to a health worker |
| **Nose** | Bead, seed, paper | Ask the person to breathe through the mouth and blow the nose gently while closing the free nostril. **Never poke with a stick or pin** |
| **Eye** | Dust, insect, eyelash | Do not rub. Wash out with clean water; lift the upper lid over the lower one. If it is stuck or is a chemical, **wash with plenty of clean water and go to hospital** |
| **Throat (choking)** | Food, sweet, coin | Encourage coughing; bend a child forward and give firm **back blows between the shoulder blades**; get help at once |
| **Skin** | Thorn, splinter, jigger | Clean the area, remove with clean tweezers or a sterile needle, then apply antiseptic |
| **Stomach (swallowed)** | Coin, battery, drugs, paraffin | **Do not make the person vomit**, especially for paraffin or chemicals. Take them to hospital immediately with the container if there is one |

**The golden rule:** if the object is not easy and safe to remove, **take the person to a
health worker** — trying too hard usually pushes it deeper and causes injury.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- First aid aims to **save life, prevent things getting worse and relieve pain**.
- **Burn = dry heat; scald = moist heat.**
- Cool a burn with **clean cool water for 10–20 minutes**; never apply oil, dung or ash.
- Normal body temperature is about **37 °C**.
- During convulsions: lay the person **on the side**, loosen clothing, and **put nothing in the mouth**.
- For drowning: **reach, do not jump in** unless you can swim well; lay the person on the side.
- For fainting: lay the person flat and **raise the legs**.
- Never make a person vomit after swallowing **paraffin or chemicals**.
- If in doubt, **take the person to a health worker**.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Injury caused by hot water or steam → a scald.
- First thing to do for a burn → cool it with clean cool water.
- Why we do not put anything in the mouth during a fit → the person may bite, choke or break teeth.
- Position for a person who has nearly drowned → on their **side**.
- First aid for fainting → lay them flat and raise the legs.
- Insect in the ear → pour a little cooking oil to float it out.
- Swallowed paraffin → **do not** cause vomiting; go to hospital at once.
- Three items in a first-aid kit → cotton wool, bandage, antiseptic.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. First aid is the immediate help given to an injured or suddenly sick person before a health worker arrives or before the person reaches hospital. A **first aider** is the person who gives it.
2. Any four of: to save life; to prevent the injury from getting worse; to relieve pain; to promote recovery; to get the person quickly to proper medical care.
3. Any five of: calm and confident; quick but careful; clean; kind and comforting; observant; knows his or her limits; sends for help early.
4. Any eight of: cotton wool, bandages, gauze, plaster, scissors, antiseptic, gloves, a thermometer, safety pins, clean water, a torch, ORS, a splint, a notebook and pen.
5. A **burn** is caused by dry heat, for example fire or a hot iron; a **scald** is caused by moist heat, for example hot water or steam.
6. Remove the person from the source of heat (switching off electricity if needed) → cool the part with clean cool water for 10–20 minutes → remove tight clothing near the burn before swelling → cover with a clean dry non-fluffy cloth → give fluids if the person is fully awake → take them to a health centre if the burn is large, deep or on the face or hands.
7. Any four of: cow dung, engine oil, paraffin, mud, butter, toothpaste, ash, ice.
8. Any four of: keep children away from the fireplace and kitchen; turn saucepan handles inwards; put out fires properly; lock away paraffin, petrol and matches; do not overload sockets; put out candles before sleeping; keep hot drinks away from table edges.
9. Fever is a body temperature higher than normal. The normal body temperature is about **37 °C**.
10. Move the child to a cool airy place → remove heavy clothing → **sponge with lukewarm water** → give plenty of fluids → give the correct dose of fever medicine if available → take the child for a malaria test and treatment quickly.
11. Convulsions are sudden uncontrollable jerking of the body. Causes: very high fever in a child, epilepsy, cerebral malaria or meningitis (any three).
12. **Do:** stay calm and call for help; lay the person on the floor **on their side** away from fire, water and sharp objects; loosen tight clothing; put something soft under the head; after the fit, keep them on their side and take them to a health centre. **Do not:** put anything in the mouth; hold the person down or pour cold water on them.
13. Reach out to the person with a stick, rope or floating object; once out, lay them **on their side** so water drains; check breathing; keep them warm; if they are not breathing, give rescue breathing if trained and keep calling for help; take them to hospital. A **weak swimmer should not jump in** because the drowning person panics and clings, and both can drown.
14. Any five of: learn to swim; never swim alone or in flooded rivers; do not overload boats and wear life jackets; never play near wells or tanks; cover wells and tanks; watch small children near any water; do not swim after a heavy meal or in a storm.
15. Fainting is a short loss of consciousness caused by too little blood reaching the brain. Causes: standing too long in the sun, hunger, tiredness, shock or fear (any three). **First aid:** lay the person flat in a cool airy place, raise the legs, loosen tight clothing, allow fresh air, sponge the face, and give water when they wake.
16. Because raising the legs helps **blood flow back to the brain**, which is what was lacking, so the person recovers quickly.
17. A foreign body is anything that enters the body where it does not belong. **Insect in the ear:** pour a little clean cooking oil or warm water to float it out, then see a health worker if it remains. **Dust in the eye:** do not rub; wash it out with clean water, or lift the upper eyelid over the lower one.
18. The child should be taken to hospital **immediately**, carrying the container if possible, and kept calm and on their side. **Never make the child vomit**, because paraffin coming back up can enter the lungs and cause serious damage.
19. Encourage the person to **cough**; bend a child forward and give firm **back blows between the shoulder blades**; keep checking the mouth and remove anything you can see easily; call for help at once if the object does not come out.
20. Because water that entered the lungs can cause **breathing problems and infection several hours later**, so the person must be checked by a health worker even if they seem to have recovered.

# P.6 QUICK REVISION

- First aid: **save life · stop it getting worse · relieve pain · get help**
- **Burn = dry heat · Scald = moist heat** · cool with **water 10–20 min**, never oil or dung
- Fever: cool room, **lukewarm sponging**, fluids, malaria test
- Convulsions: **on the side**, loosen clothes, **nothing in the mouth**
- Drowning: **reach, don't jump** · lay on the side · hospital even if well
- Fainting: lie flat, **raise the legs**, fresh air
- Swallowed paraffin: **do not make them vomit** — hospital now
