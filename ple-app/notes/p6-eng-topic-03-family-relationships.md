# PRIMARY 6 ENGLISH

# TOPIC 3: FAMILY RELATIONSHIPS

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Six Curriculum, Set One, English Topic 3, 24 periods
(`curriculum/P6-SET-ONE-2010.pdf`, topic outline page 24 and pages 33–36).*

**What you already know:** You can name the members of your family. In P.6 you learn the
**full family vocabulary in English**, how to draw and read a **family tree**, and the
grammar of **prepositions, adverbs (*just* and *already*) and adjectives**.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- describe family relationships using the correct words
- explain the relationship between any two members of a family
- draw and interpret a family tree
- use prepositions, the adverbs *just* and *already*, and adjectives correctly
- read and interpret information about population
- write compositions and poems about the family

## 1. FAMILY VOCABULARY

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **grandparent** | The parent of your father or mother |
| **stepmother** | The wife your father married after your own mother |
| **stepfather** | The husband your mother married after your own father |
| **stepbrother / stepsister** | The son or daughter of your stepparent, not related to you by blood |
| **half-brother / half-sister** | A brother or sister with whom you share **one** parent only |
| **uncle** | The brother of your father or mother (also your aunt's husband) |
| **auntie (aunt)** | The sister of your father or mother (also your uncle's wife) |
| **cousin** | The child of your uncle or aunt |
| **nephew** | The son of your brother or sister |
| **niece** | The daughter of your brother or sister |
| **brother-in-law** | Your sister's husband, or your husband's or wife's brother |
| **sister-in-law** | Your brother's wife, or your husband's or wife's sister |
| **mother-in-law / father-in-law** | The mother or father of your husband or wife |
| **twins** | Two children born at the same birth |
| **triplets** | Three children born at the same birth |
| **quadruplets** | Four children born at the same birth |

### Other words in the syllabus list

**elder** (older, of people in the same family) · **eldest** (the oldest of all) ·
**marry** · **give birth to** · **tribe** · **populated** · **densely** · **scarcely** ·
and the serious words **defile**, **rape** and **incest**, which name **crimes against a
person** — any child who is threatened in this way must **tell a trusted adult at once**,
and it is never the child's fault.

## 2. TALKING ABOUT RELATIONSHIPS

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Who is your mother's sister?* | She is my **aunt**. |
| *What do you call your aunt's son?* | He is my **cousin**. |
| *What is your sister's daughter to you?* | She is my **niece**. |
| *What is your brother's wife to you?* | She is my **sister-in-law**. |
| *What is your father's father to you?* | He is my **grandfather**. |
| *What are you to your grandmother?* | I am her **grandson / granddaughter**. |
| *What is your mother to your father's mother?* | She is her **daughter-in-law**. |

**A useful sentence pattern:** *Marsha is Maria's sister.* · *Peter is Ann's uncle.* ·
*Sarah and Sam are twins.*

---

## 3. THE FAMILY TREE

**Family tree:** A drawing showing the members of a family and how they are related.

```
            Grandfather = Grandmother
                        |
        -----------------------------------
        |                |                |
     Father = Mother   Uncle = Aunt     Aunt
        |                     |
   -----------            ---------
   |         |            |       |
  Me     Sister         Cousin  Cousin
```

**How to read it:** the **equals sign (=)** shows a marriage · a **vertical line** joins
parents to their children · people on the **same level** are of the same generation.

**Questions a family tree can answer:** Who is the eldest child? · How many grandchildren
are there? · What is Cousin A to Me? (a cousin) · Who is Me's aunt? · How many people are in
the third generation?

## 4. GRAMMAR

### (a) Prepositions

Prepositions show position, direction, time or relationship: *in, on, at, to, from, of,
with, between, among, beside, near, under, above, into, over, for, since*.

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| **married to** (not "married with") | *My aunt is married **to** a teacher.* |
| **between** two, **among** many | *He sat **between** his parents and **among** his cousins.* |
| **at** a small place, **in** a bigger one | *He lives **at** Kabale, **in** Uganda.* |
| **on** a day, **in** a month, **at** a time | *We travel **on** Monday, **in** June, **at** 6 o'clock.* |
| **related to** | *Musa is **related to** me.* |

### (b) The adverbs *just* and *already*

| Adverb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **just** | A very short time ago | *My uncle has **just** arrived.* |
| **already** | Sooner than expected; before now | *My cousins have **already** eaten.* |

Both are used with the **present perfect** (*has/have* + past participle), and they come
**between the helping verb and the main verb**.

### (c) Adjectives: *young, old, elder, eldest*

| Word | Use |
|---|---|
| **young / old** | General: *a young child, an old man* |
| **younger / older than** | Comparing two people: *Ann is **older than** Peter.* |
| **elder / eldest** | Only for people in the same family, and used before a noun: *my **elder** brother, the **eldest** son* |

**Careful:** we say *Ann is **older than** Peter*, never "elder than".

## 5. POPULATION LANGUAGE

The syllabus asks you to read maps, charts and graphs about population.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Populated** | Having people living there |
| **Densely populated** | Having **many** people in a small area |
| **Sparsely / scarcely populated** | Having **few** people in a large area |
| **Population** | The number of people in a place |

*Examples:* *Kampala is **densely** populated because of jobs and services.* ·
*Karamoja is **sparsely** populated because it is dry.*

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Nephew** = brother's or sister's **son**; **niece** = their **daughter**.
- **Cousin** = the child of an uncle or aunt.
- **Half-brother** shares **one parent**; a **stepbrother** shares **none**.
- **-in-law** words come from **marriage**.
- Twins **2**, triplets **3**, quadruplets **4**.
- Say **married to**, not "married with".
- **Elder/eldest** are used only inside a family and **before a noun**; use **older than** when comparing.
- *Just* and *already* go **between the helping verb and the main verb**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- Your father's brother → your **uncle**. His child → your **cousin**.
- Your sister's daughter → your **niece**.
- Your brother's wife → your **sister-in-law**.
- Three children born at the same birth → **triplets**.
- *My uncle has ____ arrived* (a moment ago) → **just**.
- *They have ____ finished eating* → **already**.
- *My aunt is married ____ a doctor.* → **to**
- An area with very many people → **densely populated**.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing *"married with"* instead of **married to**.
- Saying *"elder than"* instead of **older than**.
- Confusing **nephew** (boy) with **niece** (girl).
- Confusing a **half-brother** with a **stepbrother**.
- Putting *just* and *already* in the wrong place: *"He has arrived just"* → *He **has just** arrived.*
- Using *between* for more than two people.
