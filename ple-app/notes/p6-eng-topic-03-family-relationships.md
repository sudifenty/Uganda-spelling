**What you already know:** You can name the members of your family. In this topic
you master the **full family vocabulary in English** — every relationship including
marriage, step and half relationships, twins and multiple births — plus family
size, family trees, good relationships, values, culture, safety, and the grammar
and language structures of family talk.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- describe family relationships appropriately and differentiate family members
- interpret family trees
- use prepositions, verbs, adverbs and adjectives correctly
- use the structures **rather…than, too…to, just, such…that** and **is…but…is**

## A. INTRODUCTION TO FAMILY RELATIONSHIPS

**1. What is a family?**

A family is a group of people who are related to one another by birth, marriage or
adoption.

**2. What is a family relationship?**

A family relationship is the connection between members of a family.

**3. Why are family relationships important?**

Family relationships are important because family members provide love, care,
support, guidance and protection to one another.

**4. What makes a good family relationship?**

A good family relationship is built on love, care, respect, trust, forgiveness,
cooperation and good communication.

**5. What is a family member?**

A family member is a person who belongs to a particular family.

**6. What is a nuclear family?**

A nuclear family is a family consisting mainly of parents and their children.

**7. What is an extended family?**

An extended family is a family that includes other relatives such as grandparents,
uncles, aunts, cousins, nephews and nieces in addition to the immediate family.

**8. What is an immediate family?**

An immediate family normally consists of one's parents, brothers and sisters, and
children where applicable.

**9. What is a relative?**

A relative is a person who is related to another person by birth, marriage or
adoption.

## B. FAMILY MEMBERS AND RELATIONSHIPS

**10. Who is a father?**

A father is a male parent.

**11. Who is a mother?**

A mother is a female parent.

**12. Who is a son?**

A son is a male child of a parent or parents.

**13. Who is a daughter?**

A daughter is a female child of a parent or parents.

**14. Who is a brother?**

A brother is a male child of the same parent or parents as another person.

**15. Who is a sister?**

A sister is a female child of the same parent or parents as another person.

**16. Who is a husband?**

A husband is a married man in relation to his wife.

**17. Who is a wife?**

A wife is a married woman in relation to her husband.

**18. Who is a grandfather?**

A grandfather is the father of one's father or mother.

**19. Who is a grandmother?**

A grandmother is the mother of one's father or mother.

**20. Who is a grandparent?**

A grandparent is either a grandfather or grandmother.

**21. Who is an uncle?**

An uncle is the brother of one's father or mother, or a man who is an uncle through
marriage.

**22. Who is an auntie?**

An auntie is the sister of one's father or mother, or a woman who is an aunt
through marriage.

**23. Who is a cousin?**

A cousin is the child of one's uncle or aunt.

**24. Who is a nephew?**

A nephew is the son of one's brother or sister.

**25. Who is a niece?**

A niece is the daughter of one's brother or sister.

## C. RELATIONSHIPS THROUGH MARRIAGE

**26. Who is a stepmother?**

A stepmother is a woman married to one's father who is not one's biological mother.

**27. Who is a stepfather?**

A stepfather is a man married to one's mother who is not one's biological father.

**28. Who is a stepbrother?**

A stepbrother is the son of one's step-parent or, depending on the family
situation, a boy who becomes one's brother through a parent's marriage.

**29. Who is a stepsister?**

A stepsister is the daughter of one's step-parent or a girl who becomes one's
sister through a parent's marriage.

**30. Who is a mother-in-law?**

A mother-in-law is the mother of one's husband or wife.

**31. Who is a father-in-law?**

A father-in-law is the father of one's husband or wife.

**32. Who is a brother-in-law?**

A brother-in-law is the brother of one's husband or wife, or a man married to
one's sister.

**33. Who is a sister-in-law?**

A sister-in-law is the sister of one's husband or wife, or a woman married to
one's brother.

**34. What does "in-law" mean?**

"In-law" refers to a relationship created through marriage.

## D. HALF-BROTHERS AND HALF-SISTERS

**35. Who is a half-brother?**

A half-brother is a brother who shares only one biological parent with another
person.

**36. Who is a half-sister?**

A half-sister is a sister who shares only one biological parent with another person.

**37. What is the difference between a brother and a half-brother?**

A brother may share both biological parents with you, while a half-brother shares
only one biological parent with you.

**38. What is the difference between a sister and a half-sister?**

A sister may share both biological parents with you, while a half-sister shares
only one biological parent with you.

## E. TWINS AND MULTIPLE BIRTHS

**39. Who are twins?**

Twins are two children born to the same mother at the same birth.

**40. Who are triplets?**

Triplets are three children born to the same mother at the same birth.

**41. Who are quadruplets?**

Quadruplets are four children born to the same mother at the same birth.

**42. What is a multiple birth?**

A multiple birth is when two or more babies are born from the same pregnancy.

**43. What is the difference between twins and triplets?**

Twins are two babies born at the same birth, while triplets are three.

**44. What is the difference between triplets and quadruplets?**

Triplets are three babies born at the same birth, while quadruplets are four.

## F. AGE AND FAMILY RELATIONSHIPS

**45. What does "young" mean?**

Young means having lived for a relatively short time or being at an early stage of
life.

**46. What does "old" mean?**

Old means having lived for a long time or having existed for a long period.

**47. What does "elder" mean?**

Elder means older than another person, especially when referring to members of a
family or group.

**48. What does "eldest" mean?**

Eldest means the oldest among two or more people.

**49. What is the difference between "elder" and "eldest"?**

"Elder" is used to compare a person with another person, while "eldest" refers to
the oldest person in a group.

**50. Give a sentence using "elder".**

"My elder brother is in Primary Seven."

**51. Give a sentence using "eldest".**

"Sarah is the eldest child in our family."

## G. MARRIAGE AND CHILDBIRTH

**52. What does "marry" mean?**

To marry means to enter into a legally or socially recognised marriage with
another person.

**53. What does "married" mean?**

Married means being joined to another person in marriage.

**54. Give a sentence using "marry".**

"Many adults choose to marry when they are ready for marriage."

**55. What does "give birth to" mean?**

"Give birth to" means to produce a baby through childbirth.

**56. Give a sentence using "give birth to".**

"My aunt has given birth to a baby girl."

**57. What is childbirth?**

Childbirth is the process through which a baby is born.

## H. FAMILY SIZE

**58. What is family size?**

Family size is the number of people in a family.

**59. What is a small family?**

A small family has relatively few members.

**60. What is a large family?**

A large family has many members.

**61. What is a populated family?**

A populated family is a family that has people living in it.

**62. What does "populated" mean?**

Populated means having people living in a particular place.

**63. What does "scarcely populated" mean?**

Scarcely populated means having very few people living in a place or area.

**64. What does "densely populated" mean?**

Densely populated means having many people living closely together in a particular
area.

**65. What is population?**

Population is the number of people living in a particular place at a particular time.

**66. What is population density?**

Population density is the number of people living in a given area of land.

**67. What is an overcrowded family?**

An overcrowded family is a family in which too many people live in a limited amount
of space or resources.

**68. What can cause overcrowding in a family?**

Overcrowding may result from having many children or many relatives living together
in a small home.

**69. What are some advantages of a manageable family size?**

A manageable family size can make it easier for parents or guardians to provide:

- food;
- clothing;
- education;
- healthcare;
- shelter;
- love and attention.

**70. What are some challenges of a very large family?**

A very large family may experience difficulties such as:

- inadequate food;
- limited living space;
- difficulty paying school fees;
- inadequate healthcare;
- limited parental attention;
- insufficient clothing and other necessities.

**71. Why is family planning important?**

Family planning helps parents make informed decisions about the number and spacing
of children so that they can better care for their family.

## I. FAMILY TREE

**72. What is a family tree?**

A family tree is a diagram that shows how members of a family are related to one
another.

**73. Why is a family tree important?**

It helps people understand and identify family relationships.

**74. What information can be shown on a family tree?**

A family tree can show:

- parents;
- children;
- grandparents;
- brothers and sisters;
- uncles and aunts;
- cousins;
- other relatives.

**75. How can you identify a person's parents on a family tree?**

The parents are normally shown above their children and connected to them by lines.

**76. How can you identify brothers and sisters?**

Children who have the same parents on the family tree are brothers and sisters.

**77. How can you identify cousins?**

Cousins are usually the children of one's uncles and aunts.

**78. Why should learners be able to interpret a family tree?**

It helps them identify and describe relationships between different family members.

**79. What does it mean to interpret a family tree?**

It means to study the diagram and correctly explain the relationships shown in it.

## J. GOOD FAMILY RELATIONSHIPS

**80. What is a good family relationship?**

It is a relationship in which family members care for, respect, support and
communicate well with one another.

**81. How can family members show love?**

They can show love by:

- caring for one another;
- helping one another;
- listening to one another;
- sharing;
- protecting one another;
- showing kindness.

**82. How can family members show care?**

They can provide food, shelter, clothing, education, healthcare and emotional support.

**83. Why is respect important in a family?**

Respect helps family members live together peacefully.

**84. Why is communication important in a family?**

Communication helps family members understand one another and solve problems.

**85. Why is forgiveness important in a family?**

Forgiveness helps family members overcome disagreements and restore good
relationships.

**86. What does it mean to forgive?**

To forgive means to stop blaming or being angry with someone for a wrong they have
done.

**87. Why should family members cooperate?**

Cooperation helps family members accomplish tasks and solve problems together.

**88. What can cause disagreements in a family?**

Disagreements may result from:

- misunderstandings;
- lack of communication;
- jealousy;
- unfair treatment;
- failure to share responsibilities;
- differences in opinions.

**89. How can family disagreements be solved?**

They can be solved through:

- discussion;
- listening;
- forgiveness;
- apology;
- compromise;
- seeking advice from responsible adults.

## K. FAMILY VALUES

**90. What is love?**

Love is a strong feeling of care and affection for another person.

**91. What is care?**

Care means giving attention, help and protection to someone.

**92. What is appreciation?**

Appreciation means recognising and valuing the good qualities or efforts of someone.

**93. Why should children appreciate their parents and guardians?**

They should appreciate them because parents and guardians provide care, guidance,
protection and support.

**94. What is respect?**

Respect means treating people with consideration and recognising their rights and
worth.

**95. Why should children respect older family members?**

They should respect older family members because they have experience and can
provide guidance and support.

## L. FAMILY AND CULTURE

**96. What is culture?**

Culture is the way of life of a particular group of people.

**97. How can families teach children culture?**

Families teach culture through:

- language;
- traditions;
- customs;
- stories;
- ceremonies;
- food;
- dressing;
- behaviour.

**98. Why is it important to know one's culture?**

Knowing one's culture helps a person understand their identity, traditions and
community.

**99. What is a tribe?**

A tribe is a social group of people who share common ancestry, traditions, culture
or identity.

**100. Why should we respect other people's cultures?**

We should respect other people's cultures because people have different traditions
and ways of life.

## M. FAMILY SAFETY AND PROTECTION

**101. What is defilement?**

Defilement is a serious sexual offence involving a child.

**102. What is rape?**

Rape is a serious sexual offence involving sexual activity without a person's consent.

**103. What is incest?**

Incest is sexual activity between close relatives where such a relationship is
prohibited by law or social rules.

**104. Why are defilement, rape and incest serious issues?**

They can cause serious physical, emotional, psychological and social harm to
victims and are serious offences.

**105. What should a child do if someone makes them feel unsafe?**

The child should move to a safe place and tell a trusted adult such as a parent,
guardian, teacher or another responsible person.

**106. Why should children speak out about abuse?**

Speaking out can help them receive protection, support and appropriate help.

## O. ADVERBS: JUST AND ALREADY

**114. What is an adverb?**

An adverb is a word that gives more information about a verb, adjective or another
adverb.

**115. How is "just" used?**

"Just" can show that something happened a very short time ago.

**116. Give a sentence using "just".**

"My aunt has just given birth to a baby."

**117. How is "already" used?**

"Already" shows that something has happened before now or earlier than expected.

**118. Give a sentence using "already".**

"My grandmother has already arrived."

**119. What is the difference between "just" and "already"?**

"Just" often shows that something happened very recently, while "already" shows
that something has happened before the present time or earlier than expected.

## R. LANGUAGE STRUCTURE: RATHER...THAN

**131. What does "rather...than" express?**

"Rather...than" is used to show a preference for one thing over another.

**132. Give an example.**

"I would rather have twins than triplets."

**133. Complete the sentence: "I would rather live with my parents ______ live alone."**

Answer: "I would rather live with my parents than live alone."

**134. Make a sentence using "rather...than" about family.**

"I would rather visit my grandmother than stay at home."

## S. LANGUAGE STRUCTURE: TOO...TO

**135. What does "too...to" mean?**

"Too...to" shows that something is more than is suitable or possible for a
particular action.

**136. Give an example.**

"Your niece is too young to get married."

**137. Complete the sentence: "The baby is too young ______ walk."**

Answer: "The baby is too young to walk."

**138. Make a family-related sentence using "too...to".**

## U. LANGUAGE STRUCTURE: SUCH...THAT

**142. What does "such...that" show?**

"Such...that" is used to show that something has a particular quality to such a
degree that it causes a result.

**143. Give an example.**

"My cousin was such a beautiful girl that many people admired her."

**144. Complete the sentence: "It was such a large family ______ everyone had to help."**

Answer: "It was such a large family that everyone had to help."

**145. Make a family-related sentence using "such...that".**

"They were such caring parents that their children trusted them."

## V. LANGUAGE STRUCTURE: IS...BUT...IS

**146. What does "is...but...is" show?**

It is used to contrast two related facts or people.

**147. Give the NCDC-style example.**

**148. Give another example.**

"John is my cousin but Mary is my sister."

**149. Complete the sentence: "Sarah is my niece but Peter ______ my nephew."**

Answer: "Sarah is my niece but Peter is my nephew."

## IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- A **family** is related by **birth, marriage or adoption**; a **nuclear family**
  is parents and children; an **extended family** adds grandparents, uncles, aunts,
  cousins, nephews and nieces.
- **In-law** relationships come through **marriage**: mother-in-law, father-in-law,
  brother-in-law, sister-in-law.
- A **half-brother/half-sister** shares **only one** biological parent.
- **Twins = 2, triplets = 3, quadruplets = 4** babies at the same birth.
- **Elder** compares one person with another; **eldest** names the oldest in a group.
- On a family tree, **parents sit above their children**, connected by lines;
  cousins are the children of uncles and aunts.
- A good relationship is built on **love, care, respect, trust, forgiveness,
  cooperation and communication**; disagreements are solved through discussion,
  listening, forgiveness, apology and compromise.
- A manageable family size eases **food, clothing, education, healthcare, shelter
  and attention**; family planning helps parents decide the **number and spacing**
  of children.
- **Just** = very recently; **already** = before now or earlier than expected.
- Structures: **rather…than** (preference), **too…to** (beyond suitable),
  **such…that** (degree and result), **is…but…is** (contrast).
- If a child feels unsafe: **move to a safe place and tell a trusted adult**.

## EXAMINATION POINTS

  the favourites.

  = nephew; husband's mother = mother-in-law) — practise both directions.

  such…that, is…but…is**.
- Family-size questions: advantages of manageable size vs challenges of a very
  large family — keep the two lists separate.
- Safety questions: the correct answer is always **move to safety and tell a
  trusted adult**.
