## SUB-TOPIC 2A: INFORMAL LETTERS

### Definition

**Informal (personal) letter:** A letter written to someone you know well — a parent, a
brother, a friend, a classmate or a pen pal.

### When an informal letter is written

To greet somebody · to thank them · to invite them to a party or ceremony · to ask for
something · to give news · to congratulate or console · to apologise · to reply to a
letter.

### The parts of an informal letter, in order

1. **Your address** — written at the top **right-hand** corner (never your name above it).
2. **The date** — written under the address.
3. **The salutation (greeting)** — *Dear Ann,* *Dear Uncle,* followed by a **comma**.
4. **The introduction** — a short opening: *How are you? I hope you are fine.*
5. **The body** — the message, in paragraphs.
6. **The conclusion** — *Greet everyone at home. I look forward to hearing from you.*
7. **The complimentary close** — *Yours affectionately,* *Yours sincerely,* *Your friend,*
8. **Your first name only** — *Sarah*

### Example layout

```
                                        P.7 Blue
                                        Kabale Junior School
                                        P.O. Box 45
                                        Kabale
                                        12th August 2026

Dear Grace,

    How are you? I hope you and the family are well. ...

    ... (body of the letter in paragraphs) ...

    Please greet everyone at home. I am waiting for your reply.

                                        Yours affectionately,
                                        Sarah
```

### Vocabulary from the syllabus

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| address | The details of where a person lives or receives post |
| personal letter | A letter to somebody you know well |
| stamp | A small printed label paid for and stuck on an envelope |
| envelope | The paper cover in which a letter is posted |
| occasion | A special event |
| affectionately | With love; used to close a letter to a relative |
| sincerely | Truly; used to close a letter to a person you named |
| body | The main part of the letter |
| purpose | The reason for writing |

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **… is likely to …** | *Anna **is likely to** write to me. / I **am likely to** forget your address.* |
| **… hardly …** | *There is **hardly** anyone who has bothered to reply.* |

**Note:** *hardly* means "almost not". It is already negative, so never add another
negative: *I can **hardly** hear you* (not "can't hardly").

### Grammar focus: the present continuous tense

**Form:** am / is / are + verb + **-ing**

- *I **am writing** to invite you to my birthday party.*
- *They **are preparing** for the ceremony.*

It is used for something happening **now** or **around now**, and also for a **fixed
future arrangement**: *We **are travelling** to Mbale on Friday.*

## SUB-TOPIC 2B: FORMAL LETTERS

### Definition

**Formal (official) letter:** A letter written to someone in their official position — a
head teacher, a manager, an editor, a district officer — usually somebody you do not know
personally.

### When a formal letter is written

To apply for a job or a place in school · to ask for permission · to make a request ·
to complain · to apologise · to invite officially · to give or ask for information · to
reply to an advertisement.

### The parts of a formal letter, in order

1. **Your address and the date** — top **right**.
2. **The receiver's address** — on the **left**, below your date: *The Head Teacher, …*
3. **The salutation** — *Dear Sir,* / *Dear Madam,* / *Dear Sir/Madam,*
4. **The heading (Re:)** — underlined: *RE: APPLICATION FOR THE POST OF A TYPIST*
5. **The body** — short, polite and to the point, in paragraphs.
6. **The complimentary close** — **Yours faithfully,** if you began *Dear Sir/Madam*; **Yours sincerely,** if you used the person's name.
7. **Your signature**, then **your full name** in capital letters below it.

### Example layout

```
                                        P.O. Box 120
                                        Mbarara
                                        3rd September 2026

The Head Teacher
Rukungiri Modern Primary School
P.O. Box 33
Rukungiri

Dear Sir,

RE: REQUEST FOR A PLACE IN SENIOR ONE

    I am writing to request ...

                                        Yours faithfully,
                                        (signature)
                                        JOHN MUGISHA
```

### Informal and formal letters compared

| Point | Informal letter | Formal letter |
|---|---|---|
| Written to | A relative or friend | An official or a stranger |
| Receiver's address | Not written | **Written on the left** |
| Salutation | *Dear Ann,* | *Dear Sir,* / *Dear Madam,* |
| Heading (Re:) | Not used | **Used and underlined** |
| Language | Friendly, may use short forms | Polite, official, no short forms |
| Closing | *Yours affectionately / Your friend* | **Yours faithfully / Yours sincerely** |
| Signing off | First name only | **Signature + full name in capitals** |

### Abbreviations you must know

| Abbreviation | Full meaning |
|---|---|
| **Re:** | Reference — the subject of the letter |
| **pp** | On behalf of (signing for another person) |
| **c.c.** | Carbon copy — a copy sent to another person |
| **P.O. Box** | Post Office Box |
| **Dr** | Doctor · **Rev** — Reverend · **Hon** — Honourable · **Prof** — Professor |
| **Mr, Mrs, Miss, Ms** | Titles for men, married women, unmarried women, and women whether married or not |
| **e.g.** | For example · **i.e.** — that is · **etc.** — and the rest |
| **N.B.** | Note well · **P.S.** — added at the end of a letter |
| **NGO** | Non-Governmental Organisation · **UNEB** — Uganda National Examinations Board |

### Language structures

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **Barely …** (at the start) | ***Barely** 30% of the people applied for the position advertised.* |
| **… barely …** (inside the sentence) | *The letter was **barely** legible.* |

*Barely* means "only just" or "almost not".

### Grammar focus: forming adverbs

Most adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to an adjective.

| Adjective | Adverb | Note |
|---|---|---|
| quick | quickly | simple + ly |
| careful | carefully | |
| happy | happily | y → i + ly |
| gentle | gently | drop the e, add y |
| true | truly | irregular spelling |
| good | **well** | completely irregular |
| fast | **fast** | no change |
| hard | **hard** (*hardly* means something else!) | |

### Filling in official forms

Write in **CAPITAL LETTERS** unless told otherwise · use **black or blue ink** · write your
**surname first** where asked · write dates as **day/month/year** · leave nothing blank —
write **N/A** (not applicable) where a question does not concern you · do not overwrite —
cancel neatly with one line · sign where a signature is asked for; do not print your name
there.

| Salutation | The greeting at the beginning of a letter |
| Complimentary close | The polite ending, such as *Yours faithfully* |
| Re: | The reference or subject of a formal letter |
| c.c. | Carbon copy — a copy sent to another person |
| pp | Signing on behalf of another person |
| Vacancy | A job or place that needs to be filled |
| Referee | A person who speaks for your character or ability |
| Adverb | A word that tells how, when or where an action is done |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Dear Sir / Dear Madam → Yours faithfully.** **Dear Mr Okello → Yours sincerely.**
- The receiver's address is written **only in a formal letter**, on the **left**.
- **Re:** is written and **underlined** in a formal letter only.
- Sign an informal letter with your **first name**; a formal letter with your **signature and full name in capitals**.
- Never use short forms (*don't, can't, I'm*) in a formal letter — write them in full.
- *Yours* has **no apostrophe**: *Yours faithfully*, never "Your's".
- Only the first word of the closing takes a capital letter: *Yours faithfully*, not *Yours Faithfully*.
- **N/A** means "not applicable" when filling a form.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- A letter beginning *Dear Madam* must end with **Yours faithfully**.
- The full meaning of **c.c.** — carbon copy. Of **Re:** — reference. Of **pp** — on behalf of.
- One difference between formal and informal letters — the receiver's address is written in a formal letter only.
- The adverb from *happy* → **happily**; from *good* → **well**.
- A woman's family name before marriage — her **maiden name**.
- A job that is free and needs to be filled — a **vacancy**.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing "Your's faithfully" — it is **Yours faithfully**.
- Ending *Dear Sir* with *Yours sincerely*.
- Writing your **name above your address** at the top of the letter.
- Putting the receiver's address in an **informal** letter.
- Using slang or short forms in a formal letter.
- Forgetting the **comma** after the salutation and after the closing.
- Writing *Re:* in an informal letter.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. An **informal letter** is written to a person you know well, such as a friend or relative, using friendly language; a **formal letter** is written to somebody in their official position, using polite official language, and it includes the receiver's address and a heading (Re:).
2. Sender's address; date; receiver's address; salutation; heading (Re:); body; complimentary close; signature and full name. (Any six in order.)
3. Sender's address; date; salutation; introduction; body; conclusion; complimentary close; first name. (Any five.)
4. **Yours faithfully.**
5. **Yours sincerely.**
6. Re: — **reference**; c.c. — **carbon copy**; pp — **on behalf of**; N.B. — **note well**; P.S. — a note **added at the end of a letter**.
7. **Maiden name** — a woman's family name before she married. **Vacancy** — a job or place that is free and needs to be filled.
8. On the **left-hand side**, below the date, and only in a **formal letter**.
9. careful → **carefully**; happy → **happily**; true → **truly**; good → **well**; fast → **fast**.
10. **hardly**
11. **Barely 30% of the people applied.**
12. *Learner's sentence,* for example: *He **is likely to** come tomorrow.*
13. **am writing**
14. Any four of: write in capital letters; use black or blue ink; write the surname first where asked; write dates as day/month/year; leave nothing blank — write N/A; do not overwrite; sign where a signature is required.
15. For example:
    *P.O. Box 12*
    *Masaka*
    *4th May 2026*
    (written at the top right, with no name above the address)
16. **RE: APPLICATION FOR THE POST OF A COOK** (written and underlined).
17. *Learner's letter.* It must have: the sender's address and date (right), the head teacher's address (left), *Dear Sir/Madam,*, an underlined **RE:** line, a polite body giving the reason and dates, **Yours faithfully,** a signature and the full name in capitals.
18. *Learner's letter.* It must have: the sender's address and date (right), *Dear …,* a friendly opening, a body in paragraphs, a closing greeting, *Yours affectionately/Your friend,* and the first name only. **No** receiver's address and **no** Re: line.
19. **Informal:** to greet or give news, to invite a friend, to thank somebody, to apologise (any two). **Formal:** to apply for a job or a place, to ask permission, to complain, to request information (any two).
20. It should read: *Dear Sir, … **Yours faithfully**, Ann.* — the close must match *Dear Sir*, *Yours* has no apostrophe, and *faithfully* takes a small letter.

# P.7 QUICK REVISION

- **Dear Sir/Madam → Yours faithfully** · **Dear Mr/Mrs (name) → Yours sincerely**
- Formal letter only: **receiver's address (left)** + **RE: underlined** + signature and full name
- Informal letter: address, date, *Dear …,* body, *Yours affectionately,* **first name**
- **Yours** — no apostrophe · small letter for *faithfully / sincerely*
- Re: = reference · c.c. = carbon copy · pp = on behalf of · N/A = not applicable
- Adverbs: happy → happily · true → truly · good → **well**
