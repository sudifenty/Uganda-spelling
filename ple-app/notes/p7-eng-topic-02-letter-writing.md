## About this topic

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Seven Curriculum, Set One, English Topic 2 — Letter
Writing, 28 periods (pages 36–38). This app topic covers sub-topics 2A Informal Letters
and 2B Formal Letters using the owner's question-and-answer notes.*

**Background (from the syllabus):** Letter writing is an important functional use of
language. A learner in P.7 is expected to communicate through writing, like when applying
for jobs, making friendship and giving information. This topic equips learners with the
skills of writing informal and formal letters. It builds on what was covered under Topic 4,
Letter Writing, in Primary Five, and it enables learners to differentiate between formal
and informal letters.

**Learning outcome (from the syllabus):** The learner writes informal and formal letters
using the appropriate format.

**Life skills and indicators (from the syllabus):**

- **Effective communication** — logical presentation of ideas, accuracy, articulation
- **Creative thinking** — logical reasoning, initiative, finding different ways of doing things
- **Critical thinking** — selecting and evaluating information, making best use of information, analysing statements, taking decisions
- **Self-esteem** — talking about self, friendliness

**Values (from the syllabus):** appreciation and respect.

## A. Understanding letter writing

**1. What is letter writing?**

Letter writing is the act of communicating a message to another person or organisation in
written form.

**2. Why is letter writing important?**

Letter writing is important because it enables people to:

- give information.
- make requests.
- apply for jobs.
- make friends.
- thank people.
- apologise.
- give invitations.
- maintain relationships.
- communicate with people who are far away.

**3. What are the two main types of letters studied in Primary Seven?**

The two main types are:

1. Informal letters.
2. Formal letters.

**4. What is an informal letter?**

An informal letter is a personal letter written to someone we know well, such as a friend,
relative or classmate.

**5. What is a formal letter?**

A formal letter is an official letter written to a person, organisation or institution for
a particular official purpose.

**6. What is the difference between an informal and a formal letter?**

An informal letter is mainly used for personal communication, while a formal letter is
used for official communication.

**7. What determines the type of letter one should write?**

The person being addressed and the purpose of writing determine the type of letter.

**8. What is the main learning outcome of Letter Writing in Primary Seven?**

The learner should be able to write informal and formal letters using the appropriate
format.

## B. Informal letters

**9. What is an informal letter?**

An informal letter is a personal letter written to a friend, relative, classmate or
another person whom the writer knows well.

**10. When are informal letters written?**

Informal letters are written when people want to:

- give personal information.
- share news.
- maintain friendship.
- invite someone.
- thank someone.
- ask for information.
- describe an experience.
- congratulate someone.
- apologise.

**11. Who can receive an informal letter?**

An informal letter can be written to:

- a friend.
- a relative.
- a classmate.
- a brother.
- a sister.
- a parent.

**12. What are the main components of an informal letter?**

The main components include:

1. Address.
2. Date.
3. Salutation.
4. Introduction.
5. Body.
6. Conclusion.
7. Complimentary close.
8. Name or signature.

**13. What is an address?**

An address is information showing where a person lives or where a letter is being sent
from.

**14. Where is the writer's address placed in an informal letter?**

It is normally placed at the top right-hand side of the letter.

**15. What is the date?**

The date shows when the letter was written.

**16. Where is the date placed?**

It is normally written below the writer's address.

**17. What is a salutation?**

A salutation is the greeting used at the beginning of a letter.

**18. Give examples of salutations used in informal letters.**

Examples include:

- Dear John,
- Dear Mary,
- Dear Aunt,
- Dear Uncle,
- Dear Peter,

**19. What is an introduction?**

An introduction is the opening part of a letter that introduces the purpose or subject of
the letter.

**20. What is the body of a letter?**

The body is the main part of the letter where the writer gives the information or message.

**21. What is a conclusion?**

A conclusion is the final part of the message where the writer brings the letter to an end.

**22. What is a complimentary close?**

A complimentary close is the polite expression used at the end of a letter before the
writer's name.

**23. Give examples of complimentary closes for informal letters.**

Examples include:

- Yours affectionately,
- Yours lovingly,
- Yours sincerely,
- Best wishes,
- With love.

**24. What does "affectionately" mean?**

Affectionately means in a loving or caring manner.

**25. What does "sincerely" mean?**

Sincerely means honestly or genuinely.

## D. Language structures in informal letters

**40. What language structure is taught under informal letters?**

One structure is:

**... is likely to ...**

**41. What does "is likely to" mean?**

It means that something will probably happen.

**42. Give the NCDC example of "is likely to".**

Anna is likely to write to me.

**43. Give another example.**

John is likely to visit his uncle.

**44. Make a sentence using "am likely to".**

I am likely to forget your address.

**45. Make a sentence using "are likely to".**

They are likely to arrive tomorrow.

**46. What is another structure taught under informal letters?**

**... hardly ...**

**47. What does "hardly" mean?**

Hardly means almost not or almost never.

**48. Give the NCDC example using "hardly".**

There is hardly anyone who has bothered to reply.

**49. Give another example using "hardly".**

I hardly see my old friend these days.

**50. Make a sentence using "hardly".**

She hardly writes letters to her relatives.

## E. Grammar in informal letters

**51. What tense is taught under informal letters?**

The present continuous tense.

**52. What is the present continuous tense?**

The present continuous tense describes an action that is happening now or around the
present time.

**53. How is the present continuous tense formed?**

It is formed using:

**am/is/are + verb-ing**

**54. Give an example.**

I am writing a letter.

**55. Give another example.**

Mary is writing to her friend.

**56. Give an example using "are".**

They are preparing a letter.

**57. What is an adjective?**

An adjective is a word that describes a noun.

**58. Give examples of adjectives that can be used in informal letters.**

Examples include:

- happy.
- friendly.
- kind.
- interesting.
- exciting.
- beautiful.
- wonderful.

**59. Make a sentence using "interesting".**

I had an interesting experience during the holiday.

**60. Make a sentence using "friendly".**

My new classmate is very friendly.

## F. Reading informal letters

**61. What should a learner do when reading an informal letter?**

The learner should read carefully and identify the writer, receiver, purpose and important
information.

**62. What questions can be asked about an informal letter?**

Questions may ask about:

- the writer.
- the receiver.
- the date.
- the purpose.
- the main message.
- important details.
- vocabulary.

**63. How can a learner identify the purpose of a letter?**

The learner should read the letter carefully and identify the main reason why it was
written.

**64. Why is careful reading important?**

Careful reading helps the learner understand the message and answer comprehension
questions correctly.

## G. Example of an informal letter

**65. Write an informal letter to your friend telling him or her about your holiday.**

**Example:**

P.O. Box 145, Kampala. 15th September, 2026.

Dear John,

How are you? I hope you are fine. I am also fine. I am writing to tell you about my
holiday.

During the holiday, I visited my grandparents in the village. I helped my grandmother with
some housework and assisted my grandfather on the farm. I also played football with my
cousins.

I enjoyed my holiday very much. I wish you had been there with us.

Please write back and tell me how you spent your holiday.

Yours affectionately,

Peter

**66. Identify the address in the letter above.**

**P.O. Box 145, Kampala.**

**67. Identify the date.**

**15th September, 2026.**

**68. Identify the salutation.**

**Dear John,**

**69. Identify the body of the letter.**

The paragraphs between the salutation and the closing form the body of the letter.

**70. Identify the complimentary close.**

**Yours affectionately,**

**71. Who is the writer?**

Peter.

**72. Who is the receiver?**

John.

**73. Why did Peter write the letter?**

He wrote to tell John about his holiday.

## H. Completing informal letters

**74. What does it mean to complete an informal letter?**

It means filling in the missing words, sentences or parts to make the letter complete.

**75. What should you consider when completing an informal letter?**

You should consider:

- the type of letter.
- the purpose.
- the receiver.
- the information already given.
- grammar.
- spelling.
- punctuation.

**76. Why must the language match the letter?**

The language should be suitable for the person receiving the letter and the purpose of
writing it.

## I. Formal letters

**77. What is a formal letter?**

A formal letter is an official letter written to a person, organisation or institution for
a particular purpose.

**78. When are formal letters written?**

Formal letters may be written to:

- apply for a job.
- make a request.
- make a complaint.
- give official information.
- apologise.
- express regret.
- respond to an advertisement.

**79. Who can receive a formal letter?**

A formal letter may be written to:

- an employer.
- a head teacher.
- a manager.
- a government official.
- an organisation.
- an institution.

**80. What are the main components of a formal letter?**

They include:

1. Writer's address.
2. Date.
3. Receiver's address.
4. Salutation.
5. Subject or reference where appropriate.
6. Introduction.
7. Body.
8. Conclusion.
9. Complimentary close.
10. Signature.
11. Name.

**81. What is a formal salutation?**

A formal salutation is a polite official greeting used at the beginning of a formal
letter.

**82. Give examples of formal salutations.**

Examples include:

- Dear Sir,
- Dear Madam,
- Dear Sir/Madam.

**83. What complimentary close is normally used with "Dear Sir" or "Dear Madam"?**

**Yours faithfully** is normally used.

**84. What complimentary close may be used when the person's name is known?**

**Yours sincerely** may be used.

## K. Abbreviations in formal letters

**101. What is an abbreviation?**

An abbreviation is a shortened form of a word or group of words.

**102. What does "pp" mean?**

In formal correspondence, **pp** can be used to indicate that someone signs a document on
behalf of another person. The NCDC curriculum also lists it as "person present" in its
vocabulary.

**103. What does "c.c." mean?**

**c.c.** means carbon copy. It indicates that a copy of the letter is sent to another
person.

**104. What does "Re:" mean?**

**Re:** means reference and is used to show the subject or matter being referred to.

**105. What does "Mr" mean?**

**Mr** is a title used before the name of a man.

**106. What does "Mrs" mean?**

**Mrs** is a title traditionally used before the name of a married woman.

**107. What does "Ms" mean?**

**Ms** is a title used before a woman's name without indicating her marital status.

**108. What does "Miss" mean?**

**Miss** is traditionally used before the name of an unmarried girl or woman.

**109. What does "Hon." mean?**

**Hon.** is an abbreviation for Honourable.

**110. What does "Dr" mean?**

**Dr** is an abbreviation for Doctor.

**111. What does "Rev." mean?**

**Rev.** is an abbreviation for Reverend.

**112. What does "Prof." mean?**

**Prof.** is an abbreviation for Professor.

**113. Why are abbreviations useful?**

They provide shorter forms of words and phrases commonly used in written communication.

## N. Formal application letters

**131. What is an application letter?**

An application letter is a formal letter written to request consideration for a job or
other position.

**132. What is a job application?**

A job application is a formal request to be considered for a particular job.

**133. What is a job advertisement?**

A job advertisement is a public notice announcing an available job.

**134. What is a job vacancy?**

A job vacancy is an available job position.

**135. What information can be obtained from a job advertisement?**

It may give:

- the name of the organisation.
- the available post.
- qualifications required.
- experience required.
- closing date.
- application instructions.

**136. What should a learner do before writing an application letter?**

The learner should read and understand the job advertisement carefully.

**137. Why should an application letter be relevant?**

It should focus on the job or position being applied for.

**138. What should an applicant mention in an application letter?**

The applicant may mention:

- the post being applied for.
- relevant qualifications.
- relevant skills.
- relevant experience.
- where the vacancy was advertised.

**139. Why should information in an application letter be truthful?**

False information can mislead the person receiving the application.

## O. Example of a formal application letter

**140. Write a formal letter applying for an advertised post.**

**Example:**

P.O. Box 125, Kampala. 15th September, 2026.

The Manager, Bright Star Company, P.O. Box 450, Kampala.

Dear Sir/Madam,

**RE: APPLICATION FOR THE POST OF OFFICE ASSISTANT**

I am writing to apply for the post of office assistant advertised recently.

I am a hardworking and responsible person. I have good communication skills and I can work
well with other people.

I would be grateful if my application were considered. I am ready to attend an interview at
your convenience.

Yours faithfully,

Peter Ssenyonga

**141. What is the purpose of this letter?**

It is to apply for the post of office assistant.

**142. Is this letter formal or informal?**

It is a formal letter.

**143. Why is it formal?**

It is written for an official purpose and uses formal language and format.

## IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- An **informal letter** is a **personal** letter to someone the writer knows well; a **formal letter** is an **official** letter for an official purpose.
- The writer's address goes at the **top right-hand side** of the letter; the **date** is written **below the address**.
- Components of an informal letter: **address, date, salutation, introduction, body, conclusion, complimentary close, name or signature**.
- A formal letter also includes the **receiver's address** and a **subject or reference**.
- Informal closes: **Yours affectionately, Yours lovingly, Yours sincerely, Best wishes, With love**.
- **Dear Sir / Dear Madam** takes **Yours faithfully**; when the name is known, use **Yours sincerely**.
- **"is likely to"** means will probably happen — *Anna is likely to write to me.* (NCDC example)
- **"hardly"** means almost not — *There is hardly anyone who has bothered to reply.* (NCDC example)
- **"barely"** means only just — *Barely 30% of the people applied for the position advertised.* (NCDC example); **legible** means clear enough to be read.
- The **present continuous** is formed with **am/is/are + verb-ing**; many **adverbs** are made by adding **-ly** to adjectives (careful → carefully).
- **c.c.** = carbon copy; **Re:** = reference; **pp** = signing on behalf of another person.
- A **referee** gives information about an applicant; a **draft** is the first version of a piece of writing.
- The values emphasised in Letter Writing are **appreciation and respect**.

## EXAMINATION POINTS

- Expect **parts-of-a-letter** identification: address, date, salutation, body, complimentary close, writer, receiver.
- Expect **complimentary close choices**: Dear Sir/Madam → **Yours faithfully**; name known → **Yours sincerely**.
- Expect **abbreviation meanings**: c.c. = **carbon copy**, Re: = **reference**, Dr = **Doctor**, Rev. = **Reverend**, Prof. = **Professor**, Hon. = **Honourable**.
- Expect **adverb formation**: careful → **carefully**, polite → **politely**, accurate → **accurately**.
- Expect sentences with **"is likely to"**, **"hardly"** and **"barely"**.
- Expect **present continuous** examples: *I am writing a letter.*
- Expect **application letter** content: the post applied for, qualifications, skills, experience, where the vacancy was advertised.
- Expect **informal vs formal** differences: personal vs official; friendly vs polite official language.
- Question words to expect: **Mention… Give… Identify… Write… Complete… What does … mean?**
