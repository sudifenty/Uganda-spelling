## 1. WHAT PHYSICAL FEATURES ARE

### Definitions

**Physical features:** The land forms that exist on the earth's surface and give the land its shape.

**Relief features:** Physical features to do with the shape and height of the land, such as mountains, highlands, plateaux, lowlands, rift valleys and coastal plains.

**Drainage features:** Physical features made of water, such as rivers, lakes, seas and oceans.

### Important Points

- Physical features are **natural resources**. People depend on them to earn a living.
- Some physical features form **international boundaries** — for example, Lake Victoria is shared by Uganda, Kenya and Tanzania.
- Physical features are formed in different ways: by **folding**, by **faulting**, by **volcanicity**, by **erosion** and by **deposition**.

---

## 2. MOUNTAINS AND HIGHLANDS OF AFRICA

### Definitions

**Mountain:** A very high, steep area of land that rises far above the land around it.

**Highland:** A large area of land that is high above sea level but not as steep as a mountain.

### The main mountains and highlands named in the syllabus

| Mountain / Highland | Country / Region | How it was formed |
|---|---|---|
| **Atlas Mountains** | Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia (North Africa) | Folding |
| **Drakensberg Mountains** | South Africa and Lesotho (Southern Africa) | Faulting and uplift |
| **Cameroon Highlands (Mt Cameroon)** | Cameroon (West Africa) | Volcanicity |
| **Jos Plateau** | Nigeria (West Africa) | Uplift of an old plateau |
| **Ahaggar (Hoggar) Mountains** | Algeria (Sahara, North Africa) | Volcanicity |
| **Tibesti Mountains** | Chad (North Africa) | Volcanicity |
| **Ethiopian Highlands** | Ethiopia (Horn of Africa) | Volcanicity and faulting |

### Well-known mountains of East Africa (for comparison)

| Mountain | Country | Formation |
|---|---|---|
| Mt Kilimanjaro (5,895 m) | Tanzania | Volcanic |
| Mt Kenya | Kenya | Volcanic |
| Rwenzori Mountains | Uganda / D.R. Congo | Fault-block |
| Mt Elgon | Uganda / Kenya | Volcanic |

### The three ways mountains are formed

**1. Folded (fold) mountains** — layers of soft rock are squeezed from both sides until they bend upwards into folds.
*Examples:* Atlas Mountains, Cape Ranges of South Africa.

**2. Fault-block mountains** — the land cracks (faults) and a block of land between the cracks is pushed upwards.
*Examples:* Rwenzori Mountains, Usambara Mountains.

**3. Volcanic mountains** — molten rock (magma) comes out of the earth through a weak point, cools and piles up.
*Examples:* Mt Kilimanjaro, Mt Cameroon, Tibesti, Ahaggar, Mt Elgon.

### Important Points

- **Mt Kilimanjaro** is the highest mountain in Africa.
- **Mt Cameroon** is the highest mountain in West Africa and is still active.
- **Ras Dashen** in the Ethiopian Highlands is the highest peak in Ethiopia.
- The **Atlas Mountains** protect the Mediterranean coast from the dry, dusty winds of the Sahara.

## 3. PLATEAUX, RIFT VALLEYS AND DEPRESSIONS

### Definitions

**Plateau:** A large area of fairly flat land raised high above the land around it. It is sometimes called a *table land*.

**Rift valley:** A long, deep, steep-sided valley formed when land between two parallel faults sinks.

**Depression:** A low area of land which is lower than the land around it, and sometimes lower than sea level.

### Examples in Africa

| Feature | Where it is found |
|---|---|
| Jos Plateau | Nigeria |
| Ahaggar Plateau | Algeria |
| Katanga (Shaba) Plateau | D.R. Congo |
| Bié Plateau | Angola |
| **The Great Rift Valley** | From the Red Sea through Ethiopia, Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania and Malawi down to Mozambique |
| Qattara Depression | Egypt |
| Danakil (Afar) Depression | Ethiopia and Eritrea |
| Lake Assal | Djibouti — the **lowest point in Africa** |

### Important Points

- The Great Rift Valley has an **eastern arm** (through Kenya and Tanzania) and a **western arm** (through Uganda, D.R. Congo, Rwanda, Burundi, Tanzania, Zambia and Malawi).
- Many long, deep lakes lie in the floor of the Rift Valley — Tanganyika, Malawi, Albert, Edward and Turkana.
- Most of Africa is a **plateau continent**, which is why so many African rivers have waterfalls and rapids where they drop off the plateau edge.

## 4. RIVERS OF AFRICA

### Definitions

**River:** A large natural stream of water flowing across the land into a lake, a sea or an ocean.

**Source:** The place where a river begins. **Mouth:** The place where a river enters a lake, sea or ocean.

**Delta:** The fan-shaped area of flat land made of soil deposited where a river enters a lake or sea through several channels.

**Estuary:** The wide mouth of a river where it meets the sea and fresh water mixes with salt water.

### Major rivers named in the syllabus

| River | Source | Flows into | Countries it passes through |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Nile** | Lake Victoria (White Nile) | Mediterranean Sea | Uganda, South Sudan, Sudan, Egypt |
| **Blue Nile** | Lake Tana, Ethiopia | Joins the White Nile at Khartoum | Ethiopia, Sudan |
| **River Congo** | Highlands of D.R. Congo / Zambia | Atlantic Ocean | D.R. Congo, Congo, Zambia |
| **River Niger** | Guinea Highlands | Atlantic Ocean (Gulf of Guinea) | Guinea, Mali, Niger, Nigeria |
| **River Zambezi** | North-western Zambia | Indian Ocean | Zambia, Angola, Zimbabwe, Mozambique |
| **Orange River** | Drakensberg Mountains | Atlantic Ocean | Lesotho, South Africa, Namibia |
| **Limpopo River** | South Africa | Indian Ocean | South Africa, Botswana, Zimbabwe, Mozambique |
| **River Volta** | Burkina Faso and Ghana | Atlantic Ocean | Burkina Faso, Ghana |

### Important Points

- The **Nile** is the longest river in Africa. It flows **northwards** — a river does not have to flow south.
- The **Congo** is the deepest river in Africa and it crosses the Equator **twice**.
- The **Niger Delta** in Nigeria and the **Nile Delta** in Egypt are the two most famous deltas in Africa.
- **Victoria Falls** is on the River Zambezi, between Zambia and Zimbabwe.
- Rivers are used for water, fishing, transport, irrigation and generating hydro-electric power.

### Which ocean or sea does each river flow into?

- **Mediterranean Sea:** the Nile
- **Atlantic Ocean:** Congo, Niger, Volta, Orange, Senegal
- **Indian Ocean:** Zambezi, Limpopo, Rufiji, Tana

## 5. LAKES OF AFRICA

### Definition

**Lake:** A large body of water surrounded by land on all sides.

### Types of lakes with African examples

| Type of lake | How it was formed | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Rift valley (down-warped/faulted) | Water filling the floor of a rift valley | Tanganyika, Malawi, Turkana, Albert |
| Depression | Water collecting in a sunken basin | Victoria, Chad, Ngami |
| Crater | Water filling the mouth of an old volcano | Katwe, Nyamunuka |
| Man-made | Water held back by a dam built by people | Nasser, Volta, Kariba |
| Ox-bow | A bend of a river cut off from the main channel | Small lakes along the Nile and Congo |

### Important Points

- **Lake Victoria** is the largest lake in Africa and the second largest fresh-water lake in the world.
- **Lake Tanganyika** is the deepest lake in Africa.
- **Lake Chad** is a shallow inland lake shared by Chad, Niger, Nigeria and Cameroon. It has been **shrinking** because of drought and irrigation.
- **Lake Ngami** in Botswana is a shallow lake near the Okavango swamps.
- **Lake Nasser** (Egypt/Sudan) was made by the Aswan High Dam; **Lake Volta** (Ghana) by the Akosombo Dam; **Lake Kariba** (Zambia/Zimbabwe) by the Kariba Dam.

## 6. COASTAL PLAINS, DESERTS AND ISLANDS

### Definitions

**Coastal plain:** A flat, low area of land lying along the shore of a sea or an ocean.

**Desert:** A very dry area which receives very little rainfall.

### Examples

| Feature | Where |
|---|---|
| East African coastal plain | Kenya, Tanzania, Mozambique |
| West African coastal plain | Senegal to Nigeria |
| Sahara Desert | North Africa — the largest hot desert in the world |
| Kalahari Desert | Botswana, Namibia, South Africa |
| Namib Desert | Namibia — a coastal desert |
| Madagascar | The largest island in Africa |

### Important Points

- Coastal plains are hot and often crowded because they are good for ports, trade, fishing and tourism.
- Many great African cities are on coastal plains: Lagos, Dar es Salaam, Mombasa, Alexandria, Maputo.
- The Namib Desert is dry partly because of the **cold Benguela ocean current** offshore.

---

## 7. HOW PHYSICAL FEATURES AFFECT PEOPLE

### Uses of physical features

- Mountains and highlands receive **relief rainfall**, have **fertile volcanic soils**, and attract farming and tourism.
- Rivers and lakes give **water, fish, transport, irrigation and hydro-electric power**.
- Plateaux hold most of Africa's **minerals**.
- Coastal plains hold **ports and towns**.
- All of these attract **tourists**, and tourism brings foreign exchange.

### Problems caused by physical features

- Mountains make **road and railway building** difficult and expensive.
- Rivers and lakes may **flood** and destroy crops and homes.
- Deserts have **too little water** for farming.
- Swamps and lakes breed **mosquitoes and tsetse flies**, spreading malaria and sleeping sickness.
- Steep slopes lead to **soil erosion** and landslides.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Physical features are the land forms that exist on the earth's surface and give the land its shape.
2. A plateau is a large area of fairly flat land raised high above the land around it.
3. A delta is the fan-shaped area of flat land formed where a river deposits soil as it enters a lake or a sea through several channels.
4. A rift valley is a long, deep, steep-sided valley formed when the land between two parallel faults sinks.
5. Atlas Mountains, Cape Ranges, and the folded ridges of the Atlas system in Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia.
6. Any three of: Mt Kilimanjaro, Mt Cameroon, Mt Elgon, Mt Kenya, Tibesti, Ahaggar.
7. The Sahara Desert, the Kalahari Desert and the Namib Desert.
8. Lake Nasser — Aswan High Dam; Lake Volta — Akosombo Dam; Lake Kariba — Kariba Dam; Lake Kainji — Kainji Dam.
9. The River Nile.
10. Lake Tanganyika.
11. The River Zambezi.
12. The Mediterranean Sea.
13. Lake Victoria.
14. The land cracks along two parallel faults. The block of land between the cracks is pushed upwards, or the land on either side sinks, leaving the middle block standing high as a mountain.
15. Long droughts with little rainfall, and too much water being taken from the rivers that feed it for irrigation. (Also: silting and the growth of the population around it.)
16. Because most of Africa is a plateau. Where a river leaves the high plateau and drops towards the coast, the sudden fall in the land makes waterfalls and rapids.
17. The River Congo, then the Atlantic Ocean.
18. **Help:** they receive relief rainfall for farming, have fertile volcanic soils, attract tourists, and are sources of rivers. **Difficulty:** they make the building of roads and railways hard and expensive (steep slopes also cause soil erosion and landslides).
19. Because the cold Benguela ocean current flows along that coast. Air passing over cold water carries little moisture, so very little rain falls on the land.
20. *Learner's map.* The Niger should be shown in West Africa flowing into the Gulf of Guinea, Lake Chad in the middle of West/Central Africa, and the Atlas Mountains in the far north-west.
