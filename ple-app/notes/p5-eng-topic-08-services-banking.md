## 1. WHAT A BANK IS

A **bank** is a place where people **keep their money safely**, take it out when they need
it, and may borrow money. Keeping money in a bank is safer than keeping it at home, and
savings may earn a little extra money called **interest**.

## 4. DEPOSITING AND WITHDRAWING — THE STEPS

### Depositing money

```
1. Get a deposit form.
2. Write your name, your account number, the date and the amount.
3. Count the money.
4. Take the form and the money to the cashier.
5. The cashier counts it, stamps the form and gives you a receipt.
```

### Withdrawing money

```
1. Get a withdrawal form.
2. Write your name, your account number, the date and the amount.
3. Sign the form with the same signature the bank has.
4. Give it to the cashier with your pass-book or identity card.
5. The cashier checks the signature, pays the money and records the new balance.
```

## 5. GRAMMAR: NEITHER … NOR

**Neither … nor** means **not one and not the other**. It is the opposite of *either … or*.

| Example |
|---|
| He is **neither** a manager **nor** a cashier at that bank. |
| Wafula will **neither** withdraw **nor** deposit money today. |
| **Neither** the cheque **nor** the form was signed. |

> **A note on one printed example:** the syllabus prints *"Neither is he a Manager nor a
> cashier at that bank."* In good English the words come in this order: **"He is neither a
> manager nor a cashier at that bank."** The syllabus wording is quoted so you know what it
> says; the corrected order is the one to use.

**Remember:** with *neither … nor* the sentence is already negative, so we never add another
*not*.

- ✗ He is neither **not** a manager nor a cashier.
- ✓ **He is neither a manager nor a cashier.**

---

## 6. GRAMMAR: AS SOON AS

**As soon as** means **immediately after**. It joins two actions that follow each other
closely.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| … **as soon as** … | The cashier gave Akello money **as soon as** she presented the withdrawal form. |
| **As soon as** … , … | **As soon as** Apio gave in the account number, the bank manager told him his balance. |
| … **as soon as** … | I will open an account **as soon as** I get the money. |

**Tense rule:** when the sentence is about the future, the part after *as soon as* uses the
**present** tense, not *will*.

- ✗ I will go **as soon as** the bank **will open**.
- ✓ I will go **as soon as** the bank **opens**.

---

## 7. GRAMMAR: THE TENSES USED IN THIS TOPIC

| Tense | Example |
|---|---|
| **Present simple** | The bank **opens** at nine o'clock. |
| **Past simple** | The cashier **paid** the money and **stamped** the form. |
| **Future** | Wafula **will deposit** his savings tomorrow. |

**Past simple of useful verbs:** deposit → **deposited** · withdraw → **withdrew** ·
pay → **paid** · sign → **signed** · give → **gave** · count → **counted** ·
bounce → **bounced**.

## 8. A CONVERSATION AT THE BANK

> **Sarah:** Good morning. I would like to deposit some money, please.
> **Cashier:** Good morning. May I have your deposit form and your account number?
> **Sarah:** Here they are. The amount is one hundred fifty thousand shillings.
> **Cashier:** Thank you. *(counts the money)* That is correct. Here is your receipt.
> **Sarah:** Could you also tell me my balance, please?
> **Cashier:** As soon as I enter this deposit, I will print your statement.
> **Sarah:** Thank you very much.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Deposit** = money in. **Withdraw** = money out.
- The **balance** is what remains.
- **Neither … nor** = not one and not the other; never add another *not*.
- **Either … or** is the positive pair; **neither … nor** is the negative pair.
- After **as soon as**, use the **present tense** for a future action.
- A cheque **bounces** when the account has too little money.
- To **forge** a signature is a crime.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing *neither … or* instead of **neither … nor**.
- Adding a second *not* in a *neither … nor* sentence.
- Writing "as soon as the bank will open" — use **opens**.
- Confusing **deposit** with **withdraw**.
- Writing *withdrawed*; the past of *withdraw* is **withdrew**.
- Leaving the amount in words blank on a form.
