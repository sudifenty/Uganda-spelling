**What you already know:** You can name common vehicles and describe simple actions. In P.5

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- name and describe the parts of a car and of a bicycle
- name the tools used in vehicle repair and say what each is used for

- use *if* and *in order to* in sentences
- read and write short texts about vehicle repair and maintenance

## 1. PARTS OF A VEHICLE — VOCABULARY

### Parts of a car

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **engine** | The machine that makes the vehicle move |
| **tyre** | The rubber ring fitted round a wheel |
| **wheel** | The round part on which the vehicle runs |
| **steering** | The wheel the driver turns to guide the vehicle |
| **windscreen** | The big glass in front of the driver |
| **wiper** | The arm that wipes rain off the windscreen |
| **driving mirror** | The mirror that lets the driver see behind |
| **boot** | The space at the back for carrying luggage |
| **seat** | Where a person sits |
| **seat belt** | The strap that holds a person safely in the seat |
| **head lamp** | The light in front of the vehicle |
| **indicator** | The blinking light that shows which way the driver will turn |
| **brakes** | The parts used to slow down or stop the vehicle |

### Parts of a bicycle

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **spokes** | The thin rods that join the rim of the wheel to its centre |
| **chain** | The row of metal links that turns the back wheel |
| **mudguard** | The cover above a wheel that keeps off mud |
| **carrier** | The frame at the back for carrying goods or a passenger |
| **saddle** | The seat of a bicycle |
| **reflector** | The red disc that shines when light falls on it |
| **bell** | The part rung to warn people |
| **pedal** | The part pressed with the foot to turn the chain |
| **handlebar** | The bar held with the hands to steer |

> **A note on one word:** the printed syllabus list spells this word "**peddle**". The part
> of a bicycle is correctly spelt **pedal**. *Peddle* is a different word meaning to sell
> goods from place to place.

## 2. GRAMMAR: MUST AND MUSTN'T

**Must** = it is necessary; it is a rule.
**Mustn't** (must not) = it is forbidden; do not do it.

| Must | Mustn't |
|---|---|
| You **must** repair your vehicle. | You **mustn't** drive with flat tyres. |
| A driver **must** fasten the seat belt. | You **mustn't** ride a bicycle without brakes. |
| You **must** check the brakes before a journey. | Children **mustn't** play near a moving vehicle. |

**Form:** *must* and *mustn't* are followed by the verb with **no** *to* and **no** *-s*.

- ✗ He must **to** repair the car. → ✓ He **must repair** the car.
- ✗ She mustn't **drives** fast. → ✓ She **mustn't drive** fast.

## 3. GRAMMAR: HAS AND HAVE, AND THE PRESENT PERFECT TENSE

**Has** is used with **he, she, it** and one person or thing.
**Have** is used with **I, you, we, they** and more than one.

| Sentence |
|---|
| Ogule **has** bought new tyres for his car. |
| The children **have** broken the windscreen of Mr Kasozi's car. |
| The mechanic **has** repaired the engine. |
| We **have** washed the vehicle. |

**The present perfect tense** is formed like this:

```
has / have  +  past participle
```

It is used for something that has **just happened**, or that happened at **some time
before now**, where the exact time is not important.

| Present simple | Present perfect |
|---|---|
| He repairs the car. | He **has repaired** the car. |
| They buy new tyres. | They **have bought** new tyres. |
| She cleans the windscreen. | She **has cleaned** the windscreen. |

**Useful past participles:** buy → **bought** · break → **broken** · drive → **driven** ·
take → **taken** · put → **put** · mend → **mended** · fix → **fixed**.

| Tool | What it is used for |
|---|---|

| **pliers** | Holding, bending and cutting wire |

| **tester** | Checking whether electricity is passing |
| **wire** | A thin metal thread that carries electricity |

## 5. GRAMMAR: THE CONDITIONAL "IF" AND "IN ORDER TO"

**If** shows that one thing depends on another.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| **If** … , … will … | **If** I want to remove a tyre, I **will** use a car jack. |
| … will … **if** … | I **will** buy a spanner tomorrow **if** I go to town. |
| **If** … , … | **If** the tyre bursts, the vehicle stops. |

**In order to** shows the **reason** or **purpose** for doing something.

| Example |
|---|
| Kirya used a pair of pliers **in order to** cut the wire. |
| The mechanic lifted the car **in order to** change the wheel. |
| We service the vehicle **in order to** avoid accidents. |

*In order to* can usually be shortened to **to**: *He came **to** repair the car.*

## 6. WHY VEHICLES ARE MAINTAINED

A short text to read aloud:

> A vehicle in a dangerous mechanical condition can kill. Brakes that do not hold, bald
> tyres and broken lights cause many accidents on our roads. A good driver checks the
> engine oil, the water, the tyres and the brakes before every long journey. He must also
> take the vehicle for servicing. He mustn't wait until something breaks.

**Questions on the text:** What can a vehicle in a dangerous condition do? Name three
things a good driver checks. What mustn't the driver wait for?

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Maintenance | Looking after something so that it stays in good order |
| Service | To check and repair a vehicle regularly |
| Mechanic | A person whose work is repairing machines and vehicles |
| Garage | A place where vehicles are kept or repaired |
| Puncture | A small hole that lets air out of a tyre |
| Spare | An extra part kept in case one is needed |
| Present perfect tense | The tense formed with *has* or *have* and a past participle |
| Conditional | A sentence that uses *if* to show one thing depends on another |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Must** = it is necessary. **Mustn't** = it is forbidden.
- After *must* and *mustn't*, the verb never takes **to** and never takes **-s**.
- **Has** goes with he, she, it. **Have** goes with I, you, we, they.
- The present perfect = **has / have + past participle**.
- *Buy* becomes **bought** and *break* becomes **broken** in the present perfect.
- **In order to** answers the question *why?*
- The bicycle part is spelt **pedal**.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- You **mustn't** drive with flat tyres.
- Ogule **has** bought new tyres for his car.
- The children **have** broken the windscreen.
- **If** I want to remove a tyre, I will use a car jack.
- Kirya used a pair of pliers **in order to** cut the wire.
- A **car jack** lifts the vehicle; a **spanner** loosens nuts.
- The seat of a bicycle is the **saddle**.

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing "He must to repair the car" — leave out *to*.
- Writing "She has broke the mirror" — the participle is **broken**.
- Using *have* with *he* or *she*.
- Confusing the **windscreen** (the glass) with the **wiper** (the arm that cleans it).
- Spelling the bicycle part as *peddle*.
- Using *must* when you mean *mustn't* in a warning.
