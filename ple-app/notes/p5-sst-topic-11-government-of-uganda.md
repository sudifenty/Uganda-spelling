## 1. WHAT GOVERNMENT IS

**Government:** The group of people who make laws, carry them out and settle disputes in a country.

### Why we need a government

To make and enforce **laws** · to keep **peace and security** · to provide **services** —
schools, hospitals, roads, water · to **collect revenue** and spend it for the country ·
to **protect the rights** of the people · to represent Uganda in **other countries**.

---

## 2. THE THREE ARMS OF GOVERNMENT

| Arm | Who it is | Its work |
|---|---|---|
| **The Legislature (Parliament)** | The Speaker, the Deputy Speaker and Members of Parliament | **Makes the laws**, approves the budget and taxes, and checks on the Executive |
| **The Executive** | The **President**, the Vice President, the **Prime Minister**, Cabinet Ministers and the civil service | **Carries out (implements) the laws** and runs the day-to-day work of the country |
| **The Judiciary** | The Chief Justice, judges and magistrates in the courts of law | **Interprets the laws** and settles disputes; punishes those who break the law |

**Separation of powers** means each arm does its own work and checks the others, so that
no one person or group has all the power.

### Some duties of the President

Head of **State**, Head of **Government** and **Commander-in-Chief** of the armed forces ·
appoints ministers, judges and ambassadors (with Parliament's approval) · signs Bills into
law · opens Parliament · represents Uganda abroad.

### How a law is made

A **Bill** is drafted → it is **tabled in Parliament** and read → it is **debated** and
discussed in committee → Parliament **passes** it → the **President signs** it → it is
**published in the Gazette** and becomes an **Act of Parliament (law)**.

---

## 3. LOCAL GOVERNMENT

Uganda is governed at the local level through the **Local Council (LC)** system, under
**decentralisation** — the giving of powers and services from the centre to the districts.

| Level | Name | Leader |
|---|---|---|
| **LC I** | Village | Chairperson LC I |
| **LC II** | Parish | Chairperson LC II |
| **LC III** | Sub-county | Chairperson LC III (with the Sub-county Chief) |
| **LC IV** | County | Chairperson LC IV |
| **LC V** | **District** | **District Chairperson**, with the **Chief Administrative Officer (CAO)** as head of the civil service |

**Work of local governments:** keeping law and order · settling small disputes · running
primary schools and health centres · maintaining feeder roads and water sources ·
collecting local revenue · planning and supervising development projects · mobilising
people for immunisation, cleaning and other programmes.

---

## 4. ELECTIONS AND THE ELECTORAL PROCESS

**Election:** The process of choosing leaders by voting.

**The Electoral Commission** organises and supervises elections and referenda in Uganda.

### The steps

1. **Registration** of voters and display of the voters' register.
2. **Nomination** of candidates.
3. **Campaigning** within the set period and rules.
4. **Polling day** — voters queue, are checked, are marked with ink and cast a **secret ballot**.
5. **Counting** the votes at the polling station before agents and observers.
6. **Declaring** the results.
7. **Swearing in** of the winners; complaints go to the courts.

### Qualifications of a voter

Be a **citizen of Uganda**, be **18 years or above**, be **registered**, and be of sound
mind.

### Challenges of elections and their solutions

**Rigging** · bribery · violence and intimidation · illiteracy · late materials · low
turnout. **Solutions:** an independent Electoral Commission, a clean register, voter
education, observers and agents, punishing offenders, and settling disputes in **court**
rather than by fighting.

---

## 5. THE CITIZEN

**Citizen:** A full member of a country who owes it loyalty and enjoys its rights and protection.

**Ways of becoming a Ugandan citizen:** by **birth**, by **descent**, by **registration**
(for example through marriage or long residence) and by **naturalisation**.

| Rights of a citizen | Responsibilities of a citizen |
|---|---|
| The right to **life** | Obeying the laws of the country |
| The right to **education** | **Paying taxes** honestly |
| The right to **own property** | Protecting public property and the environment |
| **Freedom of speech, worship and association** | **Voting** in elections |
| The right to **health care** | Respecting the rights and property of others |
| The right to a **fair trial** | Defending the country and keeping peace |
| The right to **vote and to be voted for** | Reporting crime and cooperating with leaders |
| Freedom of **movement** | Working hard and caring for one's family |

---

## 6. GOVERNMENT REVENUE

**Revenue:** The money the government collects to run the country.

### Sources

**Taxes** — income tax, VAT, customs duty, excise duty and local service tax (collected by
the **Uganda Revenue Authority**) · **licences and permits** · **fees** for services ·
**fines** from courts · **rent** from government property · **loans and grants** from other
countries and from bodies such as the World Bank · earnings from **exports** and from
government businesses.

### How it is used

Paying **civil servants, teachers, doctors, soldiers and police** · building and running
**schools, hospitals, roads and water systems** · **security and defence** · paying **debts**
· running Parliament, the courts and the districts · **emergency relief** in disasters.

**Why every citizen should pay tax:** taxes are the country's own money — when people pay
honestly, government does not have to borrow so much, and services reach the people faster.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Government | The people who make, carry out and interpret the laws of a country |
| Legislature | The arm that makes laws (Parliament) |
| Executive | The arm that carries out the laws |
| Judiciary | The arm that interprets the laws and settles disputes |
| Bill | A proposed law before it is passed |
| Act of Parliament | A Bill that has been passed and signed into law |
| Decentralisation | Transferring powers and services from the centre to local governments |
| Election | The process of choosing leaders by voting |
| Secret ballot | Voting in a way that nobody else sees your choice |
| Revenue | The money government collects to run the country |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The three arms: **Legislature (makes), Executive (carries out), Judiciary (interprets)**.
- Parliament is headed by the **Speaker**; the Judiciary by the **Chief Justice**; the Executive by the **President**.
- The President is **Head of State, Head of Government and Commander-in-Chief**.
- A **Bill** becomes an **Act** when Parliament passes it and the **President signs** it.
- Local councils run from **LC I (village) to LC V (district)**; the CAO heads the district civil service.
- The **Electoral Commission** organises elections; a voter must be **18 and registered**.
- Ways of becoming a citizen: **birth, descent, registration, naturalisation**.
- Taxes are collected by the **Uganda Revenue Authority (URA)**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- The arm of government that makes laws → the **Legislature (Parliament)**.
- The arm that interprets laws → the **Judiciary**.
- The head of the Judiciary → the **Chief Justice**.
- The head of a district council → the **District Chairperson (LC V)**.
- The head of the civil service in a district → the **Chief Administrative Officer**.
- The body that organises elections → the **Electoral Commission**.
- The lowest local council → **LC I**, at the village.
- One source of government revenue → **taxes** (or licences, fines, loans, grants).
- One responsibility of a citizen → paying taxes (or obeying the law, voting).

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Government is the group of people who make laws, carry them out and settle disputes in a country. Reasons: to make and enforce laws; to keep peace and security; to provide services; to collect and spend revenue; to protect people's rights; to represent the country abroad (any four).
2. **Legislature (Parliament)** — makes the laws; **Executive** — carries out the laws and runs the country; **Judiciary** — interprets the laws and settles disputes.
3. Legislature — the **Speaker**; Executive — the **President**; Judiciary — the **Chief Justice**.
4. Any four of: Head of State; Head of Government; Commander-in-Chief of the armed forces; appoints ministers, judges and ambassadors; signs Bills into law; opens Parliament; represents Uganda abroad.
5. A **Bill** is drafted and tabled in Parliament → it is read and **debated**, and studied by a committee → Parliament **passes** it → the **President signs** it → it is published and becomes an **Act of Parliament**.
6. Decentralisation is the transfer of powers, responsibilities and services from the central government to **local governments** such as districts and sub-counties.
7. **LC I** — village, Chairperson LC I; **LC II** — parish; **LC III** — sub-county; **LC IV** — county; **LC V** — district, the **District Chairperson**.
8. Any five of: keeping law and order; settling small disputes; running primary schools and health centres; maintaining feeder roads and water sources; collecting local revenue; planning development projects; mobilising people for immunisation and clean-ups.
9. An election is the process of choosing leaders by voting. It is organised by the **Electoral Commission**.
10. Registration of voters → nomination of candidates → campaigning → polling day with a secret ballot → counting the votes → declaring the results → swearing in the winners.
11. A voter must be a **citizen of Uganda**, **18 years or above**, **registered** as a voter, and of sound mind.
12. **Challenges:** rigging; bribery; violence and intimidation; illiteracy; late delivery of materials; low turnout (any four). **Solutions:** an independent Electoral Commission with a clean register and voter education; observers, agents and police at polling stations, with offenders punished and disputes settled in court.
13. By **birth**, by **descent**, by **registration** and by **naturalisation**.
14. **Rights (any five):** life; education; property; freedom of speech, worship and association; health care; a fair trial; voting; movement. **Responsibilities (any five):** obeying the law; paying taxes; protecting public property and the environment; voting; respecting others' rights; keeping peace; reporting crime.
15. **Sources (any five):** taxes; licences and permits; fees for services; court fines; rent from government property; loans and grants; earnings from exports. **Uses (any five):** paying civil servants; building schools and hospitals; making roads and water systems; defence and security; paying debts; running Parliament and the courts; emergency relief.
