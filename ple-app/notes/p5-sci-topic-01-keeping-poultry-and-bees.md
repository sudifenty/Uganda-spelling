## 1. POULTRY

**Poultry:** Birds kept at home for meat, eggs and money — **chicken, turkeys, ducks, guinea fowls, geese and pigeons**.

### External parts of a domestic fowl

**Comb** · **wattle** · **beak** · eyes · **feathers** · wings · **legs with scales** ·
**claws** · tail · vent.

| Cock (male) | Hen (female) |
|---|---|
| Big comb and wattles, bright feathers | Smaller comb and wattles |
| Long, pointed tail feathers and spurs | Rounded body, lays eggs |
| Crows | Clucks and broods |

### Types and breeds of chicken

| Type | Kept for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Layers** | Eggs | White Leghorn, Isa Brown |
| **Broilers** | Meat | Hybrid broilers |
| **Dual purpose** | Meat and eggs | Rhode Island Red, Sussex |
| **Local (indigenous)** | Meat, eggs, hardiness | The local kienyeji chicken |

### Systems of keeping poultry

| System | Description |
|---|---|
| **Free range** | Birds move about freely and look for their own food |
| **Deep litter** | Birds kept in a house on a floor covered with coffee husks or wood shavings |
| **Battery cage** | Birds kept in cages, mainly for layers |
| **Fold system** | A movable pen shifted to fresh ground each day |

### Care of poultry

Clean, dry, well-ventilated **house** with **perches, feeders, drinkers and laying nests** ·
**clean water always** · balanced feeds — starter, growers' mash, layers' mash, maize bran,
greens and **mineral licks** · **vaccination** (against Newcastle, gumboro, fowl pox) ·
deworming and dusting against parasites · **isolate sick birds** · remove wet litter ·
protect from thieves, snakes, hawks and dogs · keep records.

### Diseases and parasites

**Newcastle disease** (viral, kills many birds — prevent by vaccination) · **coccidiosis**
(bloody droppings) · **fowl typhoid** · **fowl pox** · **gumboro** · parasites: **lice,
mites, fleas, ticks and worms**.

## 2. BEE KEEPING (APICULTURE)

### The three types of bees in a colony

| Bee | Work |
|---|---|
| **Queen** | The only fertile female; **lays all the eggs** |
| **Drones** | Males; their work is to **mate with the queen** |
| **Workers** | Sterile females; **collect nectar and pollen, make honey, build the comb, feed the young, guard and clean the hive** |

### Hives

**Traditional** — log hives, pot hives, basket hives.
**Modern** — the **Kenya Top Bar (KTB)** hive and the **Langstroth** hive, which are easier
to inspect and give clean honey.

### Siting and managing an apiary

Place hives in a **quiet, shady place** near water and flowering plants, away from homes,
paths and animals · hang them on wires with grease or oil to stop ants · inspect regularly ·
avoid smoke, noise and bad smells near the hives.

### Harvesting honey

Harvest in the **evening or early morning**, wearing **protective clothing (a bee suit,
gloves, gumboots and a veil)** and using a **smoker** to calm the bees · cut only the combs
with **sealed honey**, leaving some for the bees · use clean containers · strain the honey
and store it in a cool, dry place.

**Bee products:** honey, **beeswax** (candles, polish, cosmetics), propolis, royal jelly,
bee venom, and **pollination** of crops.

---

## 3. POULTRY AND BEE FARMING AS A BUSINESS

Both need **little land and little capital** to start · they give **food and income** —
eggs, meat, honey and wax · they create **employment** · droppings give **manure** · bees
**pollinate** crops so yields improve · products are easy to sell locally.

**To run it as a business:** keep **records**, feed and treat the stock properly, find a
**market** before producing, add value (packing honey and candles), and save part of the
income for expansion.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Poultry | Birds kept at home for meat, eggs and money |
| Brooding | Keeping chicks warm |
| Deep litter | A system where birds are kept on a floor covered with litter |
| Apiary | The place where beehives are kept |
| Colony | All the bees living together in one hive |
| Queen bee | The only fertile female, which lays all the eggs |
| Drone | A male bee whose work is to mate with the queen |
| Smoker | The tool used to blow smoke and calm bees |
| Comb | The wax structure in which bees store honey and raise young |
| Apiculture | The keeping of bees |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Poultry: **chicken, turkeys, ducks, guinea fowls, geese, pigeons**.
- **Layers** give eggs; **broilers** give meat.
- Systems: **free range, deep litter, battery cage, fold**.
- **Newcastle disease** is the most feared poultry disease — prevented by **vaccination**.
- A bee colony has a **queen (lays eggs)**, **drones (mate)** and **workers (do all the work)**.
- Modern hives: **Kenya Top Bar** and **Langstroth**.
- Harvest honey **in the evening**, with a **smoker** and **protective clothing**.
- Bees also **pollinate** crops, raising yields.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- The only fertile female in a hive → the **queen**.
- The bee that does all the work in the hive → the **worker**.
- The tool used to calm bees → the **smoker**.
- The system where birds are kept on coffee husks → **deep litter**.
- Chicken kept for eggs → **layers**; for meat → **broilers**.
- Disease prevented by vaccinating chicken → **Newcastle disease**.
- One product of bees other than honey → **beeswax** (or propolis, royal jelly).
- Why honey is harvested in the evening → the bees are **calm and inside the hive**.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Poultry are birds kept at home for meat, eggs and money. Any five of: chicken, turkeys, ducks, guinea fowls, geese, pigeons.
2. Any six of: comb, wattle, beak, eyes, feathers, wings, legs with scales, claws, tail, vent.
3. A **cock** has a big comb and wattles, bright feathers, long pointed tail feathers and spurs, and it crows; a **hen** has a smaller comb, a rounded body, lays eggs and broods.
4. **Free range, deep litter, battery cage and fold.** *(Describe two, for example: in **deep litter** birds are kept in a house on a floor covered with coffee husks or wood shavings; in **free range** the birds move about freely and look for their own food.)*
5. Any three: White Leghorn — eggs; broilers — meat; Rhode Island Red — meat and eggs; local kienyeji — meat, eggs and hardiness.
6. Any six of: provide a clean, dry, well-ventilated house; give clean water always; feed a balanced ration; vaccinate; deworm and dust against parasites; isolate sick birds; remove wet litter; protect from predators and thieves; keep records.
7. **Diseases:** Newcastle, coccidiosis, fowl typhoid, fowl pox, gumboro (any four). **Parasites:** lice, mites, fleas, ticks, worms (any three).
8. **Queen** — the only fertile female, lays all the eggs; **drones** — males that mate with the queen; **workers** — sterile females that collect nectar, make honey, build combs, feed the young, guard and clean the hive.
9. **Traditional:** log hives and pot or basket hives. **Modern:** the **Kenya Top Bar** hive and the **Langstroth** hive.
10. Any four of: a quiet, shady place; near water and flowering plants; away from homes, paths and animals; safe from thieves; protected from ants by greasing the wires.
11. Harvest in the **evening or early morning** → wear **protective clothing** → light the **smoker** and calm the bees → open the hive quietly → cut only combs with **sealed honey**, leaving some for the bees → put the combs in clean containers → close the hive → strain the honey and store it in a cool, dry place.
12. Because bees **sting** to defend the hive. Protective clothing keeps the stings off the body, and the **smoke calms the bees** so they do not attack the harvester.
13. Any five of: honey, beeswax, propolis, royal jelly, bee venom, and pollination services.
14. Any five of: they need little land and little capital; they give food and income; they create employment; droppings give manure; bees pollinate crops; the products sell easily; they can be run beside other work.
15. Any four of: numbers of birds bought and sold; eggs collected each day; feeds used and their cost; vaccinations and treatments given; deaths; income and expenses.
