## 1. THE STRUCTURE OF THE HUMAN SKELETON

### Definition

**Skeleton:** The framework of bones that supports the body and gives it shape.

An adult human skeleton has about **206 bones**. A baby is born with more (about 300),
because some bones join together as the child grows.

### The two main parts

| Part | Bones it contains |
|---|---|
| **Axial skeleton** | Skull, vertebral column (backbone), ribs and breastbone (sternum) |
| **Appendicular skeleton** | Arms, legs, shoulder girdle and hip (pelvic) girdle |

---

## 2. NAMES OF DIFFERENT BONES

| Region | Bone | Where it is |
|---|---|---|
| Head | **Cranium** | The box that protects the brain |
| Head | **Mandible** | The lower jaw — the only movable bone of the skull |
| Chest | **Ribs** (12 pairs) | Protect the heart and lungs |
| Chest | **Sternum** | The breastbone at the front |
| Back | **Vertebral column** | 33 small bones called vertebrae |
| Shoulder | **Clavicle** (collar bone), **Scapula** (shoulder blade) | Shoulder girdle |
| Upper arm | **Humerus** | Between shoulder and elbow |
| Lower arm | **Radius** and **Ulna** | Between elbow and wrist |
| Hand | **Carpals, metacarpals, phalanges** | Wrist, palm and fingers |
| Hip | **Pelvic girdle** | Supports the trunk on the legs |
| Thigh | **Femur** | The longest and strongest bone in the body |
| Knee | **Patella** | The knee cap |
| Lower leg | **Tibia** (shin) and **Fibula** | Between knee and ankle |
| Foot | **Tarsals, metatarsals, phalanges** | Ankle, sole and toes |

**The smallest bone** in the body is the **stapes (stirrup)** in the middle ear.

---

## 3. JOINTS

### Definition

**Joint:** The place where two or more bones meet.

| Type of joint | Movement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Immovable (fixed)** | No movement | Joints of the cranium; joints of the pelvic girdle |
| **Slightly movable** | A little movement | Joints between the vertebrae; joints of the ribs |
| **Freely movable (synovial)** | Free movement | Shoulder, hip, elbow, knee, neck |

### Types of freely movable joints

| Joint | Movement | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Ball and socket** | Moves in all directions | Shoulder, hip |
| **Hinge** | Moves in one direction only, like a door | Elbow, knee, fingers |
| **Pivot** | Turning or rotating | Neck (between the skull and the first vertebra) |
| **Gliding** | Bones slide over each other | Wrist, ankle |

### Parts that hold joints together

| Part | Work |
|---|---|
| **Ligament** | Joins **bone to bone** |
| **Tendon** | Joins **muscle to bone** |
| **Cartilage** | Soft, smooth tissue at the ends of bones; stops rubbing |
| **Synovial fluid** | Oils the joint so that it moves smoothly |

---

## 4. MUSCLES

### Definition

**Muscle:** A body tissue that contracts (shortens) and relaxes to cause movement.

| Type | Control | Where it is found |
|---|---|---|
| **Voluntary muscles** | Under our control | Arms, legs, face — attached to the skeleton |
| **Involuntary muscles** | Work on their own | Stomach, intestines, blood vessels |
| **Cardiac muscle** | Works on its own, never tires | Only in the heart |

### How muscles work in pairs (antagonistic muscles)

Muscles can only **pull**, never push, so they work in pairs.

- To **bend** the arm: the **biceps contracts** and the **triceps relaxes**.
- To **straighten** the arm: the **triceps contracts** and the **biceps relaxes**.

---

## 5. FUNCTIONS OF THE MUSCULAR–SKELETAL SYSTEM

- **Support** — holds the body upright.
- **Shape** — gives the body its form.
- **Protection** — the cranium protects the brain, the ribs protect the heart and lungs, the vertebral column protects the spinal cord.
- **Movement** — bones, joints and muscles work together.
- **Production of blood cells** — red bone marrow makes red and white blood cells.
- **Storage of minerals** — bones store calcium and phosphorus.
- **Attachment** — muscles and organs are attached to bones.

## 6. DISEASES AND DISORDERS

| Condition | Cause | Sign |
|---|---|---|
| **Rickets** | Lack of vitamin D and calcium in children | Bowed (bent) legs, soft weak bones |
| **Osteomalacia** | Lack of vitamin D in adults | Soft, painful bones |
| **Osteoporosis** | Loss of calcium, common in old age | Bones break easily |
| **Arthritis** | Inflammation of joints | Painful, swollen, stiff joints |
| **Polio** | A virus | Wasting and paralysis of muscles |
| **Fracture** | Accident or a heavy knock | A broken or cracked bone |
| **Dislocation** | A bone forced out of its joint | Pain, swelling, deformity |
| **Sprain** | Torn or stretched ligaments | Pain and swelling at a joint |
| **Cramp** | Muscle contracting suddenly and not relaxing | Sharp pain in the muscle |
| **Muscle strain** | Overworking or overstretching a muscle | Pain and stiffness |

### Types of fractures

| Fracture | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Simple (closed)** | The bone breaks but does not pierce the skin |
| **Compound (open)** | The broken bone pierces through the skin |
| **Greenstick** | The bone bends and cracks on one side — common in children |
| **Comminuted** | The bone breaks into many pieces |

### First aid for a fracture

Keep the person calm and still · do **not** try to force the bone back · support the limb with a **splint** and a sling · stop any bleeding · take the person to a health worker at once.

## 7. KEEPING THE SYSTEM HEALTHY

- Eat a **balanced diet** rich in calcium (milk, fish with bones, greens) and protein.
- Get **vitamin D** from sunshine in the early morning and evening.
- Take **regular exercise** to keep muscles and bones strong.
- **Rest and sleep** enough.
- Avoid accidents — play safely, do not carry loads that are too heavy.
- **Immunise** children against polio.
- Treat injuries and infections early.
- Sit, stand and walk with **correct posture**.

### Importance of correct posture

- It keeps the vertebral column straight and healthy.
- It prevents back, neck and chest pain.
- It allows the lungs to fill properly with air and helps digestion.
- It prevents deformities such as a bent back.
- It makes a person look smart and confident.

**Good posture:** sit upright with the back straight and both feet on the floor; stand with the shoulders back and the head up; bend the knees, not the back, when lifting a load.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- An adult skeleton has about **206 bones**.
- The **femur** is the longest and strongest bone; the **stapes** is the smallest.
- The **mandible** is the only movable bone of the skull.
- Ball and socket — shoulder and hip. Hinge — elbow and knee. Pivot — neck.
- **Ligament joins bone to bone; tendon joins muscle to bone.**
- Biceps and triceps are **antagonistic** muscles.
- **Cardiac muscle** is found only in the heart.
- **Rickets** is caused by lack of vitamin D and calcium.
- Red bone marrow makes **blood cells**.
