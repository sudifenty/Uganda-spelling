# PRIMARY 4 SOCIAL STUDIES

**Theme: Living Together in Our District**

# TOPIC 3: VEGETATION IN OUR DISTRICT

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Four Social Studies Syllabus, Term II, Topic 3,
25 periods (`curriculum/P4-SOCIAL-STUDIES-2010.pdf`, pages 23–26).*

**How to use this topic.** The syllabus asks about the vegetation of **your own district**.
These notes teach you the types of vegetation, their uses, how people damage them and how to
care for them. You name the ones found where you live.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- say what vegetation is
- name the types of vegetation
- tell natural vegetation from planted vegetation
- give the uses of vegetation
- name the activities of people that damage vegetation
- suggest ways of caring for vegetation

---

## 1. WHAT VEGETATION IS

**Vegetation** means **all the plants growing in a place**. It is sometimes called the
**land cover**, because plants cover the land.

Vegetation includes big trees, small trees, bushes, grass and even the plants growing in
water.

---

## 2. TYPES OF VEGETATION

The syllabus names these five:

| Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Forests** | Many tall trees growing close together, with thick shade |
| **Swamps** | Wet land covered with water plants such as papyrus and reeds |
| **Shrubs** | Short woody plants and bushes, shorter than trees |
| **Grass** | Land covered mainly with grass, with a few scattered trees |
| **Crops** | Plants that people have planted for food or for sale |

---

## 3. NATURAL AND PLANTED VEGETATION

| | **Natural vegetation** | **Planted vegetation** |
|---|---|---|
| Who put it there | Grew **on its own** | **Planted by people** |
| Kinds of plants | Many different kinds mixed together | Usually one kind together |
| Arrangement | Grows anyhow, not in lines | Grows in **straight lines** |
| Care | Nobody weeds it | People weed, prune and water it |
| Examples | Natural forest, natural swamp, wild grassland, bushes | A pine or eucalyptus plantation, a banana garden, a tea or sugar-cane estate, trees planted at school |

**A quick test:** if the trees stand in **straight rows**, people planted them.

---

## 4. USES OF VEGETATION

The syllabus lists these uses:

| Use | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Source of herbs for medicine** | Many local medicines come from leaves, roots and bark |
| **Source of firewood** | Wood and charcoal for cooking |
| **Source of food** | Fruits, vegetables, mushrooms, honey and yams |
| **Provides oxygen** | Plants give out the oxygen we breathe |
| **Supports in rain formation** | Trees give off water vapour, which helps rain to form |
| **Controls soil erosion and wind** | Roots hold the soil; trees act as a windbreak |
| **For beauty** | Trees, flowers and lawns make a place look good |

**More uses you should also know**

- **Timber** for building and furniture
- **Shade** for people and animals
- **Grazing** — grass feeds cattle, sheep and goats
- **Homes for wild animals, birds and insects**
- **Tourism** — visitors pay to see forests and the animals in them
- **Materials** — papyrus for mats and baskets, reeds for roofing, bark for crafts

---

## 5. HOW PEOPLE AFFECT VEGETATION

The syllabus lists these activities:

| Activity | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Burning bushes** | Kills small plants, insects and young trees |
| **Over cultivation** | Uses up the soil so plants can no longer grow well |
| **Over grazing** | Too many animals eat the grass down to the roots |
| **Deforestation** | Cutting down trees without replacing them |
| **Making bricks** | Clearing swamps and burning firewood to bake the bricks |
| **Construction of roads** | Trees and grass are cleared for the road |
| **Building houses for settlement and industries** | Land is cleared for homes and factories |

**What happens when vegetation is destroyed**

- **Soil erosion** — nothing holds the soil, so rain washes it away
- **Less rain** — fewer trees means less water vapour in the air
- **Drought** and dry streams
- **Wild animals lose their homes**
- **Shortage of firewood, timber and herbs**
- Strong winds blow off roofs because there is no windbreak
- **Deserts** can form where the land is left bare

---

## 6. CARING FOR VEGETATION

| Way | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Afforestation** | Plant trees where there were none |
| **Reafforestation** | Plant trees again where they were cut |
| **Selective cutting** | Cut only the mature trees, leave the young ones |
| **Avoid bush burning** | Never set fire to the bush |
| **Control grazing** | Keep only as many animals as the land can feed; use paddocks |
| **Use other fuels** | Use energy-saving stoves, biogas or gas instead of much firewood |
| **Gazetting** | Government marks off forest reserves and national parks and protects them by law |
| **Educating people** | Teach people why vegetation matters |
| **Crop rotation and mulching** | Keep the soil fertile so land is not cleared again and again |

**What a pupil can do:** plant a tree at home and at school, water it, put a small fence
round it, and never break young plants.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Vegetation | All the plants growing in a place |
| Natural vegetation | Vegetation that grew on its own |
| Planted vegetation | Vegetation planted and cared for by people |
| Forest | Many tall trees growing close together |
| Swamp | Wet land covered with water plants |
| Shrub | A short woody plant or bush |
| Deforestation | Cutting down trees without replacing them |
| Afforestation | Planting trees where there were none |
| Reafforestation | Planting trees again where they were cut |
| Over grazing | Keeping too many animals on the same land |
| Over cultivation | Growing crops on the same land until the soil is worn out |
| Gazetting | Marking off land by law so that it is protected |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Vegetation means **all the plants growing in a place**.
- The five types in the syllabus are **forests, swamps, shrubs, grass and crops**.
- **Natural** vegetation grows on its own; **planted** vegetation is put there by people and
  usually grows in **straight lines**.
- Plants give us **oxygen**, and they **help rain to form**.
- Roots of plants **hold the soil** and stop **soil erosion**.
- **Deforestation** leads to soil erosion, less rain and drought.
- **Afforestation** = new trees. **Reafforestation** = trees again.
- Cutting **only mature trees** is called **selective cutting**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- What is vegetation? All the plants growing in a place.
- Name any three types of vegetation — forests, swamps, shrubs, grass, crops.
- Give one difference between natural and planted vegetation.
- Give two uses of vegetation — firewood, medicine, food, oxygen, timber, shade.
- Name two activities of people that destroy vegetation — bush burning, deforestation.
- Give two ways of caring for vegetation — planting trees, avoiding bush burning.
- What gas do plants give to people? **Oxygen.**

---

# COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Saying vegetation means only trees — grass and crops are vegetation too.
- Calling a planted forest a **natural** forest.
- Writing "cutting trees" as a way of **caring** for vegetation.
- Mixing up **afforestation** and **reafforestation**.
- Giving a **use** when the question asks how people **destroy** vegetation.
- Saying plants give us carbon dioxide — they give us **oxygen**.
