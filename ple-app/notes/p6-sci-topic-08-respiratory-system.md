## 1. RESPIRATION AND THE RESPIRATORY ORGANS

### Definitions

**Respiration:** The process by which the body breaks down food to release **energy**.

**Breathing:** The taking in of air (inhaling) and giving out of air (exhaling).

**Note carefully:** breathing is only the movement of air; **respiration** is the release of
energy from food inside the cells.

### The organs and their work

| Organ | Work |
|---|---|
| **Nose (nostrils)** | Takes in air; the **hairs and mucus** trap dust and germs, and the air is warmed and moistened |
| **Mouth** | Can also take in air, but the air is not filtered |
| **Pharynx (throat)** | A passage for air and food |
| **Epiglottis** | A flap that closes the windpipe when swallowing, so food does not enter |
| **Larynx (voice box)** | Produces sound; contains the vocal cords |
| **Trachea (windpipe)** | Carries air to the lungs; kept open by rings of cartilage; lined with cilia and mucus that trap dust |
| **Bronchi** | The two tubes branching from the trachea, one into each lung |
| **Bronchioles** | The smaller tubes inside the lungs |
| **Alveoli (air sacs)** | Tiny thin-walled sacs where **oxygen enters the blood** and carbon dioxide leaves |
| **Lungs** | Two spongy organs in the chest, protected by the ribs |
| **Diaphragm** | The sheet of muscle below the lungs that helps in breathing |
| **Ribs and intercostal muscles** | Protect the lungs and help them expand and relax |

**Why we should breathe through the nose:** the nose filters, warms and moistens the air,
which the mouth does not.

## 2. BREATHING IN AND OUT

| Breathing in (inhaling) | Breathing out (exhaling) |
|---|---|
| The **diaphragm flattens** and moves down | The diaphragm **relaxes** and curves up |
| The **ribs move up and out** | The ribs move down and in |
| The chest **increases** in size | The chest **decreases** in size |
| Air rushes **into** the lungs | Air is pushed **out** of the lungs |

### Comparing inhaled and exhaled air

| Gas | Inhaled air | Exhaled air |
|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | About 21% | About 16% |
| Carbon dioxide | Very little (0.03%) | **About 4%** |
| Water vapour | Varies | **More** |
| Nitrogen | About 78% | About 78% (unchanged) |
| Temperature | Cooler | **Warmer** |

**A simple test:** breathe onto a cold mirror — the water vapour condenses. Breathe through
a straw into lime water — it turns **milky**, showing carbon dioxide.

## 3. RESPIRATION, FOOD AND OXYGEN

Inside every cell, food (mainly **glucose** from carbohydrates) is combined with **oxygen**
to release energy:

**Food (glucose) + oxygen → energy + carbon dioxide + water**

- The energy is used for **movement, growth, warmth, and the working of all organs**.
- The **carbon dioxide and water** produced are waste and are breathed out.
- Without enough oxygen or enough food, the body becomes **weak and tired**.

**Two kinds:** respiration **with** oxygen is called aerobic respiration; when a person
works very hard and the muscles run short of oxygen, some energy is released without
oxygen, which causes **muscle cramps and tiredness**.

**The link with the circulatory system:** the **red blood cells** carry oxygen from the
alveoli to every cell and bring carbon dioxide back to the lungs.

## 4. DISEASES AND DISORDERS OF THE SYSTEM

| Disease | Cause | Signs | Prevention |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tuberculosis (TB)** | Bacteria spread by coughing and sneezing | Long cough, chest pain, weight loss, night sweats | BCG immunisation, ventilation, complete the treatment |
| **Pneumonia** | Bacteria or viruses | Fast breathing, fever, chest pain | Warmth, good nutrition, early treatment, immunisation |
| **Bronchitis** | Infection or irritation of the bronchi | Cough with phlegm | Avoid smoke and dust; treat colds early |
| **Asthma** | Narrowing of the air tubes; often triggered by dust, cold or smoke | Wheezing, difficulty in breathing | Avoid triggers; carry prescribed medicine |
| **Common cold and flu** | Viruses | Blocked nose, sneezing | Cover the mouth; keep warm |
| **Whooping cough** | Bacteria | Violent cough with a whoop | Immunisation (DPT) |
| **Lung cancer** | Mainly smoking | Persistent cough, blood in sputum | Do not smoke; avoid passive smoking |
| **Diphtheria** | Bacteria | Sore throat with a grey covering | Immunisation |

---

## 5. KEEPING THE SYSTEM HEALTHY

- **Do not smoke**, and stay away from other people's smoke.
- Keep rooms **well ventilated** — open windows and doors.
- Avoid **dust and smoke**; cook in a well-ventilated place or use an outdoor or
  energy-saving stove.
- **Cover the mouth and nose** when coughing or sneezing, and use a handkerchief.
- Take children for **immunisation** (BCG, DPT).
- Eat a **balanced diet** and take **regular exercise** to strengthen the lungs.
- **Breathe through the nose**, not the mouth.
- Avoid **overcrowded, poorly ventilated** rooms.
- Treat coughs, colds and chest pains **early**; complete any TB treatment given.
- Plant trees, which give out **oxygen** and clean the air.

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Breathing** is the movement of air; **respiration** is the release of energy from food.
- Air path: **nose → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli**.
- Gas exchange happens in the **alveoli**.
- **Food + oxygen → energy + carbon dioxide + water.**
- When breathing in, the **diaphragm flattens and the ribs move up and out**.
- Exhaled air has **less oxygen, more carbon dioxide and more water vapour**, and is warmer.
- **Lime water turns milky** in the presence of carbon dioxide.
- Breathe through the **nose**, because it filters, warms and moistens the air.
- TB is prevented by **BCG immunisation** and good ventilation.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. **Breathing** is simply taking in and giving out air, while **respiration** is the process by which the body breaks down food to release energy inside the cells.
2. Any six of: nose, pharynx, epiglottis, larynx, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli, lungs, diaphragm.
3. **Nose** — takes in air and filters, warms and moistens it; **epiglottis** — closes the windpipe when swallowing; **trachea** — carries air to the lungs; **alveoli** — where oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves; **diaphragm** — the muscle that flattens and relaxes to help breathing.
4. Nose → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → **alveoli** → blood.
5. Because the nose has **hairs and mucus that trap dust and germs**, and it **warms and moistens** the air before it reaches the lungs; the mouth does none of these.
6. The **diaphragm flattens and moves downwards**, the **ribs move upwards and outwards**, the chest cavity increases in size and air rushes into the lungs.
7. Any three of: inhaled air has more **oxygen** (21% against 16%); exhaled air has much more **carbon dioxide** (about 4% against 0.03%); exhaled air has more **water vapour**; exhaled air is **warmer**.
8. Breathe out through a straw into **lime water**. The lime water turns **milky**, showing that carbon dioxide is present. (Comparing with air blown in by a pump shows the difference.)
9. Breathe onto a **cold mirror or glass**. Tiny drops of water form on it, showing that exhaled air contains water vapour.
10. **Food (glucose) + oxygen → energy + carbon dioxide + water**
11. Any four of: movement and work; growth and repair of the body; keeping the body warm; the beating of the heart and the working of other organs; thinking; digestion.
12. Any five of: tuberculosis, pneumonia, bronchitis, asthma, the common cold and flu, whooping cough, lung cancer, diphtheria.
13. Tuberculosis is caused by **bacteria spread through coughing and sneezing** by an infected person. Signs: a cough lasting more than two weeks, chest pain, weight loss, night sweats, fever (any two).
14. Any six of: do not smoke and avoid other people's smoke; keep rooms well ventilated; avoid dust and cooking smoke; cover the mouth when coughing; immunise children; eat a balanced diet; exercise regularly; breathe through the nose; avoid overcrowded rooms; treat coughs and colds early; plant trees.
15. The blood, especially the **red blood cells**, carries **oxygen** from the alveoli to every cell of the body, and carries the waste **carbon dioxide** back to the lungs to be breathed out. Without the circulatory system, the oxygen would never reach the cells.
