## 1. WHAT SOIL IS AND HOW IT IS FORMED

**Soil:** The loose top layer of the earth's surface in which plants grow.

### How soil is formed

| Process | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Weathering of rock** | Rocks break into small particles through **heat and cold** (physical), **water and chemicals** (chemical), and **plant roots and animals** (biological) |
| **Decomposition of matter** | Dead plants and animals rot and form **humus**, which mixes with the rock particles |

Soil formation is **very slow** — it can take hundreds of years to form a few centimetres.

---

## 2. TYPES OF SOIL

| Type | Particles | Properties | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sandy soil** | Large | Rough; **drains very fast**; poor at holding water and nutrients; light and easy to dig; warms quickly | Groundnuts, cassava, water melons; brick making |
| **Clay soil** | Very small | Smooth and sticky when wet, hard when dry; **holds too much water**; poor drainage and air; heavy to dig | Rice, pottery, bricks, dams |
| **Loam soil** | Mixed sand, clay and humus | **The best for farming** — holds enough water, drains well, has air and plenty of humus | Nearly all crops |

**Soil profile:** the layers seen when you dig a pit — **top soil** (dark, with humus and
roots), **sub soil** (lighter, with more stones), **weathered rock**, then **bedrock**.

---

## 3. COMPONENTS OF SOIL AND HOW TO SHOW THEM

| Component | Simple experiment |
|---|---|
| **Mineral (rock) particles** | Put soil in a bottle of water, shake and let it settle — it separates into **sand at the bottom, silt, then clay**, with humus floating on top |
| **Air** | Drop dry soil into water — **bubbles rise**, showing air was in the spaces |
| **Water** | Heat soil in a dry test tube — **drops of water** form on the cool part |
| **Humus (organic matter)** | Heat soil strongly — it **darkens, smokes and smells** as the humus burns; the soil becomes lighter in colour |
| **Living things** | Look closely — worms, ants, termites, roots, bacteria and fungi |

---

## 4. IMPORTANCE OF SOIL

- Plants get **anchorage, water and mineral salts** from it.
- It is used for **farming** — food, cash crops and pasture.
- It is a **home** for earthworms, ants, termites and micro-organisms.
- It is used for **building** — bricks, mud and wattle, murram roads.
- It is used for **pottery** — pots, plates, tiles.
- It **holds and filters water**, keeping wells and springs clean.
- Some soils contain **minerals** that are mined.

---

## 5. SOIL EROSION AND SOIL EXHAUSTION

**Soil erosion:** The carrying away of the top soil by water, wind or animals.

| Type | How it happens |
|---|---|
| **Sheet erosion** | A thin layer is washed off evenly |
| **Rill erosion** | Small channels form on the slope |
| **Gully erosion** | Deep, wide channels form |
| **Wind erosion** | Dry, bare soil is blown away |
| **Landslides** | Whole masses of soil slide down a steep slope |

**Causes:** cutting trees, **bush burning**, overgrazing, farming on steep slopes,
over-cultivation, poor road drainage, heavy rain on **bare soil**.

**Soil exhaustion:** The loss of soil fertility caused by growing the same crop again and
again, over-cultivation and not returning any manure.

### Effects

Poor yields and famine · silting of rivers and lakes · loss of vegetation and habitats ·
gullies that cut roads and gardens · dust storms · landslides · poverty.

---

## 6. SOIL CONSERVATION

| Method | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Mulching** | Covers the soil, holds moisture, stops rain hitting it directly |
| **Terracing** | Steps on a slope slow the running water |
| **Contour ploughing** | Ploughing across the slope, not up and down |
| **Strip cropping** | Crops planted in strips along the contour |
| **Crop rotation** | Different crops each season, so the soil is not exhausted |
| **Applying manure and compost** | Returns humus and nutrients |
| **Planting cover crops and trees** | Protect the soil surface and hold it with roots |
| **Controlled grazing** | Prevents the land being left bare |
| **Fallowing** | Resting the land to recover |
| **Building trenches and check dams** | Trap running water in gullies |

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Soil | The loose top layer of the earth in which plants grow |
| Weathering | The breaking down of rocks into small particles |
| Humus | Decayed plant and animal matter in the soil |
| Soil profile | The layers of soil seen in a pit |
| Loam | The best farming soil — a mixture of sand, clay and humus |
| Soil erosion | The carrying away of top soil by water, wind or animals |
| Soil exhaustion | The loss of soil fertility |
| Mulching | Covering the soil with dry grass or leaves |
| Crop rotation | Changing the crop grown on a plot each season |
| Fallowing | Resting the land for a season |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- Soil is formed by **weathering of rocks** and **decomposition of dead matter**.
- The three types: **sandy, clay and loam**; **loam is the best** for farming.
- Components: **mineral particles, air, water, humus and living things**.
- Bubbles from soil in water show **air**; drops in a heated tube show **water**; smoke and darkening show **humus**.
- **Sandy** soil drains fastest; **clay** holds the most water.
- Erosion types: **sheet, rill, gully, wind** and landslides.
- Conservation: **mulching, terracing, contour ploughing, crop rotation, manure, cover crops**.

---

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- The best soil for farming → **loam**.
- The soil that holds most water → **clay**; the fastest draining → **sandy**.
- Decayed plant and animal matter in soil → **humus**.
- Experiment showing soil contains air → **bubbles rise when dry soil is put in water**.
- The carrying away of top soil → **soil erosion**.
- Erosion that forms deep channels → **gully erosion**.
- One way of conserving soil on a hill → **terracing** (or contour ploughing).
- Growing a different crop each season → **crop rotation**.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Soil is the loose top layer of the earth's surface in which plants grow. It is formed by the **weathering of rocks** and the **decomposition of dead plant and animal matter**.
2. **Physical** — heat and cold cracking rocks; **chemical** — water and chemicals dissolving rocks; **biological** — plant roots and burrowing animals breaking rocks.
3. **Sandy** — large particles, drains very fast, poor at holding water; **clay** — very small particles, sticky when wet and hard when dry, holds too much water; **loam** — a mixture with plenty of humus, holds enough water and drains well.
4. **Loam**, because it holds enough water but still drains well, contains **air and plenty of humus**, and is easy to work.
5. **Clay**, because its very fine particles stick together when wet and become hard when dried or fired.
6. **Top soil → sub soil → weathered rock → bedrock.**
7. Mineral (rock) particles, **air**, **water**, **humus** and **living things**.
8. Drop **dry soil into a container of water**. **Bubbles rise** to the surface, showing that air was trapped in the spaces between the soil particles.
9. Put soil in a **dry test tube and heat it gently**. **Drops of water** appear on the cooler upper part of the tube, showing that the soil contained water.
10. Heat soil **strongly** in a tin or on a metal sheet. It gives off **smoke and a burning smell** and becomes **lighter in colour**, showing that the humus has burnt away.
11. Any six of: it anchors plants and supplies water and mineral salts; it is used for farming; it is a home for soil animals; it is used for building bricks and roads; it is used for pottery; it holds and filters water; some soils contain minerals.
12. Soil erosion is the carrying away of the top soil by water, wind or animals. Types: **sheet, rill, gully, wind erosion** and landslides (any four).
13. Any five of: cutting down trees; bush burning; overgrazing; farming on steep slopes; over-cultivation; poor road drainage; heavy rain falling on bare soil.
14. Soil exhaustion is the **loss of soil fertility**. It is caused by growing the same crop again and again, over-cultivation, and failing to add manure or rest the land.
15. Any eight of: mulching; terracing; contour ploughing; strip cropping; crop rotation; applying manure and compost; planting cover crops and trees; controlled grazing; fallowing; digging trenches and check dams.
