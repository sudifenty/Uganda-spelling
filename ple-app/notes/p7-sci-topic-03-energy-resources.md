# PRIMARY 7 INTEGRATED SCIENCE

**Theme: The Environment**

# TOPIC 3: ENERGY RESOURCES IN THE ENVIRONMENT

*Curriculum reference: NCDC Primary Seven Curriculum, Set One, Integrated Science Topic 3,
12 periods (`curriculum/PRIMARY-SEVEN-SET-ONE.pdf`, topic outline page 77 and pages 91–94).*

**What you already know:** You know that energy makes things work. In P.7 you study the
**energy resources found in the environment** — where each one comes from, how it is used,
and why some will run out.

**By the end of this topic you should be able to:**

- name the energy resources in the environment and their sources
- describe energy from water, fossil fuels, plants, animals and wind
- state the importance of the different energy resources
- explain the difference between renewable and non-renewable resources
- suggest ways of conserving energy

---

## 1. WHAT ENERGY IS

### Definitions

**Energy:** The ability to do work.

**Energy resource:** Anything in the environment from which we can get energy.

### Forms of energy

Heat · light · sound · electrical · chemical · mechanical · nuclear.

**The sun is the main source of energy on earth.** Almost every other energy resource can
be traced back to the sun.

---

## 2. ENERGY RESOURCES FROM WATER

| Resource | How it works | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydro-electric power** | Falling or fast-flowing water turns turbines that drive generators | Nalubaale and Kiira (Jinja), Bujagali, Isimba, Karuma; Aswan in Egypt; Kainji in Nigeria |
| **Steam engines** | Water is heated until it makes steam; the steam pushes pistons or turbines | Old railway engines, thermal power stations, sugar factories |
| **Tidal energy** | The rise and fall of sea tides turns turbines | Coastal countries with a big tidal range |

### Advantages of water energy

It is **renewable**, it does not pollute the air, it is cheap to run once built, and the
dam can also store water for irrigation and fishing.

### Disadvantages

Building a dam is **very expensive**, it may flood people's land and homes, it can affect
fish and wildlife, and it fails when the water level drops during long droughts.

---

## 3. ENERGY RESOURCES FROM FOSSIL FUELS

### Definition

**Fossil fuels:** Fuels formed from the remains of plants and animals that died and were buried millions of years ago. They are **coal, petroleum (crude oil) and natural gas**.

| Fuel | Formed from | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Coal** | Buried plant remains | Heating, thermal power stations, industries |
| **Petroleum (oil)** | Buried sea animals and plants | Petrol, diesel, paraffin, jet fuel, lubricants |
| **Natural gas** | Found with oil deposits | Cooking, heating, generating electricity |

### Important Points

- Fossil fuels are **non-renewable** — once used, they cannot be replaced in our lifetime.
- Burning them releases smoke and gases that cause **air pollution, acid rain and global warming**.
- Uganda has discovered oil in the **Albertine Graben** in the west.

---

## 4. ENERGY RESOURCES FROM PLANTS

| Resource | Description |
|---|---|
| **Firewood and charcoal** | The commonest fuels in Ugandan homes; from trees |
| **Biofuel (biodiesel, ethanol)** | Fuel made from crops such as sugar cane, maize and jatropha |
| **Biogas from plant waste** | Gas produced when plant material rots without air |
| **Plant residues** | Coffee husks, maize cobs, rice husks and bagasse burnt as fuel |

**Care:** cutting too many trees for firewood and charcoal causes **deforestation**,
soil erosion and less rainfall. Use energy-saving stoves and plant more trees.

---

## 5. ENERGY RESOURCES FROM ANIMALS

| Resource | Description |
|---|---|
| **Animal (muscle) energy** | Oxen ploughing, donkeys and camels carrying loads, horses pulling carts |
| **Biogas from dung** | Cow dung is put in a digester where bacteria break it down without air, producing gas for cooking and lighting |
| **Animal fats and oils** | Once used in lamps and candles |

### Advantages of biogas

It is cheap and renewable · it uses waste that would otherwise pollute · the used slurry
makes very good manure · it reduces the cutting of trees · it burns cleanly with no smoke.

---

## 6. ENERGY FROM WIND, THE SUN AND THE EARTH

| Resource | How it is used | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Wind** | Windmills pump water and turn turbines to make electricity; wind drives sailing boats | Free and clean, but the wind is not always blowing |
| **Solar** | Solar panels make electricity; solar cookers and driers use the heat | Very useful in Uganda because there is sunshine all year |
| **Geothermal** | Heat from hot rocks inside the earth makes steam that drives turbines | Found near hot springs, for example in western Uganda and in Kenya |

---

## 7. RENEWABLE AND NON-RENEWABLE RESOURCES

| Renewable (can be replaced) | Non-renewable (get used up) |
|---|---|
| Solar, wind, water (hydro and tidal), biogas, firewood if trees are replanted, geothermal, animal energy | Coal, petroleum, natural gas, and other minerals |

### Importance of energy resources

- Cooking, lighting and heating in homes.
- Running **industries and machines**.
- **Transport** — vehicles, boats, trains and aeroplanes.
- Pumping and treating **water**.
- Powering **hospitals, schools and offices**.
- **Communication** — radio, television, phones, internet.
- Creating **jobs** and income.
- Ploughing and other farm work.

### Ways of conserving energy

Switch off lights and appliances that are not in use · use **energy-saving bulbs and
stoves** · use solar and biogas instead of firewood · plant trees for fuel wood ·
maintain vehicles well so they use less fuel · walk or share transport for short journeys ·
insulate and cover cooking pots to save heat.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Energy | The ability to do work |
| Energy resource | Anything from which energy can be obtained |
| Fossil fuel | Fuel formed from remains of plants and animals buried long ago |
| Renewable resource | A resource that can be replaced or does not run out |
| Non-renewable resource | A resource that gets used up and cannot be replaced |
| Hydro-electric power | Electricity produced by falling water |
| Biogas | Gas produced when organic waste rots without air |
| Biofuel | Fuel made from crops or plant material |
| Geothermal energy | Energy from heat inside the earth |
| Tidal energy | Energy from the rise and fall of sea tides |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- The **sun** is the main source of energy on earth.
- The three fossil fuels are **coal, petroleum and natural gas** — all **non-renewable**.
- HEP stations in Uganda: **Nalubaale, Kiira, Bujagali, Isimba and Karuma**, all on the Nile.
- **Biogas** is made from dung and other waste rotting **without air**, and the leftover slurry is good manure.
- Renewable: solar, wind, water, biogas, geothermal.
- Burning fossil fuels causes **air pollution and global warming**.
- Most Ugandan homes still cook with **firewood and charcoal**, which causes deforestation.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Energy is the ability to do work. Forms: heat, light, sound, electrical, chemical, mechanical (any four).
2. **The sun.**
3. Hydro-electric power, steam energy and tidal energy.
4. Coal, petroleum (crude oil) and natural gas.
5. They pollute the air and cause global warming; they are non-renewable and will get used up. (Also: mining and drilling damage the environment.)
6. Any two of: firewood, charcoal, biofuel (ethanol or biodiesel), biogas from plant waste, crop residues such as bagasse.
7. Biogas is a gas produced when animal dung or other organic waste rots in a digester **without air**. The gas is piped away and burnt for cooking and lighting.
8. Any three of: it is cheap and renewable; it uses waste; the slurry left over is good manure; it reduces the cutting of trees; it burns cleanly without smoke.
9. Animals give **muscle energy** — oxen ploughing, donkeys carrying loads — and their **dung is used to make biogas**.
10. Renewable resources can be replaced or never run out, such as solar and wind; non-renewable resources get used up and cannot be replaced, such as coal and oil.
11. Renewable: **solar, wind**. Non-renewable: **coal, natural gas**.
12. Any three of: Nalubaale, Kiira, Bujagali, Isimba, Karuma.
13. **Advantages:** it is renewable and it does not pollute the air (also cheap to run, and the dam stores water). **Disadvantages:** dams are very expensive to build and they may flood people's land (also affected by drought).
14. Any four of: cooking and lighting, running industries, transport, pumping water, powering hospitals and schools, communication, creating jobs, farm work.
15. Any four of: switch off lights and appliances not in use; use energy-saving bulbs and stoves; use solar or biogas instead of firewood; plant trees for firewood; cover pots while cooking; service vehicles regularly; walk or share transport for short journeys.
