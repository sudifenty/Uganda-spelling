## 1. WHAT PHYSICAL FEATURES ARE

**Physical features:** The natural land forms found on the earth's surface.

They include **mountains, hills, plateaux, plains, rift valleys, lakes, rivers, waterfalls,
swamps, islands and valleys**.

**Rift valley:** A deep and wide valley that stretches for a long distance, formed when land
between two faults sinks.

---

## 2. MOUNTAINS AND HILLS OF UGANDA

| Mountain | Where | How it was formed | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rwenzori** | Kasese, western Uganda | **Block (faulting)** | The highest in Uganda — **Margherita Peak, 5,109 m**; has snow |
| **Elgon** | Mbale, eastern Uganda | **Volcanic** | The largest base in Uganda; Sipi Falls are on its slopes |
| **Muhavura, Mgahinga, Sabinyo** | Kisoro, south-west | Volcanic (the Virunga range) | Home of the mountain gorillas |
| **Moroto, Kadam, Napak** | Karamoja | Volcanic | In the dry north-east |
| **Morungole** | Kaabong | Volcanic | Near Kidepo Valley |

**Hills:** Kampala's hills (Nakasero, Kololo, Namirembe, Rubaga, Mengo), Tororo Rock, the
hills of Kigezi.

### How mountains are formed

| Type | How | Example in Uganda |
|---|---|---|
| **Block (fault-block)** | Land between two faults is pushed up or the sides sink | **Rwenzori** |
| **Volcanic** | Molten rock comes out of the earth, cools and piles up | **Elgon, Muhavura, Moroto** |
| **Folded** | Layers of rock are squeezed and bend upwards | Not common in Uganda (the Atlas in North Africa is an example) |
| **Residual** | Hard rock left standing after the softer rock around is worn away | Tororo Rock |

---

## 3. THE RIFT VALLEY, PLATEAUX AND PLAINS

- The **western arm of the Great Rift Valley** (the Albertine Rift) runs along Uganda's
  western border, holding **Lakes Albert, Edward and George** and the **Semliki** valley.
- Most of Uganda is a **plateau** about 1,000–1,300 metres above sea level.
- **Plains** and lowlands lie around Lake Kyoga and in the north.
- **Depressions** and **valleys** are found between the hills.

---

## 4. LAKES, RIVERS, WATERFALLS AND SWAMPS

### Lakes

| Lake | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Lake Victoria** | The largest in Uganda and in Africa; shared with Kenya and Tanzania |
| **Lake Kyoga** | A shallow, swampy lake in the middle of the country |
| **Lake Albert** | In the rift valley, shared with D.R. Congo |
| **Lake Edward and Lake George** | In the rift valley, joined by the Kazinga Channel |
| **Lake Bunyonyi** | Very deep, in Kabale |
| **Lake Wamala, Bisina, Opeta, Nakivali** | Smaller inland lakes |
| **Crater lakes** — Katwe, Nyamunuka | Formed in the mouths of old volcanoes |

**How lakes are formed:** by **faulting** (Albert, Edward, George), by **down-warping**
(Victoria, Kyoga), by **volcanic action** (crater lakes such as Katwe), and by **people**
building dams (man-made lakes).

### Rivers

**The Nile** — the longest, leaving Lake Victoria at **Jinja** as the Victoria Nile, passing
Lake Kyoga and Lake Albert, then flowing north as the **Albert Nile** · **Katonga** ·
**Kafu** · **Mpologoma** · **Aswa** · **Semliki** · **Sezibwa** · **Manafwa**.

### Waterfalls

**Murchison (Kabalega) Falls** on the Victoria Nile · **Karuma Falls** · **Bujagali** and
**Owen Falls** near Jinja · **Sipi Falls** on Mt Elgon · **Itanda Falls**.

### Swamps and islands

Swamps lie around Lake Kyoga, Lake Victoria and along many rivers, and are important for
water, papyrus and fish. Uganda's islands include the **Ssese Islands**, **Buvuma** and
**Koome** in Lake Victoria.

---

## 5. HOW PHYSICAL FEATURES AFFECT PEOPLE'S LIVES

### The good side

- Mountains and highlands receive **relief rainfall** and have **fertile volcanic soils**, so farming is good.
- Lakes and rivers give **water, fish, transport and hydro-electric power** (Nalubaale, Kiira, Bujagali, Isimba, Karuma).
- Physical features attract **tourists** — the Rwenzori, Murchison Falls, the crater lakes, the source of the Nile.
- Swamps supply **papyrus, clay and water** and control floods.
- They form **natural boundaries** between districts and countries.

### The difficult side

- Mountains make **road building** hard and expensive.
- Steep slopes cause **soil erosion and landslides**, as at Bududa on Mt Elgon.
- Rivers and lakes **flood** and destroy crops and homes.
- Swamps and lakes breed **mosquitoes and bilharzia snails**.
- Some areas are cut off during the rains.

### Caring for physical features

Plant trees on hills and river banks · dig **terraces and contour trenches** on slopes ·
do not build or farm in **swamps and wetlands** · avoid pouring waste into lakes and rivers ·
avoid **bush burning** and stone quarrying without restoring the land · obey the laws that
protect wetlands, forests and lake shores.

# KEY DEFINITIONS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Physical features | The natural land forms of the earth's surface |
| Rift valley | A deep, wide valley formed when land between two faults sinks |
| Plateau | A large area of raised, fairly flat land |
| Block mountain | A mountain formed when land between faults is pushed up |
| Volcanic mountain | A mountain formed from molten rock that cooled and piled up |
| Crater lake | A lake formed in the mouth of an old volcano |
| Waterfall | Water falling from a high place in a river |
| Swamp | Waterlogged land covered with papyrus and reeds |
| Island | Land completely surrounded by water |
| Landslide | The sliding down of soil and rock on a steep slope |

---

# IMPORTANT FACTS TO REMEMBER

- **Rwenzori** is the highest mountain in Uganda (**Margherita Peak, 5,109 m**) and is a **block** mountain.
- **Elgon, Muhavura and Moroto** are **volcanic** mountains.
- **Lake Victoria** is the largest lake; **Lake Bunyonyi** is very deep; **Lake Kyoga** is shallow and swampy.
- **Lakes Albert, Edward and George** lie in the **western arm of the Great Rift Valley**.
- The **Nile leaves Lake Victoria at Jinja** and flows **north**.
- **Murchison (Kabalega) Falls** is on the Victoria Nile.
- **Bududa landslides** happen because people farm on steep, cleared slopes.

# EXAMINATION POINTS

- The highest mountain in Uganda → **Rwenzori**; its highest peak → **Margherita**.
- A block mountain in Uganda → **Rwenzori**. A volcanic one → **Elgon** (or Muhavura).
- The lake formed in the mouth of a volcano → a **crater lake**, e.g. Katwe.
- The waterfall on the Victoria Nile → **Murchison (Kabalega) Falls**.
- The channel joining Lakes Edward and George → the **Kazinga Channel**.
- One problem caused by mountains → difficult and costly road building (or landslides).
- One way of caring for hills → planting trees and digging terraces.

# REVISION QUESTIONS

1. What are physical features? Name any six found in Uganda.
2. Name the highest mountain in Uganda, its highest peak and its height.
3. Name any three volcanic mountains in Uganda and say where each is.
4. Give the difference between a block mountain and a volcanic mountain, with one Ugandan example of each.
5. What is a rift valley? Name three lakes found in Uganda's rift valley.
6. Name any five lakes in Uganda and say how each type was formed.
7. What is a crater lake? Give one example in Uganda.
8. Trace the course of the River Nile through Uganda, naming its two parts.
9. Name any four waterfalls in Uganda and the rivers on which they are found.
10. Name any three islands of Uganda.
11. Give any five ways in which physical features help the people of Uganda.
12. Give any four problems caused by physical features.
13. Why do landslides happen at Bududa, and how can they be prevented?
14. Give any five ways of caring for physical features.
15. Draw the map of Uganda and mark on it Mt Rwenzori, Lake Victoria and Murchison Falls.

# ANSWERS TO REVISION QUESTIONS

1. Physical features are the natural land forms found on the earth's surface. Any six of: mountains, hills, plateaux, plains, rift valleys, lakes, rivers, waterfalls, swamps, islands.
2. **Mt Rwenzori**; its highest peak is **Margherita**, about **5,109 metres** high.
3. Any three: **Mt Elgon** — Mbale in the east; **Muhavura** — Kisoro in the south-west; **Moroto** — Karamoja in the north-east (also Kadam, Napak).
4. A **block mountain** is formed when land between two faults is pushed up or the sides sink, for example **Rwenzori**; a **volcanic mountain** is formed when molten rock comes out of the earth, cools and piles up, for example **Elgon**.
5. A rift valley is a deep, wide valley formed when the land between two faults sinks. Lakes: **Albert, Edward and George**.
6. Any five, for example: **Victoria and Kyoga** — down-warping; **Albert, Edward and George** — faulting; **Katwe** — volcanic (crater); a lake behind a dam — man-made.
7. A crater lake is a lake formed in the mouth of an old volcano. Example: **Lake Katwe** (also Nyamunuka).
8. The Nile leaves **Lake Victoria at Jinja** as the **Victoria Nile**, flows through **Lake Kyoga**, over **Murchison Falls** into **Lake Albert**, and then flows north as the **Albert Nile** into South Sudan.
9. Any four: **Murchison (Kabalega) Falls** and **Karuma Falls** — the Victoria Nile; **Bujagali** and **Owen Falls** — the Nile near Jinja; **Sipi Falls** — the River Sipi on Mt Elgon.
10. Any three of: the **Ssese Islands**, **Buvuma**, **Koome** (all in Lake Victoria).
11. Any five of: mountains give relief rainfall and fertile soils; lakes and rivers give water, fish and transport; rivers give hydro-electric power; features attract tourists; swamps give papyrus, clay and water; they form natural boundaries.
12. Any four of: mountains make road building hard; steep slopes cause soil erosion and landslides; rivers and lakes flood; swamps breed mosquitoes and bilharzia snails; some areas are cut off in the rains.
13. Because people have **cleared the trees and farm on very steep slopes** on Mt Elgon, so heavy rain loosens the bare soil and it slides. Prevention: plant trees and grass on the slopes, dig terraces and contour trenches, avoid cultivating very steep land, and resettle people from dangerous slopes.
14. Any five of: plant trees on hills and river banks; dig terraces and contour trenches; do not build or farm in swamps; do not pour waste into lakes and rivers; avoid bush burning; restore quarried land; obey environmental laws.
15. *Learner's map.* Rwenzori in the west, Lake Victoria in the south, and Murchison Falls on the Victoria Nile in the north-west.
